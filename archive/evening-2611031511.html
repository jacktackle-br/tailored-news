Election 2026 · Exit Polls

Early exit polls show an electorate unhappy with Trump and the economy as the first polls close

+

The first Election Day exit-poll numbers are out, and they show a sour mood. NBC News’s exit poll put President Trump’s approval among voters at 38%. CBS News said that is on track to be the lowest approval of any president at a midterm. NBC found that 43% of voters said one reason for their vote was to oppose Trump, far more than in 2018. Three in four said the economy is in bad shape, 65% said Trump’s policies had made it worse, and just under eight in ten said inflation is causing hardship for their families. The economy was the top issue at 39%, followed by the state of democracy at 32%. CNN’s early numbers broadly match. They show about two-thirds saying Trump has made the economy worse, and a slight Democratic edge on who voters trust to handle it.

The first polls closed at 6 p.m. Eastern in parts of Indiana and Kentucky. Most of Georgia, South Carolina, Virginia and Vermont close at 7 p.m., followed by North Carolina, Ohio and West Virginia at 7:30. NPR warns that early exit-poll figures are weighted and revised as real votes come in. Its analysts say to wait for actual returns before drawing conclusions, and its early races to watch include Kentucky’s 6th District and Indiana’s 5th, where Rep. Victoria Spartz (R) faces J.D. Ford (D). These are preliminary network exit polls, not counted votes. No AP or Reuters exit-poll write-up could be reached for this edition.

Election 2026 · Voting

Voting runs mostly smoothly, with scattered machine glitches and flooding, as experts shift their worry to the count

+

By late afternoon Eastern time, no widespread disruption had been reported, and there were no confirmed cases of immigration agents at polling places. Problems were local. In North Carolina, officials reported technical issues at precincts in four counties, including Wilson, and some precincts were expected to stay open late, delaying early results. In Westchester County, New York, a programming error stopped ballot scanners from working, so officials reprogrammed backup machines to scan paper ballots. Chester County, Pennsylvania, kept three polling places open until 8:30 p.m. Heavy rain and flooding in the Southwest led the Navajo Nation to move several polling places, and rural San Juan County, Utah, let some voters cast ballots electronically.

NPR reports that nearly 60 million people voted before Election Day with hardly any problems. Election-security expert David Becker told NPR he is confident about the voting itself and more worried about what happens after the polls close, when slow but normal counts in states such as California and Alaska could be presented as evidence of fraud. DHS Secretary Markwayne Mullin has said immigration agents will “absolutely not” intimidate voters and would appear only if there were a threat. Federal law (18 U.S.C. § 592) bars sending armed federal personnel to polling places. Advocacy groups in Arizona and elsewhere still trained volunteers to watch polling sites.

Iran War · Diplomacy

Tehran says a deal is still far off as Qatari mediators carry a draft to Tehran and back, and Iran braces for a possible post-midterm attack

+

A senior Iranian official told Reuters on Tuesday that Iran and the United States are still far from a deal to end the war, a day after the U.S. strikes on Qeshm Island. Qatari mediators flew to Tehran on Monday night to discuss a version of a draft deal and then returned to Doha. Qatar’s foreign ministry spokesman, Majed al-Ansari, said both sides remain committed to the mediation. Iran’s stated price for reopening the Strait of Hormuz is a waiver of oil sanctions, an end to the U.S. naval blockade and the release of frozen funds. The official said nuclear talks could start only after those conditions are met. President Pezeshkian said the supreme leader had given the government “certain authorities” over talks with Washington. Parliament speaker Ghalibaf warned that Iran’s response to any U.S. “miscalculation” would be unpredictable.

Washington’s timing is the key question. Trump has said he would not start new attacks before the vote and has called the talks productive, but he has not ruled out military action afterward. NPR reports that Iran is preparing for renewed strikes once the midterms are over, and that Gulf states fear another round would hit their own territory and energy exports. Semafor likewise describes the region as watching for signs of war. The two sides’ core demands are still far apart, so the next 36 hours of feedback to Qatar are the near-term test.

Europe · Cyber

Apparent cyberattacks knock out supermarket freezers across northern Europe, the second wave in a month, and investigators look at Iran

+

Supermarkets in Belgium, Luxembourg, Sweden, Ireland and Estonia have reported refrigeration failures since Sunday night. NPR also lists the Netherlands and Finland. Ahold Delhaize and Colruyt said an overnight IT incident hit fridges and freezers in dozens of stores in Belgium and Luxembourg. Carrefour and Intermarché each said about 15 Belgian stores were affected, Musgrave reported 13 SuperValu stores in Ireland, and Swedish grocers ICA and Coop were also hit. The head of the Belgian refrigeration contractor Sabcobel blamed a cyberattack that switched evaporators into defrost mode. The incidents follow an attack in mid-October that sent temperatures in hundreds of French supermarkets above 50°C (122°F). French investigators tied that attack to vulnerable Danfoss remote-control systems.

No one has claimed or been formally blamed for the attacks. NPR, citing two former officials, reports that investigators are treating the outages as a cyberattack and that some evidence points toward Iran, though with low confidence. NPR links the pattern to a May attack on an Israeli food plant and to outages at U.S. military commissaries in August. If confirmed, it would be a campaign against civilian infrastructure in the West that runs alongside the Iran war, chosen to cause economic damage and public unease without bloodshed. The Washington Post separately reports a British official warning that AI-assisted attacks on critical infrastructure are coming by 2028.

Sources: NPR, Nov 3, NPR, Nov 2, Washington Post, Nov 2, CNBC, Nov 3, Washington Post, Nov 2

Election 2026 · Election Day

Polls open in the midterms as the final Reuters/Ipsos survey puts Trump’s approval at 32% and the Democrats’ generic-ballot lead at eight points

+

Voting began early Tuesday in the midterm elections, with the first polls opening in Vermont. All 435 House seats, 35 Senate seats and dozens of governorships are on the ballot. Republicans hold the Senate 53–47, so Democrats need a net gain of four seats to take control. More than 54 million ballots were cast before Election Day. The first polls close at 6 p.m. Eastern in parts of Indiana and Kentucky, and the last, in Alaska’s Aleutian Islands, at 1 a.m. Eastern. NPR warns that with Senate margins this close, control of that chamber may not be known for days.

The final Reuters/Ipsos poll before the vote, completed Monday, found President Trump’s approval at 32%, one point above the record low it recorded two weeks earlier. Among registered voters, Democrats led the generic congressional ballot 46% to 38%. A week earlier their lead was 11 points. Reuters links part of the Republicans’ weakness to the rise in fuel prices since the Iran war began in February. NPR’s election-night guide says to watch the early-closing states for signs of a Democratic wave, and to watch youth and Latino turnout. Wayne County, Michigan, and the Dallas suburbs are among its bellwethers. Asked a week ago whether he would accept the results, Trump said he would “if they’re honest elections.”

Election 2026 · Federal Monitors

The Justice Department sends a record 1,000-plus monitors to polling places as states set limits on their access

+

The Justice Department says it is sending more than 1,000 election monitors to all 50 states on Tuesday. It sent 289 in the 2022 midterms and 714 in 2024. It has not published a full list of places and says it will release one on Election Day. In Pennsylvania the monitors are going to Philadelphia, Allegheny, Luzerne and Lehigh counties. The department says monitors “will not be acting as law enforcement personnel” and will only watch for possible violations of federal law. It also says they will not interfere with voting.

Several states have set limits. Pennsylvania’s Department of State says monitors have no more right to enter polling places than the general public. Michigan says it treats them as poll watchers. Colorado, Indiana and Wyoming say they will keep monitors a set distance from polling places. The NAACP calls the deployment voter intimidation. A temporary restraining order that a federal judge in San Francisco, Trina Thompson, issued on October 30 also remains in force until November 16. It bars the Justice Department and Homeland Security from threatening to prosecute election workers who count ballots that the administration says are ineligible. The administration is appealing.

Iran · Strait of Hormuz

U.S. officials confirm limited “defensive” strikes on Iran’s Qeshm Island, and oil climbs back after Monday’s 7.6% plunge

+

U.S. forces have carried out limited strikes on Qeshm Island, the largest island in the Strait of Hormuz, U.S. officials said late Monday. They described the strikes as defensive, a response to Iranian surface-to-air missile fire at American aircraft, and said they were “not a resumption of major combat operations.” The Wall Street Journal reported that the strikes are part of a series of limited, deliberately unannounced U.S. attacks in recent weeks. The Pentagon has not announced them, and Central Command did not respond to requests for comment. Iranian state media reported explosions on the island. The Revolutionary Guards claimed to have downed a U.S. drone, which Central Command called false. None of these claims has been independently verified.

The strikes came hours after oil had its biggest one-day fall since July. Brent fell 7.6% on Monday to $104.77 a barrel after the U.S. and Iran both signalled openness to a diplomatic deal and a draft proposal circulated after the Doha talks. Brent rose to about $105 early Tuesday on news of the strikes. Iran says the outcome now depends on Washington’s response to its shortened, two-day plan to reopen the strait. Analysts note that the market has sold off on peace rumours before, only for the war to escalate again.

Russia · Ukraine

Witkoff says the U.S. will mediate Russia–Ukraine talks in Abu Dhabi on November 10–11, the first round since February

+

U.S. envoy Steve Witkoff said Monday that Washington will mediate working-level talks between Russia and Ukraine in Abu Dhabi on November 10 and 11. He said the agenda will cover post-war security, de-escalation, humanitarian issues and “next steps” toward peace. Witkoff and Jared Kushner will lead the U.S. side. The last round of U.S.-mediated three-way talks was in February, before the war with Iran interrupted the process. In October, Putin told Witkoff he agreed “in principle” to resume them.

Expectations are low. Secretary of State Marco Rubio said the U.S. is not “claiming that we’re on the verge of a negotiated settlement,” and analysts say the gaps over a ceasefire and security guarantees remain wide. The announcement came as Ukrainian officials raised the death toll from Russia’s bombing of an aid centre in Zaporizhzhia region to 26. President Zelenskyy said Ukrainian intelligence expects a large Russian strike on energy and logistics targets timed to the U.S. midterms.

Israel · After the Election

Bennett and the Arab parties line up behind Eisenkot as Herzog prepares to start coalition talks on Wednesday

+

President Isaac Herzog will begin consultations with Israel’s 11 Knesset factions on Wednesday, starting with the smallest. His office says the aim is to “encourage thinking outside the box” after last month’s election left neither bloc with a majority. Naftali Bennett’s party says it will recommend Gadi Eisenkot, leader of the Yashar party, to form the next government. The Joint List and Ra’am are also expected to back him. Avigdor Liberman has so far refused to endorse Eisenkot, and he is the main obstacle.

Eisenkot, Bennett and Liberman all campaigned against governing with Arab parties. One option under discussion is a government that survives because United Torah Judaism and Ra’am abstain. Herzog must name a first candidate by November 11. If that candidate fails, he can give the task to another Knesset member for 28 days. If that also fails, Israel is likely headed for another election. Over the weekend, thousands rallied in Tel Aviv urging Eisenkot to form a Jewish–Arab coalition.

Tech · Chips

Marvell agrees to buy Lumentum in a $115 billion all-stock deal for the optical links inside AI data centres

+

Chipmaker Marvell Technology said Monday it will buy Lumentum, which makes lasers and optical parts, in an all-stock deal that values Lumentum at $115 billion including debt. The equity value is about $102.5 billion. Each Lumentum share will become 4.5 Marvell shares, a 23.9% premium to the price before reports of the talks. Marvell shareholders will own about 67% of the combined company. Marvell CEO Matt Murphy will run it, and Lumentum CEO Michael Hurlston will join the board. The companies say the deal gives them a combined market of $550 billion and will save more than $1 billion a year.

The aim is to own more of the optical links that connect the chips in AI data centres. Investors were not convinced. Both stocks fell sharply, Marvell by more than 10%, on a day when worries about an AI bubble hit the Nasdaq. The deal is expected to close in the first half of 2028 if shareholders and regulators approve. Marvell would owe Lumentum a $3.2 billion break fee if regulators block it.

Biomedicine · HIV

FDA clears the first generic of the twice-yearly HIV prevention shot, priced at $40 a year for poorer countries

+

The FDA has given tentative approval to Dr. Reddy’s Laboratories for a generic version of lenacapavir, Gilead’s injectable HIV prevention drug, which is given every six months. “Tentative” is the FDA’s standard label for a generic while the original is still under patent in the U.S. It lets U.S. government programmes buy the drug for use abroad. Under a 2025 deal with Unitaid, the Clinton Health Access Initiative and Wits RHI, Dr. Reddy’s will sell it in 120 low- and middle-income countries for $40 per person per year, about the cost of daily PrEP pills. The branded version, Yeztugo, costs an estimated $28,000 a year in the U.S.

STAT reports that the company plans to start shipping next month. Countries that require their own approval may take longer. In trials, lenacapavir almost completely prevented HIV infection, and public-health groups see a cheap twice-yearly shot as one of the best tools for cutting new infections. Generics from other licensed manufacturers have not yet been approved.

Electrification · Trucks

Electric trucks pass half of China’s heavy-truck sales for the first time in a month, and diesel demand falls

+

China sold 33,100 battery-electric heavy trucks in September, 50.3% of all heavy-truck sales, Electrek reported Monday. Sales for the year to date are 226,400, up 64% on last year. Analytics firm Kpler estimates that LNG and electric trucks together are displacing about 680,000 barrels a day of diesel in China this year, about 230,000 of that from electric trucks. Kpler expects China’s diesel demand to fall by about 230,000 barrels a day in 2026.

The effect on fuel is coming faster than the fleet itself is changing. Kpler expects electric trucks to make up only about 8% of China’s heavy-truck fleet by the end of the year, but heavy trucks drive long distances and burn much more fuel than cars. Earlier counts that put the share above half included plug-in hybrids and range-extenders. The 50.3% figure is for battery-electric trucks only. With oil above $100 a barrel because of the Iran war, the switch also cuts China’s exposure to the Gulf.

Sources: Electrek, Nov 2, electrive, Jan (background)

Premier League · Monday Night

Barry punishes a goalkeeping error as Everton win 1–0 at Newcastle, their second win over them in four days

+

Everton won 1–0 at St James’ Park on Monday night, their first away league win of the season. Thierno Barry scored four minutes into the second half, tapping in after Newcastle goalkeeper Lukas Hornicek spilled the ball.
