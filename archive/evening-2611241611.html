Congress · Nvidia · New Today

Three House GOP chairmen say Huang’s return “does not erase” the national-security questions, as more Republicans break with Trump

+

A day after Nvidia’s board reinstated Jensen Huang following President Trump’s intervention, Republican criticism grew on Tuesday. House Intelligence Chair Rick Crawford, Foreign Affairs Chair Brian Mast and China Select Committee Chair John Moolenaar said in a joint statement that “the NVIDIA board’s brief internal investigation and reinstatement of Jensen Huang do not erase the national security threats and questions that must be addressed.” They said Huang and Nvidia general counsel Tim Teter have confirmed they will sit for transcribed interviews on Dec 1 and Dec 2, and Mast has said he will subpoena Huang if he does not appear voluntarily. Sens. John Cornyn, Pete Ricketts and John Curtis and Reps. Don Bacon and Pat Harrigan also criticized or questioned the move; Harrigan called it a “mistake.” Sen. Chris Coons asked what role Huang played in the sale of advanced AI chips. A public Senate hearing is reported for Dec 3.

Attorney General Todd Blanche declined on Fox News on Monday night to say whether Huang had been cleared of national-security concerns or possible legal violations, and the Justice Department will not confirm whether there is an investigation. Trump brushed off the Republican criticism: “When I speak to them, they’re going to be great.”

White House · Honors

Trump gives Rudy Giuliani the Presidential Medal of Freedom at a ceremony closed to the press

+

President Trump awarded former New York City Mayor Rudy Giuliani, 82, the Presidential Medal of Freedom on Tuesday at a small White House gathering of relatives, friends and supporters. The press pool was not invited, and the White House did not say why. The White House cited Giuliani’s record as a federal prosecutor against organized crime and his leadership after the Sept. 11 attacks. Coverage noted that Giuliani was disbarred in New York and Washington, D.C., over false claims about the 2020 election, that he faces a $148 million defamation judgment over false claims about two Georgia election workers, and that Trump pardoned him last year for potential federal crimes. Israel · Gaza · Ceasefire

Israel kills Hamas’s internal-security chief in a Gaza City strike, the second leader of that service it has taken out in three months

+

An Israeli airstrike in western Gaza City on Tuesday killed Naim Abu Nahal (also spelled Abu Nahel), head of Hamas’s internal security apparatus, according to both Israel and Hamas. Several other people were reported wounded. Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu and Defense Minister Israel Katz said he had been advancing attacks on Israeli soldiers and civilians and helping armed groups rebuild in violation of the ceasefire. They said his predecessor, Muayyan al-Arabid, was captured by Israeli forces about three months ago. Hamas confirmed the death, called the strike a war crime and accused Israel of trying to “foment chaos” in Gaza. The internal-security service is the arm that pursues Palestinians suspected of collaborating with Israel.

Analysis: The strike shows the pattern of the truce since October 2025: Israeli strikes almost every day, and Gaza’s health ministry counts at least 1,679 people killed since it began. Going after Hamas’s policing arm, rather than only its military wing, cuts at Hamas’s hold on territory west of the “yellow line.” It also complicates the handover to the stabilization and governance setup that the U.S.-backed Board of Peace warned last week was at risk of collapse. Netanyahu still says Israel will withdraw only once Hamas has fully disarmed.

Ukraine · Russia · Libya

Kyiv calls its sea-drone strike on Russian ships in Libya lawful self-defense, as Russian strikes on Zaporizhzhia kill two

+

Ukraine’s Foreign Ministry said on Tuesday that its drone attack on Russian vessels in Benghazi, which reportedly sank the corvette Merkuriy and damaged the cargo ship Angara, was self-defense under Article 51 of the UN Charter and was aimed only at Russian military targets. Foreign Minister Andrii Sybiha argued that the right of self-defense is not limited to the territory where the aggression takes place. Libya’s Tripoli-based Government of National Unity condemned the strike as a violation of Libyan sovereignty. The eastern authorities in Benghazi, aligned with Russia-backed commander Khalifa Haftar, said the drones appeared to have been launched from western Libya and asked the UN to investigate. Separately, President Zelensky confirmed a drone strike on the sanctioned Russian cargo ship Sparta II in the Gulf of Finland. Overnight, AP reported that a Russian attack on Zaporizhzhia killed two civilians and wounded 44, including four children, and Poland said a Russian drone briefly entered its airspace.

Analysis: Kyiv is now hitting Russian ships far from the Black Sea, in North Africa and the Baltic. That raises the cost of Russia’s logistics links to Haftar’s Libya, but it opens a new diplomatic front with a Libyan government that Ukraine needs on its side. At the same time Kyiv says it is short of air-defense interceptors after a Financial Times report that a U.S. Patriot transfer has stalled; the Pentagon says its policy has not changed. Putin has ruled out any limited energy or maritime ceasefire, even as U.S.-brokered talks continue.

White House · Nvidia · National Security

Nvidia’s board reinstates Jensen Huang after Trump says “I made the deal,” and lawmakers in both parties demand answers

+

Nvidia’s board voted unanimously on Monday to reinstate Jensen Huang as president, chief executive and a board member, effective immediately. Huang had resigned on Nov 12 after reports of an affair with a woman U.S. officials suspect of working for Chinese intelligence; that suspicion has not been proven, and Huang has not been accused of a crime. In its filing, the board said it acted after receiving “input from senior levels of the United States government” that his return “poses no national security risk,” and said its internal committee’s investigation will continue. The board’s chair had said on last week’s earnings call that it had no intention of bringing Huang back. President Trump told CNBC on Monday that he had spoken to the board “directly and indirectly” and “convinced them,” adding, “I made the deal.” CNBC, citing people familiar with the board’s thinking, reported that the decision was sealed by Trump’s personal assurance on Sunday that Huang had not broken national security laws. Nvidia shares closed up about 31% and the Nasdaq rose about 6.5%, its best day since April 2025.

Criticism came from both parties. Senate Democratic leader Chuck Schumer said Democrats would use “full power” to investigate any wrongdoing by the administration, Huang or other Nvidia executives, and on the Republican side Sen. Scott said the government owes the public answers about what happened. The Senate Banking Committee said Huang will testify at a public hearing in December, and he is also reported to be scheduled for a closed-door session with three House committees. On the same day, the Commerce Department added Megaspeed, the Singapore chip firm linked to the woman at the center of the case, to its trade blacklist (see Science & Tech).

Justice Department · Death Penalty · California

At the Justice Department, Trump attacks Newsom over 548 death-row commutations and says he will order prosecutors to seek death for child murderers

+

President Trump spoke at Justice Department headquarters on Monday at an event billed as “protecting American families.” Most of the speech was aimed at California Gov. Gavin Newsom, a likely 2028 Democratic candidate, who commuted the death sentences of 548 prisoners. Trump called the commutations “one of the most shocking acts of injustice our country has ever seen” and handed the microphone to relatives of murder victims, who challenged Newsom to defend the decision to them. Newsom said “to the families standing with him today, your grief is real,” and that everyone on California’s former death row will die in prison. He called the Justice Department “not Donald Trump’s law firm.” Most of the commutations still need approval from the California Supreme Court, and the Criminal Justice Legal Foundation has said it will challenge them there.

Iran · Hormuz · Sanctions

Iran’s Guards claim three tankers hit mines in Hormuz despite a U.S. escort, as Treasury hits Iran’s shadow-banking network

+

Iran’s Revolutionary Guards said on Monday that three oil tankers they called “violating” caught fire after hitting naval mines in the Strait of Hormuz, one near the southern coast and two in the eastern part of the strait. They said the ships were under U.S. air cover and escort, which “failed to prevent” the blasts, and warned that U.S. escorts do not exempt shipowners from Iran’s rules for the strait. There was no independent confirmation of the fires or the mines, and U.S. Central Command had not responded. The U.S. has disputed earlier IRGC mine claims. Reported attacks in and around the strait were higher in October and November than in any month since the U.S. and Israel attacked Iran in late February.

The same day, the Treasury Department sanctioned 22 entities and eight people it described as part of Iran’s shadow-banking network, including exchange houses, front companies and the Iran Center for Exchange of Currency and Gold, which Treasury says handles tens of billions of dollars in trade a year. The action falls under “Operation Economic Outcast,” the pressure campaign Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent began in August, and Treasury said it will widen secondary-sanctions exposure for firms that keep dealing with Iran. Despite the threats, oil fell (see Science & Tech): rising supply elsewhere is outweighing the risk in the Gulf.

EU · Russia · Sanctions

Brussels proposes a 22nd Russia sanctions package: about 60 more banks off SWIFT, 70-plus shadow-fleet ships, and a ban on Russian teams competing under their flag

+

The European Commission on Monday proposed its 22nd package of sanctions on Russia. It would extend the transaction ban to about 60 more Russian banks, cutting them off from the SWIFT payments messaging system, and target crypto and payment networks used to get around sanctions. More than 70 shadow-fleet tankers would be added to the list, and the Commission wants to ban all tankers insured in Russia. EU companies would be barred from helping Russia build, operate or repair energy infrastructure, including refineries. Former Russian combatants in Ukraine would be banned from entering the EU, and EU citizens would no longer be allowed to sit on the governing bodies of companies in Russia.

The most unusual measure would bar Russian teams and athletes from competing under the Russian flag at events in EU countries, and ban official Russian symbols there. This comes after the International Olympic Committee lifted its suspension of Russian athletes in July, ahead of the 2028 Los Angeles Games. All 27 member states must approve the package. Past packages have been held up by single governments, and China’s role as the main route around sanctions is again a sticking point. Euronews reports that Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky is due at a special EU summit in Brussels later this week.

Chips · Export Controls

Commerce blacklists Megaspeed and six affiliates over suspected Nvidia chip diversion, as the intelligence chief says China appears to have gotten the chips

+

The Commerce Department on Monday added Singapore-based Megaspeed International and six affiliates in Singapore, Malaysia and Thailand to its Entity List. It cited a significant risk that they could divert advanced computing products to unauthorized users. Megaspeed is the company formerly linked to Alice Huang, the woman at the center of the Nvidia scandal. Commerce investigators had asked Nvidia for Megaspeed sales records in May 2025, and Nvidia put the firm on its internal no-sell list the following month, but the government had not blacklisted it until now. The Financial Times reported this month that Commerce had postponed new Entity List additions. Bessent has said other agencies backed listing Megaspeed for months, but that “Commerce has the pen.”

Reports disagree on how much the listing restricts. Semafor quotes Commerce saying it is now unlawful to export advanced chips to Megaspeed “even if the chips are produced outside the United States.” Wccftech reports that the rule leaves out the foreign direct product rule, which would matter because Nvidia’s chips are made in Taiwan. Director of National Intelligence Jay Clayton told CNN that “it appears … that there was access to those chips, yes,” but did not say which chips or how many. He confirmed that investigations into Alice Huang and Megaspeed are active, and the Justice Department is examining whether Megaspeed broke export controls.

Biomedicine · Cancer

FDA approves Roche’s giredestrant after surgery for early breast cancer, the first new endocrine therapy in that setting in more than 20 years

+

The FDA on Monday approved Roche’s Wilserdly (giredestrant), a daily pill, to be taken after surgery by adults with estrogen receptor-positive, HER2-negative stage I–III breast cancer at intermediate to high risk of recurrence. This is the most common form of the disease. The approval rests on the phase III lidERA trial of more than 4,100 patients. Giredestrant cut the risk of invasive recurrence or death by 30% compared with standard endocrine therapy (tamoxifen or an aromatase inhibitor): 140 patients on giredestrant and 196 on standard therapy had such an event. Fewer patients stopped treatment because of side effects (5.3% vs 8.2%).

Giredestrant is an oral SERD, a class of drugs that degrades the estrogen receptor instead of only blocking it. It was first approved a week earlier, on Nov 16, in combination with everolimus for advanced breast cancer with ESR1 mutations. Roche says it is now the only SERD approved for both early and advanced disease. Adjuvant endocrine therapy is usually taken for five years or more, so the early-stage approval opens a much larger market than the advanced-disease approval.

Energy · Oil Markets

Oil drops to a three-month low as returning supply outweighs the threat in Hormuz

+

Brent crude fell about 3.5% on Monday to settle a little above $89 a barrel, its lowest close since Aug 31, and slipped further below $89 in Asian trading on Tuesday. U.S. crude fell to about $80. The drop came even as Iran’s Guards claimed three tankers had hit mines in the Strait of Hormuz. Traders are focused on supply: Libya’s Sharara pipeline has reopened, and waivers on Russian oil sanctions, meant to keep markets supplied during the Gulf crisis, have widened the pool of ships and suppliers available to India and China. Reported settlement prices differ slightly between outlets.

Flows through Hormuz have still fallen, to an average of about 9.5 million barrels a day in the 10 days to Nov 19, from 12.2 million in October. Before the war, the strait carried about 20 million barrels a day. JPMorgan expects persistent oversupply to keep Brent in the high $70s in the first half of 2027. That is a sign the market now sees the disruption as something it can work around, not a shock that will push prices up again.
