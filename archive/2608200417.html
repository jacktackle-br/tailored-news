Trump’s endorsement record takes its worst hit of 2026 as Rep. Cory Mills and other backed candidates fall

+

In the Aug. 18 primaries across Alaska, Florida and Wyoming, several Trump-endorsed Republicans lost, pushing the president’s 2026 primary-endorsement defeats to roughly 10 candidates for statewide or congressional office — equal, by CNN and NBC tallies, to his combined losses across 2018, 2020 and 2024, though still short of the 12 he absorbed in 2022. Courts · DOJ

DOJ defends its Comey ‘86 47’ prosecution, calling presidential control of prosecutors ‘a feature, not a bug’

+

In more than 100 pages of filings entered late Tuesday, Aug. 18 and reported Aug. 19, the Justice Department urged a court not to dismiss its case against former FBI Director James Comey over a May 2025 Instagram post showing seashells arranged as “86 47.” Prosecutors wrote there is “no serious dispute that an objective viewer” could read the post to mean “Kill President Trump,” and suggested Comey staged it to promote a forthcoming novel. The filings — the government’s most detailed defense yet — responded to Comey’s motions to dismiss on grounds including vindictive and selective prosecution and that the post was not a “true threat.” Notably, DOJ argued that presidential direction of prosecutors is “a feature, not a bug, of our constitutional system.”

Comey’s defense contends investigators misled judges, withheld facts and never showed he intended a physical threat or knew “86” could connote violence; his team also alleges he was surveilled at Trump’s direction. The case sits in the Eastern District of Virginia — the same office where interim U.S. Attorney Lindsey Halligan’s appointment was later ruled unlawful, a thread the vindictive-prosecution claims lean on. (Facts from The Washington Post, Lawfare and CNBC.)

The Left-Leaning Take

The directional left reads the filing as the DOJ saying the quiet part out loud — conceding it is a political instrument of Trump — while stretching an ambiguous beach photo into a capital-threat charge. The New Republic and MSNBC legal coverage cast the whole prosecution as retribution dressed as law, and flag the “Trump controls us, and that’s fine” argument as an authoritarian tell. (Directional.)

The Right-Leaning Take

Even parts of the right have found the case weak, with National Review having argued the indictment “fails to charge a ‘true threat.’” No clean in-window pro-prosecution WSJ/NR editorial was isolated (Directional). The anti-Trump right (intra-right dissent — Bulwark lane) goes furthest: Paul Rosenzweig, writing at The Bulwark, calls the case legally doomed and damaging to the rule of law, citing veteran prosecutor John Gleeson’s view that reading “86” as mob jargon for “kill” is “preposterous.”

Middle East · US–Iran War

The UAE suspends all trade with Iran after a missile scare; Tehran denies firing

+

The United Arab Emirates announced Wednesday, Aug. 19 that it is suspending all trade and financial transactions with Iran, cutting off one of Tehran’s last major export routes as the US–Iran war grinds on. The move followed the UAE Defense Ministry’s report that its air defenses detected two ballistic missiles launched toward the country from Iran on Tuesday night, Aug. 18 — the first nationwide shelter warning to sound in the Emirates in weeks. The suspension is a significant escalation given that the UAE is Iran’s second-largest commercial partner. Iranian Foreign Ministry spokesperson Esmail Baghaei denied that Iran launched any missiles at the UAE, so the trigger is disputed and the launch is not independently verified.

The decision comes against continued Iranian pressure on the Strait of Hormuz. Reporting cites roughly 20 tankers owned by Abu Dhabi’s state-owned ADNOC struck by missiles and drones in the strait since the conflict began, including four in the prior two weeks, with one person killed and about 20 wounded — figures attributed to officials and reporting rather than independent confirmation. Shipping through Hormuz remains far below normal: only six to nine commodity vessels crossed in recent single-day counts (Kpler data), versus a pre-war daily average near 110. Trump said this week the US would tighten financial pressure as talks remain stalled, while Iran’s parliament speaker Mohammad Bagher Ghalibaf laid out conditions Tehran wants met before reopening the strait. (Facts from CNN, Fortune, RTÉ and Al Jazeera.)

Science · Astronomy

Astronomers clock S301, the Milky Way’s fastest known star, whipping around Sagittarius A* at 15,500 miles a second

+

In a study published in Nature and announced by the European Southern Observatory on Aug. 19, astronomers report S301, a faint main-sequence star whipping around Sagittarius A* — the Milky Way’s four-million-solar-mass central black hole — at a peak of about 25,000 km/s, or roughly 15,500 miles per second. Detected with the GRAVITY instrument on ESO’s Very Large Telescope Interferometer, S301 completes an orbit in just 8.7 years and, at closest approach, swings in to roughly Saturn’s distance from the Sun, nearer than any star previously tracked.

Because it passes so deep into the black hole’s gravitational field, its orbit is sensitive to frame-dragging — the twisting of spacetime by a rotating mass — meaning that following S301 over its next orbits could yield the first direct measurement of Sagittarius A*’s spin and a fresh test of general relativity. (Facts from ESO, the journal Nature, Space.com and Phys.org.)

Sources: ESO, Nature, Space.com, Phys.org
Energy · Grid

DOE orders a Maryland coal-and-gas unit to keep running past its limits to shore up the mid-Atlantic grid

+

Energy Secretary Chris Wright invoked Section 202(c) of the Federal Power Act to authorize PJM Interconnection, working with Talen Energy, to keep operating Unit 4 at the Wagner Generating Station in Anne Arundel County, Maryland, beyond its permitted environmental operating hours. The order runs Aug. 20 through Nov. 17, 2026. PJM had already leaned on Wagner Unit 4 during this summer’s heat waves — exhausting its allowed hours — and told DOE it still needs the unit on hand to meet peak demand and avert outages across the region.

The move underscores how surging load, driven by heat and data-center growth, is colliding with tight reserve margins and forcing regulators to extend the life of aging fossil units. The same day, Texas’s governor directed grid operator ERCOT and the state Public Utility Commission to audit the data centers waiting in the interconnection queue. Climate · Oceans

Copernicus data show a record marine heat wave cooking the Mediterranean and NE Atlantic

+

Analysis publicized Aug. 19 shows an intense, persistent marine heat wave gripping the western Mediterranean and parts of the northeastern Atlantic through summer 2026. Copernicus Marine Service data put sea-surface anomalies at roughly 6°C above average in the western Mediterranean — worst in the Gulf of Lion off southern France — with the basin averaging about 27°C in July, its hottest July on record, and a buoy near Mallorca hitting around 33°C in early August.

Scientists call it the most extreme July marine-heat-wave conditions on record for the western Mediterranean, the Celtic Sea and the Bay of Biscay–Iberian region, and attribute the bulk of it to human-caused warming — an estimated +2°C in the Mediterranean and about +1.4°C in western European seas — threatening marine ecosystems and coastal communities that depend on them. (Facts from the Copernicus Marine Service, the EU’s Copernicus programme, UPI and The Portugal News.)

Sources: Copernicus Marine Service, Copernicus / EU Space, UPI, The Portugal News
