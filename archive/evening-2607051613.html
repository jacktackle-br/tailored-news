The America Party

Musk says he has formed the “America Party” and spends the Fourth’s aftermath crowdsourcing its platform

+

Elon Musk announced on X on Saturday that he had formed the “America Party” — “to give you back your freedom” — formalizing the third-party threat he first floated during his public rupture with President Trump over the “big, beautiful” budget bill. Through Sunday he solicited policy feedback from X users and sketched a narrow strategy: rather than contest the presidency, target roughly “two or three Senate seats” and “eight to ten House districts” to hold the balance of power on close votes. This is a fresh development since this morning — the effort has moved from a threat to a named entity gathering a platform in real time.

The obstacles are steep and largely structural. The Federal Election Commission lacks the quorum needed to grant a new national committee its status and higher contribution limits — it has been below strength since May, and Trump has not nominated new members after firing its chair — and 50-state ballot access is a multi-year slog; analysts (NOTUS, CBS) judge a functioning national party by the 2026 midterms all but impossible. Republican leaders reacted warily: Speaker Mike Johnson warned Musk “would be splitting our party,” Sen. Jerry Moran called the project a “personal vendetta,” and Majority Leader John Thune allowed Musk-backed candidates could siphon votes — though from which side is unclear. A Newsweek poll found about 40% of respondents at least somewhat likely to back America Party candidates, rising to 57% among male Republicans.

The Left-Leaning Take

A billionaire treating a political party like another acquisition (Vox, Slate, Mother Jones): the venture is unlikely to clear ballot-access reality, and the real story is oligarchic money reshaping politics on a whim. The schadenfreude is undisguised — anything that fractures Trump’s coalition and splits the MAGA vote heading into the midterms is, on this read, a gift.

The Right-Leaning Take

Third parties are spoilers that hand seats to Democrats (National Review, WSJ editorial): the leadership fears — Johnson, Thune — are well founded, and the whole thing reads as a Musk–Trump grudge, not a movement. A libertarian aside (Reason) is warmer to cracking the two-party duopoly but doubts Musk is the vehicle. Intra-right dissent: The Bulwark (anti-Trump right) would welcome anything that weakens Trump while questioning whether Musk has the discipline to sustain it.

Sources: PBS NewsHour, CBS News, Time, The Hill, NOTUS
The Counter-Fourth

“Free America” protests hit all 50 states as the opposition stages a rival vision of the 250th

+

As Trump delivered his storm-delayed Mall address, the anti-Trump opposition mounted the weekend’s largest counterprogramming to the semiquincentennial. “Free America Weekend,” organized by the Women’s March in concert with the grassroots 50501 movement, staged demonstrations in all 50 states across roughly 300 cities on July 4 — the latest in a chain that includes June’s “No Kings” rallies. Organizers aimed at Trump’s immigration crackdown, the dismantling of federal agencies, and the Project 2025 blueprint, and encouraged marches, cookouts and street parties as forms of civic protest. Verified nationwide turnout figures — unavailable this morning — were still being compiled by Sunday evening, but the 50-state footprint is now confirmed.

The protests are the sharp end of a broader argument about who owns the anniversary. Two days earlier, New York Mayor Zohran Mamdani — the democratic socialist whose primary wins Trump has repeatedly invoked in his warnings of a “communist menace” — delivered a dueling 250th address from behind a desk once owned by George Washington, flanked by newly naturalized citizens, rejecting the notion that America “becomes less the more people it welcomes.” The Reuters/Ipsos finding that most Americans — about three-quarters of Democrats and half of Republicans — think the 250th grew too political frames the split.

The Left-Leaning Take

Evidence of a broad, cross-state pro-democracy movement (Guardian opinion, Vox, Mother Jones): a Fourth turned into 50-state protest is the rebuttal to a president who used the milestone to pitch voter restrictions and warn of enemies within. Mamdani’s inclusive vision, on this read, is what a 250th should sound like — the country, not one man.

The Right-Leaning Take

Familiar activist theater (National Review, WSJ editorial): the Women’s March and 50501 turning out their base is not a national verdict, and most Americans simply enjoyed the fireworks. Casting Independence Day as an anti-Trump rally, they argue, is itself the politicization critics claim to lament. A libertarian aside (Reason): peaceable protest and public festivity are both fitting tributes on the Fourth.

Sources: Newsweek, Time, NBC News, CNN

Iran

On the largest day of Khamenei’s funeral, an on-stage eulogist calls for Trump’s death — and successor Mojtaba stays hidden

+

The mourning for slain Supreme Leader Ayatollah Ali Khamenei reached its climax on July 5, with hundreds of thousands filling Tehran and chanting “Death to America” and “Death to Israel.” The development since this morning is the tone: the funeral’s master of ceremonies, poet Mohammad Rasouli, made an explicit call for the U.S. president’s death from the stage — “Why is the biggest bastard in the world still alive?… Why should we not kill the man who killed my imam?… It would be a disgrace if we did not kill your killer” — reported (AP, Washington Post) as the first direct threat to Trump’s life voiced during the rites, delivered as Trump spoke at the Mall a world away. Posters and graffiti at the Grand Mosalla urged death to Prime Minister Netanyahu as well.

President Masoud Pezeshkian and Parliament Speaker Mohammad Bagher Ghalibaf attended, and three of Khamenei’s sons prayed over the coffins — but his named successor, Mojtaba, now filling the supreme-leader role, again failed to appear. No public image of Mojtaba has surfaced since the Feb. 28 strike that killed his father; U.S. Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth said in March he had been “wounded and likely disfigured,” and Reuters reported in April that he was recovering from severe facial and leg injuries. His continued invisibility at the regime’s central show of cohesion leaves the succession’s consolidation an open question even as processions continue toward burial in Mashhad.

Sources: The Hill (AP), Washington Post, CNBC, RFE/RL, Times of Israel
NATO / Ukraine

Two days out, a draft Ankara declaration brands Russia a threat and locks in €140bn for Ukraine

+

Ahead of the July 7–8 NATO summit in Ankara — the alliance’s 36th, and the second hosted by Turkey — a draft final declaration reported by Reuters would name Russia a threat to Euro-Atlantic security and reaffirm an “ironclad commitment” to Article 5 collective defense: “An attack on one is an attack on all.” Europe and Canada are set to pledge €70bn a year in military support to Ukraine across 2026 and 2027 — €140bn over two years — a figure that folds in the defense share of the EU’s €90bn loan plus bilateral commitments rather than representing all new money. All 32 allies attend; Trump participates and is slated for a July 8 bilateral with Zelensky, who joins as an invited guest.

The summit lands directly atop the diplomacy flagged in this morning’s brief: Trump’s 90-minute July 4 call with Putin, in which the Kremlin said he “reaffirmed his readiness to facilitate” an end to the war. That is the tension worth watching in Ankara. Trump arrives cast by Moscow as a would-be broker eager to help wind the war down, while the alliance around him prepares to codify Russia as a durable threat and commit two years of funding to Kyiv. Secretary General Mark Rutte — who has warned Russia could be ready to attack NATO by 2030 — has built the agenda around higher defense spending, industrial output and Ukraine, and how squarely Trump aligns with that posture will be the summit’s real headline.

Sources: Reuters via U.S. News, Ukrainska Pravda, Turkish Minute, NATO
Gaza

Trump’s postwar Gaza plan stalls as both Hamas and Netanyahu play for time

+

The transition envisioned in Trump’s postwar plan for Gaza remains stuck. Hamas is edging toward dissolving its governing body in favor of a technocratic National Committee for the Administration of Gaza, meant to run the Strip under the U.S.-backed “Board of Peace” — but the committee has not entered Gaza amid Hamas’s continued refusal to disarm. Israeli reporting (Times of Israel) describes both sides stalling: Hamas is betting that Netanyahu treats any concession on Gaza as political suicide ahead of a Knesset election, and is content to let the indirect talks drift, having made little meaningful progress in months.

The October 2025 ceasefire holds unevenly, with repeated violations logged on both sides and a Palestinian death toll now topping 66,000. Netanyahu has downplayed his own announcements about advancing to a “next phase,” calling one such statement a mainly “declarative move” — a signal that the diplomacy Washington keeps touting is, for now, more architecture than momentum. It is the quieter counterpoint to the week’s louder Iran and Ukraine files: a war paused rather than ended, with the political incentives on both sides pointing toward delay.

Sources: PBS NewsHour, Times of Israel, Time

The 250th Speech

Trump caps the 250th with a storm-delayed, campaign-style Mall address vowing to end mail-in voting

+

President Trump delivered the headline “Salute to America 250” address on the National Mall late Saturday night, taking the stage around 11:15 p.m. after storms forced organizers to evacuate the crowd earlier in the evening. Wire and network reporting (NPR, CNBC, PBS NewsHour, Washington Post) described the remarks as “darkly political” and campaign-style — a departure from the traditionally unifying July 4 addresses of prior presidents. Trump thanked veterans, displayed several antique U.S. flags (including one he said had draped Abraham Lincoln's coffin), praised the founders and his war with Iran, and warned repeatedly of a “communist menace.” He used the platform to push his stalled SAVE America Act, telling the crowd that “all voters must show voter ID… proof of citizenship” and that “there will be no mail-in ballots, except for illness, disability, military deployment or travel.”

The vow followed a July 3 kickoff speech at Mount Rushmore in which Trump called communism “a mortal threat to American liberty… the greatest threat to our country.” The SAVE Act remains stalled in Congress — Democrats are uniformly opposed, and passage would likely require eliminating the filibuster — and the push lands after a recent Supreme Court ruling that mail-in ballots postmarked by Election Day must be counted, a decision Trump cited as reason to pass the bill. A Reuters/Ipsos poll found a majority of Americans, including about three-quarters of Democrats and half of Republicans, believe the 250th commemorations have become too political.

The Left-Leaning Take

A national milestone bent into a voter-suppression pitch (Mother Jones, Slate, Vox, Guardian opinion): the mail-in-ballot vow and the proof-of-citizenship rule are, on this read, a pre-midterm attempt to shrink the electorate, and voting-rights groups warn the SAVE Act's documentation requirements could trip up millions of eligible citizens. Turning the Fourth into a grievance rally, they argue, is the opposite of what a semiquincentennial should be.

The Right-Leaning Take

Common-sense election integrity, and a birthday worth celebrating (National Review, WSJ editorial): requiring ID and proof of citizenship is broadly popular, and a republic at 250 is entitled to fireworks and pageantry. The intra-right dissent is unsparing — The Bulwark (anti-Trump right) called the speech “unpatriotic garbage,” arguing Trump used Independence Day to spread rage and revisionist history and to make the day about himself.

Sources: NPR, CNBC, PBS NewsHour, Washington Post, The Bulwark
A Scrambled Fourth

Record heat and evening storms scramble the 250th nationwide as a majority say the day got too political

+

Extreme weather upended America's semiquincentennial across the East Coast. Washington reached a preliminary 102°F — its hottest July 4 on record — after organizers had already canceled the city's Independence Day parade citing an extreme-heat warning. On the Fourth itself, storms forced a weather evacuation of the National Mall: spectators sheltered in nearby museums and government buildings for hours before returning to a field near the Washington Monument, pushing Trump's address to roughly 11 p.m. The Mall program still closed with a 40-minute fireworks display billed as a world-record attempt, and reporting noted parallel heat-and-storm disruptions in New York City and Philadelphia.

The logistical chaos fed a broader argument about the tone of the anniversary. A Reuters/Ipsos survey found most Americans — across roughly three-quarters of Democrats and half of Republicans — think the 250th events have become too political, a rare bipartisan read on a commemoration that Congress had originally envisioned as a unifying national moment. Anti-Trump “Free America” demonstrations were staged nationwide over the weekend, though verified turnout figures had not yet been compiled as of Sunday morning.

The Left-Leaning Take

The mishaps expose the spectacle (Vox, Slate, Mother Jones): a 250th over-engineered around one man's late-night marathon speech was always going to strain under a heat emergency, and the lopsided poll showing most of the country finds the day too political is read as vindication that the celebration lost the plot.

The Right-Leaning Take

Weather is not a verdict (WSJ editorial, National Review): a heat dome and a passing storm do not diminish a genuine national milestone, and the “too political” framing owes more to hostile coverage than to the fireworks most Americans enjoyed. A libertarian aside (Reason) notes that peaceable protest and public festivity on the Fourth are both, in their way, fitting tributes.

Sources: Al Jazeera, NPR, CNN, Washington Post

Ukraine / Russia

Trump offers Putin help ending the war in a 90-minute July 4 call, days before a NATO summit in Ankara

+

On U.S. Independence Day, President Trump and Russian President Vladimir Putin held a roughly 90-minute phone call — their fourth this year — that the Kremlin described as “businesslike and highly constructive.” Putin congratulated Trump on the 250th anniversary, and, per Kremlin aide Yuri Ushakov, Trump “reaffirmed his readiness to facilitate the earliest possible cessation of hostilities” in Ukraine, signaling that envoys Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner were prepared to return to Moscow. Trump also spoke separately with Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky on Saturday; Zelensky said they discussed the roughly 1,200-km front line and that “there is a real prospect to end this war,” adding that “American resolve will have a crucial meaning.”

The diplomacy lands just ahead of a NATO summit in Ankara on July 7–8 and follows this week's exceptionally deadly Russian barrage on Kyiv and Ukraine's deep strikes on Russian oil infrastructure. Note the framing gap: much of the readout comes from Moscow and emphasizes Trump's willingness to “help,” while the Ukrainian side stressed conditions and Western resolve — no ceasefire or concrete terms were announced. Germany's Chancellor Friedrich Merz separately reassured Zelensky of continued support for air defenses.

Sources: CNN, NBC News, Al Jazeera, Bloomberg
Iran

Vast Tehran crowds mark a second day of Khamenei's funeral as successor Mojtaba stays out of view

+

Enormous crowds filled Tehran on July 5 for the second day of the state funeral for slain Supreme Leader Ayatollah Ali Khamenei, centered on mass funeral prayers at the Imam Khomeini Grand Mosalla. President Masoud Pezeshkian and Parliament Speaker Mohammad Bagher Ghalibaf — Iran's lead negotiator with Washington — were shown praying over the coffins of Khamenei and family members killed in the February war. Notably, Khamenei's son and named successor, Mojtaba, appeared absent from the day's rites, while three other sons — unseen since the war — attended; mourners voiced calls for revenge against the U.S. and Israel.

The multi-day mourning, running July 4–9, is set to continue with processions across several Iranian and Iraqi cities before final burial in Mashhad. Mojtaba's absence from such a high-visibility moment is being reported as conspicuous rather than officially explained, and the reason remains unconfirmed — a notable gap given open questions about the legitimacy and consolidation of the succession. The ceremonies double as a display of regime cohesion amid the fragile, paused U.S.–Iran diplomacy that followed the war.

Sources: CNN, Times of Israel, Al Jazeera, CBS News
Strait of Hormuz

Oman backs a UK–France plan to secure Hormuz shipping as Iran warns off “extra-regional” navies

+

Britain and France are advancing a multinational naval mission to restore commercial shipping through the Strait of Hormuz following the interim U.S.–Iran deal that paused the war. On July 4, Oman — which controls waters on the strait's southern side — agreed to cooperate with London and Paris to keep its territorial waters safe for navigation, a key enabler for a mission expected to focus on mine-clearing, vessel escorts and air policing. The effort is backed by a broad coalition (reports range from around 20 to more than 40 participating nations in planning), with the UK's HMS Dragon pre-positioned and France having moved the carrier Charles de Gaulle to the region.

Tehran reacted sharply. Iran's deputy foreign minister said “the Strait of Hormuz is not a theater for military display by extra-regional powers,” insisting the waterway's security “lies with the coastal states” and warning that “crisis-mongers will be responsible for the consequences of their adventurism.” The standoff sets up a fresh flashpoint over one of the world's most critical oil chokepoints just as U.S.–Iran diplomacy remains on hold for Khamenei's funeral.

Sources: France 24, Bloomberg, USNI News

Biotech & Gene Editing

FDA clears the first CRISPR gene therapy for toddlers, extending Casgevy to sickle-cell patients as young as two

+

The FDA has expanded approval of Casgevy (exagamglogene autotemcel) — the first CRISPR-based medicine authorized anywhere in the world — to children as young as two with sickle cell disease or transfusion-dependent beta-thalassemia. The decision, announced July 1 and drawing broad coverage through the holiday weekend, lowers the prior floor of age 12 and puts a potentially curative one-time treatment within reach of an estimated 5,500 additional U.S. children. Developed by Vertex Pharmaceuticals with CRISPR Therapeutics, the therapy edits a patient's own blood stem cells to reactivate fetal hemoglobin.

The goal is to head off the pain crises, strokes and organ damage that define severe sickle cell disease before they begin in early childhood. Casgevy remains the only CRISPR/Cas9 medicine cleared for human use, so extending it to toddlers is a notable marker of how quickly a Nobel-winning laboratory tool has moved toward routine pediatric care — even as cost and the intensity of the stem-cell procedure keep access far from universal.

Sources: FDA, HCPLive, Xtalks
Climate & Health

A new analysis pins roughly 20,000 deaths on Europe's late-June heat — most attributable to warming

+

A rapid analysis circulating over the weekend estimates that the record late-June European heat wave caused on the order of 20,000 excess deaths — far above official tallies to date. Climate scientist Christopher Callahan of Indiana University Bloomington puts the figure near 20,390 across Europe for June 22–28, using a statistical heat-mortality model. An important caveat: the estimate is a preprint posted to Zenodo and has not been peer-reviewed, and official counts remain lower (the WHO cited more than 1,300 excess deaths by June 28, with Spain's figures pushing the reported continental total above 2,000 by July 1).

The heat wave — which World Weather Attribution called the most severe ever recorded for the region it studied and “virtually impossible” without human-caused warming — broke temperature records across at least a dozen countries and shuttered schools and transport. With Europe warming at roughly twice the global average, researchers frame the event less as an outlier than as a preview of a new summer baseline. (Partial — the 20,000 figure is an unreviewed preprint.)

Sources: World Weather Attribution, Al Jazeera, phys.org
Oil & Energy

OPEC+ approves another 188,000-bpd output hike for August as Hormuz keeps real barrels below quota

+

At a July 5 meeting, OPEC+ agreed to raise August production targets by 188,000 barrels per day, extending a run of similar monthly increases and pushing the seven core members' cumulative quota rise since April toward 800,000 bpd. The added supply is largely notional for now: the U.S.–Israeli war on Iran and the closure of the Strait of Hormuz have kept actual exports from key producers — Saudi Arabia, Kuwait and Iraq — below pre-war levels, though output began recovering in June.

The decision reflects the group's continued pivot away from defending prices and toward reclaiming market share, even amid a fragile supply picture and the risk that a Hormuz standoff could disrupt shipments again. For now the gap between OPEC+'s paper quotas and the barrels actually reaching market is the story — a reminder that headline production targets and physical flows have diverged sharply since the war.

Sources: CNBC, OPEC
Space

Hubble marks the 250th with a red-white-and-blue portrait of an ancient Milky Way star cluster

+

For the holiday, NASA released a new Hubble Space Telescope image rendering one of the Milky Way's oldest globular star clusters as a field of red, white and blue points of light — a “stellar sparkler” timed to the nation's 250th anniversary. The differing colors trace real stellar physics: hotter stars glow blue, cooler ones red, with the cluster's most ancient suns among the oldest objects in the galaxy.

This is an outreach release rather than a new scientific result, but it was the one genuinely July-4-dated space item of the week; the period's more substantive astronomy news — the record GWTC-5 gravitational-wave catalog of some 390 detections, and a third dark-matter-free galaxy found with the Keck telescopes — both broke in late June. Even as a holiday flourish, the portrait doubles as a reminder of Hubble's endurance decades past its planned life.

Sources: ScienceDaily, ScienceDaily (GWTC-5, related)
