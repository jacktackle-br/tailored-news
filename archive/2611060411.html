Pentagon · Impeachment Threat

Hegseth calls Massie’s impeachment articles a “sham” in a defiant video, and Trump says he is not replacing him, though NBC reports he has asked allies about successors

+

Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth answered Rep. Thomas Massie’s resign-or-be-impeached ultimatum on Thursday with a video posted to X. He called the eight articles of impeachment a “sham” brought by a “disgruntled, defeated congressman,” compared them to the two impeachments of Trump, and called the scrutiny “the politics of personal destruction.” Pentagon spokesman Sean Parnell said Hegseth “will not back down or be deterred by politicians seeking to score partisan points.” Massie lost his primary after crossing Trump. He says he will force a House vote when the lame-duck session returns, as early as next week, unless Hegseth resigns. The articles accuse Hegseth of waging war without congressional authorization, extrajudicial killings, and kidnapping a sovereign foreign leader. Speaker Mike Johnson has called the effort a stunt.

In public, Trump says he is not planning to replace Hegseth and that he has done “a fantastic job.” NBC News, citing two people familiar with the discussions, reports that Trump has privately asked some allies who should succeed him. The two accounts conflict. Republicans still hold the House until January, so impeachment would need several Republicans to join every Democrat. CNN reports that multiple GOP sources expect the measure to pass, but no whip count has been published. The pressure comes as Republicans, Vice President JD Vance among them, blame the Iran war for Tuesday’s midterm losses.

Courts · Washington Projects

A federal panel approves Trump’s 250-foot arch 8–4, and within an hour Judge Chutkan blocks construction for 14 days

+

On Thursday the National Capital Planning Commission approved President Trump’s planned 250-foot memorial arch at Memorial Circle by a vote of 8–4, after a hearing of about nine and a half hours. All four no votes came from commissioners Trump did not appoint. The administration had said it might break ground right away. Within an hour, U.S. District Judge Tanya Chutkan issued a temporary restraining order that bars construction for 14 days while the case continues. She had already required the government to notify her within an hour of any commission vote. The plaintiffs are a group of Vietnam veterans, the District of Columbia and Virginia. In a 33-page opinion, Chutkan wrote that the arch “appears to contravene” the Commemorative Works Act, which requires Congress to authorize new memorials on federal land in Washington. She called it “improbable” that a 1925 bridge statute, which the administration cites, authorizes the arch.

Chutkan also questioned the government’s security argument. In a court declaration, Defense Secretary Hegseth called the arch a “generational national defense opportunity” that could help counter drones. Chutkan wrote that these “recent and unsupported attestations” do not appear in the planning documents. The AP reports that the arch, the White House ballroom, the Kennedy Center closure and the East Potomac golf course overhaul may now face a second obstacle: Democrats who take control of Congress in January say they will use appropriations to challenge the projects. One outlet described the order as indefinite. Most, including CBS and Forbes, report the 14-day term.

Iran · Diplomacy

Iran tells Reuters it has sent Washington a nuclear proposal through Qatar, but says it will not discuss it until the U.S. meets seven conditions

+

A senior Iranian official told Reuters on Thursday that Tehran has sent the United States a nuclear proposal through Qatari mediators. The official said Iran will discuss it only after Washington meets seven demands, including lifting sanctions, releasing frozen Iranian assets and paying war reparations. Senior security official Mohsen Rezaei said that until the conditions are met, “we will neither open the Strait of Hormuz nor have negotiations.” Washington says it is reviewing the proposal but has given no details. It has said nuclear issues must be part of any first round of talks.

Attacks at sea continued. On Thursday Iran’s Revolutionary Guards said two tankers and a tugboat caught fire after trying to use “unauthorised” routes through the strait. UKMTO data show at least five vessels have reported incidents since Sunday, close to a record pace. Iran’s accounts have not been independently verified. Analysis: the two tracks pull in opposite directions. Tehran is using the strait as leverage before any talks start, while Reuters sources still expect Trump to resume strikes after the midterms. Eurasia Group has cut its estimate of renewed U.S. strikes only modestly, from 65% to 60%.

Russia · Ukraine

Melania Trump hosts Russian and Ukrainian officials at the White House, then the president meets Putin’s envoy and predicts a deal that “might involve all of Europe”

+

On Thursday, First Lady Melania Trump hosted Russian and Ukrainian representatives at the White House to mark a six-day local ceasefire she helped broker in Oleshky, a Russian-occupied town in Ukraine’s Kherson region. That ceasefire has since ended. She called for a “more enduring” truce. The Russian delegation included Kremlin envoy Kirill Dmitriev and human rights commissioner Yana Lantratova, who is under U.S. and EU sanctions. Ukraine’s ombudsman Dmytro Lubinets was expected. Dmitriev thanked her for helping reunite children separated by the war, about three dozen so far. Her role is disputed in Ukraine. CNN reports that Ukrainian prosecutors have accused Lantratova of organizing the deportation of Ukrainian children to Russia.

Afterward, President Trump met Dmitriev privately. He did not meet the Ukrainian delegation. Trump did not say what they discussed, but he predicted “a big, beautiful agreement” soon that “might involve all of Europe.” A U.S. team is due to mediate Russia–Ukraine talks in Abu Dhabi on November 10–11. Analysis: the White House event took place in the same week Russia carried out one of the war’s deadliest strikes on civilians, a bombing on a school shelter in the Zaporizhzhia region. Trump’s optimism has not yet been matched by any public sign of movement from Moscow or Kyiv.

Tech · Chips

AMD, Qualcomm and Nvidia all fall more than 9% as chip stocks have their worst day in over a year, despite strong AI revenue

+

Semiconductor stocks collapsed on Thursday. The Philadelphia Semiconductor Index fell more than 12%, the Nasdaq had its worst day since April 2025, and the S&P 500 fell about 4.4%. Earnings were mixed. AMD said third-quarter revenue rose 21% to $16.5 billion on strong AI hardware sales, yet its stock fell about 17%. Qualcomm beat estimates but gave a weaker outlook and fell about 9%. Intel guided fourth-quarter sales lower. Nvidia fell about 9% to a seven-month low, $181.60, without reporting anything itself.

Several outlets link the sell-off partly to worries that the incoming Democratic Congress will tighten oversight of AI and data centers, but that explanation is speculative. Chip stocks were rebounding in premarket trading on Friday. One Jefferies strategist called Thursday’s move “reactionary” rather than a fundamental reset. Figures differ slightly between outlets.

Climate · COP31

COP31 opens in Antalya on Monday, with finance the main dispute and UNEP warning that current plans lead to 2.6°C of warming

+

The UN climate summit begins in Antalya, Türkiye, on November 9 and runs until November 20, with a leaders’ segment on November 11–12. Under an unusual arrangement, Türkiye hosts and presides while Australia leads the negotiations, working closely with Pacific island states. Turkish officials say 91 heads of state and government have confirmed, a number that has not been independently checked. Developing countries want rich nations to meet their Paris finance commitments, and that is expected to be the main fight. Climate Home News expects the main negotiated outcome to be a “cover decision.” Australia has pointed to a new mechanism for a fair transition as a likely result. The second global stocktake also begins at this COP.

The background is grim. UNEP’s Martin Krause says current national plans still lead to about 2.6°C of warming this century. UNEP’s October Emissions Gap report concluded that keeping to 1.5°C without overshooting it is no longer possible. A strong El Niño is forecast to make climate impacts worse. Before the summit, Turkey’s energy minister set out a plan to move away from coal that he said needs $500 billion of investment over 13 years.
