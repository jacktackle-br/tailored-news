The Administration · Ethics

Vance says he “doesn’t love” it and Rubio won’t comment as Democrats press Ambassador Guilfoyle over a reported $100,000 ask to a donor

+

Late Wednesday the Wall Street Journal reported on Signal messages it had reviewed. In them, Kimberly Guilfoyle, now U.S. ambassador to Greece, asks Trump donor Eric Deters to wire about $100,000 to pay her American Express bill. The messages are dated July 3, 2025, days before her Senate confirmation hearing. In one she wrote that the payment “won’t show up anywhere” if wired to American Express. According to the Journal, Deters was seeking help with tax and legal problems at the time and had offered her up to $1 million if she could get him attention from the new administration. He says he had already paid her about $300,000 and never received a favor. He did not pay the bill, and he now calls the arrangement “absolutely” a quid pro quo. Guilfoyle’s lawyer, Jesse Binnall, says she disputes that the messages are authentic but has not said which ones are false. A U.S. Embassy official said she “remains committed” to serving with integrity.

On Thursday reporters asked Vice President JD Vance about an official asking for money in exchange for access. He said, “obviously I don’t love that,” but added that he did not know the details. Secretary of State Marco Rubio declined to comment and would not say whether the State Department would investigate. House Oversight ranking member Robert Garcia asked Guilfoyle for her financial records and her communications with administration officials. The top Democrats on the House and Senate foreign affairs committees called for an inquiry, and Rep. Gregory Meeks said she should be removed if the allegations are true. None of the allegations has been proven.

The Left-Leaning Take

Slate argues that the scandal shows the administration’s corruption reaches well beyond the president. Its view is that an ambassadorship given to a loyalist turned into an offer to sell access to officials such as the IRS commissioner. A second Slate column calls Guilfoyle “the purest MAGA figure in America,” a grifter cast in Trump’s own mold.

The Right-Leaning Take

No in-window National Review, Dispatch or WSJ editorial piece turned up. Taking the libertarian angle, Reason argues that Guilfoyle’s alleged grift is “at least as offensive as Hunter Biden’s,” so conservatives who condemned the one should condemn the other. Representing intra-right, anti-Trump disagreement rather than the mainstream right, The Bulwark calls it “Our Big Fat Greek Bribery Scandal” and portrays the posting as a patronage reward.

The Courts · Press Freedom

Judge sounds skeptical of the White House ban on CNN, MS NOW and Politico and keeps their access in place while he decides

+

On Thursday U.S. District Judge Timothy Kelly heard arguments on whether to issue a longer-term order blocking President Trump’s Sept. 18 ban on the three outlets. Trump had cited what he called biased coverage. Kelly pressed the Justice Department on its national security rationale and asked its lawyer to point to “the strongest things in the record” supporting it. He said he was bound by D.C. Circuit precedent, including a precedent the administration objects to. He also said reporters could be barred for security reasons in some contexts. Kelly extended for a few days his temporary order requiring the White House to let the journalists onto its grounds, and suggested he would rule by the end of Tuesday. Any preliminary injunction is likely to be appealed.

Kelly’s Sept. 24 restraining order found that the outlets’ credentials were probably revoked without due process. Since then the White House has still kept CNN and Politico out of pool slots in the Oval Office and on Air Force One. The Justice Department argues that the president may deny access to enforce professional standards and protect national security information. Seventy-seven media organizations have filed in support of the outlets. Note: CNN, the main source for the hearing account, is a plaintiff.

No in-window piece from Mother Jones, Vox, Slate or Guardian opinion turned up. Editorials elsewhere and the press-freedom coalition’s brief call the ban viewpoint discrimination: the government punishing reporters for “negative” coverage, which the First Amendment forbids. The administration argues that White House access, especially to the Oval Office and Air Force One, is “a privilege, not a right,” and that the president has compelling interests in professional standards and protecting sensitive information. Middle East · The US–Iran War

A day after reports of pre-election strike planning, Trump says the U.S. won’t attack Iran before Nov. 3 but will keep the blockade

+

On Thursday President Trump posted on Truth Social that the U.S. is “having productive discussions with the Islamic Republic of Iran” and “will not be attacking Iran at any time prior to the midterm elections” on November 3. He wrote that the naval blockade, which has cut off Iranian oil exports, “will remain in full force and effect,” and that his central aim is still to keep Iran from obtaining a nuclear weapon. He did not rule out strikes after the election. The post came a day after The Atlantic and Axios reported that the White House had asked the Pentagon for strike options before the vote. On Thursday the U.S. also imposed sanctions on what remains of Iran’s “shadow fleet” of tankers. In Tehran, an Iranian news agency quoted Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi as saying officials would respond to Washington “within the next few days.” Talks are being held indirectly through mediators.

Analysis: The pledge looks aimed as much at voters and markets as at Tehran. A Reuters/Ipsos poll finds about 60% of Americans, including one in four Republicans, disapprove of Trump’s handling of Iran, and reporting from earlier this week said even supporters of limited strikes doubted they would lower gasoline prices before Election Day. Brent fell sharply on the post. The Strait of Hormuz itself is still the main risk. The Washington Post reports that Iran hit at least nine vessels there last week, the most in months, citing UN and Kpler data, and Kpler says tanker traffic recently fell to its lowest since late July. A pledge not to strike does nothing about attacks on shipping, and the blockade Iran has demanded be lifted stays in place. Tehran’s formal reply will show whether the “productive” talks are real.

Sources: Washington Post, Oct 8, CNN, Oct 8, Wire report (via U.S. News), Oct 8, Washington Post, Oct 8 (Hormuz), CBS News live updates
Europe · The War in Ukraine

Russian glide bomb hits two packed buses in Kramatorsk, killing at least 30, a day after a deadly barrage on Putin’s birthday

+

On Thursday morning a Russian guided bomb struck a street in Kramatorsk as two passenger buses were passing. Kramatorsk is a city in Donetsk region about 15–20 km from the front line. Ukraine’s State Emergency Service said at least 30 people were killed and 18 injured. Some outlets, citing regional officials, put the toll at 33, but at least one report counts three of those deaths in nearby Bilenke. Regional governor Vadym Filashkin accused Russia of deliberately targeting civilians and suspended public transport in Kramatorsk and neighboring Sloviansk. Prosecutors opened a war-crimes investigation, and President Volodymyr Zelensky said the strike “will not go without our response.” Moscow has not commented publicly on the strike.

Analysis: The strike came a day after one of the war’s heaviest barrages. On Wednesday, Putin’s 74th birthday, Russia fired about 70 missiles and dozens of drones, killing at least 28–32 people nationwide, most of them in an apartment block in Pryluky. Zelensky called it one of Russia’s “most vile strikes.” The Washington Post puts the Kramatorsk attack in the context of Russia’s push on the “fortress belt” of Kramatorsk, Sloviansk and nearby towns, which Moscow needs to take all of Donbas. Russia demands that Ukraine give up the rest of the region before fighting stops, and Kyiv refuses. Kremlin officials say there is “no clear path” to peace. Reported death tolls differ between agencies and may change.

Sources: Washington Post, Oct 8, France 24, Oct 8, RFE/RL, ABC (Australia), Oct 8, Moscow Times (Reuters report), Oct 7

The White House · The Midterm Operation

Trump threatens to fire his top midterm strategists over paid work for a pro-Russian Balkan government, then says there’s no conflict

+

On Tuesday the New York Times reported that Chris LaCivita and James Blair traveled to Republika Srpska before its elections. The two run President Trump’s midterm operation at the super PAC MAGA Inc. Republika Srpska is the Serb-run, Russia-aligned territory inside Bosnia and Herzegovina. According to the Times, each man was paid hundreds of thousands of dollars through a Washington law firm that the territory’s government hired to advise it on voting issues. Blair was White House deputy chief of staff until this spring and now leads MAGA Inc. LaCivita co-managed Trump’s 2024 campaign.

On Wednesday Trump posted on Truth Social that the two are “outside consultants” who “do not work for me.” He wrote that he would ask them and, “if there’s a conflict, I will terminate them, immediately!” Later, in the Oval Office, he told reporters, “They don’t have a conflict.” Reuters said a Trump ally described the president as caught off guard and angered by the trip, and called the episode a sign of a possible rift between Trump and the advisers he put in charge of defending slim congressional majorities on Nov. 3. The Washington Post described it as a public rebuke that came while Republicans are trailing.

The Courts · Campaign Money

DNC sues the administration, calling taxpayer-funded pro-Trump TV ads illegal propaganda

+

On Wednesday the Democratic National Committee sued in federal court in Washington. The suit names Trump, the White House, the Department of Homeland Security and the Office of Management and Budget. It alleges that a series of government-funded TV spots praising the president break the federal ban on spending appropriated money for “publicity or propaganda,” and also cites the Anti-Deficiency Act and the Purpose Statute. AP reports that the money came from a $175 million package Congress gave DHS for immigration enforcement. DHS awarded a $20 million contract for the campaign; the ad tracker AdImpact says more than $12 million has been spent airing the ads so far. The DNC alleges Trump personally directed the effort and asks the court to declare the ads illegal and stop the spending. A coalition that includes Common Cause filed a separate suit the same day.

The White House calls the spots public service announcements like those earlier administrations ran. The suit follows Trump’s statement on Monday that MAGA Inc. would pay for the ads from now on. Asked whether taxpayers would be reimbursed for ads that have already aired, he said, “we’ll decide.” No court has ruled.

Middle East · The US–Iran War

White House asks the Pentagon for Iran strike options before the midterms, and oil climbs back above $100

+

The White House has asked the Pentagon for options to strike Iran before the November 3 midterm elections, The Atlantic reported Wednesday, citing two administration officials. U.S. Central Command is drawing up the options. Officials are still discussing how large the strikes would be and what they would hit, and no final decision has been made. Axios separately reported that CENTCOM had been told to finish preparing for a possible return to major combat. One of its sources said the chance of action before the election is “not high but we don’t rule it out.” A White House official said only that Trump “has all options available at any time.” The day before, Vice President JD Vance told Reuters in an exclusive interview that Iran must make a “meaningful” cut to its enrichment capacity to end the war. He said “we’re not going to trade words for actions,” and that Washington is unsure who actually makes decisions in Tehran. A senior Iranian official told Reuters the U.S. “requests and ideas” are at odds with Iran’s demands. Separately, UK Maritime Trade Operations (UKMTO), the British maritime security agency, said late Wednesday that a tanker about 51 nautical miles off Qatar had been hit by several projectiles, with casualties. It did not say who fired them.

Analysis: Several outlets cite officials who tie the planning to Trump’s wish to show progress in the war and bring down fuel prices before the vote. But even supporters of limited strikes reportedly admit that strikes alone would not reopen the Strait of Hormuz or lower gasoline prices by Election Day. Some advisers also fear U.S. casualties could hurt Republican candidates. Markets reacted right away. Bloomberg put Brent above $102 and CBS reported it above $104 early Thursday, up from about $99 on Wednesday. Outlets also differ on how long the war has run (“seventh” vs. “eighth” month) and on how firm the plans are: Axios’s sourcing makes pre-election timing sound less likely than the headlines suggest.

Sources: Reuters (via U.S. News), Oct 6, Axios, Oct 7, Bloomberg, Oct 8, Al Jazeera, Oct 7
Middle East · Britain and Israel

Britain renames its Jerusalem consulate the “UK Mission” as Israel’s closure deadline runs out

+

Israel’s 30-day deadline to shut Britain’s nearly 200-year-old consulate in East Jerusalem ended Thursday with an agreement reached late Wednesday. Israel had ordered the closure in retaliation for London’s sanctions on West Bank settlements, including a ban on trade in settlement goods. Israeli Foreign Minister Gideon Sa’ar said the consulate was “ceasing operations.” He said 20 British diplomats, including the consul general, would leave Israel, and that seven could keep working from the building in a limited role. He added that Britain had agreed not to retaliate. Foreign Secretary Ed Miliband gave a different account: the building “will not be closing, and will be known as the UK Mission, Jerusalem from today.” He said Britain does not accept that Israel has the legal right to close it, and that some staff and families had been “temporarily withdrawn” for safety.

Analysis: Both sides can claim a win. Israel removes the consulate title and most of the staff, and Britain keeps a staffed office that deals with Palestinians and avoids a tit-for-tat expulsion. Earlier, Israeli media had reported that London threatened to expel 27 Israeli diplomats. Miliband said Britain would hold back from reciprocal action only “while such discussions are underway and credible” and plans to “build back up” its presence, so the dispute is paused rather than settled. Outlets also disagree on the facts: Israeli outlets say the office keeps official status serving Palestinians, while Reuters-based copy stresses the loss of consulate status.

Europe · Germany’s Spy Scandal

Merz calls for a swift inquiry as Germany’s former spy chief is held on treason charges

+

Chancellor Friedrich Merz called for “a swift and thorough investigation into the egregious allegations” against August Hanning, 80, who led Germany’s foreign intelligence service (BND) from 1998 to 2005. Federal prosecutors had him arrested and placed in pretrial detention this week on suspicion of “treasonous espionage, spying out state secrets, attempted treason, and espionage.” Prosecutors say Manfred D., chief of staff to several later BND presidents from 2012 until this year, supplied him with classified material for payment. Some reports put it at about 2,000 documents. Hanning allegedly used the material in his private consulting work. In at least one case, prosecutors suspect he used classified information to prepare an analysis for an employee of a foreign intelligence service.

Analysis: Prosecutors have not named the foreign service. Der Spiegel and Bild report it was Azerbaijani intelligence, which Baku denies, and AFP reports it was not Russia. The documents reportedly included sensitive assessments of Iran’s nuclear program and the Russian army. German officials worry the leak will damage trust between the BND and allied services, especially during the Iran war. Accounts differ on when the alleged arrangement started. These are allegations only, and no one has been convicted.

Sources: NPR/AP, Oct 6, AP (via U.S. News), Oct 7, Euronews, Oct 7, Irish Times, Oct 6

Energy & Climate · Hurricanes

Isaias, the season’s first hurricane, takes a quarter of Gulf oil output offline with crude near $100

+

Isaias became a hurricane on Wednesday, the first of the 2026 Atlantic season. According to NOAA data cited by CBS, that is the latest first hurricane in 121 years, just short of the 1905 record. A strong El Niño has kept the season unusually quiet, with wind shear tearing storms apart. The National Hurricane Center expects landfall between coastal Mississippi and the Florida Panhandle late Friday or early Saturday. Storm-surge warnings run from the mouth of the Mississippi to Yankeetown, Florida, and 3–7 inches of rain are forecast, with up to 10 in places.

Offshore, producers had shut in about 511,600 barrels a day as of Wednesday, roughly 25% of U.S. Gulf oil output, plus about 16% of Gulf gas, according to figures from a U.S. regulator reported by Reuters. Shell pulled crews from its Mars, Olympus, Ursa, Vito and Appomattox platforms, and Chevron and BP also began shut-ins or evacuations. The disruption lands with crude already near $100 because of the Iran war, though one risk modeler expects it to last no more than a week if the track holds.

Sources: NPR, Oct 8, Reuters (via BOE Report), Oct 7, CNBC, Oct 7, CBS News
Technology · AI Chips

Broadcom seeks $50 billion-plus in debt so OpenAI can buy its custom chips

+

Broadcom is in early talks to raise more than $50 billion in debt so OpenAI can buy the custom AI accelerators the two companies are developing together, the Wall Street Journal reported and Bloomberg followed on Wednesday. Private-credit firms Apollo Global Management and Blackstone are among the lenders approached. The money would bridge the gap between OpenAI paying up front for hardware and earning revenue from the compute. Talks are preliminary, no deal is expected before year-end, and neither company has commented. Some outlets put the figure nearer $30 billion, so the final size is open.

The plan mirrors a structure Broadcom put together days earlier for Anthropic, reported at more than $60 billion. Together the two packages would top $110 billion, a sign that chipmakers are now arranging their customers’ financing as well as supplying the silicon. The same day, Bloomberg reported that CoreWeave will open its first data centers in India, taking 240 MW at AdaniConneX’s Navi Mumbai campus with an option to double it.

Space · Human Spaceflight

Crew-12 undocks from the space station and heads for a Pacific splashdown off California

+

NASA astronauts Jessica Meir and Jack Hathaway, ESA’s Sophie Adenot and Roscosmos cosmonaut Andrey Fedyaev undocked their SpaceX Dragon from the station’s Harmony module at 8:05 a.m. EDT on Wednesday. NASA is targeting splashdown in the Pacific off California at about 11:34 a.m. EDT (8:34 a.m. PT) Thursday, after a deorbit burn of about nine minutes. CBS says the landing will end a mission of about 237 days.

Adenot, on her first flight, made three spacewalks totaling 19 hours, 42 minutes. Fedyaev becomes the first cosmonaut to fly on Dragon twice. Crew-13, which launched Oct. 1, now runs the station and is starting a new round of health research.

Electrification · EV Charging

California offers up to $100,000 a port for public fast chargers as the EV market cools

+

California’s Fast Charge California Project opened applications on Wednesday. It has at least $28 million for public DC fast chargers, pays up to $100,000 per charging port, and can cover up to 100% of eligible installation costs. Businesses, nonprofits, public agencies and tribes can apply, as long as the chargers are open to the public.

The state is spending its own money on charging while the U.S. EV market softens without federal consumer tax credits: GM sold just 1,905 Chevy Equinox EVs in the third quarter, down 92% from a year earlier. Electrek also reported Wednesday that Britain’s Portsmouth International Port has opened what it calls the country’s first high-voltage, multi-berth shore-power system, which lets docked ships plug into the grid instead of burning fuel, though high power prices may limit how much it is used.

English Football · The Managers’ Market

The champions tie Arteta down to 2030 in what may be the Premier League’s richest manager’s deal

+

The biggest English football news of the international break came on Wednesday, when Arsenal announced that Mikel Arteta has signed a new contract to June 2030 and that his coaching staff have renewed for four years.
