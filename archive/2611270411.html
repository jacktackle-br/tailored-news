Courts · First Amendment · FCC

Judge halts the FCC’s early review of ABC’s station licenses, saying Chairman Carr took Trump’s rhetoric as his “marching orders”

+

U.S. District Judge Loren AliKhan in Washington issued a preliminary injunction on Wednesday. It bars the Federal Communications Commission from taking further steps in its early license-renewal review of the eight stations owned by Disney’s ABC while Disney’s lawsuit proceeds. The order blocks a hearing that could have led to revocation of the licenses. AliKhan also denied the FCC’s motion to dismiss. She found ABC likely to succeed on its First Amendment retaliation claim: “This is not a close case.” She wrote that early renewal proceedings create a risk sufficient to chill “a broadcaster of ordinary firmness,” and that “Chairman Carr has taken President Trump’s rhetoric as his marching orders.”

FCC Chairman Brendan Carr ordered the early filings in April, a day after Trump urged ABC to fire Jimmy Kimmel. The licenses were not due for renewal until October 2028 at the earliest. The ruling does not bar the FCC from conducting future reviews. ABC called it “an important victory for the First Amendment.” The FCC said it was “wrong on the facts and wrong on the law—as controlling Supreme Court precedent makes clear” and that it would keep holding broadcasters to their public-interest obligations, which signals an appeal.

No in-window piece on the ruling turned up at Mother Jones, Vox, Slate or Guardian Opinion. The critique in the coverage, echoed by ABC and press-freedom advocates, is that the ruling confirms what critics have said since April. In their view, the license review was a pressure campaign against a network the president dislikes, and a regulator acting on presidential cues is the kind of state action the First Amendment exists to stop.

The Right-Leaning Take

No in-window reaction turned up at National Review, The Dispatch, Reason or the WSJ editorial page. The administration’s position, stated by the FCC, is that Supreme Court precedent gives the commission authority to review whether broadcasters serve the public interest, and that a district judge has improperly second-guessed a lawful regulatory process. Libertarian and free-speech conservatives have long been uneasy with using licensing to police content, so the right is not unified on Carr’s approach.

Weather · Infrastructure · Travel

A Thanksgiving Day storm cuts power to roughly 900,000 customers across the Midwest and East, snarls flights and pushes the Macy’s parade to Saturday

+

A powerful storm brought high winds, heavy rain and coastal flooding to the Midwest and Northeast on Thursday. More than 800,000 customers were without power Thursday night, according to PowerOutage.us, and some counts put the peak near 1 million. Virginia, Maryland and Pennsylvania were among the hardest-hit states. FlightAware counted more than 2,000 delays and roughly 400–460 cancellations, and the FAA briefly halted departures at Reagan National during thunderstorms. United, Delta, American and JetBlue issued travel waivers. New Jersey and New York declared states of emergency, and streets flooded from the Bronx to Long Island.

Macy’s postponed its Thanksgiving Day Parade to Saturday, Nov. 28, citing high winds after consulting city officials. Executive producer Will Coss said the safety of participants and spectators “is and always will be our top priority.” Reports say crews had restored part of the lost power by early Friday, but outage and cancellation totals were still changing, and outlets give different figures depending on when they were counted.

Gulf · Iran · Kuwait · Hormuz

A drone strike on Kuwait kills two Italian defense workers, and Kuwait and Italy blame Iran; Tehran denies it as the IRGC claims another tanker hit a mine in Hormuz

+

Kuwait’s Defense Ministry said two Italian nationals died of injuries suffered on Thursday when a drone hit what it called a “vital site.” Kuwaiti and Pakistani nationals were also wounded. Reports identify the Italians as technicians working for the defense company Rheinmetall. Kuwaiti officials called the strike “a heinous Iranian aggression,” and Italy’s foreign minister said the drone was Iranian-made. Rome said it would lodge a formal protest with Iran’s embassy. Iran’s armed forces spokesman called it a “false flag” operation. Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi called the accusations baseless and proposed a joint investigation with Italy and Kuwait. Saudi Arabia, Bahrain, Qatar and the UAE condemned the attack, and European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen called for more pressure on Tehran.

At sea, the IRGC Navy said that on Friday morning two tankers tried to “secretly pass” through an unauthorized route north of Ghavam Island. It said one exploded after hitting a mine and the other turned back. That follows Iranian claims that two more tankers struck mines off Oman’s Khasab on Thursday. The IRGC has not identified the ships, and Reuters has said it could not independently verify such claims. Brent rose toward $96–97 on Friday. Analysis: Tehran says normal Hormuz traffic will not resume until the U.S. naval blockade of Iranian ports is lifted. Deaths of European civilians in a Gulf state widen the circle of countries with a direct stake in how this war of attrition ends.

Ukraine · Russia · U.S.

Zelenskyy tells the BBC that Trump calling Putin a “friend” is like befriending Hitler in WWII, as Trump says Putin is welcome at the Miami G20

+

In a BBC interview at his office in Kyiv, President Volodymyr Zelenskyy called President Trump’s public embrace of Vladimir Putin “strange.” He compared calling Putin a friend to treating Hitler as a friend during World War II. Speaking in English, he said Putin “attacks, he is a killer. He cannot be a friend.” He said what Trump posts is up to Trump, but argued that Washington’s stance encourages Russia to keep attacking and that the U.S. is not doing enough to push Moscow toward peace. A week earlier, at the APEC summit in China, Trump had called Putin “my friend,” and he later posted a photo of the two captioned “My Friend.” Trump has defended the rapport as a negotiating tool.

Speaking to reporters at Mar-a-Lago on Thursday, Trump said Putin “can come” to the G20 summit in Miami on Dec. 14–15 and that he would “like to have him,” although he doubted Putin would attend. The Kremlin says no decision has been made. Analysis: the interview is Zelenskyy’s sharpest public criticism of Trump in months. It comes while Kyiv is still waiting for Washington to set a date for the next round of talks and Moscow resists European participation. If Putin attends, it would be his first in-person G20 since 2019, and European leaders, led by Germany’s Friedrich Merz, are pushing for Zelenskyy to be invited too.

Chips · Export Controls · AI

Nvidia tells Dell and Supermicro to pause AI-server shipments to Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand and Indonesia after a new U.S. licensing rule

+

Nvidia has told manufacturing partners, including Dell and Super Micro, to stop shipping servers built around its chips to Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand and Indonesia. The Financial Times first reported the move on Wednesday, citing three people familiar with it. The FT said Dell and Supermicro told customers in those countries their orders would not be sent. The Wall Street Journal described it as a pause of unspecified length while Nvidia checks whether regional partners meet U.S. export requirements. No violation has been attributed to any named company, and Nvidia, Dell and Supermicro did not comment.

The move follows a Bureau of Industry and Security rule, effective Nov. 19, that requires licenses for exports of covered advanced chips to those four countries and Brazil. The rule targets Chinese firms that rent AI computing from Southeast Asian data centers. The Journal cited an Institute for Progress estimate that such remote capacity supplies about 24% of China’s AI compute. U.S. markets were closed Thursday, so Nvidia, Dell and Supermicro shares first trade on the news Friday, after chip stocks fell into the holiday.

Oil · OPEC+ · Energy Markets

OPEC+ reaches an in-principle deal to keep output targets unchanged through Q1 2027, sources say, as Gulf attacks push Brent toward $97

+

Three sources told Reuters that OPEC+ producers have agreed in principle to leave oil production targets unchanged for the first quarter of 2027. The group is due to formalize the decision at an online meeting on Sunday, Nov. 29. The meeting is also expected to tie quotas from 2027 onward to members’ maximum capacity, based on a first capacity review by the U.S. consultancy DeGolyer and MacNaughton. Disputes over those capacity estimates and the Iran war have complicated the talks, and the group itself has not confirmed the rollover.

Brent crude rose to about $96–97 a barrel in Friday trading, extending a three-day rally, and WTI traded near $85–86. Traders cited Thursday’s drone strike on Kuwait, new claims of tankers hitting mines in Hormuz, and this week’s strike claims against Saudi energy infrastructure. Even so, Brent remains well below its Oct. 30 close of about $117. The EIA expects prices to slide toward $77 by late 2027 as supply recovers.

Arsenal

Arseblog’s Tim Stillman argues Arsenal’s attack is evolving faster than the results show, ahead of Sunday’s visit from Manchester City

+

Writing on Arseblog on Wednesday, Tim Stillman argued that Arsenal’s attacking improvements this season have not yet shown up fully in results. He framed the argument around the recent draws with Newcastle and Borussia Dortmund, in which Arsenal led but could not add a second goal. He credits Mikel Arteta with making the final-third play “less austere.” Roles are more fluid: Jurriën Timber now pushes up from right-back to stand alongside the No. 10 in build-up, and Viktor Gyökeres can take a single touch and play a runner in behind. Stillman notes that Declan Rice leads the league in assists, all from open play.

Arsenal host City at the Emirates on Sunday, Nov. 29 (4:30 p.m. GMT). The goalkeeping question is still open: David Raya’s availability after the shoulder knock at Newcastle has not been confirmed, and outlets disagree on whether he is in contention. Arteta’s pre-match press conference should settle it.

English Football · Europe

Thomas Frank gets his first Crystal Palace win, ten-man Bournemouth stay perfect at the top of the Europa League, and Brighton rout Craiova

+

At Selhurst Park on Thursday, Crystal Palace came from behind to beat Real Sociedad 2–1 in the Europa League, Thomas Frank’s first win as manager. Mamadou Sarr put the visitors ahead on 66 minutes. Jon Martin was then sent off for a second yellow card for holding Jean-Philippe Mateta in the box, and Mateta scored the penalty. Substitute Dwight McNeil won it around the 81st minute. Yeremy Pino had a stoppage-time penalty saved by Unai Marrero. The win lifts Palace into a play-off place.

Bournemouth won 2–1 at Celta Vigo with ten men after Bafodé Diakité’s first-half red card. They came back from Iago Aspas’s opener to stay the only side with five wins from five, top of the table. BBC Sport noted that they are only the third club to win their first five European fixtures. In the Conference League, Brighton beat ten-man Universitatea Craiova 4–0 at the Amex. Ibrahim Osman scored twice, and the win moved the Seagulls up to second on 10 points.

NFL · Thanksgiving

A Thanksgiving of last-second finishes: the Bears stun the Lions, the Eagles erase a 21-point deficit in Dallas, and Tyler Bass sinks the Chiefs from 57 yards

+

All three Thanksgiving games were decided in the final seconds. In Detroit, the Bears rallied from 11 points down in the fourth quarter to win 39–35. Caleb Williams found Rome Odunze for a 6-yard touchdown with three seconds left, Odunze’s second score in the last 7:20. Jahmyr Gibbs scored three touchdowns for Detroit, including a 70-yard run, and the Lions lost despite leading for 53:31, the most by any losing team this season. In Arlington, Dallas led 31–10 before Philadelphia came back to win 39–38 on Jake Elliott’s 59-yard field goal with two seconds left, the largest comeback in NFL Thanksgiving history.

The nightcap in Buffalo was played in freezing rain and wind and ended Bills 13, Chiefs 10. A partially blocked punt and Greg Dortch’s return set up Tyler Bass’s 57-yard field goal as time expired. Earlier, Bass had tied it with a 54-yarder and Harrison Butker missed from 46. Patrick Mahomes went 16 of 29 for 174 yards. Buffalo is 10–1 with seven straight wins, and Kansas City’s three-game winning streak ended at 8–3.
