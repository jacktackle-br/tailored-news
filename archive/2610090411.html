Immigration · High-Skilled Visas

Vance bars Microsoft, Adobe and six outsourcing firms from the main green-card route for foreign workers, alleging fraud

+

At a White House news conference on Thursday, Vice President JD Vance announced that the Labor Department is suspending Microsoft and Adobe, along with six IT outsourcing firms, from the Permanent Labor Certification program (PERM). PERM is the step employers use to sponsor foreign workers, many of them on H-1B visas, for green cards. Reports name the outsourcing firms as Cognizant, Infosys, Tata Consultancy Services, Wipro, HCL Technologies and Capgemini. The affected companies can no longer file new PERM applications or have pending ones processed. The action does not cancel anyone’s H-1B status, existing green cards or current work authorization. No end date has been set: Vance said access would stay suspended until Microsoft shows it is serious about putting American workers first. He said the move came out of an investigation by the anti-fraud task force he chairs.

Vance said Microsoft laid off about 6,000 American workers last year while benefiting from 6,300 H-1B visas and nearly 3,000 green cards, and that it replaced each laid-off worker with “one and a half foreign indentured servants.” AP noted that these numbers do not show that each visa or green card was a new hire replacing a laid-off worker. Microsoft said about 80% of the roughly 6,000 H-1B applications it filed last fiscal year were to extend or change the status of people already employed there, “not to hire new people.” The administration has not publicly laid out the evidence behind the fraud allegation.

The White House · Ethics

Trump’s disclosure shows August trades in Nvidia and other tech stocks, released the day he gave science medals to Huang, Musk and Brin

+

A financial disclosure made public Thursday shows that President Trump’s accounts bought as much as $1.6 million and sold as much as $1 million of Nvidia stock in August, Reuters reported. The Nvidia trades were among hundreds made that month. Federal rules report trades in value ranges, so the real amounts may have been lower. The filing, signed September 17, also lists trades in Tesla, SpaceX, Alphabet, AMD, Dell and Microsoft. Later the same day, Trump awarded the National Medal of Science to Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang, Elon Musk, Google co-founder Sergey Brin and AMD CEO Lisa Su. Michael Dell and Microsoft CEO Satya Nadella received the National Medal of Technology and Innovation. “The entire world is running on Nvidia,” Trump said at the ceremony.

White House spokesman Davis Ingle said third-party firms manage the president’s portfolio independently and track indexes such as the Schwab 1000. “There are no conflicts of interest,” he said. The disclosure does not show that Trump chose any individual trade. Reuters noted that the administration is weighing policies that affect Nvidia directly, including rules on exporting its advanced chips.

The Midterms · Health Care

$90 Medicare payments begin arriving with an email from Trump praising his policies, paid for by draining a 2008 fund

+

One-time $90 payments to about 20.8 million people on traditional Medicare began arriving by direct deposit on or around Thursday. Under a CMS FAQ, each deposit is followed by an email or letter from the president. The Washington Post reported that Trump’s email told seniors he was taking money Congress had set aside for Medicare improvements and sending it to them, and that it promoted his policies. The payment covers less than half of one month’s standard Part B premium of $202.90. People in Medicare Advantage, those receiving Medicaid premium help and higher earners who pay income-related surcharges are excluded, which AP says leaves about 40 million beneficiaries without a check. Others will receive paper Treasury checks later in October.

The roughly $1.9–2 billion comes from the Medicare Improvement Fund, which Congress created in 2008. KFF says the payments will effectively drain it, and no earlier administration has used the fund to pay enrollees directly. The statute does not clearly authorize cash payments. The checks follow $500 ACA “refund” checks and Trump’s campaign promise of $5,000 per adult if Republicans keep Congress. Separately, a Reuters/Ipsos poll released this week found 48% of 2024 Harris voters “very enthusiastic” about voting on Nov. 3, compared with 30% of Trump voters.

They argue the money is too small to matter, since KFF projects next year’s Part B increase will eat up most of it, and that it uses up a fund meant for lasting improvements to the program. International Law · The Nobel Peace Prize

Navi Pillay, the South African jurist and former UN rights chief, wins the Nobel Peace Prize as a defense of international law

+

On Friday in Oslo, the Norwegian Nobel Committee awarded the 2026 Peace Prize to Navanethem “Navi” Pillay, 85. Pillay is a South African anti-apartheid lawyer who became a judge at the International Criminal Tribunal for Rwanda and then the International Criminal Court. She served as UN High Commissioner for Human Rights and until recently chaired the UN commission of inquiry on the occupied Palestinian territories. She now sits as an ad hoc judge at the International Court of Justice in the Myanmar genocide case. The committee credited her work in ensuring that war crimes, crimes against humanity and genocide are prosecuted. Chair Jørgen Watne Frydnes called the prize a message to “those who are undermining the international system and institutions.” The committee said it could not reach her before the announcement. Speaking afterward in Nuremberg, Pillay said she first thought it was a joke, and quipped that if the audience clapped more, “I may share this prize with Trump.”

Analysis: The choice reads as a direct statement about the pressure on international courts. AP notes that the Trump administration sanctioned the ICC last year, and Trump said this week it would be “a great discredit” to the committee not to pick him. Pillay’s Gaza commission work made her a target of Israeli criticism, so the award will also be read through that conflict. Outlets differ on the prize money: Al Jazeera reports 12 million Swedish kronor, while other listings give a lower figure.

+

The Houthis are warning of a long conflict. Reports that Pakistani jets joined the coalition strikes are unconfirmed, and a Pakistani official said Islamabad’s help would focus on air defense. Attacks on Gulf airports add to the strain on regional shipping and aviation at a time when oil is already near $100.

Climate · Energy

Isaias strengthens to Category 2 and heads for the northern Gulf Coast, with nearly two-thirds of Gulf oil output now shut in

+

Hurricane Isaias strengthened to a Category 2 storm as it moved toward the northern Gulf Coast. Forecasters expect landfall between Alabama and the Florida Panhandle late Friday or early Saturday. Offshore shutdowns grew quickly. On Wednesday about 25% of Gulf of Mexico oil production had been halted. By Thursday the figure was about 62.9%, or about 1.28 million barrels a day, along with 57.4% of the Gulf’s natural-gas output. Operators had evacuated 121 of the 371 staffed production platforms, and Chevron shut in five of its Gulf assets.

The bigger concern for fuel prices is onshore. The Gulf Coast holds more than half of U.S. refining capacity, and S&P Global’s Jim Burkhard said the key question is whether Isaias comes ashore as a strong storm near refineries. Offshore wells are sealed below the seabed during storms and are restarted after inspection, so those losses are usually temporary. The storm hits while crude is already near $100 because of the Iran war.

Tech · Chips

Samsung forecasts an $80 billion quarter, the largest operating profit any tech company has reported, as TSMC also posts record quarterly sales

+

Samsung Electronics estimated third-quarter operating profit of 107.4 trillion won (about $80 billion), up from 12.17 trillion a year earlier. It is the first time a South Korean company has topped 100 trillion won in a quarter. The figure is above the $63.7 billion operating income Nvidia reported for its latest quarter, making it the largest ever reported by a tech company. Memory chips drove the gain, as AI data centers buy memory faster than it can be made and prices rise. Analysts estimate the memory business alone earned more than the group total, which suggests losses elsewhere; the devices unit is thought to have lost about 2 trillion won because of costlier components. Revenue of about 195 trillion won was slightly below forecasts, and the shares fell. Full results are due October 29.

The same day, TSMC reported September revenue of NT$511.86 billion, up 54.6% from a year earlier and down 0.6% from August. That brought third-quarter sales to a record of about NT$1.49 trillion ($46.7 billion), above both analyst estimates and TSMC’s own guidance. Taken together, the two companies’ numbers show AI spending still accelerating at the supply end, even as investors worry about slower price gains and how dependent chipmakers have become on a few large AI customers.

Biology · Aging

Why big dogs die young: a 900-dog study ties faster aging to “jumping genes” breaking loose

+

A Dog Aging Project study published Thursday in Science analyzed 1,640 DNA-methylation profiles from 894 dogs across about 120 breeds. It found that large dogs build up age-related epigenetic changes faster than small ones, and males faster than females, even males neutered young. Much of the effect involves LINE-1 retrotransposons, or “jumping genes,” which can copy themselves around the genome and are normally kept silent by methylation. More than 40% of LINE-1-associated regions lost methylation with age. Giant breeds lost about 35% more per year than small breeds. Dogs whose epigenetic age was higher than their actual age were more likely to die within two years.

The result offers a molecular explanation for a long-standing puzzle: across species bigger animals usually live longer, but within dog breeds the bigger ones die younger. The authors, led by Noah Snyder-Mackler at Arizona State, say they cannot yet tell whether LINE-1 activity drives aging or is a symptom of it, and that the data explain only part of the variation in dogs’ epigenetic ages.

Cleveland storms back to beat the White Sox 9–5 and force a decisive ALDS Game 5

+

Facing elimination in Chicago on Thursday night, the Guardians beat the White Sox 9–5 to even their ALDS at 2–2. Cleveland lost the first two games at home, so it has now come back from 0–2 to force a fifth game. In the fifth inning, catcher Patrick Bailey hit a homer to right on a 3–1 pitch from Erick Fedde, and José Ramírez followed with a two-run homer that gave Cleveland its first lead. Game 5 is Saturday at Progressive Field, and the winner faces the Tampa Bay Rays in the ALCS. It is the only Division Series still going. The Dodgers and Brewers open their NLCS on Sunday.
