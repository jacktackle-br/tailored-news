Elections · Justice Department

California pushes back in court as an L.A. County check of the Justice Department’s list of 20,000 “likely noncitizen” voters finds nearly 40% of sampled names show U.S. birth

+

California and Los Angeles County filed responses Tuesday to the Justice Department’s emergency request, NPR reports. The department wants a federal judge in Los Angeles to bar officials from counting mail ballots from about 20,000 registered voters it calls likely noncitizens until their citizenship is checked. Los Angeles County reviewed 200 of the names in a preliminary analysis. Nearly 40% of those records “indicate that they were born in the United States, which is strong evidence of citizenship,” and 71.2% show no voting history. The state says doing what the department asks this close to the election would be unworkable and would likely break federal and state election law. Acting Deputy Attorney General Trent McCotter has acknowledged that the list may include citizens. He says the 20,000 are the names the government is most confident about. No ruling has been reported.

On Monday the department announced charges against 28 green-card holders in California under what it calls “Operation Protect the Vote.” Prosecutors say 21 of them cast ballots illegally. Some are also accused of lying about citizenship on registration forms or hiding their voting history when applying to naturalize. Eleven had been arrested as of the announcement, and officials said all 28 were on the list given to the state. Secretary of State Shirley Weber says Washington has not shared the data or method behind the list. Gov. Gavin Newsom called the demand “dangerous nonsense.” Legal analysts point out that the suit cites no statute requiring extra citizenship checks of registered voters and rests on the Supremacy Clause instead. Election Day is Nov. 3.

The Left-Leaning Take

Democrats and voting-rights advocates call the list a pre-election intimidation tactic. They note that matching incomplete voter files against immigration databases has produced many false positives before. In Nevada, DHS first flagged nearly 16,000 people, and the state says it then failed to show a single noncitizen on the rolls. The L.A. County sample, in their view, confirms those concerns. They also see the timing of the criminal charges as meant to frighten eligible naturalized voters. (No in-window Mother Jones, Vox, Slate or Guardian opinion piece found; summary drawn from Democratic officials and advocates as reported.)

The Right-Leaning Take

The administration and its allies argue that federal law already bars noncitizens from voting for federal office, so holding the flagged ballots aside until citizenship is confirmed is a modest step. They describe California’s refusal to review the names as the real risk to election integrity, and they cite the 28 charging documents as proof that the problem is real. (No in-window National Review, Dispatch, WSJ editorial or Reason piece found; summary reflects the Justice Department’s stated position.)

White House · Executive Power

Trump and Vance launch a $25 million cash-for-tips fraud program, and at the same Oval Office event Trump says Rep. Ilhan Omar “could go to jail” or be deported

+

On Tuesday President Trump and Vice President JD Vance announced the Fraud Whistleblower Incentive Program, or WHIP, a $25 million effort the Justice Department will run. The White House says anyone who submits a tip through fraud.gov that leads to a fraud conviction or a significant federal enforcement action can receive up to $30,000. Trump described the payouts differently, as a 1% commission on the fraud uncovered, and said “I will be the arbitrator” when more than one person claims the same tip. YouTube commentator Nick Shirley received a $10,000 check as the program’s first payment. Vance has led a fraud task force since March.

A reporter asked whether questions about Omar’s age on her immigration paperwork could lead to her deportation. Trump said she could be deported and could also go to jail, adding, “This is just my opinion.” He cited no charge or new evidence and called Somalia, where she was born, a “s***house.” He also said Minnesota Gov. Tim Walz was “100 percent guilty” in the state’s fraud scandal. Neither Omar nor Walz has been charged in connection with those cases. Federal agents searched Omar’s Washington home on Oct. 12 in an investigation into whether her birth year was misstated when she naturalized in 2000. DHS Secretary Markwayne Mullin and border czar Tom Homan have called it a “strong case.” Omar denies wrongdoing and calls the investigation political persecution.

The Left-Leaning Take

Democrats, including House Minority Leader Hakeem Jeffries, say a president publicly predicting jail for a sitting member of Congress who has not been charged is an attempt to influence a pending investigation. Former President Obama on Sunday called the probe “appalling.” Critics also say a tip program in which the president personally decides disputed rewards invites politicized enforcement. (No in-window Mother Jones, Vox, Slate or Guardian opinion piece found.)

The Right-Leaning Take

Supporters present WHIP as a way to recruit citizens against the program fraud uncovered in Minnesota and elsewhere, and say paying informants is already standard at the SEC and IRS. On Omar, officials say they are only following the evidence in an immigration-fraud case. (No in-window National Review, Dispatch, WSJ editorial or Bulwark piece found; summary reflects administration statements as reported.)

Russia · Ukraine

Witkoff meets Putin in St. Petersburg to test a new peace framework, and Russia frees Stephen Hubbard, the last American the U.S. had declared wrongfully detained there

+

U.S. envoy Steve Witkoff met Vladimir Putin in St. Petersburg on Wednesday, a day after talks with Volodymyr Zelenskyy in Norway. A U.S. official told CNN the visit was meant to “test” whether a new plan could serve as the basis for negotiations and to “create a pathway toward direct trilateral and broader multilateral negotiations.” Witkoff urged Putin to resume three-way technical talks between the U.S., Russia and Ukraine. According to TASS, Putin raised the idea of restoring economic cooperation with Washington. Jared Kushner, who has the flu, was to join remotely. Zelenskyy called his talks with the Americans constructive but repeated: “Nothing about Ukraine without Ukraine.” Russia’s publicly stated demands are unchanged: all of the Donbas, a smaller Ukrainian army and a pledge never to join NATO.

During the meeting Witkoff announced that Russia had released Stephen Hubbard, 74, a retired Michigan teacher. Hubbard was sentenced in 2024 to nearly seven years on mercenary charges and is reported to be in poor health; he is to be flown to a military hospital in Texas. Trump said Hubbard was freed “out of respect” for him and that the U.S. gave nothing in return; that claim has not been independently confirmed. As many as 10 other Americans, including dual nationals, remain in Russian custody. Analysis: The release follows Trump’s waiver of some Russia sanctions. The Washington Post reports that the Kremlin has welcomed Washington’s recent turn against Zelenskyy as a win. Moscow is getting gestures of goodwill from Washington without having moved on territory.

Gulf · Yemen

Britain sends RAF Typhoons to defend Saudi cities and oil sites from Houthi missiles and drones after strikes that hit Riyadh’s main airport

+

The U.K. said Tuesday it will send Royal Air Force Typhoon jets to Saudi Arabia to help defend it against Houthi missile and drone attacks. The attacks have hit oil facilities and Riyadh’s main airport. The Ministry of Defence says the jets will fly defensive missions over Saudi cities and key infrastructure and will not attack Houthi forces. The package also extends British air-to-air refueling for Saudi operations and adds an A400M transport plane and military advisers at Saudi command headquarters. Defence Secretary Wes Streeting singled out the strike on the Riyadh airport in condemning the attacks. The Washington Post reports that Riyadh is pressing the Trump administration for more help as well.

Analysis: The deployment draws a second European power further into the Gulf front of the Iran war. Some legal commentators note that London has acknowledged refueling Saudi aircraft that strike Houthi targets inside Yemen, and argue that this could make Britain a party to that conflict before its jets intercept anything. The move also comes as Tehran tells mediators it will not negotiate until its conditions are met, which keeps Gulf shipping and energy infrastructure exposed.

Sources: Washington Post, Oct 20, Al Jazeera, Oct 20, Militarnyi, Diplomacy & Law (analysis)
India · U.S. Diplomacy

Rubio arrives in Mumbai looking for energy relief and a trade deal, as 50% U.S. tariffs over Russian oil and Washington’s outreach to Pakistan strain ties

+

Secretary of State Marco Rubio arrived in Mumbai on Tuesday for his second India visit this year. He is meeting External Affairs Minister S. Jaishankar and goes on Wednesday to Reliance Industries’ Jamnagar refinery complex, hosted by Mukesh Ambani. The Washington Post frames the trip as part of Washington’s search for relief from the global energy crunch. U.S. tariffs on India total 50%, including a 25% penalty for buying Russian oil, and one official called a trade deal “very near.” Pressed by Indian reporters, Rubio defended continued U.S. engagement with Pakistan. He said Washington must keep relations with strategically important countries even when it disagrees with them.

Analysis: There was friction before the trip began. Indian opposition leaders objected after the U.S. ambassador said Washington expected Jaishankar to travel to meet Rubio, which critics called the foreign minister being “summoned” on home soil. The U.S. wants India to buy less Russian crude while global supply is tight, and India wants tariff relief. Each side needs something from the other, but neither has much room to give.

Sources: Washington Post, Oct 20, Express Tribune, Al Jazeera, Oct 18 (background)

Tech · Space & AI

SpaceX revenue jumps 161% to $14.6 billion in its first big beat as a public company, and Musk says Tesla and SpaceX have set up special committees to study a merger

+

SpaceX, which listed on Nasdaq in June in the largest IPO on record, reported Tuesday that third-quarter revenue rose 161% to $14.6 billion. The LSEG consensus was $12.72 billion. Net income was $4.4 billion, compared with a $1.1 billion loss a year earlier, and AI revenue grew more than ninefold. The company forecast first-quarter 2027 revenue of $33 billion to $37 billion, well above the roughly $22 billion analysts expected. Shares rose about 8% after hours and more than 10% in Wednesday premarket trading, after closing Tuesday at $147.50, below the $150 IPO open.

On the earnings call Musk confirmed that the boards of both companies have formed committees of independent directors to explore combining them, “at the request of shareholders.” As a conflicted party he cannot take part. He cautioned that treating a deal as certain “would be overstating it.” He also noted that a Tesla deal would need approval from 50% of all outstanding shares, not just shares voted, which he called “quite a high hurdle.” Fortune puts the combined value at about $3.5 trillion, and any merger would face antitrust review. Tesla reports its own results after Wednesday’s close.

Tech · Platforms & Kids

Court records show TikTok staff repeatedly warned leaders about child sexual content and suicide videos, and the company skipped age surveys over fears the FTC would see them

+

NPR reported Tuesday on depositions and internal documents filed in the federal multidistrict litigation over social media and young users, in which TikTok is a defendant alongside Meta, Google and Snap. The records show employees repeatedly raising alarms. Minors were appearing in sexual content that viewers could pay to access, so TikTok stood to profit from it, which staff flagged as a possible financial crime. Children were exposed to suicide content, and under-18 users were using loopholes to reach age-restricted material and hide their activity from parents. The documents also show TikTok avoided surveys of its users’ ages out of concern that it would have to give the results to the Federal Trade Commission. “I have been fighting about fixing this for months with people who didn’t think the harms are severe enough,” one executive wrote.

TikTok says the material was “clearly cherry-picked at the behest of trial lawyers.” It points to more than 50 preset teen safety, privacy and security settings, including Family Pairing. The company has not admitted wrongdoing, and the claims remain allegations in the litigation.

Sources: NPR, Oct 20, TechCrunch, Oct 20, OPB/NPR, Oct 20
Energy · Oil & Fuel

Gulf crude exports drop to about 8.5 million barrels a day as attacks on ships intensify; Brent eases near $109 on the Russia sanctions waiver, and U.S. diesel stays above $6.30

+

Kpler data cited by Reuters show that attacks on commercial ships cut Gulf crude exports last week to a seven-day average of about 8.5 million barrels a day, from more than 20 million the week before. Brent fell to about $109 a barrel early Tuesday after reaching $110.70 on Monday, its highest since May 20. Traders weighed Trump’s executive order waiving some Russia-related sanctions against the supply losses in the Gulf. Secretary of State Marco Rubio has described a 60-day period to assess the waiver’s economic effects.

Pump prices remain high. AAA’s national average for regular gasoline was about $4.26 a gallon on Wednesday, slightly lower than Tuesday, and diesel averaged about $6.32. Diesel peaked at $6.53 in September. The IEA’s October oil report expects world demand to fall about 2.6 million barrels a day this year, with diesel accounting for a quarter of the drop, and calls any recovery fragile while attacks in the Gulf continue.

NBA · Opening Night

NBA opening night: the 76ers spoil the Knicks’ banner night 106–100 in debuts for LeBron James and Jaylen Brown, and the Pistons rally from 22 down with a 23–0 run despite 38 points from Paul George

+

At Madison Square Garden, Philadelphia spoiled New York’s championship ring and banner ceremony. Tyrese Maxey scored 28, and his steal and coast-to-coast layup made it 104–98 with 1:52 left. In their Sixers debuts, LeBron James had 14 points, nine rebounds and seven assists, and Jaylen Brown had 19, eight and six. An 11–0 run to close the third quarter put Philadelphia ahead for good. Jalen Brunson scored 21 for the Knicks on 6-of-19 shooting, and New York shot 37.2% as a team.

In the opener, Detroit beat Boston 121–113 after trailing by 22, using a 23–0 fourth-quarter run (one outlet counts it as 18–0). Paul George scored 38 in 31 minutes, shooting 7 of 12 from three, the most points ever in a Celtics debut. Jalen Duren and Cade Cunningham each scored 22 for the Pistons. In the late game, Shai Gilgeous-Alexander scored 32 as Oklahoma City beat San Antonio 116–104.

English Football · Champions League

English clubs win in Europe: Liverpool come from 2–0 down to beat Villarreal 4–3 behind Mac Allister’s double, and Foden’s hat-trick leads Manchester City past AEK 5–1

+

Villarreal led 2–0 at Anfield inside half an hour. Georges Mikautadze scored after a rare misjudgment by Virgil van Dijk, and Sergi Cardona added a second. Alexis Mac Allister headed one back from a corner on 36 minutes, and Ronald Araujo equalized in first-half stoppage time. Mac Allister put Liverpool ahead on 65 minutes from Rio Ngumoha’s pass, and substitute Florian Wirtz finished a counterattack three minutes later. Villarreal scored again on 87 minutes, but Liverpool held on. Ngumoha had two assists and seven successful dribbles. Liverpool have nine points from three games, level with PSG, who beat Barcelona 4–2 in Paris.

At the Etihad, Enzo Maresca made eight changes after Saturday’s win over Ipswich and left Erling Haaland unused on the bench. Phil Foden scored three goals, his first Champions League hat-trick, and Elliot Anderson (his first City goal) and Rayan Cherki also scored in a 5–1 win over AEK Athens. It was City’s 150th Champions League match. Tuesday’s nine games produced 40 goals.

“We’re just going to confront that and go for it”: Arteta takes Arsenal to Bayern tonight without Saliba, while Kompany is ill and Olise and Musiala are out for the hosts
