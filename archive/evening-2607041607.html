America 250

America's 250th reaches its climax under a severe-storm watch as Trump readies a billed “historical” Mall address

+

As evening falls on the Fourth, the “Salute to America 250” is underway on the Washington Monument grounds and National Mall, and the day's defining new wrinkle is the sky: after a heat dome pushed the capital toward roughly 101°F, the National Weather Service placed the Washington area under a severe thunderstorm watch, forcing organizers to halt the “Great American State Fair” again and move attendees toward exits until the risk passes. The evening program began at 7 p.m. ET; Trump is slated to speak around 9:45–10 p.m. from the Lincoln Memorial — an address a White House official billed as “historical” and “unlike any other he's given before” — ahead of a fireworks show his team advertises as the largest in history, some 850,000 effects stretched to nearly 40 minutes so the smoke can clear.

Two developments since this morning sharpen the day's contested tone. PBS, which has broadcast the Mall's fireworks for decades, is instead airing its Independence Day special from Colonial Williamsburg this year. And the Trump-aligned Freedom 250 group's state fair has been dogged by spotty attendance, logistical breakdowns, at least one Confederate flag, and the fact that not all 50 states signed on. Trump, who has waved off the heat (“I'm gonna make a really long speech”), opened the weekend Friday at Mount Rushmore with remarks that veered from American exceptionalism into warnings about a communist “threat.” The Secret Service is leading security, with FBI support.

The Left-Leaning Take

A birthday remade into a rally, now literally rained on (Mother Jones, Slate, Vox): the storm watch, the twice-shuttered fair, the Confederate flag and PBS walking away are, on this read, the visible costs of bending the semiquincentennial around one man's marathon speech rather than the country. A “historical” address to a heat-battered, half-emptied Mall is spectacle for its own sake.

The Right-Leaning Take

A republic at 250 deserves the grandest showcase (WSJ editorial, National Review), and heat and a passing storm don't diminish fireworks and pageantry most Americans will enjoy far from the partisan sniping. The intra-right dissent (The Bulwark, anti-Trump) is unsparing — warning that staging the founding's milestone as a loyalty rally, capped by a self-billed “historical” speech, gets the day's meaning backwards.

Sources: CNN, NBC News, The Hill, PBS NewsHour, Axios, Forbes
Protest

“Free America” counter-protests spread across the country as anti-Trump crowds mark the Fourth

+

As the official celebration built in Washington, the Women's March–organized “Free America” weekend put demonstrations across all 50 states — organizers count more than 150 events — framed as reclaiming the holiday “from the grip of hate and the politics of fear.” Rather than marches, many took the form of block parties, cookouts, banner drops and dance gatherings aimed at Trump's mass-deportation sweeps and ICE raids, sweeping tariffs, and cuts to social services and health care. Anti-Trump protests also popped up in the capital itself as festivities unfolded on the Mall.

Full turnout figures won't be clear until the events wrap, and the same heat and storm threat suppressing the official festivities could dampen attendance. The mobilization follows this spring's “No Kings” protests — described as the largest anti-Trump gatherings since the second inauguration — and its scale relative to that benchmark will be an early read on the opposition's durability heading toward the midterms.

The Left-Leaning Take

Reclaiming patriotism from a strongman (Vox, Mother Jones, Guardian opinion): staging cookouts and banner drops in all 50 states denies Trump a monopoly on the flag and insists that dissent is the most American act there is. The breadth of the day is offered as proof the anti-authoritarian coalition is still growing, not fading.

The Right-Leaning Take

Sour theater on the nation's birthday (National Review, WSJ editorial): protesting the president on the 250th strikes much of the right as self-marginalizing and likelier to energize his base than to persuade anyone. A libertarian aside (Reason) grants that peaceable assembly on Independence Day is itself a fitting tribute, whatever one makes of the organizers.

Sources: Newsweek, TIME, CNN, NewsNation

Iran

Millions mourn Khamenei as chants of “Revenge” fill Tehran — and his successor stays hidden

+

The week-long state funeral for Ayatollah Ali Khamenei opened Saturday with enormous crowds — Iranian officials say more than 10 million people and delegations from over 100 countries are expected — filing into Tehran's Grand Mosalla prayer complex, where his flag-draped casket lay in state amid red flags symbolizing revenge and rhythmic chants of “Our word is one! Revenge! Revenge!” The mourning, running July 4–9 across Tehran, Qom and Mashhad and into Najaf and Karbala in Iraq, is engineered to project the Islamic Republic's resilience four months after Khamenei was killed at 86 in the opening U.S.–Israeli strike of the war. The scheduling deliberately overlaps America's 250th.

The spectacle underscores a striking vacuum at the top. The whereabouts of his son and named successor, Mojtaba Khamenei — thought to have been wounded in the same strike that killed his father, mother and wife — remain unknown even to senior officials, who say they can reach him only through couriers and written statements; no plan for him to appear at the funeral has been announced. The mourning period also freezes the indirect U.S.–Iran talks in Doha, which mediators had called “positive,” with the next round set only after the ceremonies conclude.

Sources: NBC News, CNN, NPR, Al Jazeera, CBS News
Ukraine

Ukraine strikes deep — a St. Petersburg oil terminal and a Kronstadt naval base — as Russia claims a contested Donbas city

+

In a marked shift from the past week's Russian barrages on Kyiv, Ukraine went on the offensive far from the front. In the early hours of July 4, Ukrainian drones struck the St. Petersburg Oil Terminal and a military site at Kronstadt — roughly 900 km (560 miles) from Ukrainian-held territory — in a joint operation by Special Operations Forces “deep strike” units, the HUR military-intelligence directorate and the SBU. Zelenskyy confirmed the attack, saying it hit “oil infrastructure funding Russia's war.” A day earlier a strike knocked out a substation at a Belgorod power plant, and Russian officials reported widespread electricity and water outages across the city from what one governor called a “massive strike.”

On the ground, Russia's Defense Ministry claimed the capture of Kostiantynivka, a long-sought objective in the Donetsk agglomeration, but Zelenskyy denied the eastern city had fallen and said fighting continued. The dueling claims and the reach of Ukraine's long-range campaign fit a broader pattern: with Russia's summer offensive largely stalled — Kyiv put June losses near 40,000 — the war's momentum is increasingly measured in deep strikes on Russian energy and naval assets rather than shifting front lines.

Vatican

Pope Leo declares a schism and excommunicates SSPX leadership after defiant bishop consecrations

+

A decades-old rupture in the Catholic Church has reopened. On July 1, the traditionalist Society of St. Pius X (SSPX) consecrated four new bishops in Écône, Switzerland, without a papal mandate — defying a personal June 29 appeal from Pope Leo XIV to “please turn back.” On July 2 the Vatican went further than many expected, declaring not only that the six bishops involved had incurred automatic excommunication (latae sententiae) but that all SSPX priests and any lay Catholics who “adhere formally” to the group are now in schism.

The move revives a feud dating to the SSPX's rejection of the Second Vatican Council's reforms and the 1988 consecrations that first drew excommunications — penalties Pope Benedict XVI lifted in 2009 in a bid at reconciliation. By framing this breach as a formal schism rather than a personal penalty on a handful of bishops, Leo has drawn a sharper line than his predecessors, effectively closing, at least for now, the reconciliation track the Vatican pursued with the traditionalist movement for years.

Sources: Washington Post, CBS News, Religion News Service, America, EWTN

America 250

America turns 250 into a record heat wave as Trump stages a National Mall “Salute to America”

+

The United States marks its semiquincentennial on Saturday under a dangerous heat dome, with Washington forecast to reach the low triple digits — potentially the hottest July 4 on record for the capital — as President Trump headlines the “Salute to America 250 Celebration & Fireworks” on the National Mall. The heat forced organizers to push the public opening to 5 p.m. and delay the program: the event begins at 7 p.m., Trump is slated to speak at 9:45 p.m., and a fireworks display his team bills as the “largest in history” — advertised at more than 850,000 pyrotechnic effects — is set to follow. The extreme temperatures had already disrupted the run-up: Philadelphia canceled its Independence parade, several Washington suburbs scrapped or postponed fireworks, and the “Great American State Fair” on the Mall was temporarily shut and cleared as the mercury hit triple digits.

Trump opened the holiday weekend Friday at Mount Rushmore and the new Theodore Roosevelt library in Medora, N.D., where — in a speech outlets described as veering from paeans to American exceptionalism into sharply political grievance — he vowed to deliver a “really long” Mall address “even if it's 107 degrees.” The celebration is overseen by Freedom 250, the Trump-backed nonprofit that House Natural Resources Democrats last week accused of self-dealing, allegations the group calls a partisan smear. Organizers privately worried about turnout in the heat; a piece of stage set broke loose during a rehearsal, and a former Olympic canoeist was indicted for allegedly damaging the Lincoln Memorial Reflecting Pool near the fireworks launch site.

A birthday hijacked into a personality cult (Mother Jones, Slate, The New Republic): Congress asked for a unifying commemoration and got a taxpayer-funded rally built around one man's boast that he can give a marathon speech in a heat emergency. The turnout jitters, the cleared state fair, and a stage literally coming apart, on this read, are the small humiliations of a 250th engineered for spectacle rather than the country.

The Right-Leaning Take

A milestone worth a grand federal showcase (WSJ editorial, National Review): a republic turning 250 deserves fireworks and pageantry, and the Democratic “corruption” report is a manufactured distraction from a genuine national celebration. The intra-right dissent (The Bulwark, anti-Trump) is pointed — warning that staging the semiquincentennial as a loyalty rally, with a president promising to prove he can “do anything,” is precisely the wrong note for the nation's founding ideals.

FBI & Elections

FBI “surges” 260 analysts to a priority probe of Georgia's 2020 election

+

An internal FBI memo obtained by multiple outlets directs field offices nationwide to “surge” roughly 260 investigative analysts and staff-operations specialists to what it calls a “priority investigation” into the 2020 presidential election in Georgia's Fulton County. Each analyst is tasked with reviewing an estimated 708 records by July 17, and leadership has authorized overtime including weekends and holidays. FBI Director Kash Patel is ordering the deployment; it builds on a January search warrant under which the bureau seized more than 600 boxes of 2020 election materials — physical ballots, ballot images, voter rolls and tabulation records — from Fulton County storage.

The effort revives claims of fraud in a race whose Georgia result was counted three times, including a hand recount, each affirming Joe Biden's win; no court or investigation has substantiated the widespread-fraud allegations Trump and allies have made. Critics see the personnel diversion as pressure to relitigate a settled election, while the bureau frames it as a records review. The scale — pulling hundreds of analysts off other casework on a tight deadline — is what makes the move notable, and it lands as the administration presses a broader elections agenda.

The Left-Leaning Take

The federal police weaponized against a settled election (Mother Jones, Slate): diverting 260 agents to comb ballots from a race counted three times, on a holiday-overtime deadline, is not law enforcement but an effort to manufacture doubt and intimidate election officials. On this read, Patel's FBI is chasing a conspiracy the courts already rejected, at the expense of actual criminal casework.

The Right-Leaning Take

Mixed, with real unease (National Review, The Dispatch, Reason): election integrity is a legitimate concern, but reopening 2020 with a mass analyst surge invites accusations of politicizing the bureau and burns credibility Republicans need for future fights. The libertarian objection (Reason) is sharper — a federal fishing expedition through a state's ballots is exactly the kind of investigative overreach conservatives claim to oppose.

Sources: Washington Post, ABC News, PBS NewsHour (AP), U.S. News (AP)

Ukraine

+

Lebanon

Israel–Lebanon framework faces its first test as Hezbollah rejects disarmament

+

The U.S.-brokered framework signed June 26 — under which the Israeli military would withdraw from southern Lebanon and be replaced by the Lebanese Armed Forces, which would then oversee Hezbollah's disarmament under international supervision — is straining almost immediately. Hezbollah's Secretary-General Naim Qassem has called the deal a “surrender” and declared it “null and void,” vowing the group will not disarm and will keep fighting Israel. Grid & Energy

The largest U.S. grid rides out a July 4 heat wave near record demand — without blackouts

+

A prolonged heat wave that put roughly 160 million people across 30 states under extreme-temperature alerts pushed PJM Interconnection, the largest U.S. grid operator, to the brink over July 2–3. Demand hit about 163 GW on July 2 — just short of the 2006 all-time peak of 165,563 MW despite forecasts near 166,000 MW — then, on July 3, PJM ordered customers in emergency load-reduction programs to cut usage as it fought generator outages, overloaded lines and surging air-conditioning load. Wholesale prices spiked past $2,000/MWh and operating reserves fell to roughly 5,091 MW, a thin cushion, but no blackouts occurred.

The strain prompted the Department of Energy's third emergency order of 2026: Energy Secretary Chris Wright's Section 202(c) authorizations let PJM run power plants past normal pollution limits and, as a last resort, force large data centers with 50 MW-plus loads onto backup diesel within 15 minutes of a signal. The episode crystallized the year's defining energy story — AI-driven, round-the-clock data-center demand colliding with climate-intensified heat — even as the grid entered summer with more than 58 GW of new capacity, including 16.4 GW of solar and 14.7 GW of batteries.

Sources: U.S. News (Reuters), CNBC, ABC News, OilPrice

Quantum Materials

Physicists tie superconductivity in twisted graphene to the “quantum geometry” of its flat bands

+

A study published July 4 in npj Quantum Materials reports flat-band superconductivity in alternating twisted quadra-layer graphene — four sheets stacked with engineered twist angles — and, crucially, an electric-field-tunable coupling strength within the superconducting phase. The team measured a vanishing Fermi velocity alongside a large superfluid stiffness, a combination that conventional theory struggles to explain, since flat bands imply nearly immobile electrons that should not sustain robust supercurrents.

The authors attribute the effect to “quantum metric” contributions — a geometric property of the electron wavefunctions — mediated by hybridization between the material's Dirac and flat bands. If it holds up, the result strengthens a growing view that the quantum geometry of a band, not just its energy, governs whether and how strongly a material superconducts, offering a design knob for engineering superconductors in twisted two-dimensional systems.

Sources: npj Quantum Materials (Nature), Nature Nanotechnology (related work)
