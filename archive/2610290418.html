Courts · Election 2026

Judge Carter refuses the Justice Department’s bid to set aside 20,000 California mail ballots, calling it a request that would “wreak havoc”; DOJ appeals within hours

+

On Wednesday evening, U.S. District Judge David Carter of the Central District of California denied the Justice Department’s request for a temporary restraining order. The department wanted California and Los Angeles County to segregate mail ballots from about 20,000 registered voters it says are likely noncitizens while their citizenship was checked. In a 26-page ruling, Carter found the government likely lacks standing and a cause of action. He said the remedy it wanted would likely violate several federal voting laws, including the 90-day bar on systematic changes to voter rolls before an election. He wrote that the request “violates longstanding constitutional principles mandating that states have control over their elections,” and that the department’s emergency was “solely due to its own neglect.” He called its method for building the list “vague” and said its verification protocol “raises significant concerns.” Los Angeles County’s preliminary analysis found that many people on the list have no active registration.

Carter said the better remedy is prosecution after the fact, using the identifying information on returned ballot envelopes. The department filed a notice of appeal the same evening. Bill Essayli, the top federal prosecutor in Los Angeles, said the “ongoing emergency disenfranchises American citizens through dilution.” Counting continues in California ahead of Tuesday’s election. Earlier in the week, a 9th Circuit panel had dismissed the department’s first emergency appeal as premature, because Carter had not yet ruled.

Pentagon · National Security

Hegseth fires Lt. Gen. Brett Sylvia, the Joint Staff’s top planner, as an official alleges leaks about the Iran and Ukraine wars and refers the case to DOJ

+

Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth has removed Army Lt. Gen. Brett Sylvia as director for strategy, plans and policy (J5) on the Joint Staff. The Atlantic first reported the firing, and AP and CBS News confirmed it. A Pentagon official, speaking anonymously, said Sylvia was accused of leaking classified information about the Iran war and the Russia-Ukraine war, had failed three polygraph tests about contacts with the press, and had been referred to the Justice Department. The Joint Staff’s statement thanked Sylvia for 32 years of service and did not mention the accusations. Other officials said they did not know why Hegseth lost confidence in him, and noted friction with Elbridge Colby, the Pentagon’s policy chief. ABC News reported that Sylvia has retired. He has not commented publicly, and the leak allegation rests only on anonymous sourcing.

Sylvia is a former commander of the 101st Airborne. He is the second J5 removed under Hegseth, and one of more than two dozen senior officers fired or pushed out on his watch, almost always without public explanation. The firing comes as Trump has signaled possible major new strikes on Iran after next week’s midterms. No replacement has been named.

Iran · Strait of Hormuz

Iran’s Guards say three tankers hit mines on the U.S.-backed southern route and are burning, as Trump plays down a Vance letter to Tehran

+

On Thursday, the Revolutionary Guards said three tankers caught fire after hitting mines on a southern route through the Strait of Hormuz, near the Omani coast, that Tehran calls unauthorized. Washington has encouraged shippers to use that lane. Separately, Britain’s UKMTO reported a vessel ablaze about nine nautical miles north of Khasab, Oman. It classed the incident as suspicious and said the crew’s status was unknown. It was not clear whether that ship was one of the three the Guards described, and earlier Guards claims about mine strikes have been hard to verify independently. The Guards also said they captured a U.S. MQM-170 drone in the western strait.

The diplomacy is equally murky. The Wall Street Journal reported that Vice President JD Vance sent a letter to Mohsen Rezaei of Iran’s Supreme National Security Council to set up a framework for reviving talks, and that Trump directed him to send it. Trump acknowledged the outreach on Wednesday but described it as an offer of help with medical supplies, and denied he had ordered new peace talks. Axios reported the letter said Iran’s nuclear program must be addressed in any deal. Iran has rejected the latest Qatari draft, though Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi says Tehran “continues to give diplomacy a chance.” Reports that Washington and Israel are preparing major strikes after the midterms if talks fail mean any oil-market relief is likely to be short-lived.

Brazil · United States

Flávio Bolsonaro and his family land in Miami a day after receiving U.S. visas; Lula calls the departure “shameful”

+

Flávio Bolsonaro lost Sunday’s runoff to President Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva and has not conceded. He landed in Miami with his family on Wednesday, a day after a senior State Department official said the Trump administration would issue them visas. He left Brazil in secret despite a Supreme Court order to surrender his regular and diplomatic passports by noon Tuesday, issued in a money-laundering investigation in which he denies wrongdoing. Reports say he crossed into Argentina on an Italian passport, and flight-tracking data showed his plane stopping briefly in Guayaquil, Ecuador, before flying on to Miami.

Lula said he regretted the departure because Bolsonaro could have stayed to clear his name, and called it “shameful.” Trump, asked about it, said, “I didn’t take sides.” Granting a visa does not mean asylum, and no asylum claim has been reported. The bigger diplomatic risks are a confrontation over extradition if Brazilian prosecutors move against Bolsonaro, and a rift between Lula and Argentina’s Javier Milei over how Bolsonaro was allowed to cross the border.

Russia · Sanctions

The Kremlin confirms talks with Washington on lifting sanctions from its sovereign wealth fund, as a trilateral Ukraine round is pencilled in for Abu Dhabi after Nov. 10

+

Kremlin foreign-policy aide Yuri Ushakov said Moscow and Washington are discussing lifting U.S. sanctions on the Russian Direct Investment Fund (RDIF). He said its chief, presidential envoy Kirill Dmitriev, is pressing American officials directly: “I don’t know what the outcome will be.” The New York Times first reported that the fund’s delisting request is going through standard Treasury review. A U.S. official called Dmitriev a helpful intermediary. Russian officials raised the issue in talks over RDIF’s role in Russian diesel exports, following Trump’s recent deal allowing those exports and waivers on other energy sanctions. The Biden Treasury sanctioned the fund in February 2022 and called it “widely considered a slush fund” for Putin. No decision has been announced.

The talks run alongside the revived peace track. After meeting envoy Steve Witkoff, Putin agreed “in principle” to resume trilateral talks with the U.S. and Ukraine after Nov. 10, with Abu Dhabi as the likely venue. He rejected a ceasefire, and Russia hit Kyiv with a large missile and drone strike right afterward. Zelensky has said he is open to freezing the front lines. Critics in Kyiv and Washington will see sanctions relief on the RDIF before any ceasefire as giving away leverage in advance.

Tech · AI Infrastructure

Same night, opposite reactions: Azure growth speeds up to 48% and Microsoft rises, while Alphabet’s record $51.4B capex and $9.1B cash burn send it down about 10%

+

Microsoft’s fiscal first quarter, ended Sept. 30, beat expectations. Revenue rose 19% to $92.3 billion, Azure revenue grew 48% (above guidance of 44–45%), and Microsoft Cloud revenue reached $63.9 billion. Capital spending of $45.2 billion was up about 30% from a year earlier but came in below the roughly $50.7 billion analysts expected. CFO Amy Hood said some third-party data-center capacity expected in the quarter had been delayed. Free cash flow fell 27%, and the shares rose about 4% after hours.

Alphabet reported revenue up 25% to $127.6 billion, and Google Cloud revenue more than doubled to about $30.7 billion, with a $561 billion backlog. But earnings missed, partly because of an $8.7 billion paper loss on equity investments. Capital spending hit a record $51.4 billion, leaving free cash flow at negative $9.1 billion. Management said capex will rise significantly in 2027, and Fortune reports roughly $300 billion. Shares fell about 10% after hours. Outlets disagree on the size of the earnings miss. Together, the two reports show how investors now judge the AI buildout: cloud demand looks real, but they are punishing companies whose spending outpaces their cash.

Climate · Extreme Weather

Cyclone Arnab makes landfall in Andhra Pradesh, killing at least three after more than 180,000 people were evacuated

+

Cyclone Arnab, which India’s weather agency classed as a very severe cyclonic storm, made landfall near Narsapur on the Andhra Pradesh coast on Wednesday morning. It brought sustained winds of about 135 km/h, gusting to 150 km/h, and up to 263 mm of rain in 24 hours in some districts. The National Disaster Response Force has confirmed three deaths: two people electrocuted by live wires and one killed by a falling tree. Andhra Pradesh’s chief minister reported about 70,900 hectares of crops damaged, power cuts, three airports closed and trains cancelled. More than 180,000 people had been moved from coastal areas of Andhra Pradesh and Odisha before the storm arrived. It has since weakened over land.

The Bay of Bengal’s post-monsoon season regularly produces storms like this. Warmer seas are giving cyclones more energy and more rain, while India’s evacuation system has been credited with sharply cutting death tolls. Early reports said there were no deaths before later ones confirmed three. The toll may rise as damage assessments come in.

MLB · World Series

Brewers win their first World Series, 4–1 in Game 5 at Tropicana Field, behind Misiorowski and series MVP William Contreras

+

Milwaukee closed out Tampa Bay 4–1 on Wednesday night to take the series four games to one and win the first championship in franchise history. Jacob Misiorowski threw 5⅔ scoreless innings, allowing two hits, before leaving with a right calf cramp. Rookie shortstop Cooper Pratt set a World Series rookie record with six straight hits and scored on David Hamilton’s single for a 2–0 lead. William Contreras added an RBI double in the sixth and was named Series MVP.

The Rays had stayed alive with a 12-inning win in Game 4. That game forced them to use their planned Game 5 starter in relief, so Drew Rasmussen started on short rest. Milwaukee finished the postseason 11–2, beating the Padres, Dodgers and Rays.

Sources: ESPN recap, Oct 28, ESPN, CBS Sports
English Football · Carabao Cup

Fofana skies the decisive kick as Liverpool beat Chelsea 6–5 on penalties; City, Bournemouth, Sunderland and Palace also reach the quarter-finals

+

At Anfield on Wednesday night, Morgan Rogers put Chelsea ahead from the spot before Cody Gakpo equalized with a penalty three minutes later, awarded for a Wesley Fofana handball. The 1–1 draw went to a shootout that lasted eight rounds, with five of the last nine kicks missed. Giorgi Mamardashvili saved from Chelsea debutant Emmanuel Emegha, Joe Gomez scored, and Fofana sent his kick over the bar into the Kop. Liverpool have now won five straight in all competitions, and Chelsea have lost five straight shootouts to them.

Manchester City also went through on penalties after a 2–2 draw with Brighton. Geronimo Rulli saved twice in only his second game for the club, though reports disagree on whether the shootout finished 4–3 or 5–3. Bournemouth beat Aston Villa 3–0 to extend their strong run, Sunderland edged Brentford 3–2, and Crystal Palace won 1–0 at Fulham. In the quarter-finals, Liverpool host Bournemouth, City face third-tier Bradford, and Sunderland go to Palace. Everton vs. Newcastle is played Thursday.
