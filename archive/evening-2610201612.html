Elections · National Security

Washington Post: a group of current and former national security officials has quietly advised officials in 10 states on how to respond if the administration interferes in the midterms

+

The Washington Post reported Tuesday that about 10 current and former national security officials, many with Defense Department backgrounds, have spent several months consulting officials from 10 states. Those contacts include governors, election administrators and members of Congress from both parties. The scenarios they discuss include troops or immigration agents being deployed to polling places, and efforts to nullify results on claims of voter fraud. With state governments, the group has focused on tightening election procedures that a federal actor could exploit. With lawmakers, especially Republicans, it has focused on awareness and on being ready to speak out publicly.

Participants told the Post that their warnings come from their own reading of the administration’s public statements and actions, not from intelligence or information they obtained in their official roles. They described risks, not evidence of a settled plan. The reporting rests largely on anonymous sources, and the group asked that the officials it met not be named. A White House spokesperson said the administration “is taking decisive action to protect our elections,” including prosecuting election crimes and strengthening election-infrastructure security. Separately, Joint Chiefs Chairman Gen. Dan Caine wrote in August that the military had “no plans” to send troops to polling places. A Votebeat survey last week found 19 of 31 election experts thought some federal personnel deployment at or near polls was at least somewhat likely. Election Day is Nov. 3.

The Left-Leaning Take

Liberal outlets treat the report as an alarm worth heeding. The New Republic headlined it as alarmed officials “secretly” trying to block Trump election meddling. In this view, the fact that national security veterans feel they must prepare states for their own government acting against them is itself the news. (No in-window Mother Jones, Vox, Slate or Guardian opinion piece found; summary drawn from New Republic coverage.)

The Right-Leaning Take

The administration’s position, as quoted by the Post, is that it is the one protecting election integrity, through fraud prosecutions and infrastructure security. Skeptics online have questioned the report’s timing two weeks before the vote and its reliance on anonymous sources. (No in-window National Review, Dispatch, WSJ editorial or Bulwark piece found; this summarizes stated administration positions and reported reaction.)

Midterms · Senate Map

Trump rallies in Greenville, N.C., tonight for Michael Whatley, who trails Roy Cooper by 14 in a new WRAL poll, as registered Democrats cast 45% of the state’s early ballots

+

President Trump is scheduled to hold a 7 p.m. rally Tuesday at Pitt-Greenville Airport for Republican Senate nominee Michael Whatley and for Laurie Buckhout, the GOP candidate in the toss-up 1st Congressional District. A WRAL/WSOC poll has Democrat Roy Cooper leading Whatley 54% to 40% among likely voters, and Cook rates the race Likely Democrat. As of Oct. 18, registered Democrats had cast 45% of North Carolina’s early ballots and registered Republicans 19%. Vice President JD Vance campaigned with Whatley in Hendersonville on Monday. The main GOP Senate super PAC stopped spending in North Carolina earlier this month and moved money to Kansas.

NPR reports that Trump is barnstorming the country in the final two weeks, with stops in red states including Kansas, Iowa and Florida. NPR reads the schedule as a sign of GOP worry. Both parties are deciding which races to give up: Republicans have more than quadrupled ad spending in Kansas’s Senate race and tripled it in Mississippi since late September, and pulled money from House seats in Iowa, Ohio and Nebraska that now lean Democratic. Ad spending on House and Senate races has topped $4 billion.

The Left-Leaning Take

Democrats are welcoming the visit. WRAL reports that a top North Carolina Democrat’s message to Trump was “Keep doing it.” They argue that tying Whatley, a former RNC chair, to an unpopular president helps Cooper. (No in-window Mother Jones, Vox, Slate or Guardian opinion piece found.)

Republicans say the rally is meant to lift turnout for down-ballot races like NC-1. The RNC argues the GOP has been cutting into Democrats’ early-vote lead day by day. (No in-window National Review, Dispatch, WSJ editorial or Bulwark piece found; summary drawn from reported party statements.)

Ukraine · Diplomacy

After Oslo talks, Zelenskyy says U.S. missile-warning intelligence “works now”; Witkoff heads to Russia with a paper on security guarantees, and Stubb reports “genuine progress”

+

U.S. envoy Steve Witkoff met President Volodymyr Zelenskyy in Oslo on Tuesday, with Norwegian Prime Minister Jonas Gahr Støre and Finnish President Alexander Stubb taking part. Jared Kushner joined by video. Afterward Zelenskyy said the U.S. had resumed sharing early warnings of Russian ballistic-missile attacks. Reports suggest Washington had suspended that intelligence last week. “Now it works,” he said. Kyiv had accused the U.S. of withholding intelligence, a claim Secretary of State Marco Rubio had called “scandalous.” Reports differ on whether the pause lasted three days or a week. A U.S. official called the talks “constructive.” The official said they covered elements of a comprehensive peace framework: security guarantees, a postwar economic plan and wider European security arrangements. Stubb said “genuine progress” had been made but that patience would be needed.

Zelenskyy said the sides discussed NATO-style, “Article 5-like” guarantees, and that Witkoff would take a document setting out options to the Russian side. He also said Ukraine backs a U.S. proposal to halt strikes on energy facilities, and that results on Patriot interceptors and Starlink restrictions could come within 48 hours. Witkoff is due in Russia on Wednesday. Reports point to St. Petersburg, but the Kremlin has said only that it hopes a Putin meeting can be arranged soon. Analysis: Restoring the intelligence is the clearest concrete result. It takes away a lever that had fed doubts about Washington’s commitment a day after Trump praised Moscow. The harder test is Wednesday: whether the Kremlin engages with any guarantee framework that gives Ukraine Article 5-like protection.

Sources: Washington Post, Oct 20, Irish Times (Reuters), Oct 20, Axios, Oct 20, Meduza (Reuters), Oct 20, Kyiv Post
France · Unrest

After a quiet night, Lecornu tells the National Assembly that targeted curfews will stay, admits the weekend police response “was not mobile enough,” and promises a “massive” deployment, with soldiers in support, for Saturday

+

Prime Minister Sébastien Lecornu told the National Assembly on Tuesday that targeted curfews helped keep the country calm overnight. Some areas will stay under curfew, and prefects and mayors will impose new ones locally where needed. AP reports fresh curfews in the city centers of Paris and Lyon. Lecornu said putting all of Paris under curfew was “out of the question” and that a state of emergency remained possible but unnecessary for now. He conceded that the Oct. 17 deployment “was not mobile enough,” leaving the centers of Paris, Marseille and Lyon exposed. He also accused ultra-left groups of an “insurrectionary” plan. “The deployment for next Saturday will be massive,” he said. Interior Minister Laurent Nuñez said the Sentinelle military force would be used to free police for crowd control. The student union UNEF has called for protests Saturday, and the CGT says it will join and urge peaceful demonstrations. Assembly blocs agreed to set up an inquiry commission.

Analysis: The government is shifting from shock to containment. It is using local curfews rather than a national emergency, and it is using troops behind police lines, a step that is politically sensitive. President Macron is facing growing criticism for going ahead with a three-day Gulf tour during the crisis. Saturday is the next test of whether the weekend was a peak or the start of something longer.

Sources: CNBC, Oct 20, RTÉ, Oct 20, AP via Manila Times, Irish Examiner, AFP via NZ Herald

Courts · Executive Power

Trump says work on D.C.’s East Potomac golf course will start “IMMEDIATELY” and calls Judge Ana Reyes a “crazed lunatic”; at a tense hearing she signals she will keep the tree-cutting ban in place

+

On Monday morning President Trump posted that renovation of the East Potomac Golf Links, a public course on the Potomac in Washington, would begin “IMMEDIATELY.” He said Interior Department experts could do the work “as of right,” called the lawsuit “ridiculous” and called U.S. District Judge Ana C. Reyes a “crazed lunatic.” Only a day earlier he had said work would start Nov. 15. Last week Reyes ordered the government to give at least three days’ notice before any “irreparable modification” of the course. That order followed the felling of more than 400 trees, which she found the government carried out in “bad faith.” A preservation group and individual golfers brought the suit.

At a hearing later Monday, NPR and NBC report, Reyes criticized the administration’s plan. She said she was likely to extend her order barring further tree removal, which now runs through at least Oct. 26. She had already rejected a Justice Department request that she recuse herself, calling it baseless in a 13-page opinion. In a filing, Associate Attorney General Stanley Woodward argued that a redesign of the course is not an “agency action” the court can review. White House aide Stephen Miller said the judge has no jurisdiction over maintenance of the course. Government lawyers had earlier told the court the course would not close. The Hill describes the case as a test of whether courts can constrain the administration.

The Left-Leaning Take

Writers on the left treat the episode as a small case with large stakes. In their view, a president publicly announcing work “immediately” while his lawyers tell a judge otherwise shows contempt for court orders. Chris Geidner’s Law Dork, which covers courts, wrote that the day of arguments showed “how far DOJ will go to back Trump.” Critics also cite the recusal motion as part of a pattern of attacking judges who rule against the administration. (No in-window Mother Jones, Vox, Slate or Guardian opinion piece found.)

The Right-Leaning Take

Administration allies make the argument Miller and the Justice Department made: how the government maintains its own golf course is a management decision, not an agency action courts may second-guess. They also call the injunction an example of district judges overreaching. (No in-window National Review, Dispatch, WSJ editorial or Bulwark piece on the hearing found; this summarizes the administration’s stated position as reported.)

Midterms · Polling

Reuters/Ipsos puts Trump’s approval at 31%, his lowest in either term, with Democrats ahead 47–35 on the generic ballot two weeks before the election

+

A Reuters/Ipsos poll released Monday found 31% of Americans approve of President Trump’s performance. That is one point lower than two weeks ago and the lowest in Reuters/Ipsos tracking across both of his terms. Sixty-eight percent disapprove of his handling of the economy, including 37% of Republicans. Among registered voters, 47% said they would vote for Democrats for Congress and 35% for Republicans. The four-day online survey covered 1,679 adults, including 1,279 registered voters, with a margin of error of 2 points (3 points for registered voters). Reuters ties the decline to the rise in gasoline prices since the U.S. and Israel began strikes on Iran in February.

Other recent polls point the same way. The Washington Post–ABC News–Ipsos poll has Trump at 33% approval and 66% disapproval, lower than at any point in his first term. NBC and Fox News have him at 34% and 36%. On Monday Trump rallied in Kansas for Sen. Roger Marshall, whose race has tightened, and told the crowd “the polls are fake.” He has also said Republicans will blame him if they lose. Election Day is Nov. 3.

The Left-Leaning Take

Liberal commentators see the numbers as the expected result of a war-driven price spike combined with the Putin diesel deal. They argue the gap on the generic ballot leaves room for Democratic gains beyond the House. Some warn against complacency, noting that Trump outperformed his approval ratings in past elections. (No in-window Mother Jones, Vox, Slate or Guardian opinion piece on the poll found; summary drawn from mainstream coverage of Democratic reaction.)

The Right-Leaning Take

Republicans quoted by the AP privately worry that disapproval of the administration is now a liability for down-ballot candidates. Trump and his allies dismiss the polls and point to rally turnout and his promised $5,000 payments if Republicans hold Congress. (No in-window National Review, Dispatch, WSJ editorial or Bulwark piece on the poll found.)

France · Unrest

France orders local curfews after its worst rioting in years: about 3,000 businesses looted, Limoges’s city hall set on fire and more than 2,500 people detained over the weekend

+

The protests began as student-led demonstrations over conditions in schools in Paris’s northern suburbs and grew into a movement against rising fuel and food prices. Interior Minister Laurent Nuñez estimated about 550,000 people marched nationwide over the weekend; the CGT union put it at 800,000. Most marches were peaceful, but looting and arson broke out in several cities. In Limoges, masked attackers burned a municipal police vehicle and then set the 19th-century city hall on fire. Mayor Guillaume Guérin called it an attack on the Republic. Officials say about 3,000 businesses were damaged or looted. Justice Minister Gérald Darmanin put the weekend’s detentions at 2,513, with 67 people jailed. Nearly 1,000 police officers and firefighters were reported injured, and no deaths were reported.

On Monday Prime Minister Sébastien Lecornu told local officials to impose “very limited” and “localized” curfews where needed, starting Tuesday evening. In Paris, people are barred from Place de la République and Les Halles. Marseille has banned gatherings from noon to 8 p.m. Officials conceded that police “lost control” in several cities, and President Macron ordered a security review. Lecornu has not ruled out a state of emergency, but Macron has so far not declared one. At his Kansas rally Monday, Trump pointed to the unrest as a warning of what could happen in the U.S. if Democrats win.

Sources: CNN, Oct 19, NBC News, RTÉ, Oct 19, Israel Hayom, Oct 20, i24NEWS
Ukraine · Diplomacy

Zelenskyy meets Witkoff in Oslo today, hosted by Norway and Finland, a day after Trump said “Russia, frankly, has been fine on Ukraine”; Witkoff is expected to go on to Moscow

+

U.S. envoy Steve Witkoff arrived in Oslo on Tuesday for talks with President Volodymyr Zelenskyy. Norwegian Prime Minister Jonas Gahr Støre and Finnish President Alexander Stubb are hosting. Jared Kushner has the flu and is joining remotely, according to a U.S. official. Representatives of Britain, France, Germany and Poland are expected to take part. Norway says Zelenskyy asked it and Finland to organize the meeting, and Støre said the aim is to “steer the diplomatic efforts onto a more constructive path.” A joint press conference is scheduled afterward. A U.S. official said Witkoff then plans to travel to Russia to meet Vladimir Putin and present ideas developed with Ukraine and its European partners. The Kremlin said it hopes that meeting happens soon.

U.S.–Ukrainian relations are at a low point. Trump has eased restrictions on Russian diesel, waived new Graham Act sanctions on Russia, and pressed Kyiv to stop striking Russian oil infrastructure. He said Monday that “Russia, frankly, has been fine on Ukraine” and that Zelenskyy was not helping. Kyiv says Russian strikes have become deadlier as American air-defense deliveries stall. The New York Times, as cited by Ukrainian media, describes the meeting as an attempt to pressure Ukraine into a deal. No outcome had been reported at press time.

Tech · AI

At a Berlin summit, Nvidia names four German startups it will back as part of a nearly $10 billion European investment push, and Merz tells companies to “open your doors for AI”

+

At the Invest in Germany Summit in Berlin, Nvidia said it will invest in Agile Robots (AI robots), Avelios Medical (hospital software), Mirelo (AI-generated audio for video) and Reflex Aerospace (satellites). It did not disclose the amounts. The stakes are part of a commitment of nearly $10 billion to companies, universities and research institutions across Europe. Nvidia also expanded its industrial-AI work with the German government, and Siemens announced a broader partnership with Nvidia on AI-driven autonomous factories. Chancellor Friedrich Merz urged businesses, especially small and mid-sized manufacturers, to use their data for AI, naming manufacturing, machinery and autos as priorities.

Separately, Nvidia released new open-weight Nemotron models on Monday. They are aimed at running AI agents in enterprise settings and locally on PCs, which extends Nvidia’s strategy of selling software as well as chips.

Sources: CNBC, Oct 20, Seeking Alpha, The Next Web, TechCrunch, Oct 19
Energy · Oil

Brent closes near $110 after Houthi claims of strikes on Saudi airports and Aramco and Ukrainian hits on Russian oil, then slips as markets weigh Trump’s Russia sanctions waiver

+

CNBC reports that Brent crude closed Monday at $109.55 a barrel after touching $110.70 during the day, and U.S. benchmark WTI closed at $103.66. Prices rose after Iran-backed Houthi militants claimed missile and drone attacks on Saudi airports and Aramco facilities, and after Ukraine’s weekend drone strikes on Russian oil sites. Iran is also attacking tankers in the Gulf, the Strait of Hormuz and the Gulf of Oman to choke off exports. In Asian trading, the AP reported crude falling more than 1% as traders weighed Trump’s waiver of new sanctions on Russian energy, which could leave more Russian barrels on the market.

Prices this high are now a political issue as well as an economic one. Pollsters cite fuel costs as a main reason for Trump’s falling approval, and the administration has justified its Russian diesel deal as a way to lower pump prices before the Nov. 3 election. Analysts quoted by CNBC say supply risks stay high while Hormuz traffic remains far below prewar levels.

Climate · Weather

The East Coast nor’easter is now Invest 93L: gusts near 60 mph tear at Outer Banks dunes, ocean water covers N.C. 12 again, and visitors are ordered off Hatteras Island

+

The National Hurricane Center has designated the coastal low as Invest 93L, which lets forecasters run additional models on it. Its latest outlook gives it a 40% chance over seven days of briefly taking on subtropical characteristics, up from 30% on Saturday. It is expected to drift erratically, then move northeast and away from the coast late in the week. Whether or not it gets a name, the NHC warns of high winds, coastal flooding and dangerous surf along parts of the East Coast for several days. There are coastal flood advisories as far north as New Jersey.

North Carolina’s Outer Banks are taking the worst of it. Dare County has ordered visitors (not residents) to leave Hatteras Island. Weather.com reports gusts of about 60 mph tearing at the dunes, and ocean overwash on Highway 12 only weeks after a section was rebuilt. The National Weather Service told people on Hatteras and Ocracoke to prepare for the possibility of being stranded for multiple days.

MLB · ALCS Game 6

Rays beat the Guardians 7–3 to win the ALCS 4–2; Freddy Peralta and homers from Caminero and Díaz send Tampa Bay to a World Series against Milwaukee

+

Freddy Peralta allowed one run over 5⅓ innings Monday night at Tropicana Field. Richie Palacios’s second-inning double put the Rays up 2–0, and Yandy Díaz and Junior Caminero each hit two-run homers. English Football · Premier League

Spurs finally win: Maddison’s curler, Marmoush’s first goal and a late Ben Davies strike beat Coventry 3–1 and lift Tottenham off the bottom, as Kulusevski returns after about 500 days out

+

Roberto De Zerbi’s Tottenham got their first league win of the season on Monday night. James Maddison, making his first competitive start since May 2025, curled a first-time shot into the top corner in the 14th minute. Jack Rudoni equalized for Frank Lampard’s Coventry four minutes into the second half. Omar Marmoush restored the lead on 58 minutes with his first Spurs goal, turning Milan van Ewijk and shooting past Carl Rushworth from outside the box; VAR rejected Coventry’s complaint of an elbow. Ben Davies scored from the edge of the area in stoppage time to make it 3–1.

Dejan Kulusevski came on in the closing minutes, his first appearance after an injury layoff that Sky Sports puts at 526 days. The win moves Spurs off the foot of the table. Reports differ slightly on the exact minutes of Kulusevski’s entrance and Davies’s goal.

Arsenal train and fly to Munich for Wednesday’s trip to unbeaten Bayern, the first of five away games in six, with Saliba’s back a doubt and Olise suspended for the hosts
