2026 Midterms · GOP Convention

On the first anniversary of Charlie Kirk’s death, Vance keynotes the convention’s final night as Trump prepares to close

+

The developing story since this morning is Night 2. On Thursday, Sept. 10, Vice President JD Vance delivered the keynote on the closing night of the Republicans’ first-ever midterm national convention in Dallas, with President Trump slated to give the convention’s final remarks afterward. Vance’s speech mixed the personal and the political: reflections on fatherhood — his fourth child, son Alec, was born in July — and on his friend Charlie Kirk, who was shot and killed one year ago to the day (Sept. 10, 2025) at a Turning Point USA event at Utah Valley University, alongside an economic pitch built on “no tax on tips, overtime, or Social Security.”

Vance, widely cast as the GOP’s 2028 frontrunner, took the stage after speakers including House Speaker Mike Johnson, Texas Gov. Greg Abbott and Sen. Ted Cruz. The night builds directly on Trump’s Night 1 $5,000 “dividend” pledge covered in this morning’s brief. As before, the convention nominates no one and adopts no platform; its purpose is fundraising and base mobilization ahead of Nov. 3. Courts · Mail Voting

A federal appeals court keeps the USPS mail-ballot rule blocked, throwing the fight back to the Supreme Court

+

The new development on this morning’s pending case: on Thursday, Sept. 10, a three-judge First Circuit panel — Gustavo Gelpí, Julie Rikelman and Seth Aframe, all Biden appointees — denied the Trump administration’s request to lift Judge Indira Talwani’s injunction blocking the USPS “Final Rule.” The panel held the rule is likely a regulation of the “manner” of congressional elections — a power the Elections Clause assigns to the states and Congress, not to an executive agency acting without congressional authorization — and that the government failed to show the rule would not cause “chaos and widespread voter disenfranchisement” before the election.

The ruling is another setback for the Justice Department, which has already asked the Supreme Court (emergency application No. 26A305) to let the rule take effect. The rule would require states to submit ballot-envelope designs for review, add unique barcodes, and upload mail voters’ names and addresses to a federal portal. The timing pressure is unchanged from this morning: ballots are already going out in the first states. (Legal/elections story weighted to Tier-1 court reporting, SCOTUSblog and Just Security. As of last confirmable reporting the Supreme Court had not visibly ruled — treat the SCOTUS action as still pending.)

The Left-Leaning Take

The directional left calls this the guardrail holding: an appellate panel finding the administration tried to bottleneck mail voting through an agency that has no such authority, weeks before ballots land. The remaining worry is a sympathetic Supreme Court overriding two courts to permit a mid-election rule change. (Directional.)

The Right-Leaning Take

The directional right stresses the administration’s framing of modest ballot-integrity and tracking standards, and frustration that a Biden-appointed panel keeps blocking them. A libertarian-federalist strand (Reason) concedes the harder point — the panel is likely right that this is a states-and-Congress power, not an executive one. (Directional.)

Sources: CBS News, UPI, Bloomberg, SCOTUSblog, Just Security

Israel · West Bank

A 12-nation move to sanction Israeli settlements deepens Israel’s isolation — and prompts a threat to shut Britain’s Jerusalem consulate

+

A coordinated Western move against Israeli settlements has hardened into a diplomatic rupture. Twelve countries — Canada, Denmark, Finland, France, Iceland, Ireland, Norway, Poland, Portugal, Spain, Sweden and the United Kingdom — declared their intent to ban imports of goods produced in Israeli settlements in the occupied West Bank, which are illegal under international law. The measures target settlement goods specifically, not trade with Israel proper. In announcing the U.K. step, the British foreign secretary accused “settler terrorists” of “ethnic cleansing” and framed it as a reset in relations.

Israel responded angrily and said it would close the British consulate in Jerusalem, a signal of how sharply ties have deteriorated. The broader story is Israel’s growing isolation among close Western partners over settlement expansion and settler violence: a bloc that includes G7 members and much of northern Europe acting in concert marks a notable break from the more cautious, U.S.-aligned posture of the recent past. (Facts from Tier-1 reporting; the coordinated announcement dates to Sept. 8 and the fallout — consulate closure, additional national measures — was still developing through Sept. 10.)

2026 Midterms · GOP Convention

Trump promises a $5,000 ‘dividend’ for every adult citizen — but only if Republicans sweep the midterms

+

Delivering the Night 1 keynote at the first-ever Republican midterm national convention in Dallas on Wednesday, Sept. 9, President Trump announced a new $5,000 “Trump dividend” to be paid to every adult U.S. citizen — conditioned explicitly on Republicans winning both the House and the Senate in November. He said the money “must be spent in the United States of America,” suggested it would be funded partly by his tariffs, and framed it as a product of the country’s economic success. He offered no mechanism and did not say whether Congress would be asked to authorize it. With roughly 245 million adult citizens, CBS and Time estimated the cost at upward of $1 trillion — an outlet estimate, not an administration figure. The idea escalates his earlier $2,000 tariff-dividend proposal.

Across a nearly two-hour speech Trump called his second term “the greatest two years in the history of the presidency,” asked Republicans to “pretend that I’m on the ballot… just one more time,” and warned that a loss would cost voters “your border… your tax cuts… your safety and security.” He promoted Ken Paxton over incumbent Sen. John Cornyn in the Texas Senate race, and — tying to the Iran war — said he ordered strikes on Iran despite Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent’s objections that war would hurt the economy. The convention nominates no one and adopts no platform; its stated purpose is fundraising and base mobilization ahead of Nov. 3. Vice President JD Vance is slated to keynote Night 2 (Thursday, Sept. 10), with Trump giving closing remarks. (No material factual disagreement across outlets on the dividend pledge; the $1 trillion cost is an outlet estimate.)

The Left-Leaning Take

The directional left reads the pledge as a naked vote-buying gambit — a president conditioning $5,000 checks on his own party taking power, dressed up as populism while the money would come from tariffs that function as a consumer tax. Fiscally incoherent at ~$1 trillion with no plan and no congressional buy-in, and paired with “pretend I’m on the ballot” and apocalyptic “you’ll lose everything” framing, it looks like a campaign leaning on spectacle rather than a record a ~33%-approval president can run on. (Directional; opinion columns were not reachable this cycle.)

The Right-Leaning Take

The directional right splits. Populist voices cheer returning tariff revenue straight to citizens as proof the economy is booming. But the fiscal-conservative and WSJ-editorial lane would be uneasy: a ~$1 trillion, election-contingent handout is exactly the unfunded spending the right usually criticizes, it concedes tariffs are raising real revenue from consumers, and gimmick checks are a poor substitute for a durable growth message. Anti-Trump conservatives (Bulwark/Dispatch-adjacent) frame checks-for-votes as caudillo-style politics — labeled here as intra-right dissent, not the mainstream-right view. (Directional.)

Sources: CBS News, Time, NPR, NBC News, Dallas Morning News
Courts · Mail Voting

The USPS mail-ballot rule sits at the Supreme Court, still blocked, as the first states begin mailing ballots

+

The Trump administration’s third emergency application over a USPS “Final Rule” — the operative filing is No. 26A305, filed Sept. 6, which supersedes the earlier 26A297 — remains pending before the Supreme Court. The rule, implementing a Trump executive order, would require states to submit voter data (name, address) tied to a unique barcode on every mailed ballot, direct USPS to deliver only ballots addressed to voters on a state-provided list, and impose envelope-design mandates. U.S. District Judge Indira Talwani (D. Mass.) blocked it for the 2026 election, finding it “threatens disenfranchisement of millions” and intrudes on Congress’s Elections Clause powers and authority reserved to the states. Justice Ketanji Brown Jackson, as Circuit Justice, had ordered the challengers’ response due Sept. 9.

The clock is what makes it urgent: North Carolina has already begun mailing ballots, Alabama was scheduled to start Sept. 9, and at least five more states begin the week of Sept. 13. (Legal/elections story weighted to Tier-1 SCOTUS reporting and Just Security. As of the last confirmable reporting this cycle the Court had NOT visibly ruled — treat the SCOTUS action as still pending. Do not conflate with USPS v. Konan, a separate case.)

The Left-Leaning Take

The directional left sees an executive-branch attempt to bottleneck mail voting weeks before ballots go out — a fix for a nonexistent fraud problem that risks disenfranchising millions and commandeers states’ Elections-Clause authority. Talwani’s ruling is the guardrail; the worry is a sympathetic Court greenlighting a mid-election rule change. (Directional.)

The Right-Leaning Take

The directional right emphasizes the administration’s framing: modest ballot-integrity and mail-tracking standards are reasonable, and a single district judge should not be rewriting federal postal administration nationwide by injunction. A libertarian-federalist strand (Reason) would still worry the executive order intrudes on powers belonging to states and Congress. (Directional.)

Sources: SCOTUSblog, CNN, NBC News, Just Security

Iran War · Strait of Hormuz

Trump says the Iran war will end ‘immediately’ after the midterms as clashes flare in the Gulf and oil nears $100

+

Before flying to Dallas on Sept. 9, President Trump told reporters that the ongoing U.S./Israel war against Iran — which he launched with Israel in late February 2026 (start dated Feb. 28) to end Iran’s nuclear-weapons capability — “will end immediately after our election.” He claimed Iran is deliberately prolonging the war to hurt Republicans, called Tehran’s government “on the verge of collapse,” and said gas prices would fall once the war ends. He indicated Washington is not currently seeking to restart talks: “a negotiation could possibly happen, but it’s not something we’re looking at.”

On the ground, fighting reignited around Sept. 1 after a roughly month-long lull. Through Sept. 9, U.S. Central Command said it had destroyed five Iranian oil tankers linked to the IRGC, after Iran fired ballistic missiles at U.S. Navy warships; Iran’s IRGC claimed attacks on U.S. warships and a base in Jordan. Traffic through the Strait of Hormuz — through which roughly a fifth of global oil moves — has fallen even as the U.S. insists the waterway is “fully open,” and Tehran has moved to declare a restricted zone. Brent crude climbed to near $100 a barrel (outlets differ on whether it “neared $99” or “topped $100”), up from ~$70 pre-war, with analysts warning of $120+ if Hormuz disruption persists. Russia · Ukraine

Russia resumes heavy strikes on Kyiv after a failed U.S.-brokered pause; Germany digs into its own Patriot stocks

+

A three-day limited pause in fighting — timed to a Moscow visit by U.S. envoys Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner around Sept. 5 — collapsed, and Russia resumed strikes rather than move to a durable ceasefire. On Sept. 8 a drone-and-missile barrage hit Kyiv and the Odesa region; Ukraine said it downed or suppressed 2 ballistic and 31 cruise missiles and 142 drones. Casualty counts diverge — several outlets reported 2 killed in Kyiv, while Al Jazeera reported “at least five killed.” A Sept. 9 morning strike hit a 24-story residential building in Kyiv. Ukraine hit back, with defense-intelligence units striking Russia’s Novorossiysk naval base and reportedly the frigate Admiral Essen.

On the allied side, German Defense Minister Boris Pistorius announced on Sept. 8 that Germany will send PAC-2 Patriot interceptors from Bundeswehr stocks now — plus air-to-air missiles, drones and long-range ammunition — warning of an intensified Russian winter air campaign. The core 2026 dynamic holds: interceptor supply, not launchers, is the binding constraint, and Berlin drawing down its own stocks immediately (Ukraine’s contracted ~1,000 Patriot missiles will take years to arrive) signals how urgent Ukraine’s winter city- and grid-defense needs have become, especially with the Iran war straining U.S. munitions and attention. (Facts from cross-referenced Tier-1 search summaries; the Sept. 8 Kyiv death toll is disputed (2 vs. “at least 5”) and several strike details are single-sourced to Ukrainian reporting.)

Sources: US News / Reuters, Forbes, Meduza, Al Jazeera
