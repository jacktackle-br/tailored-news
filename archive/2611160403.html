Aviation · FAA · Congress

On the Sunday shows, Duffy refuses to apologize for the nationwide ground stop, blames Congress and earlier administrations, and points to contractor L3Harris

+

Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy appeared on Sunday’s talk shows two days after Friday’s nationwide FAA ground stop. He said on NBC’s “Meet the Press” that “I don’t apologize for shutting down the air space and keeping people safe.” He blamed decades of underinvestment by previous administrations and argued that the $12.5 billion Congress approved in 2025 is “not nearly enough.” He also said that money is earmarked for items such as radios and radar and cannot cover software. He renewed the administration’s request for an additional $50 billion. As for the cause, Duffy said a contractor replacing copper telecom lines with fiber at a remote FAA site set off a router failure. He said L3Harris may have made “mistakes” but that it is still unclear whether the cause was contractor error or faulty equipment. NBC reported that the replacement caused problems with another recently installed router in Amarillo, Texas. L3Harris says it is cooperating with the FAA’s investigation, and Duffy said he does not believe it was a cyberattack.

Democrats rejected the funding argument. Sen. Chris Van Hollen said it was “really rich” for the secretary to blame Congress, because Congress has met the administration’s requests. Democrats also said only about $1 billion of the 2025 money has been spent. NBC reported that on Thursday, the day before the outage, Duffy and budget director Russ Vought had asked lawmakers for $30.1 billion. The request lands in an already difficult lame-duck fight over government funding, which runs out on December 11. Disruptions continued over the weekend: CNN put Sunday cancellations above 1,500, and NPR reported that delays were expected to last into Monday.

Health · Senate · Nominations

Cassidy says he can’t predict how Wednesday’s committee vote on Trump’s FDA nominee Heidi Overton will go, and Republicans have only a one-seat edge on the panel

+

Sen. Bill Cassidy chairs the Senate Health, Education, Labor and Pensions Committee. He said on CBS’s “Face the Nation” on Sunday that questions remain about Heidi Overton, President Trump’s nominee for FDA commissioner, and that he could not predict the outcome of the committee’s vote, scheduled for November 18. Cassidy has called Overton’s role in the administration’s childhood-vaccine order “almost disqualifying.” Republicans hold only a one-vote majority on the panel, so a single GOP defection could sink the nomination if Democrats vote together against it. Sen. Lisa Murkowski has separately pressed Overton on the abortion pill mifepristone. If confirmed, Overton would oversee the FDA’s ongoing safety review of that drug.

At her September hearing, Overton called the MMR vaccine safe and effective, which appeared to break with Trump’s own comments. On mifepristone, she said only that it had met the FDA’s standard and declined to prejudge the agency’s review. Trump nominated her about three months after Marty Makary resigned as commissioner. The same committee session will also take up James O’Neill’s nomination to lead the National Science Foundation. A committee vote would only send the nomination to the full Senate. Republicans are trying to confirm as many nominees as they can before Democrats take control of the chamber in January.

Russia · Ukraine

Russia says it downed 1,336 Ukrainian drones overnight, apparently Ukraine’s biggest attack of the war, as Zelensky claims over half of Russia’s refining capacity is knocked out

+

Russia’s Defence Ministry said on Monday that its air defenses intercepted 1,336 Ukrainian drones over 16 regions overnight. If accurate, that would be Ukraine’s largest drone attack on Russia since the full-scale invasion began. Kyiv has not confirmed the number. Late on Sunday, President Volodymyr Zelensky claimed that Ukrainian strikes had disabled more than 55% of Russia’s oil-refining capacity, a figure Reuters said it could not immediately verify. Zelensky also said that in the past week Russia had fired more than 2,300 attack drones, 1,590 guided glide bombs and 15 missiles at Ukraine, concentrating for days at a time on energy infrastructure. Ukrainian officials reported that hundreds of thousands of people were without power in the Zaporizhzhia and Dnipropetrovsk regions. Separately, Russian state media quoted Crimea’s Moscow-installed leader as saying Ukrainian forces had attacked the Crimean Bridge, with road traffic restricted into Monday. That claim comes from a single Russian source.

The escalation follows a week of U.S.-brokered diplomacy that did not produce a breakthrough. The trilateral talks in Abu Dhabi on November 10–11 yielded a prisoner exchange but no ceasefire. U.S. envoys Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner then met Vladimir Putin and Zelensky separately. Zelensky has also blamed restrictions on Ukraine’s use of the SpaceX-built Starshield satellite network for letting more Russian drones through. He says the limits have hampered interceptions of jet-powered drones as winter approaches. Both sides are now hitting each other’s energy systems hard, which suggests each is trying to raise the cost of the coming winter before any deal is reached.

Iran war · Gulf

Iran launches a drone strike on a U.S. base in Bahrain, its first in months, and Bahrain says its air defenses shot the drones down

+

Iran’s Fars news agency reported on Sunday that the Revolutionary Guards had attacked a U.S. military base in Bahrain with six drones, in retaliation for what they called “hostile U.S. actions.” Iran said the target was a U.S. defense radar facility at al-Riffa. Bahrain said its air defenses intercepted and destroyed several Iranian aerial attacks, the first such interceptions since September. Its foreign ministry condemned the “continued brutal Iranian attacks,” and Saudi Arabia, Qatar, Jordan and Egypt also issued condemnations. No casualties or damage have been reported. Two days earlier, Iran had claimed a strike on a U.S. early-warning radar in Bahrain. The U.S. military denied that claim and said it had intercepted two drones without damage.

The attack comes as the Guards keep saying that tankers are hitting mines in the Strait of Hormuz, which Washington disputes, and as talks between the two governments remain stalled. Markets are watching both. Brent crude traded in the mid-$90s a barrel in early Asian trading on Monday. Prices are being pushed up by the risk of attacks on shipping and held down by the near-return of Hormuz flows to pre-war levels and the IEA’s forecast of a supply surplus. In Yemen, CNBC reported that anti-Houthi government forces continued to pull back toward Aden on the same day.

Gaza · Humanitarian

A day after the EU’s demand, Britain, Canada, Italy and Australia jointly urge Israel to let shelter aid into flood-hit Gaza

+

On Sunday, the foreign ministers of the United Kingdom, Canada, Italy and Australia issued a joint statement urging Israel to allow humanitarian aid into Gaza. They said recent floods had “brought further suffering” to Palestinians living in tents and damaged buildings. The statement follows the European Union’s Saturday call for “immediate, unimpeded” access. Advocacy groups and aid agencies say tens of thousands of tents and other shelter supplies are being held outside the territory. UN figures cited in coverage put the number of people affected by flooding at roughly 226,000 and the number of damaged shelters at more than 40,000.

Israel says it has delivered winter supplies and blames UN agencies and NGOs for delays in collecting approved aid. Neither side’s figures have been independently verified. The pressure from several Western governments at once shows how the shelter shortage has become the most visible strain on the ceasefire that began in October 2025, alongside the unresolved standoff over Hamas disarmament.

Science, Tech, Energy & Climate
Climate · COP31

COP31’s first draft deal calls for “accelerated action” to move away from fossil fuels and a tripling of adaptation finance, with five days left to reach consensus

+

On Monday, Australia, which is co-hosting the talks with Turkey, released the first draft of COP31’s overarching political decision. The draft calls on countries to speed up the transition away from coal, oil and gas. It expresses “deep concern” that the Paris Agreement’s 1.5°C limit is about to be breached. It also keeps a target of tripling adaptation finance for developing countries to at least $120 billion, a central demand of those countries. Nearly 200 nations must approve the text by consensus before the summit closes on Friday, November 20.

Every major element is still contested. Oil Change International called the draft’s ingredients “promising” but said it is too early to know whether they will survive negotiation. Climate Action Network says wealthy countries are resisting the adaptation-finance figure. India and the G77 bloc have opposed firm targets or language that names specific technologies. Observers have also noted that Turkey’s separate electrification proposal does not require the added power to come from renewables. Australia’s climate minister Chris Bowen, who is presiding over the negotiations, has said nothing in the draft is locked in.

Tech · Chips · Nvidia

Days after resigning, Jensen Huang is weighing a return as Nvidia’s CEO while allies lobby the board, the FT reports, and Treasury Secretary Bessent calls for a “dramatic shakeup”

+

The Financial Times reported on Sunday that Jensen Huang has discussed with confidants a possible return to the company he co-founded. He resigned on November 12 after reports of a relationship with an executive at Megaspeed, the Singapore firm U.S. officials suspect of routing restricted Nvidia chips to China. Reuters later reported that he had also had affairs with two Nvidia employees. According to the FT, one ally is assembling a “war room” of Silicon Valley executives to lobby the board and will seek White House support. Investors Brad Gerstner of Altimeter and Gavin Baker of Atreides wrote to the board asking it to keep “all options on the table.” Organizers say an open letter supporting Huang has drawn more than 150,000 signatures, including thousands of employees; that figure has not been independently verified.

Others oppose a return. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent has called for a “dramatic shakeup” at the company, and Wedbush analyst Dan Ives has ruled out a comeback. Even one Huang ally acknowledged that the affairs with employees could be a “fireable offence.” CFO Colette Kress is serving as interim CEO. The FT reported Friday that the board had approached Arm CEO Rene Haas, a former Nvidia executive. Nvidia told Reuters it had not formally engaged any candidate. Singapore and Malaysia are both investigating Megaspeed’s chip shipments, and Commerce is reportedly drafting tighter export rules for Southeast Asia, so Nvidia’s next leader will take over amid a regulatory crackdown as well as a governance crisis.

Space · Astronomy

Webb finds ice around the young star Beta Pictoris that most likely formed in interstellar space before the star itself

+

A team led by Sarah Betti used the James Webb Space Telescope’s near-infrared instruments to detect water ice and carbon-dioxide ice in the debris disk around Beta Pictoris. The star is about 20 million years old and 64 light-years away. The results are published in Science. The carbon dioxide appears to be mixed into the water ice rather than forming separate grains. The water ice is overwhelmingly amorphous, with crystalline ice making up less than 7% of what was detected.

That structure is what makes the finding notable. Models predicted that ice in debris disks would become more crystalline over time as it is heated and processed. The amorphous, mixed ice found here instead looks like material that formed in the cold interstellar medium and survived largely unchanged. The authors conclude that the ice “most likely” predates the star and its planets. If so, some of the water available to young planetary systems is inherited from interstellar clouds rather than made locally. Headlines saying the ice is “older than the star” are a simplified version of that more cautious conclusion.

Sports
English football · Nations League

Ten-man England win 3–2 in Madrid to end world champions Spain’s 43-match unbeaten run and top Nations League Group A3

+

England won 3–2 at the Metropolitano on Sunday in their final Nations League group game. The result ended Spain’s 43-match unbeaten run and put Thomas Tuchel’s side top of Group A3 on goal difference. Harry Kane scored a penalty just before half-time after Pau Cubarsí brought him down. Ezri Konsa headed in Declan Rice’s corner early in the second half, and minutes later Ben White finished a move started by Kane and set up by Jude Bellingham, making it 3–0 before the hour. In the 63rd minute, Elliot Anderson was sent off as the last man for pulling down Lamine Yamal. Mikel Oyarzabal scored the resulting penalty, Nico Williams added a second on 75 minutes, and James Trafford made important late saves to hold on.

Tuchel called it his team’s best performance since he took charge — “under the circumstances, against who and where we played, yes.” Kane said England had been “clinical” and “dominant” until the red card. Reports differ slightly on the exact minutes of the second-half goals. The win avenges September’s 3–2 home defeat to Spain at Wembley. The Spanish press said England had “exposed the world champions.”

Arsenal

Ben White, booed at Wembley all autumn, scores in Madrid alongside fellow Arsenal defender Ezri Konsa, as Saka stays out ahead of Saturday’s trip to Newcastle

+

Arsenal players were central to England’s win in Spain. Ben White started at right-back in place of Trent Alexander-Arnold and scored the third goal. At club level, Arseblog’s break preview noted that the squad scattered after the 1–0 win over Hull City. Bukayo Saka is still out with the quad injury he picked up against Bayern Munich, and Gabriel is travelling with Brazil for friendlies in Singapore. Rice came off against Hull but joined up with England as planned and played in Madrid. William Saliba is close to his first competitive appearance of the season after a back problem. Arsenal’s next match is at Newcastle United on Saturday, November 21. Saka’s availability for that game is the main open question.

NFL · Week 11

McCaffrey’s long touchdowns lift the 49ers to 8–1 with a 46–40 win in Dallas, despite Prescott’s five TD passes

+

San Francisco beat the Cowboys 46–40 at AT&T Stadium on Sunday. Christian McCaffrey had 223 yards from scrimmage and three touchdowns, including catches of 74 and 66 yards, and Deebo Samuel added a 70-yard score. Brock Purdy went 22 of 28 for a career-high 389 yards and three touchdowns.

Dak Prescott tied his career high with five touchdown passes, going 30 of 51 for 343 yards with one interception, and George Pickens caught a career-best three of the touchdowns. Dallas cut a 19-point deficit to six, and Prescott ran in a two-point conversion with 43 seconds left, but the comeback fell short. The Cowboys dropped to 4–6.
