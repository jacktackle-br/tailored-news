Campaign 2026 · Final Week

“I can’t take a chance”: Trump rallies for Hyde-Smith in Mississippi, heads to Texas today for Paxton, and warns Minnesota Democrats not to “play with the Votes”

+

Trump spoke for about 90 minutes on Monday night at Crossroads Arena in Corinth, Mississippi, for Sen. Cindy Hyde-Smith. “I’m here because Cindy is doing great, but I just have to make sure,” he said. “I can’t take a chance.” He called her Democratic opponent, District Attorney Scott Colom, a “Democrat communist.” Hyde-Smith told the crowd that control of the Senate is at stake on Nov. 3. Colom said the visit shows Hyde-Smith is “scared,” criticized her votes on health care and the Epstein files, and again challenged Trump to a debate. NPR and the AP both said the closing tour through states Trump won easily shows the problems Republicans face going into the election. The same day, Trump posted on Truth Social that the federal government would be “watching closely” the Minnesota Senate race, in which Democrat Peggy Flanagan leads Republican Michele Tafoya 45%–42% in a KSTP/SurveyUSA poll. According to Breitbart, he wrote that “the only way she can lose is if they cheat.” CNN said the post appeared to set up a possible fraud claim.

Today Trump holds rallies in Corpus Christi (2 p.m.) and Lubbock (6 p.m.) with Senate nominee Ken Paxton. A Fox News poll from Oct. 21 has Paxton and Democrat James Talarico tied at 49%. Talarico is holding his own event in Lubbock less than three miles away, starting about an hour after Trump. Trump carried Texas by nearly 14 points in 2024, and this is his fourth visit to the state since Labor Day. Early voting is running high. CNN puts the total at more than 30 million ballots already cast. Harris County set a midterm record on the first day of early voting, and Texas turnout is about 74% above the same point in 2022. Iowa is running behind its 2022 pace, and Michigan is about even with it.

Democrats point to the map. A president who has to defend Mississippi and Texas in the last week, they argue, is in trouble, and the Minnesota post fits a familiar pattern of questioning a result before any votes are counted. Colom’s campaign and Flanagan, who called Trump “the most corrupt president of all time,” are both using the attacks to raise money and turn out voters. Republican strategists quoted by The Hill call the red-state tour “necessary and risky.” It is not meant to win over independents. It is meant to make sure base voters who usually skip midterms turn out where Trump is most popular. Conservative outlets present the Minnesota post as a warning about ballot handling in a state hit by a major Medicaid-fraud scandal, not as an advance excuse for losing. Courts · Washington, D.C.

Judge Ana Reyes bars further tree removal at East Potomac golf course and requires notice before major changes; Trump calls the ruling unconstitutional and says the course will still close Nov. 15

+

U.S. District Judge Ana Reyes issued a 70-page preliminary injunction on Monday. It bars the administration from removing or damaging any more trees at the public East Potomac Golf Links without a court order while the lawsuit continues. It also requires 10 business days’ written notice before any “irreparable modification” to the course. The plaintiffs are the D.C. Preservation League and a group of golfers. Reyes found that the government likely violated the National Environmental Policy Act by dividing the renovation into smaller projects. She wrote that on Oct. 10, “without meaningful notice to Plaintiffs or the Court,” the government bulldozed hundreds of trees, and she called this a “chop-first, review-later approach.”

The opinion, which framed the dispute as “Lorax v. Once-ler redux,” also criticized the Justice Department for telling the court something different from what the president had said in public. Reyes wrote that DOJ rarely, if ever, asks a court not to believe the sitting president. Trump said on Truth Social that the ruling was unconstitutional and that the government would appeal, and repeated that the course will close on Nov. 15 for renovation. No appellate ruling had been reported as of this morning. At an earlier hearing, Reyes said she had received death threats in connection with the case.

The Left-Leaning Take

Critics see the case as a small example of a larger pattern: act first, then argue that the courts cannot undo it. The judge herself pointed to the mismatch between the president’s public statements and his lawyers’ filings, and critics argue that this mismatch, more than the trees, is what matters. (No in-window Mother Jones, Vox, Slate or Guardian opinion piece found; this summarizes how the ruling was received in news coverage.)

The Right-Leaning Take

Supporters of the project describe a run-down, little-used public course on federal land that the executive branch is entitled to improve. They argue that a NEPA injunction over a golf renovation is the kind of district-court overreach that higher courts have increasingly reined in. Trump also said golfers are being hit by balls on the current layout. (No in-window National Review, Dispatch, WSJ editorial or Reason piece found; this reflects the administration’s stated position.)

Israel

Israelis are voting now in the first Knesset election since Oct. 7, with polls closing at 10 p.m. local time and exit polls due immediately after

+

Polls opened at 7 a.m. on Tuesday for about 7.3 million eligible voters. Thirty-eight parties are competing for the 120 Knesset seats. The main contest is between Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu’s Likud and former army chief Gadi Eisenkot’s Yashar, which led in most final polls. The polls disagree widely by outlet. Channel 12 had Yashar at 23 seats and Likud at 21. The pro-Netanyahu Channel 14 had Likud near 30. Haaretz’s polling average gives Netanyahu’s bloc 50 seats and the opposition 54, both short of the 61 needed to govern. The IDF has imposed a general closure on the West Bank for election day.

The campaign was fought largely over Oct. 7 and the war. Eisenkot wants an independent state inquiry into the Hamas attack and to draft more ultra-Orthodox men. Netanyahu opposes both and depends on Haredi parties for his coalition, and he has warned that an opposition win would mean citizenship for “hundreds of thousands of Palestinians.” The election itself is only the first step. Results will take days to finalize, and NPR notes that it could be weeks or months before anyone is asked to form a government.

Brazil

Trump says Brazil’s runoff “looked like another rigged election” as Flávio Bolsonaro’s own party accepts Lula’s fourth-term win

+

According to Brazil’s electoral court, Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva beat Flávio Bolsonaro in Sunday’s runoff with 51.8% of valid votes to 48.2%, a margin of more than 4 million votes. CNN reports that this is the first time since direct elections returned in 1989 that a candidate who lost the first round went on to win the runoff. Bolsonaro, like his father in 2022, at first refused to concede and said he would seek to annul the result. He pointed to a Supreme Court justice’s order keeping polls open two hours longer, but one academic analysis found fewer than 35,000 votes were cast in that extra time. On Monday, his Liberal Party said it recognized the results as proclaimed by the electoral court, which in effect rules out a formal challenge. Party head Valdemar Costa Neto added that “the issues raised need to be properly clarified.”

Speaking on Monday, Trump said the vote “looked like another rigged election” and offered no evidence, Reuters and Bloomberg report. He also said he gets along “very well” with Lula, who wrote that “democracy prevailed.” Vice President Geraldo Alckmin defended Brazil’s electoral system. Markets reacted sharply. CNBC, citing LSEG data, reported that the real fell about 10% against the dollar, and a U.S.-listed Brazil ETF fell steeply before the open. For Washington the result means a second term for the Latin American leader who has most openly opposed U.S. tariffs and pressure in the region.

Sources: Reuters via U.S. News, Oct 26, Bloomberg, Oct 26 (partial — paywalled), CNN, Oct 26, NPR, Oct 25, CNBC, Oct 26, Al Jazeera, Oct 27
Russia–Ukraine

A U.S.-brokered truce for occupied Oleshky begins hours after Russian ballistic missiles hit a Kyiv university campus and the city’s Southern Bridge

+

A local ceasefire in Russian-occupied Oleshky, on the left bank of the Dnipro near Crimea, took effect at 3 a.m. local time on Tuesday. It is meant to let humanitarian aid in and prepare an evacuation through a safe corridor for the 1,700 to 2,000 residents still there, many of them elderly. The White House says First Lady Melania Trump acted as the “exclusive intermediary.” Russia’s defense ministry announced a “regime of silence” in the Kherson sector, and Ukraine’s human-rights commissioner said Kyiv was meeting its commitments. Several key details are still missing. No side has said who will monitor the truce or how the aid will be delivered. CNBC reports that it runs until Saturday, and AFP could not immediately confirm that fighting had slowed. Three-way U.S.–Russia–Ukraine talks are due to resume in November.

Russia kept up its strikes elsewhere. On Monday evening, ballistic missiles hit the campus of Kyiv’s State University of Trade and Economics, damaging academic buildings and dormitories. Most of the injured were students. Zelenskyy put the injured from that strike at 12, five of them students or staff. City officials reported one person killed in the capital, and the education ministry said no students or staff were among the dead. A second wave at about 3 a.m. damaged the Southern Bridge and the metro line that crosses it, splitting the green line in two. Zelenskyy said Russia launched 16 ballistic missiles and more than 280 drones overnight. Casualty figures are still preliminary and differ across reports.

AI · Mathematics

OpenAI says an internal model has disproved the Exponential Time Hypothesis and proved two other major open problems; the hypothesis’s original authors are organizing an independent check

+

OpenAI announced three results on Monday that it attributes to its internal models. First, a manuscript dated Oct. 25 describes a deterministic algorithm that it says counts the satisfying assignments of k-CNF formulas in subexponential time. Applied to 3-SAT, that would refute the Exponential Time Hypothesis (ETH), a widely used assumption in complexity theory that supports many “this cannot be done faster” results. Second, OpenAI claims a proof of local smoothing in every dimension, which would also settle the Kakeya and Stein restriction conjectures. Third, it claims an existence proof for four-dimensional quantum field theories, including pure Yang–Mills and QCD. OpenAI says the ETH and analysis proofs have been formally checked in the Lean proof assistant and published, but it has not released the physics work.

None of the three has been peer-reviewed. OpenAI asked outside experts to act as “shepherds” for each result. Russell Impagliazzo and Ramamohan Paturi, who proposed ETH, and MIT’s Ryan Williams are organizing an open review of that claim. Williams said their involvement “should not be taken as certification” and that they have asked OpenAI to release all raw data and Lean source. A group led by Nobel laureate David Gross is reportedly reviewing the physics claim. Early reaction from researchers has been skeptical. A Lean check confirms that the formal statement was proved, not that it matches what human researchers mean by the conjecture. If the ETH result holds, many hardness results that assume it would need to be revisited, though it would not by itself produce faster practical algorithms. Energy · Oil

Aramco contains a fire near Saudi Arabia’s Ras Tanura export hub after an overnight attack, and Brent rises back toward $116

+

Bloomberg reports that a fire broke out early Tuesday near Saudi Aramco facilities at Ras Tanura on the Gulf coast and was contained within hours. Ras Tanura has the kingdom’s main tanker-loading terminal, its largest refinery and a large tank farm. The extent of the damage was unclear, and Aramco had not made a statement. The Saudi-led coalition said that over the same period it intercepted three Houthi ballistic missiles aimed at the Eastern Province, which includes Ras Tanura, along with four drones, without casualties. It also said it struck missile launchers, storage tunnels and a depot in Yemen. No one has officially confirmed that the fire was caused by a Houthi strike. Aramco’s western refineries and its Dhahran headquarters have already been hit in recent weeks.

In early trading Brent was up about 0.4% at roughly $116 a barrel and WTI was near $102, reversing Monday’s small dip. Brent topped $117 last week, its highest level since April, after Iranian attacks on tankers near the Strait of Hormuz. Traders are weighing the attacks against signs of diplomacy, including an agreement by Iran, Saudi Arabia, Pakistan and Turkey to keep talking after a meeting in Tehran. The IEA says its World Energy Outlook, due in November, will be framed around the energy crisis tied to the Middle East conflict.

Grid · Data Centers

Arizona’s attorney general counts at least 40 likely data-center projects that would roughly double the state’s data-center power demand, while South Korea scales back its AI electricity forecast

+

KJZZ, Phoenix’s NPR station, reported Monday that Arizona utilities are struggling to keep up with data-center demand. The attorney general’s office has identified at least 40 likely projects that together would roughly double the state’s existing data-center electricity load. One proposal, the La Osa project near Eloy, was designed to draw 3 gigawatts at peak before it was rejected. Utilities are speeding up transmission and generation plans to meet the demand, and the question of who pays is becoming political. In a recent Utility Dive survey, 83% of respondents said the cost of grid upgrades for AI data centers is being passed on to residential customers.

Other countries are revising their estimates. South Korea’s minister told a National Assembly audit on Monday that the government will lower its forecast for AI data-center power demand. In August the ministry had raised its 2040 peak projection from 4 GW to 11.9 GW. He said little of that demand has actually been firmed up. The two reports show how uncertain these projections are, and they matter because utilities are building expensive gas plants, batteries and transmission lines on the basis of them.

Sources: KJZZ (NPR), Oct 26, Seoul Economic Daily, Oct 27, Utility Dive

MLB · World Series Game 3

Brewers score eight runs in the fifth inning, beat the Rays 10–1 and lead the World Series 3–0, one win from their first title

+

Game 3 at Tropicana Field was 2–0 Milwaukee until the fifth inning, when the Brewers scored eight runs with two outs. Their record-setting rally included seven consecutive hits, a first in postseason history, and was helped by a Rays defensive mistake: Junior Caminero threw to second instead of first. Jackson Chourio went 4-for-5, finished the inning with an RBI double and stole a base. At 22, he is the second-youngest player with four hits and a steal in a World Series game. Brice Turang and Cooper each drove in two runs.

Robert Gasser gave Tampa Bay almost nothing. He allowed only Yandy Díaz’s leadoff single in the first and faced the minimum 18 batters on 75 pitches over six scoreless innings. He is the first left-hander to face the minimum through six innings of a World Series game since Sandy Koufax in 1965. Milwaukee has won eight straight games this postseason and can win the series in Game 4 in St. Petersburg.
