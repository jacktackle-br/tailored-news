White House · The Iran War

Trump says he is in ‘deciding mode’ on Iran — weighing ‘wiping them out,’ letting them ‘rot,’ or a deal

+

In a Fox News interview aired Sunday, Sept. 20, President Trump said he is in “deciding mode” on the roughly eight-month US–Iran war, sketching three paths: “wiping them out,” letting Iran “rot economically,” or striking a deal to end the fighting. “My question is, if and when do I blow the entire nation up? They better behave,” he said, adding that “very big things” would happen “in the not-too-distant future” and that he would probably be open to meeting Iranian President Masoud Pezeshkian, who is due at the UN General Assembly this week.

The comments land as Iran’s security official Mohsen Rezaei says Tehran has relayed conditions to end the war through Qatari mediators — a halt to fighting on all fronts, release of frozen funds and an end to the U.S. naval blockade — while an IRGC spokesman warned that any new U.S. assault would change “the geography of the war” and the weapons Iran deploys. U.S. embassies across the Middle East issued fresh security alerts for Americans, and the war’s pressure on oil supply continues to keep gasoline and diesel prices elevated. (Trump’s options are his own framing; Iran’s terms are one side’s account, and the U.S. had not formally responded as of the latest reports.)

The Left-Leaning Take

The directional left hears in “do I blow the entire nation up?” the reckless bravado of a president who promised a quick war and is now, eight months in, musing openly about mass killing while gas and diesel bleed household budgets. They argue Iran has put real terms on the table through Qatar and that the responsible move is to take the off-ramp, not escalate — and warn that “deciding mode” theater is a way to look strong while dodging the diplomacy that could actually end it. (Directional.)

The Right-Leaning Take

The hawkish mainstream right wants the pressure kept on until Iran’s nuclear program is finished for good, and reads “they better behave” as leverage before any UN encounter. But the fracture is real: a populist-MAGA and libertarian bloc (Reason) never wanted this war, sees an eighth month of an open-ended entanglement spiking prices at home, and presses Trump to take the deal and get out. The Bulwark (anti-Trump right, an intra-right dissent) treats the “blow the nation up” talk as unserious and dangerous. (Directional.)

Drug Pricing · Transparency

Secret contracts show Trump’s Pfizer and Lilly drug-price deals included tariff shields and overseas price hikes

+

Contracts obtained through Freedom of Information Act lawsuits and reported by The Washington Post on Friday, Sept. 19 reveal previously undisclosed terms in the administration’s “most favored nation” drug-pricing deals with Pfizer and Eli Lilly. The agreements — which Trump celebrated at the White House as voluntary U.S. price cuts — also contained tariff protections for the companies, inducements to raise prices overseas, and broad confidentiality clauses. The documents show key terms were still unresolved when executives promoted the deals alongside the president, and detail commitments — such as how disputes with Pfizer would be handled — that were never made public.

The administration says it has now struck pricing deals with 26 drugmakers, including Novo Nordisk, and claims they will save Americans more than $600 billion over a decade. Critics say the newly surfaced terms undercut that headline, trading visible U.S. discounts for hidden tariff carve-outs and higher prices abroad. (The contracts are genuine but partial; several provisions remained subject to later negotiation, and the projected savings are the White House’s own estimate.)

The Left-Leaning Take

The directional left calls this the receipt on a con: splashy price-cut announcements for the cameras, while the fine print hands drugmakers tariff protection and license to gouge patients in other countries. The secrecy, they argue, is the tell — if the deals were the historic win Trump claims, there would be no need to fight FOIA suits to keep the terms hidden. (Directional.)

The Right-Leaning Take

The mainstream right still counts lower U.S. list prices as a real deliverable and frames the overseas hikes as making foreign free-riders finally pay their share. The free-market and libertarian lane (Reason) is warier: government price-setting plus bespoke tariff shields for favored firms is industrial policy and cronyism, not a market fix, and the opacity invites exactly this kind of embarrassment. (Directional.)

Sources: Washington Post, Yahoo News, NBC News, Newsmax
Congress · Government Funding

Congress is out of town with a shutdown nine days away, the ACA-subsidy fight still unresolved

+

Lawmakers left Washington for a one-week recess with government funding set to lapse at the end of Sept. 30 and no deal in sight. On Friday, Sept. 19, the House had passed a Republican continuing resolution 217–212 to fund the government through Nov. 21; the Senate then rejected both parties’ bills, with the Democrats’ version — extending the enhanced ACA premium subsidies and reversing 2025 Medicaid cuts — failing 47–45, and the House-passed GOP stopgap failing 44–48 as Lisa Murkowski and Rand Paul broke ranks.

Neither side is close to the 60 votes needed, and the core dispute is unchanged: Democrats refuse to fund the government without locking in the enhanced ACA subsidies, whose year-end expiration is projected to sharply raise premiums for the roughly 21 million-plus people on the marketplaces, while Republicans insist on a clean stopgap and a separate later vote. With only a handful of working days left after the recess, the odds of an Oct. 1 shutdown have risen. (Vote counts are consistent across outlets; whether a deal comes together in the final days is unresolved.)

The Left-Leaning Take

The directional left says Democrats are right to make their stand here: letting the subsidies lapse means real premium spikes for millions, a cliff the GOP’s own 2025 law built. With Republicans holding the House, Senate and White House and still unable to pass their own funding bill, the left argues, ownership of any shutdown falls squarely on the majority. (Directional.)

The Right-Leaning Take

The mainstream right frames it as a “Schumer shutdown” — Democrats bolting an expensive health-care demand onto a routine stopgap. But the honest read on the right notes the GOP couldn’t muster even 50: Paul balked on spending and Murkowski wanted more, an intra-party governance failure as much as a Democratic blockade. (Directional.)

Science · Cosmology

A catalog of nearly 3,000 supernovae strengthens the hint that dark energy is weakening over time

+

A University of Queensland-led team has released “Supernovae Unite,” a combined catalog of 2,884 Type Ia supernovae that merges the Pantheon+ and DES-SN5YR samples with three decades of measurements under a single, consistent calibration. Rather than confirming the standard model — in which dark energy is a fixed cosmological constant — the reanalysis adds to evidence that dark energy may be changing over time, with a roughly 3.1-sigma hint that it is weakening. That is short of the 5-sigma bar for a formal discovery, but notable because it points the same way as earlier hints.

Crucially, the supernova signal now lines up with results from the Dark Energy Spectroscopic Instrument (DESI), which has seen similar hints in the imprint of primordial sound waves — meaning two independent probes are tugging in the same direction. If evolving dark energy holds up, it would be one of the most consequential shifts in cosmology in a generation. (A 3.1-sigma result is suggestive, not conclusive; larger samples from DESI and the Vera C. Rubin Observatory should sharpen it.)

Tech · Quantum Computing

Penn physicists entangle four qubits in a room-temperature diamond in one gate — about 10 times faster

+

Researchers at the University of Pennsylvania, writing in Nature Nanotechnology, report generating a four-qubit Greenberger–Horne–Zeilinger (GHZ) entangled state inside a room-temperature diamond — linking the electron spin of a nitrogen-vacancy center with three surrounding carbon-13 nuclear spins — in about 14.8 microseconds using a single control sequence. The trick is turning the usual enemy, quantum “crosstalk” between spins, into a resource: a single dynamical-decoupling gate drives all the entangling operations in parallel.

The parallel approach cut the operation time by roughly 90% versus the conventional step-by-step method while raising fidelity to about 0.92 (from 0.69). Because it runs without cryogenic cooling, the work points toward more capable room-temperature quantum sensors and more efficient error correction in solid-state quantum processors. (A laboratory demonstration on a small register; scaling to many qubits remains the hard, unsolved problem.)

Sources: The Quantum Insider, Quantum Computing Report

+

US electric-carmaker Lucid and European ride-hailing platform Bolt announced a partnership to deploy at least 25,000 autonomous vehicles across major European cities, built on Lucid’s upcoming midsize platform and running Nvidia’s “Hyperion” autonomous-driving architecture, engineered for SAE Level 4 (no human driver in defined conditions). (The 25,000 figure is a stated ambition, not a delivery schedule; neither company has said when the first cars will carry paying passengers, and Level 4 approvals vary sharply by country.)

Energy · Grid Storage

RWE greenlights a 1.1 GWh battery in the Netherlands, paired with North Sea offshore wind

+

German utility RWE has taken a final investment decision to build a 1.1 GWh battery-storage project in the Netherlands, one of the largest in the country, according to trade-press reporting on Sept. 21. The battery is designed to work alongside a nearby 795 MW offshore wind project — developed by RWE with TotalEnergies — and to help relieve grid congestion in the Noord-Brabant region, where surging renewable generation increasingly outruns the wires that carry it.

The decision is a small but telling data point in a fast-scaling trend: grid-scale batteries were the fastest-growing power technology worldwide last year, and pairing them with offshore wind and solar is becoming the standard way to firm up variable renewables and ease the bottlenecks now constraining electrified grids across Europe. (A single company’s investment decision; construction timelines and final capacity can shift.)
