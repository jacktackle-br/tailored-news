Russia · Congress

Republicans turn on Trump’s Russia waiver: Tillis calls it “beyond irresponsible” and Cornyn says “Lindsey would be appalled,” but Congress is on recess until after Election Day

+

Trump signed an executive order Friday night waiving the Russia sanctions and tariffs in the law named for the late Sen. Lindsey Graham. Both parties reacted sharply. Sen. Thom Tillis (R-N.C.) called the order “beyond irresponsible” and asked what part of enforcing a law the Senate passed 86–11 the administration did not understand. Sen. John Cornyn (R-Texas) said “Lindsey would be appalled.” Sen. Rick Scott said it was “not the first thing I would be doing.” Sen. Darline Graham, Graham’s sister, who was appointed to his seat, said Saturday that she disagreed with the decision. Democrats including Chuck Schumer and Richard Blumenthal condemned it, and Elizabeth Warren urged colleagues to act. The White House said the sanctions “would impede ongoing U.S. efforts to negotiate a durable and comprehensive peace agreement.” Trump said critics “can’t really be serious.”

Some Republicans defended him. Sen. Bill Hagerty said Trump is “focused on Americans first.” Rep. Thomas Massie, one of the House Republicans who voted against the bill, praised the waiver. AP reports that no quick congressional counter is in sight because the House and Senate are on recess until after the Nov. 3 election. Rep. Gregory Meeks introduced a bill Friday to block Trump’s separate diesel deal with Moscow. Several of the same Republican senators, among them McConnell, Collins, Tillis and Cornyn, had already urged Trump to rescind that deal.

The Left-Leaning Take

Slate calls the Putin fuel deal “a cautionary tale for Republicans.” Its argument is that the party is now paying a political price for a president who treats Moscow as a partner. The New Republic, which is outside the usual list, makes a similar case. It says the diesel arrangement “will help Putin far more than it’ll help Americans” and cites commentators who call the volumes too small to move U.S. diesel prices. (No in-window Mother Jones, Vox or Guardian opinion piece found.)

The Right-Leaning Take

National Review has been the sharpest critic on the right. One editorial calls the diesel deal “a gross abuse of power,” and another reads it as a sign of Trump’s domestic political weakness. A Corner post framed the waiver as coming “as scrutiny of his aid to Putin intensifies,” and Cornyn circulated an NR column urging Congress to investigate. The magazine is not unanimous: another Corner post argues the waiver is “proper and prudent, for now.” Reason, from the libertarian side, argues that buying Russian diesel would break the 2022 ban on Russian energy imports. From the anti-Trump right, The Bulwark’s headline calls the president “desperate, stupid, weak.”

Midterms · Polling

Trump’s approval falls to 33% in the ABC/Post/Ipsos poll, the lowest in the series, as Democrats lead the House ballot 52–44 among likely voters and No Kings spends Saturday on early voting

+

A Washington Post–ABC News–Ipsos poll released Sunday, fielded Oct. 9–13, puts Trump’s approval at 33% with 66% disapproving, and 52% say they strongly disapprove. That is below the 38% this poll recorded after the Jan. 6, 2021, attack on the Capitol. Among likely voters, Democrats lead the generic House ballot 52% to 44%, and independents favor Democratic candidates 56% to 35%. Approval among Republicans is 79%, down from 88% early in the term. About 7 in 10 Americans say gasoline is unaffordable and the country is on the wrong track. Other recent polls show the same trend: Pew has him at 30%, Fox News at 36%, and NPR’s average at 37%.

On Saturday the No Kings coalition held more than 3,000 “No Kings: Vote Early” events. Its earlier days were mass marches. This one was mostly registration checks, voting-plan help and voting-rights briefings, held at block parties, cookouts and smaller rallies. One event, at the Lincoln Memorial, kept the anti-Trump tone of past protests. The coalition includes hundreds of groups, among them the ACLU and Black Voters Matter. NPR found no official national turnout figure, and many sites were small. About 200 people came out in Baton Rouge, for example.

The Left-Leaning Take

Left-leaning outlets have argued for months that Trump’s unpopularity is now pushing Republican candidates away from him. Slate counts 39 congressional Republicans who have resigned, retired or are running for another office. Slate and Mother Jones have long presented No Kings as a movement whose energy has to turn into votes. (No in-window piece responding to this poll or to Saturday’s events found.)

The Right-Leaning Take

Center-right writers are reading the numbers as a warning, not dismissing them. In The Dispatch, John McCormack argues that 2026 could be a bigger blue wave than 2018. National Review has written that the Senate map is “breaking bad already” for Republicans because of Trump’s ratings. Some Republicans point to past polling misses in 2016, 2020 and 2024. From the anti-Trump right, The Bulwark previewed Saturday’s turnout push approvingly. (No in-window WSJ editorial on the poll found.)

France

Hundreds of thousands join students in protests across France over living costs; cars burn in Paris, Marseille’s City Hall lobby is breached and about 400 are arrested

+

On Saturday, marchers in cities across France joined the high-school and university students who have been blockading schools since mid-September. Their complaint has widened from education to rising living costs as France’s fiscal crisis deepens. AP reports turnout in the hundreds of thousands. The CGT union backed the day of action, calling for “higher wages and pensions, lower prices” and protection of public services. Far-left presidential candidate Jean-Luc Mélenchon joined in support of the students.

Some marches turned violent. In Paris a car and a newspaper kiosk were set on fire, and police used tear gas and water cannon. In Marseille, shops and a municipal police station were damaged, and some protesters briefly forced their way into the City Hall lobby. The Interior Ministry also reported violence by masked groups in Rennes, Toulouse, Montpellier and Grenoble. Interior Minister Laurent Nuñez said about 400 people were arrested nationwide. President Emmanuel Macron had appealed for calm on Friday. Reports give no single figure for total turnout.

Sources: NPR/AP, Oct 17, NPR, Oct 17, AP via OPB, Oct 17, AP via ABC News
Czech Republic

Babiš’s ANO is shut out of the Czech Senate runoffs as pro-Western opposition candidates take 22 of 27 seats; the prime minister calls it “catastrophic”

+

Results released Saturday show that pro-Western opposition parties will keep control of the Czech Senate. In the runoffs for a third of the 81-member chamber, AP counts 22 of the 27 contested seats won by candidates tied to the opposition. Prime Minister Andrej Babiš’s populist ANO entered 13 candidates in the runoffs and won none of them. His two smaller coalition partners were also shut out. Babiš called the result “catastrophic.” The center-right STAN and its allies won about 10 seats and ODS won seven, keeping ODS the largest group. The Pirates lost their last directly elected senators.

The Senate cannot overturn most legislation from the lower house, where Babiš’s coalition has a majority. But it is the main institutional check on his government, and it must approve constitutional changes. Czech outlets say opposition-aligned groups could together hold about 61 of the 81 seats, depending on how the new senators affiliate. The result also improves Miloš Vystrčil’s chances of staying on as Senate president. AP describes the outcome as a blow to Babiš about a year after he returned to power.

Sources: AP via Washington Post, Oct 17, AP via Washington Times, Oct 17, Expats.cz, Around Prague

Wildfire · Climate

The season’s first Santa Ana winds drive fires into coastal Orange County, burning homes in San Clemente and putting about 16,000 people under evacuation orders

+

Southern California’s first Santa Ana wind event of the season brought hot, dry gusts and several fires on Friday. The worst was the Norte Fire. It started in San Clemente and was blown into San Juan Capistrano, and NPR reports it destroyed or damaged at least six homes, mostly expensive coastal properties. By Friday night Orange County Fire Authority Chief TJ McGovern said about 16,000 residents had been ordered to evacuate. Cal Fire put the fire at 570 acres and 5% contained on Saturday morning, and two firefighters were hurt. Separate fires burned near Malibu and in San Diego County.

The winds eased on Saturday. Most Norte Fire evacuation orders were lifted by Saturday evening, though fire-weather conditions and a heat advisory were expected to last through Saturday night. These fires come during an El Niño that the Post and NOAA data describe as record-strength. Hot, dry autumn weather in the region is raising concern that California’s fire season is getting longer.

AI · Industry

Replit makes OpenAI the default model behind its coding agent instead of Anthropic, citing cost and performance; people familiar with the deal say the Anthropic partnership is still in place

+

Speaking onstage at TechCrunch Disrupt, Replit CEO Amjad Masad said the coding platform has switched the default model behind Replit Agent from Anthropic’s Claude to OpenAI’s GPT models. He gave three reasons: cost, partnership terms and performance, saying “I think GPT is better now” on internal and public benchmarks. Asked whether that ended the Anthropic relationship, he said: “Yeah. I mean, yeah.” Users can still choose Claude manually. Masad said Replit had until recently been one of Anthropic’s five biggest customers.

Sources familiar with the partnership told TechCrunch the Anthropic deal is still in place, and noted that Replit recently promoted Claude models. Most customers keep the default model, so the switch could still sharply cut Replit’s spending with Anthropic. Masad also said enterprise contracts are about 15% of Replit’s revenue this year, up from 2% last year. (Disclosure: this brief is compiled with an Anthropic model.)

Sources: TechCrunch, Oct 17
AI · Data

Meta drops contract language that suggested it could crawl business websites to train AI, saying the terms only ever covered its ads and business products

+

Meta has removed language from its business terms that appeared to let it use content from business-account holders’ websites to train AI models. Site owners had complained about scraping. Meta told TechCrunch that the crawling terms applied only to its advertising and business products and that its web crawlers work the same as before.

The episode shows how sensitive publishers and site owners have become about AI training data. Contract wording that once went unnoticed now draws pushback quickly. The same week, Meta released Muse Spark Open, an open-weight model built to run locally on Apple silicon.

Sources: TechCrunch, Oct 17, TechCrunch, Oct 15
EVs · Charging

Mercedes-AMG unveils an all-electric GT SUV with up to 1,153 hp and 710 kW DC fast charging, due at U.S. dealers in summer 2027

+

Mercedes-AMG has revealed its first all-electric GT SUV. Electrek reports output of up to 1,153 horsepower and peak DC fast-charging power of 710 kW, well above what most production EVs can take today. The first cars are due at U.S. dealers in summer 2027, and pricing has not been announced.

The charging figure is the headline. Most EVs on sale today peak well below 400 kW, so a car rated for 710 kW is a bet that the next generation of high-power chargers will be built. That timing is uncertain: Canary Media reports the U.S. EV market is only now recovering after federal incentives were cut, with high gasoline prices sending more drivers back to electric.

Sources: Electrek, Oct 17, Canary Media (EVs)

MLB · ALCS

Rays beat the Guardians 7–3 in Cleveland to tie the ALCS 2–2, scoring after a ball rolled under DeLauter’s glove, and Yandy Díaz’s ninth-inning homer seals it

+

Game 4 had been pushed back by rain, and it was played Saturday night at Progressive Field. Tampa Bay won 7–3 to tie the series at two games each, so the ALCS is now a best-of-three. The score was 1–1 until the sixth, when Junior Caminero hit a go-ahead double. In the seventh, Jonathan Aranda’s single rolled under right fielder Chase DeLauter’s glove, letting two runs score, and Aranda later scored on a sacrifice fly. Nick Martinez pitched 5⅓ innings for his first postseason win. Rookie Daniel Espino took the loss after getting only one out.

Cleveland closed to 5–3 in the bottom of the seventh on Travis Bazzana’s home run and an RBI single from Brayan Rocchio. Yandy Díaz answered with a two-run homer in the ninth. CBS said Tampa’s offense “finally broke out,” while SI and Yahoo put more weight on Cleveland’s rookie mistakes in the field. The NL pennant was already settled: Milwaukee swept the Dodgers on Thursday and will play in its first World Series since 1982.
