National Security · Presidential Protection

Trump confirms he was secretly moved off Air Force One in Turkey — hidden in a catering truck — as reporting reveals the CIA had ‘low confidence’ in the Iranian threat that triggered it

+

The Washington Post reported this week that on July 8, leaving the NATO summit in Ankara, President Trump was covertly moved off Air Force One — concealed inside an airport catering truck — and flown to a base in the United Kingdom aboard a smaller, unmarked Air Force C-32A jet. The blue-and-white presidential 747 took off separately carrying senior officials, White House staff and the traveling press pool, effectively serving as a decoy; those aboard were reportedly told to keep window shades down and were not informed of any threat. The maneuver followed a resumption of U.S. strikes on Iran and was prompted by an Iranian assassination/missile threat that Israel relayed to the CIA. On Aug. 12, the Post reported that CIA analysts had assessed the intelligence as “low confidence” — Israeli-derived rather than U.S.-generated, and not compelling — even as the Secret Service and military proceeded with the extraordinary precautions.

Trump publicly confirmed the switch for the first time on Tuesday night, Aug. 12, telling reporters the “Secret Service and the military” made the call: “They wanted me to go on a different flight, a different plane.” He said “I guess there was a threat out there,” that he “didn’t really ask too much about it,” and later argued his own aircraft faced the “greater risk.” The congressional Gang of Eight was reportedly not briefed on the episode, and press-freedom advocates criticized keeping the traveling press aboard the decoy without disclosure. (Facts from The Washington Post, NPR, NBC News, CBS News and TIME.)

The Left-Leaning Take

The left centers deception and press safety — that the White House knowingly used a plane full of journalists and staff as an unwitting decoy and then let a false account of the president’s whereabouts stand, part of a pattern of secrecy and contempt for the press. Trump’s dismissive “greater risk” framing, on this read, treats both the deception and the underlying threat cavalierly. (Directional; no single in-window Vox / Mother Jones / Slate column was isolated.)

The Right-Leaning Take

The Wall Street Journal editorial board argued the focus on secrecy misses the point — “the real story is the seriousness of Iran’s efforts to kill the President.” Mainstream-right framing treats the plane swap as a legitimate, even prudent, protective operation, casts media outrage over being “duped” as self-regarding, and notes the decoy 747 was escorted by fighter jets. (Right take anchored to the WSJ editorial; broader conservative reaction is directional.)

Sources: Washington Post, NPR, NBC News, CBS News, TIME
Democracy & Elections · Executive Power

Trump declines to rule out declaring a national-security emergency to assert federal control over the 2026 midterms — ‘stranger things have happened’ — as legal scholars say he has no such power

+

In an interview aired this week on Real America’s Voice, host Wayne Allyn Root urged Trump to declare a national-security emergency to force nationwide photo ID, proof-of-citizenship registration and limits on mail-in ballots — framed as a workaround after the Senate stalled the administration-backed SAVE Act. Trump replied: “Let me just say that stranger things have happened, OK? I’ll leave it at that.” CNN and The Hill reported the exchange (Aug. 11), and reaction continued to build Aug. 12–13. Reporting also references a leaked draft executive order, developed by outside election activists, that would declare a national emergency premised on alleged foreign election interference and seek to bar most mail voting and require in-person re-registration.

Election-law scholars uniformly dispute the legal premise. UCLA’s Rick Hasen: “The United States Constitution does not give the president power over elections” — authority over federal election administration rests with the states and Congress under Article I, Section 4. Lawfare published a legal analysis, “In Case of Emergency: The Dubious Legality of Trump Allies’ Draft EO,” concluding the draft order lacks lawful basis. (Facts from CNN, The Hill and Lawfare; the Lawfare piece was partly unreachable in-window — cited by headline and thesis only.)

The Left-Leaning Take

The left frames this as an authoritarian trial balloon — a president floating the nullification of an election he fears losing. Even if legally doomed, the mere floating, on this view, normalizes the idea and lays groundwork for post-election disputes and voter intimidation; commentators invoke the “structural conditions for a coup attempt” created by a foreign war plus a consequential election. (Directional; drawn from left-of-center coverage such as The New Republic, not an isolated whitelisted column.)

Mainstream-right reaction has largely been dismissive — that Trump gave a non-answer to a friendly host’s hypothetical and that Democrats and the press are manufacturing a panic over something courts would instantly strike down. The substantive emphasis stays on the underlying goal: voter ID and citizenship verification via the SAVE Act, not any emergency mechanism. Intra-right (The Bulwark, anti-Trump right — labeled): treats the musing as consistent with a deliberate, sustained effort to seize election machinery, and a genuine threat Republicans should refuse to indulge. Economy · Inflation & the Fed

July inflation cooled to a 3.4% annual rate, in line with forecasts, easing pressure on new Fed chair Kevin Warsh and lowering the odds of a September rate hike

+

The Bureau of Labor Statistics released the July Consumer Price Index on Wednesday, Aug. 12: prices rose 0.1% month-over-month (seasonally adjusted) and 3.4% year-over-year, down from 3.5% in June and matching economist expectations. Core CPI (excluding food and energy) rose 0.2% on the month, with the annual core rate easing to 2.5% from 2.6%. Shelter inflation slowed to 3.2% and food held at 3%, while gasoline (up 24.6% year-over-year) and fuel oil (up 39.1%) remained elevated. The report follows the FOMC’s July decision — under new Chair Kevin Warsh — to hold the benchmark rate at 3.50%–3.75% on a 9–3 vote.

After the release, market-implied odds of a September rate hike fell to roughly 38% (from about 48% a day earlier, per CME FedWatch), and U.S. equities rose modestly Wednesday morning. The data feeds directly into the tariff-inflation debate, with core goods prices cited by analysts as a channel for tariff pass-through earlier in the year. (Facts from CNN Business, CNBC, CBS News and the BLS release.)

The Left-Leaning Take

Expected left framing: a headline “cooling” masks persistent pain — 3.4% is still well above the Fed’s 2% target, energy costs are sharply higher year-over-year, and tariffs keep feeding elevated goods prices, so households are still absorbing higher costs even as the White House claims vindication. (Directional; no in-window whitelisted opinion column was isolated.)

The Right-Leaning Take

Expected right framing: the report is a win for the administration’s trade posture — inflation did not spiral despite tariffs and an active Middle East conflict, and cooling prices with lower rate-hike odds suggest the economy is absorbing the policy mix without the runaway inflation critics predicted. (Directional; no in-window National Review / WSJ-editorial column surfaced.)

Russia & Japan · Territorial Dispute

Putin makes his first-ever visit to the disputed Kuril Islands, drawing a formal protest from Tokyo

+

On Thursday, Aug. 13, Russian President Vladimir Putin traveled to Iturup, one of the southernmost islands in the Kuril chain, touring a fish-processing plant, a hospital and a school — his first visit to the Russian-controlled Pacific islands that Japan also claims and calls the Northern Territories. The stop came a day after Putin visited nearby Sakhalin, where the Russian navy was conducting exercises. Japanese Foreign Minister Toshimitsu Motegi issued a sharp rebuke: “The Northern Territories are an inherent part of Japan’s territory both historically and under international law, and the government of Japan strongly protests this visit.”

Russia has held the islands since the end of World War II, and the unresolved sovereignty dispute has prevented Moscow and Tokyo from ever signing a formal WWII peace treaty. The visit is symbolically pointed: the last Russian leader to set foot there was Dmitry Medvedev in 2010, and Putin’s trip signals Moscow’s hardened stance amid deteriorating ties with U.S.-allied Japan over the Ukraine war. (Facts from The Washington Post, CNN, Al Jazeera, France 24 and Euronews.)

Sources: Washington Post, CNN, Al Jazeera, France 24, Euronews

Putin threatens to seize European vessels ‘in kind,’ branding Western interceptions of Russia’s shadow fleet as ‘piracy’

+

Speaking Wednesday, Aug. 12, while overseeing naval drills in Russia’s Far East, Putin warned Moscow would retaliate against European countries that seize Russian “shadow fleet” tankers and sell off their cargo, calling the practice “piracy and banditry” and vowing that “we will be forced to respond in kind.” He said any response need not come in the waters where Russian ships are targeted but “wherever we ourselves deem it necessary and expedient,” including the zone patrolled by Russia’s Pacific Fleet. The confrontation runs alongside an escalating Black Sea shipping war in which Ukrainian drones have struck civilian merchant ships near the Russian port of Novorossiysk (including the vessels Nadezhda and Yanina in early August), and Russia has struck cargo ships and the Ukrainian ports of Odesa and Mykolaiv. DR Congo · Ebola Outbreak

Congo’s Ebola death toll passes 2,000 in the fastest-growing outbreak on record, which the WHO says is on track to become the deadliest ever

+

Congolese government data released around Aug. 11 showed the outbreak in Ituri Province has reached roughly 4,381 confirmed cases and 2,011 deaths — a toll surpassing 2,000 nearly three times faster than the 2014–16 West African outbreak, the deadliest in history, which killed 11,310. First reported on May 14, 2026, it is now the fastest-growing Ebola epidemic ever recorded and the second-largest. On Aug. 12, WHO Director-General Tedros Adhanom Ghebreyesus warned that “at its current pace, it’s on track to eclipse the West African Ebola outbreak of 2014 to 2016.”

Response efforts are complicated because the epidemic is caused by the Bundibugyo strain of ebolavirus, whereas existing licensed vaccines and treatments were certified against the Zaire strain — leaving scientists racing to develop and deploy effective countermeasures. Science · Space & Astronomy

The first total solar eclipse to cross mainland Europe in over a century sweeps from the Arctic through Greenland, Iceland and Spain — drawing a fleet of instruments to study the Sun’s corona

+

On Wednesday, Aug. 12, a total solar eclipse tracked across the Arctic, Greenland, Iceland, the North Atlantic, northern Spain and the northeastern tip of Portugal, with a magnitude of about 1.039 and a maximum totality of roughly 138 seconds (about 2 minutes 18 seconds) along a path up to ~294 km (183 miles) wide. It was the first total solar eclipse to touch mainland Spain and continental Europe since 1905, and across most of the path totality fell in late afternoon or shortly before sunset, producing unusually low-horizon views.

NASA-funded teams chased the Moon’s shadow from about 50,000 feet aboard a WB-57 high-altitude jet and via scientific balloons to study coronal heating and the atmosphere’s response to sudden darkening, and National Solar Observatory scientists observed to refine understanding of the corona. The caveat is inherent to the science: observing conditions varied by location, some sites had cloud, and the detailed results will follow in later analysis rather than immediately. (Facts from NASA Science, EarthSky, timeanddate and the National Solar Observatory.)

Sources: NASA Science, EarthSky, timeanddate, National Solar Observatory

+

On Aug. 12, Form Energy announced a $750 million Series G led by T. Rowe Price, with participation from a broad syndicate including Sequoia Capital, Janus Henderson, Franklin Templeton, TPG Rise Climate, Breakthrough Energy Ventures, GE Vernova and Coatue, bringing the company’s total raised to roughly $2 billion. Energy & Climate · Offshore Wind Policy

A federal court clears the way for the Trump administration to reconsider the already-issued permits for the Atlantic Shores offshore wind project off New Jersey

+

The U.S. District Court for the District of Columbia granted a request to remand the construction and operations permits for Atlantic Shores — a large offshore wind project off the New Jersey coast — back to the Bureau of Ocean Energy Management, allowing the agency to reconsider approvals originally issued under the Biden administration. The court’s order stated that “remand is appropriate here in the interest of judicial economy and in light of the lack of prejudice to Atlantic Shores.”

The move adds fresh uncertainty to a project already weakened after a key developer partner exited, and fits a broader 2026 pattern of the administration seeking to slow or unwind offshore wind approvals amid ongoing litigation. The caveat: this is a procedural remand permitting reconsideration, not a final revocation of the permits — the ultimate outcome depends on BOEM’s subsequent review, and the timing of the underlying ruling was slightly ahead of the Aug. 12 coverage window. (Facts from E&E News / POLITICO and RTO Insider.)
