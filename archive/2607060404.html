The Memphis Deployment

National Guard troops fatally shoot an armed 20-year-old in Memphis — the first such killing under Trump’s city crackdown

+

Tennessee National Guard soldiers fatally shot a man in downtown Memphis just before 4 a.m. on Saturday, July 5 — what appears to be the first killing by Guard troops since President Trump began deploying them to U.S. cities. The Tennessee Bureau of Investigation identified the man as 20-year-old Tyrin Johnson, whom police say was seen carrying a handgun when officers responded to a “shots fired” call. Johnson fled on foot and was pursued by Memphis police alongside Guard members; police say he “turned toward” the soldiers with his weapon before they opened fire, killing him at the scene. No officers were hurt. The TBI is investigating at the request of Shelby County District Attorney Steve Mulroy.

The Guard has patrolled Memphis since fall 2025 as part of Trump’s “Memphis Safe Task Force” — a federal effort pairing National Guard troops with the FBI, ATF, DEA, ICE, HSI and U.S. Marshals that Trump has called a “replica” of his Washington, D.C. crackdown and extended to several Democratic-led cities. GOP Gov. Bill Lee welcomed the Memphis deployment without consulting Democratic Mayor Paul Young, who said he “did not ask for the National Guard.” The deployment drew a lawsuit from state officials; in April a state appeals court ruled challengers lacked standing. Critics note that overall Memphis crime was near multi-year lows before the troops arrived.

The Left-Leaning Take

The predictable, deadly consequence of militarizing American streets (Mother Jones, Slate, Vox): an unaccountable federal force has killed a young Black man in a majority-Black city where crime was already falling. On this read, the shooting vindicates warnings that standing Guard patrols invite exactly this kind of tragedy — and stokes fears the troops could intimidate voters heading into the November midterms.

The Right-Leaning Take

Law-and-order voices (National Review, WSJ editorial) stress that Johnson was armed and allegedly turned his weapon toward the soldiers, casting the shooting as a justified response that validates the crackdown. A libertarian strand (Reason) is warier — flagging the civil-liberties and federalism problems of standing military patrols policing civilians, and the murky accountability when soldiers rather than police pull the trigger.

Sources: NPR, Washington Post, CNN, PBS NewsHour (AP)
The NATO Summit

Trump heads to Ankara branding the alliance “one-sided,” as a bipartisan Senate delegation flies in to reassure allies

+

President Trump departs Monday for the NATO summit in Ankara (July 7–8), having called it “ridiculous” for the United States to stay on a “one-sided path” when “the relationship is not reciprocal,” and complaining that allies “were not there for us” during the Iran war. Over the July 4 weekend he held a roughly 90-minute call with Vladimir Putin — which the Kremlin called “businesslike and very constructive” — and a separate call with Volodymyr Zelensky, again offering to help broker an end to the war. Zelensky said afterward there is “a real prospect to put an end to this war” and that talks would continue at the summit.

The posture collides with the alliance’s plans: allies are set to unveil a roughly €140bn Ukraine pledge and, per a draft declaration, formally name Russia a threat to Euro-Atlantic security. A bipartisan Senate delegation, including Sen. Jeanne Shaheen, is traveling separately to signal alliance support as a counterweight to Trump’s rhetoric. The stakes sharpened overnight with Russia’s deadly pre-dawn barrage on Kyiv (see Foreign Affairs). The U.K. announced fresh sanctions on Russia’s oil “shadow fleet,” and Trump has signaled he may reimpose sanctions on Russian oil.

The Left-Leaning Take

Trump’s “one-sided” framing hands Moscow exactly the alliance fracture it wants (Guardian opinion, Vox, Mother Jones): Putin bombing Kyiv’s civilians hours after a “constructive” call, on this read, shows a credulous president being played, and reducing collective defense to a spending ledger endangers Ukraine and European security for the sake of a grievance.

The Right-Leaning Take

Burden-sharing hawks (National Review, WSJ editorial) defend the leverage play — Europe has under-invested for decades and pressure gets results — while cautioning that walking away amid Russian escalation would squander a strong U.S. hand just as talks open. A libertarian aside (Reason) is more skeptical still of open-ended American security commitments abroad.

Sources: NPR, CNBC, Reuters via U.S. News

Ukraine / Russia

Russia’s deadliest Kyiv barrage in months kills at least 11 on the eve of the NATO summit

+

Russian missiles and drones struck Kyiv in the pre-dawn hours of Monday, July 6, killing at least 11 people (the consolidating Reuters/NPR figure) and wounding roughly 60, with rescue crews digging through rubble at residential high-rises. Ukraine’s air force said Russia fired some 68 missiles — including 23 ballistic and six hypersonic weapons — plus about 351 drones; air defenses downed 37 missiles and 326 drones but intercepted none of the ballistic or hypersonic missiles. At least 15 residential buildings were damaged or destroyed, among them a nine-story block in the historic Podilskyi district. It was Russia’s second mass strike on the capital in under a week, after a Thursday attack that killed 31 — Kyiv’s deadliest of the year. (Tallies differed as the count rose: CNN put the toll at 14 in the city plus three in the surrounding region; treat “at least 11” as provisional.)

The timing is the story. The barrage lands roughly a day before the NATO summit in Ankara, where allies plan to unveil a Ukraine pledge and declare Russia a threat — and just after Trump’s July 4 call with Putin. Zelensky pinned the failure to stop the ballistic salvo on an “insufficient supply of interceptor missiles,” a pointed lobbying signal to Washington and European capitals that converts an air-defense gap into the summit’s central bargaining chit. The escalation also undercuts any reading of the Putin call as a step toward de-escalation.

Sources: NPR, Reuters via U.S. News, CNN, Kyiv Independent
Pacific

Super Typhoon Bavi makes a Category-5 landfall on Rota, battering U.S. Pacific territories

+

Super Typhoon Bavi’s eye passed over the island of Rota — the southernmost of the Northern Mariana Islands, about 50 miles north of Guam — on Monday morning local time as a Category-5-equivalent storm, with sustained winds reported near 180 mph earlier in the day and gusts topping 150 mph at landfall. Local authorities reported “major damages” but a full assessment was hampered by communications outages, with flash floods, flipped and submerged cars, and forecasts of up to 20 inches of rain across the territory. Bavi had strengthened to Category 5 on Saturday, July 4.

The disaster is compounding: the Northern Marianas were still recovering from Super Typhoon Sinlaku in April, with an estimated 1,300 families living in tents when Bavi hit. The communications blackout means the human toll is likely to worsen in reporting over the next day or two; watch for federal disaster declarations and any impact on Guam’s strategic military bases, a key U.S. hub in the western Pacific.

Sources: NPR, Al Jazeera, Hawaii News Now
Southern Europe

Wildfires tear across Portugal, Spain, France and Greece after Europe’s brutal June heat

+

Fast-moving wildfires erupted across southern Europe over the July 4–5 weekend and into Monday, burning more than 190 square kilometers (roughly 42,000 acres) across four countries. In Spain’s Costa Brava, a blaze burned more than 5,400 acres in 48 hours, prompting shelter-in-place or evacuation orders for nearly 50,000 people. In central Portugal, a fire near Vouzela spread across three municipalities, scorching more than 2,400 hectares, injuring six and requiring 1,200 firefighters. Near Perpignan in southwestern France, 700 firefighters and aircraft battled a “gigantic” blaze with more than 10,000 residents evacuated. In Greece, authorities warned of toxic smoke after a recycling-plant fire near Thessaloniki’s Oraiokastro triggered evacuation alerts — including at a facility housing 157 people with disabilities.

The fires follow one of Europe’s worst June heat waves, which drove thousands of excess deaths, and mark an early, severe start to the Mediterranean fire season. With four countries battling major fronts at once, the strain falls on shared EU civil-protection resources; expect activation of the EU Civil Protection Mechanism and cross-border mutual-aid requests as the blazes multiply.

Sources: PBS NewsHour, Euronews, EU Joint Research Centre

Space

A commercial spacecraft launches to rescue NASA’s falling Swift telescope — a first of its kind

+

A commercial servicing spacecraft built by Katalyst Space Technologies launched July 3 — from the Marshall Islands aboard a Northrop Grumman Pegasus XL — on a mission to save NASA’s Neil Gehrels Swift Observatory. The 22-year-old gamma-ray and X-ray telescope has been sinking: its orbit has decayed from roughly 600 km at its 2004 launch to about 400 km, accelerated by an atmosphere puffed up during the 2024 solar maximum, and it faced uncontrolled reentry by the end of 2026.

If the servicing craft successfully docks and boosts Swift’s orbit, it will be the first commercial spacecraft ever to dock with a government satellite never designed for on-orbit servicing — potentially extending the observatory’s life by a decade or more. Beyond the astronomy, it is a marquee proof point for the emerging in-space servicing industry, which aims to refuel, repair and reposition satellites rather than let them burn up.

Sources: NASA Science, CNN, Science (AAAS)
AI & Chips

Anthropic joins the custom-silicon race, opening talks with Samsung on a 2-nanometer AI chip

+

Reports from The Information, picked up by Bloomberg and TechCrunch, say Anthropic is in early talks with Samsung Electronics to manufacture its first custom AI chip, eyeing Samsung’s 2-nanometer process and advanced packaging. The project is nascent — the company reportedly has not yet fixed what the chip will do, how powerful it will be, or how it fits into a server rack — and Anthropic is also said to be talking to Microsoft and the U.K. startup Fractile. The move mirrors a broader push by AI labs to control their own hardware destiny: OpenAI has partnered with Broadcom on a custom inference chip, and Anthropic’s hire of Clive Chan, who worked on OpenAI’s chip program, signals a shift from exploration toward active development. For context, Samsung, SK Hynix and Micron all took part in Anthropic’s roughly $65 billion funding round earlier this year. (Bloomberg’s report is paywalled.)

Energy Policy

The July 4 clock runs out on federal wind and solar tax credits, capping a construction rush

+

July 4, 2026 was the hard deadline set by last year’s One Big Beautiful Bill Act for wind and solar developers to lock in the 30%-plus federal tax credits (the 45Y and 48E provisions) that had been slated to run into the early 2030s. To qualify, projects had to show “physical work of a significant nature” begun before July 5, which secures a four-year safe-harbor window to finish and still claim the full credit. The deadline triggered a construction rush — hundreds of gigawatts of projects raced to safe-harbor status, with roughly 85% doing so before December 2025 to get ahead of tightening rules.

Layered on top are January Treasury “foreign entity of concern” rules that strip credits from projects using Chinese-linked equipment or financing. Energy Secretary Chris Wright marked the deadline with a dismissive video (“The wind doesn’t always blow, and the sun doesn’t always shine”). The upshot: developers who missed the window now face materially worse economics for new U.S. wind and solar — just as electricity demand is surging.

Sources: Canary Media, Georgetown Climate Center, Thomson Reuters Tax
Grid & Climate

A holiday heat dome pushes the U.S. grid toward record demand — with renewables carrying the growth

+

An early-July heat dome across the eastern United States drove electricity demand toward all-time highs over the holiday weekend. Grid operator PJM forecast peaks around 166,000 MW, edging past its 2006 record of 165,563 MW, with National Weather Service highs of 102–104°F in parts of Maryland and Virginia; New England saw price spikes as demand climbed. The stress test comes as AI data centers pile structural load on top of weather-driven peaks.

Notably, the Energy Information Administration expects renewables to “almost entirely” cover this year’s demand growth, projecting solar generation up about 19% and wind up about 10% for 2026. Batteries are increasingly the shock absorber: solar-plus-storage made up roughly 91% of new U.S. generating capacity added in the first quarter, and nearly half of new residential solar installs now pair with a battery — a growing fleet of distributed storage shaving peak demand exactly when the grid is tightest.

Sources: Utility Dive, Al Jazeera

World Cup Round of 16

England survive the Azteca with 10 men; Haaland’s late double dumps Brazil out

+

In the standout tie of the tournament so far, England beat Mexico 3–2 at the Estadio Azteca on Sunday, July 5 to reach the World Cup quarterfinals. Jude Bellingham scored a two-minute brace to put England 2–0 up before Julián Quiñones pulled one back for the hosts just before halftime. Thomas Tuchel’s side were reduced to 10 men in the 54th minute when Jarell Quansah was shown a straight red (after VAR) for a dangerous challenge on Jesús Gallardo. Harry Kane converted an Anthony Gordon-won penalty, Raúl Jiménez answered with a VAR-awarded penalty, and England held on through the closing half-hour. They face Norway on Saturday in Miami.

Earlier Sunday in East Rutherford, Norway stunned Brazil 2–1 at MetLife Stadium. Erling Haaland scored twice in the final 11 minutes — his sixth and seventh goals of the tournament — to send Norway to their first World Cup quarterfinal in 28 years. Bruno Guimarães had a first-half penalty saved, and Neymar’s stoppage-time penalty was only a consolation as the five-time champions crashed out. Morocco and France had already advanced from Saturday’s ties.

Sources: ESPN (England–Mexico), ESPN (Brazil–Norway), CBS Sports, Al Jazeera
