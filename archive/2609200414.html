Press Freedom · White House

The White House turns away CNN, MS NOW and Politico reporters at the gate, and the outlets vow to sue

+

On Saturday, Sept. 19, the White House acted on President Trump’s Friday Truth Social post and denied entry to journalists from CNN, MS NOW (the former MSNBC) and Politico, deactivating or confiscating their hard-pass credentials. MS NOW’s Akayla Gardner said on air that a Secret Service officer asked her to hand over a badge that had been “disabled”; Politico’s Cheyenne Haslett was turned away and had her pass taken. Trump’s post accused the three of reporting “FICTION and LIES” and suggested other outlets could follow. CNN called it “an illegal assault” on its constitutional right to report; Politico said it would “vigorously defend” its First Amendment rights.

The White House Correspondents’ Association said the action “violates the First Amendment,” and press-freedom lawyers lined up against it: Theodore Boutrous called it unconstitutional “on its face,” and Columbia’s Jameel Jaffer predicted a challenge would succeed, citing the 1977 Sherrill v. Knight standard of viewpoint-neutral access and the pending AP v. Budowich case from the 2025 exclusion of the Associated Press. As of Sunday the outlets had signaled but not yet filed suit. (The Saturday denials are consistent across outlets; the constitutional question is unresolved and headed for court.)

The Left-Leaning Take

The directional left reads this as authoritarian escalation, not a media spat: punishing specific newsrooms for critical coverage is textbook viewpoint discrimination and part of a pattern — universities, law firms, the AP — of using state power to coerce institutions. Courts have slapped this down before and likely will again, they argue, but the real harm is the chilling effect and the normalization of a president deciding who counts as legitimate press. (Directional.)

The Right-Leaning Take

Much of the right-of-center reaction was skeptical of the ban rather than defensive. Libertarian Reason (Eugene Volokh) argued that excluding outlets by viewpoint from broadly open spaces like the briefing room is unconstitutional, even if the calculus differs for a one-on-one Oval Office interview. The Bulwark (anti-Trump right, an intra-right dissent) went furthest, with Bill Kristol urging rival newsrooms to walk out in protest. No full-throated National Review or WSJ editorial defense of the ban appeared in-window. (Directional.)

Sources: NPR, Washington Post, PBS NewsHour, CNN, Reason (Volokh), The Bulwark
Congress · Government Funding

The Senate rejects both parties’ stopgaps and Congress leaves town, pushing the odds toward an Oct. 1 shutdown

+

On Friday, Sept. 19, the House passed a Republican continuing resolution 217–212 on a near party-line vote, funding the government through Nov. 21 with no health provisions. The Senate then took up both parties’ bills, each needing 60 votes. The Democrats’ CR — permanently extending the enhanced ACA premium subsidies that expire at year’s end, rolling back Medicaid cuts from the 2025 reconciliation law, restoring rescinded funds and adding congressional-security money — failed 47–45. The House-passed Republican CR then failed 44–48, with Lisa Murkowski and Rand Paul among the Republican no votes.

Neither side is anywhere near 60, and lawmakers left for a one-week recess with only a couple of scheduled working days before funding lapses at the end of Sept. 30. The knot is unchanged from the 2025 fight: Democrats will not fund the government without an ACA-subsidy extension — the expiration is projected to raise premiums sharply for the roughly 21 million-plus people on the marketplaces — while Republicans insist on a clean stopgap first. (The vote counts are consistent across outlets; whether a deal materializes before Oct. 1 is unresolved.)

The Left-Leaning Take

The directional left says Democrats are rightly holding the line on health care: letting the enhanced subsidies lapse means steep premium spikes for millions, a crisis the GOP’s own 2025 law created. With Republicans controlling all three branches and still unable to pass their own bill, the left argues, any shutdown is theirs to own. (Directional.)

The Right-Leaning Take

The mainstream right frames it as Democrats manufacturing a “Schumer shutdown” by loading unrelated health-care demands onto a routine stopgap. But note the fracture: Paul objected on spending grounds and Murkowski wanted a fuller package, so the GOP could not hold even 50 — an intra-right governance problem, not only a Democratic blockade. (Directional.)

Sources: NOTUS, NLIHC (vote recap), Congress.gov, 2026 U.S. Federal Budget (Wikipedia)

US–Iran War · Gulf Front

The war reaches Riyadh as a Houthi missile is fired at the capital — and Iran floats ceasefire terms through Qatar

+

The Gulf front widened on Saturday, Sept. 19. Yemen’s Houthis fired a ballistic missile at Riyadh — the first attack on the Saudi capital since fighting reignited — triggering the city’s first air-raid alert of the renewed war. The Saudi-led coalition said air defenses intercepted the missile and thwarted further attempts, reporting no casualties or damage. That claim sits uneasily beside independent reporting and widely shared video showing a fire at a fuel-storage depot near King Khalid International Airport, a black-smoke plume and major flight disruptions; the exact cause of the airport blaze — a missile impact or falling debris — is not independently resolved. (The Houthi claim of responsibility is that group’s own assertion.)

Separately, Iran’s security official Mohsen Rezaei told Al Jazeera that Tehran has sent Washington, through Qatari mediators, its conditions to end the roughly seven-month war: a halt to fighting on all fronts, the release of frozen Iranian funds and an end to the U.S. naval blockade. He said there are seven conditions in all but named only three, and that Iran awaits Trump’s reply while remaining ready for a “decisive war.” (Iran’s terms are one side’s account; the U.S. had not responded as of the latest reports and has recently said it is not seeking to reopen talks.)

NATO · U.S. Force Posture

Pentagon is said to weigh pulling up to 40,000 troops from Europe — and hits rare pushback from Trump’s own party

+

The Pentagon is weighing a withdrawal of at least 25,000 — and possibly up to 40,000 — of the roughly 80,000 U.S. troops in Europe, a cut of as much as a third and the largest since the post–Cold War 1990s. Germany, Italy and Spain, hosts of the biggest deployments, would be most affected. The options are part of a six-month posture review: EUCOM chief and NATO’s Supreme Allied Commander Europe Gen. Alexus Grynkewich was due to hand an initial assessment to Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth around Sept. 18, with a final recommendation expected in early November before anything reaches Trump. No decision is final.

The prospect has drawn unusual bipartisan alarm, including from Republicans: one called cutting 25,000 troops “absolutely unacceptable” — “you can’t deter the Russians on the cheap” — and the Senate Armed Services Committee is moving to bar the Pentagon from dropping below about 76,000 troops in Europe without justifying it to Congress. The party is split: Trump ally Sen. Eric Schmitt vowed to “vehemently oppose” such limits. The debate lands amid live NATO anxiety over Russian airspace provocations and Polish warnings. (The figures reflect internal deliberations, not a finalized order.)

Sources: NBC News, CBS News, The Hill, NOTUS

+

Leaders are arriving for the UN General Assembly’s high-level general debate, which opens Tuesday, Sept. 22 and runs through Sept. 28 under the theme “Restoring trust, managing transformation.” The active US–Iran war, Russia’s war on Ukraine, Gaza and Palestinian statehood, and AI risk are the headline issues — and the week brings a rare same-hall presence of adversaries. Tech · Semiconductors

China’s CXMT says its fifth-generation DRAM is in mass production — a homegrown rival to Samsung, SK Hynix and Micron

+

At the World Manufacturing Convention in Hefei on Sept. 20, ChangXin Memory Technologies (CXMT) said its fifth-generation (“G5”) DRAM platform has entered volume production, led by two 24Gb LPDDR5X mobile-memory chips it says are already shipping in mainstream Chinese flagship phones. CXMT claims the node reaches a roughly 11.95nm half-pitch using quadruple patterning — notably without EUV lithography, which is export-restricted to China — and yields at least 50% more gross die per wafer than its previous generation.

CXMT, which completed a roughly $8.6 billion Shanghai IPO in July and calls itself China’s largest DRAM maker and the world’s fourth-largest by share, says it has scaled to about 300,000 wafers a month and aims for 350,000–375,000 by year-end. If the claims hold, it narrows one of the last big gaps in China’s push for chip self-sufficiency. (The performance and yield figures are company claims made at a promotional event, not independently verified.)

Sources: Reuters (via WBOW), Seoul Economic Daily, Analytics Insight
Science · Astrophysics

Supercomputer simulations recreate JWST’s ‘Little Red Dots’ — and point to fast-growing black holes in the early universe

+

A study in Nature, led by Sunmyon Chon of the Max Planck Institute for Astrophysics with Japan’s National Astronomical Observatory, reports the most detailed simulations yet of the mysterious “Little Red Dots” that the James Webb Space Telescope has found in the early universe. Running on the ATERUI III supercomputer, the team found that intense far-ultraviolet radiation from neighboring galaxies suppresses ordinary star formation in a gas cloud, letting it collapse into a single supermassive star and then a heavy black-hole “seed” that grows far faster than is possible today.

Those objects develop dense, optically thick disks that emit broad hydrogen-alpha light closely matching what JWST actually sees — a compact, reddish glow. Coverage from the observatory, ScienceDaily and Phys.org is dated Sept. 17. (This is a single simulation study offering a candidate explanation, not a direct observational confirmation; the origin of the Little Red Dots remains an open debate.)

Sources: Nature, NAOJ, ScienceDaily, Phys.org
Energy · Grid Storage

A 206 GWh battery-cell deal — one of the largest on record — ties China’s EVE Energy to storage integrator Fluence

+

On Sept. 18, China’s EVE Energy said its battery unit signed a five-year framework agreement to supply the US-listed grid-storage integrator Fluence (founded by Siemens and AES) with 206 GWh of energy-storage cells over 2027–2031 — 16 GWh committed for 2027 and 190 GWh reserved thereafter. The volume is roughly 1.7 times EVE’s entire 2025 power-and-storage shipments, and the company cast it as a breakthrough into the storage supply chain feeding AI data centers. EVE shares rose several percent.

The deal is a marker of how fast grid-scale storage — the technology that shifts abundant midday solar into the evening peak — is scaling, and of how central Chinese cell makers remain to the buildout even as Western integrators try to diversify. (It is a framework arrangement: specifications, quantities, prices and delivery schedules are subject to later purchase orders, and EVE cautioned that policy or market shifts could affect implementation.)

Sources: pv magazine USA, Energy-Storage.news, pv magazine Global
Tech · AI Infrastructure

Crusoe raises $3.9 billion at a $30.9 billion valuation to build AI data centers and modular ‘AI factories’

+

AI-infrastructure company Crusoe announced on Sept. 17 the initial close of an oversubscribed $3.9 billion Series F at a $30.9 billion post-money valuation, co-led by Atreides Management, Mubadala Capital and Valor Equity Partners, with Founders Fund, GIC, NVIDIA, the Qatar Investment Authority and TPG among the participants. Crusoe says its contracted power capacity has grown to more than 6 gigawatts.

The money will fund existing sites — including the large Abilene, Texas campus used by OpenAI — and the expansion of its rapidly deployable modular “Spark” data centers. The raise is another data point in the extraordinary capital wave behind AI compute, where power availability, not chips alone, is increasingly the binding constraint. (A private funding round; contracted capacity figures are the company’s own.)

Arsenal

Champions humbled at the Amex: Brighton 3–0 ends the perfect start, and Arteta calls it a ‘big lesson’

+

Arsenal’s 100% start to the title defence was blown apart at the Amex on Saturday, Sept. 19, with Brighton winning 3–0. Pascal Gross and Charalampos Kostoulas struck from range in the first half and Chema Andrés headed in a third early in the second — Arsenal’s first league defeat of the season, arriving just before the international break. Arseblog’s verdict was scathing: its report, “Shoddy Gunners get what they deserve,” called it Arsenal’s “meekest 45-minute performance in recent memory” and said the scoreline flattered them because “it could have been a lot worse,” with David Raya looking uncertain on the ball as the malaise spread.

A visibly disappointed Mikel Arteta admitted his side were taught a “big lesson” in complacency: “From the first throw-in you could sense it… we put ourselves in trouble one time, two times, three times and we need to get out of that much quicker.” He framed it as a reminder of “how tough every game” in the league is — now three weeks to stew on it. (Arseblog is the intended fan-voice lead, corroborated via ESPN and reaction wires.)

Baseball · MLB Pennant Races

Division crowns fall as the Brewers and Dodgers clinch, and a Judge-less AL East comes down to the wire

+

With about two weeks left in the regular season, the postseason field is taking shape. The Milwaukee Brewers have clinched a fourth straight NL Central title and the Los Angeles Dodgers have wrapped up the NL West — their 13th division title in 14 years. The sharpest race is the AL East, where the Tampa Bay Rays hold a narrow lead over the New York Yankees with a four-game head-to-head series still to come — and the Yankees must navigate it without Aaron Judge, back on the injured list for the rest of the regular season. The AL Central and AL West remain live as well. (Standings and clinch scenarios as of the weekend’s reporting.)
