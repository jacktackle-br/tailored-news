2026 Midterms · Michigan Senate Primary

Michigan Democrats vote today in a marquee open-seat Senate primary — Sanders- and AOC-backed Abdul El-Sayed against Schumer-backed Rep. Haley Stevens

+

Michigan holds its primary Tuesday (Aug. 4) to fill the open Senate seat of retiring Democrat Gary Peters — a contest central to Democrats’ hopes of retaking the chamber. On the Democratic side the field narrowed to two after state Sen. Mallory McMorrow suspended her campaign July 5 (her name remains on the ballot): Rep. Haley Stevens, a four-term congresswoman from suburban Detroit backed by Senate Minority Leader Chuck Schumer, Peters and much of the state party, running on manufacturing, the economy and electability; and Abdul El-Sayed, a former Wayne County health director and 2018 gubernatorial candidate backed by Sen. Bernie Sanders and Rep. Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez, running on Medicare for All, campaign-finance reform and ending U.S. military aid to Israel. El-Sayed would be the first Muslim U.S. senator.

Late polling put El-Sayed ahead — an Emerson College/WLNS survey (July 26–27) had him up 54–39 — though Stevens out-raised him and benefited from more than $50 million in outside spending, much of it from pro-Israel groups including AIPAC, in one of that group’s largest-ever investments in a single race. Israel and Gaza became the defining fault line, alongside a flap over El-Sayed appearing with streamer Hasan Piker. On the Republican side former Rep. Mike Rogers is effectively unopposed and awaits the Democratic winner in the Nov. 3 general election in a genuine swing state.

The Left-Leaning Take

This is the clearest test yet of where Democratic voters want the party to go — and, the progressive read holds, Israel is defining these primaries precisely because unconditional military aid has shifted from a mainstream position to a disqualifier among the base. An El-Sayed win in a must-win purple state would be a rebuke of AIPAC money and of a cautious establishment. (Synthesized from Slate and NOTUS reporting.)

The Right-Leaning Take

An El-Sayed nomination reads as a gift to Republican Mike Rogers: a fierce Israel critic praised by the far left and comfortable alongside figures like Hasan Piker would be far easier to nationalize and beat in November. In this framing Democrats are poised to hand away a winnable swing-state seat to satisfy their activist wing. (Synthesized from conservative campaign coverage, incl. Fox News and PJ Media.)

Sources: Washington Post, NPR, Axios, Time, Slate
Justice Department · Confirmation Fight

Blanche formally rescinds the $1.8B “anti-weaponization” fund, winning over holdouts Cornyn and Tillis as the Judiciary Committee vote on his AG nomination arrives Tuesday

+

Acting Attorney General Todd Blanche — President Trump’s former personal defense lawyer — signed an order over the weekend rescinding the roughly $1.8 billion “Anti-Weaponization Fund” the Justice Department had created via a May 18 order, declaring it “rescinded and shall have no force or effect.” The fund grew out of the settlement of Trump’s lawsuit over the leak of his tax returns and could have been tapped to compensate people claiming the government was “weaponized” against them — a pool critics warned might flow to Trump allies and Jan. 6 defendants. Republican Sens. John Cornyn (Texas) and Thom Tillis (N.C.), who had forced the committee to postpone a vote around July 29 by demanding written assurances, said Sunday into Monday they would now support Blanche. The Senate Judiciary Committee vote to advance the nomination was set for 9 a.m. Tuesday (Aug. 4), and with the holdouts on board it is expected to clear the Republican-led panel on party lines.

CNN and other outlets flag a caveat as a potential factual dispute: Blanche’s order rescinds the May implementing order but not the underlying settlement provision that requires the attorney general to establish such a fund — terms that, per that reporting, can be altered only by written agreement between the Trumps and the government, meaning the obligation arguably survives and could be revived. Cornyn and Tillis also secured an acknowledgment that a related IRS/tax-audit immunity arrangement applies only to specific named plaintiffs.

The Left-Leaning Take

The concession is cosmetic. Blanche has argued Trump has a “right” and “duty” to steer investigations of political enemies, oversaw charged probes of Trump critics, and — per the “loophole” reporting — left the settlement obligation itself intact. Cornyn and Tillis, both leaving the Senate, took a paper win while handing the department to a loyalist who will not provide independence. (Synthesized from Slate and Washington Post / Philadelphia Inquirer editorial opinion.)

The Right-Leaning Take

Unusually, marquee conservative outlets oppose the nominee: National Review’s editorial board (“No to Todd Blanche for Attorney General”) and Reason urged rejection, Reason arguing he “blessed corruption” by facilitating a taxpayer-funded payout to Trump’s circle. Countervailing pro-Trump right: The Federalist (“National Review Is Wrong About Todd Blanche”) dismisses that as Never-Trump droning and says the Senate should confirm a nominee committed to ending Biden-era “weaponization.”

Sources: NPR, Washington Post, Texas Tribune, CNN, National Review (editorial)
Foreign Policy · US–Iran War

Trump calls off a planned strike and casts talks as Iran’s “last chance” — but Tehran denies any US negotiations, saying its only talks are with Oman over Hormuz

+

President Trump said Saturday night the U.S. was canceling a planned military strike on Iran — which he described as a would-be “massive attack” — citing progress toward a deal and requests from Gulf allies (Saudi Arabia, the UAE, Qatar). He claimed a proposed agreement would reopen the Strait of Hormuz immediately and end “Iran’s nuclear threat,” with talks to begin Monday. After Tehran publicly denied any direct negotiations, Trump lashed out Monday on Truth Social, calling Iran’s leadership “unbelievably duplicitous” and accusing it of lying about talks under way “whether Iran wants to admit it or not.” By Tuesday (Aug. 4) he was framing negotiations as Iran’s “last chance” to end the war at the table.

Iran’s account differs sharply — a genuine dispute to flag. Foreign Ministry spokesman Esmaeil Baghaei and Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi say Iran is not in direct talks with Washington and is negotiating only with Oman over a “temporary” arrangement for safe passage of ships through Hormuz, which Araghchi called in its “final stages.” Tehran says the U.S. “violated all of its commitments” when a June ceasefire collapsed, and military adviser Mohsen Rezaei warned U.S. “vessels and forces will face serious risks” if a naval blockade of Iranian ports continues. It is at least the eighth or ninth time Trump has announced an imminent deal or halted strikes during the months-long war, which began without congressional authorization.

The Left-Leaning Take

The on-again, off-again threats are reckless improvisation that keeps the U.S. on the brink of a major Middle East war with no strategy and no congressional authorization — and a public feud over whether talks even exist undercuts the diplomacy Trump claims to be pursuing while handing Tehran leverage. (Synthesized from liberal / anti-war opinion.)

The Right-Leaning Take

The right is harsh from the opposite side: the Wall Street Journal editorial board mocked the whiplash — “Will the 9th time be the charm?” — calling it Trump’s “chaotic way of war” that signals weakness, and National Review argues Tehran is exploiting his indecision. The libertarian read (Reason) casts the climb-down as more bluster than strategy.

Sources: CNN (live), Al Jazeera (live), CNBC, PBS NewsHour

Middle East · Israel–Lebanon Diplomacy

US-brokered Israel–Lebanon talks open in Rome to implement a July framework — even as Hezbollah’s leader calls the negotiations “nothing but shame”

+

Israeli and Lebanese delegations arrived at the U.S. Embassy in Rome on Tuesday morning (Aug. 4) for a fresh round of direct, U.S.-mediated talks scheduled to run through Aug. 6. The technical teams — the Lebanese side including a military delegation, the Israeli side including IDF officials — are working to implement the framework the two signed last month, which envisions ending the war that erupted March 2, disarming Hezbollah, deploying the Lebanese Armed Forces (LAF) in the south, and a phased Israeli withdrawal via two “pilot zones” (Israel recently pulled out of Zawtar al-Gharbiyeh as the LAF moved in). On the agenda: expanding the pilot-zone process, resolving outstanding border points, and building toward a comprehensive deal. Just before the session, the LAF publicized weapons it had intercepted near the Syrian border — a signal it is taking disarmament seriously.

The talks open under strain. In a televised address marking the Shia Arbaeen commemoration, Hezbollah Secretary-General Sheikh Naim Qassem said direct negotiations with Israel would bring Lebanon “nothing but shame, humiliation, and successive compromises,” and accused President Joseph Aoun of having “become biased and divisive.” The core standoff persists: Beirut wants Israel to complete withdrawals from remaining occupied border points first, while Israel conditions further pullback on the LAF eliminating Hezbollah’s presence at the frontier — leaving the round at risk of stalling. (Outlets variously describe this as the sixth or seventh round.)

Sources: Times of Israel (live), Naharnet, Jerusalem Post, Middle East Eye, Arab News
Europe · Russia’s War on Ukraine

The war surges into the Black Sea’s shipping lanes — Russia claims it struck seven cargo ships overnight, days after Ukrainian sea drones sank a Rosatom-owned container vessel

+

Russia’s Defense Ministry said that on the evening of Aug. 3 into Aug. 4 it hit seven dry-cargo ships said to be carrying supplies for Ukraine’s army — at the port of Mykolaiv and in transit in the Black Sea — using Geran kamikaze drones, and also struck an automotive-equipment plant and a port terminal in the Odesa region (Moscow’s claims, not independently confirmed by Kyiv). It followed Ukraine’s Aug. 1 sinking of the Yanina, a container ship owned by a shipping subsidiary of Russian state nuclear firm Rosatom (FESCO), near Novorossiysk; at least two Ukrainian sea drones struck it and all 17 crew were rescued, with Rosatom saying it carried civilian cargo and no nuclear material. Zelensky said Ukraine also struck three Russian oil refineries in the same window. The mutual targeting of merchant shipping raises international-law questions around attacks on civilian vessels and freedom of navigation.

Separately, the toll rose from Monday’s (Aug. 3) drone incident at the Black Sea resort of Gelendzhik, where a drone came down on a packed beach at Arkhipo-Osipovka during a strike on Russia’s Krasnodar region. Russian officials and CNN report at least seven killed — including three children — and roughly 40 wounded, with counts climbing over the day (earlier Russian tallies cited four, then six). Russia blames Ukraine; a Ukrainian official countered that Russian air defense downed the drone over the crowded beach.

Sources: CNN, The Moscow Times, Kyiv Independent, The Maritime Executive
Middle East · Gaza

A Board of Peace envoy presses Netanyahu to halt Gaza strikes under the disarmament deal — but Israel refuses to stop or withdraw until Hamas disarms, and the strikes go on

+

Nikolay Mladenov, the Board of Peace’s high representative for Gaza, and senior adviser Aryeh Lightstone met Prime Minister Netanyahu and his team Monday (Aug. 3) for what was called “constructive and detailed” talks on the disarmament agreement the Board reached with Hamas. Per two people familiar with the meeting, they urged Netanyahu “to stop the strikes in Gaza as Israel pledged to do” under Trump’s 20-point plan (the “historic” deal was announced July 30). An Israeli political source rebuffed the appeal, saying Israel “will not withdraw from the current line” and will keep striking perceived threats until Hamas is fully disarmed. On Aug. 4 the Board itself said there will be no Israeli withdrawal before Hamas disarms — while Hamas negotiator Ghazi Hamad says his side will not implement the deal unless Israel first halts attacks and pulls back. The result is a mutual first-move deadlock.

Israeli strikes continued throughout. Reporting on Aug. 3 put the day’s Gaza death toll at roughly 13 to 19 killed (figures vary by outlet), after a weekend toll of at least 26; an Israeli minister said there is “no deal” to halt attacks. Gaza health authorities say more than 1,230 Palestinians have been killed since the October 2025 ceasefire, including dozens since the disarmament deal was announced.

Sources: AP via US News, NBC News, Al Jazeera, Washington Post (partial — paywalled)

Artificial Intelligence

Alibaba releases Qwen3.8-Max, a 2.4-trillion-parameter model it says rivals or beats top US frontier systems — on its own, unverified benchmarks

+

Alibaba’s Qwen team launched Qwen3.8-Max on Aug. 2–3, its largest model yet: a mixture-of-experts design with roughly 2.4 trillion total parameters but only about 95 billion activated per request, a structure meant to hold down inference cost and latency. The company published benchmark tables positioning it against OpenAI’s GPT-5.6 “Sol” and Anthropic’s Claude models, reporting, for example, 93.0 on PaperBench and 82.8 on IFBench, and 86.6 on the Terminal-Bench 2.1 agentic-coding test — while trailing on some coding suites (67.7 on SWE-bench Pro).

The model is available now via QwenCloud, and Alibaba said it will open-source the weights on Hugging Face and ModelScope within about a week. An important caveat: the figures are Alibaba’s self-reported results and have not been independently verified. Coverage framed the release as the latest sign of Chinese labs closing the gap with U.S. frontier developers.

Sources: Bloomberg, SiliconANGLE, MarkTechPost, Neowin
Energy · Solar

The world crosses roughly 3 terawatts of installed solar capacity — a threshold reached far faster than the two before it

+

In an Aug. 3 analysis, Bloomberg reported that global cumulative installed solar PV passed the 3-terawatt (3,000 GW) mark in 2026, with the exact crossing date debated among analysts. The trajectory underscores the acceleration: after passing 100 GW in 2012, it took about a decade to reach the first terawatt (~2022); the second took under three years; and the third came less than two years after that. The IEA’s Photovoltaic Power Systems Programme pegged end-2025 cumulative capacity at roughly 2,974 GW, meaning the world entered 2026 right at the threshold.

The milestone lands amid shifts flagged by BloombergNEF’s New Energy Outlook 2026, which projects solar to become the single largest source of electricity globally by 2032 and cumulative solar to exceed 9 TW by 2036; BNEF has also noted that about 90% of new power-generating capacity added to grids last year came from solar, wind and batteries. (This is distinct from yesterday’s China solar-vs-coal item — it concerns total global installed PV.)

Sources: Bloomberg, Energy Connects, pv magazine, BloombergNEF
Space · Human Spaceflight

NASA sets Sept. 12 as the target launch for SpaceX’s Crew-13 mission to the ISS, with a three-agency crew

+

At two briefings at Johnson Space Center on Aug. 3, NASA and SpaceX said they are targeting no earlier than Sept. 12 for the Crew-13 launch to the International Space Station. The four-person crew will fly a Crew Dragon atop a Falcon 9 from Space Launch Complex 40 at Cape Canaveral Space Force Station for a long-duration science expedition.

The crew spans three agencies: NASA’s Jessica Watkins (spacecraft commander) and Luke Delaney (pilot), Canadian Space Agency astronaut Joshua Kutryk and Roscosmos cosmonaut Sergey Teteryatnikov, both mission specialists. Watkins is set to become the first NASA astronaut to fly on a Crew Dragon twice, while the flight will be Delaney’s first trip to space.

Sources: NASA, Houston Public Media, SpacePolicyOnline
Clean Transport · EVs

BYD posts its strongest month of 2026 as overseas EV exports hit a record — even as its year-to-date total slips at home

+

In monthly figures reported at the start of August, BYD said it delivered 419,211 new-energy vehicles in July — up 21.8% year over year and 3.9% from June, its best month of the year and a third straight month of annual growth. The standout was exports: overseas sales of passenger vehicles and pickups reached 179,841 units, up 124.3% year over year and a fresh record, as BYD leans on international demand amid a bruising domestic price war.

The picture is mixed beneath the headline. For January through July, BYD’s cumulative NEV sales were 2,227,722, down 10.5% from the same period in 2025, leaving its full-year target increasingly hard to reach without a sharp second-half acceleration. Its premium off-road brand Fangchengbao was a bright spot, selling 41,213 units in July (up 190.6% year over year), an all-time high for the marque.

Sources: CnEVPost, Semafor, Electric Cars Report

With under three weeks to kickoff, the transfer window enters its decisive stretch as Arsenal and Man City ready for the Cardiff Community Shield

+

Tennis · WTA DC Open

Alexandra Eala stuns top seed Jessica Pegula 4–6, 6–4, 6–0 to win the DC Open — the first Filipina to capture a WTA singles title

+

The 21-year-old Filipina completed a breakthrough week in Washington, beating three top seeds en route to her maiden WTA title at the Mubadala DC Open. In a rain-delayed final that finished Monday (Aug. 3), Eala dropped the opening set to American No. 1 seed Jessica Pegula before roaring back, taking the last set 6–0 to win 4–6, 6–4, 6–0.

The result lifts Eala to a new career-high ranking of world No. 20 and stands as a landmark for Philippine tennis; her run included an upset of No. 3 seed Naomi Osaka in the semifinals. Pegula was chasing a second DC title and her 12th WTA crown overall.
