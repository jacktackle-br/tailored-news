Courts · Alaska Senate

Peltola’s campaign, Senate Democrats and ACLU-backed voting groups move to join the Justice Department’s Alaska certification suit and ask a judge to throw it out

+

On Saturday, two sets of parties asked to intervene in the federal lawsuit the Justice Department filed Thursday. The suit seeks to stop Alaska from certifying its U.S. Senate result if Republican Sen. Dan Sullivan loses narrowly. The first motion came from Democrat Mary Peltola’s campaign, Alaskans for Mary, and the Democratic Senatorial Campaign Committee. The second came from the League of Women Voters of Alaska, Common Cause and two individual voters, represented by the American Civil Liberties Union. NBC News reports that the Democratic filers also asked the judge to dismiss the case. Their argument is that federal executive power does not extend to running state elections. They also say the National Voter Registration Act, which the department relies on, does not let Washington set candidate eligibility or ballot design. They call the suit an unprecedented federal attempt to take over election administration while early voting is already under way.

The Justice Department’s theory is that voters may confuse the incumbent with a second candidate on the ballot, retired teacher Daniel J. Sullivan Jr. The department asks that certification be held up only if his votes could have changed the outcome, pending an investigation. Alaska’s courts had already ordered him onto the ballot. Trump has said he ordered the suit. Election-law scholars quoted in coverage, including Notre Dame’s Derek Muller, doubt it will advance before Election Day, if at all. Sen. Lisa Murkowski, a Republican, has said the department “should never have filed this lawsuit.” The court has not yet ruled on the motions.

The Left-Leaning Take

Democrats see a lawsuit built in advance to overturn a result the president dislikes. The DSCC called it a “sham lawsuit to overturn the will of Alaska voters and steal an election,” and Peltola called it “flagrant corruption.” Their legal point is that states run elections, Alaska’s own courts have already settled the ballot question, and the federal government is trying to reopen it. (No in-window Mother Jones, Vox, Slate or Guardian opinion piece found; this draws on the DSCC, Peltola and the intervenors’ filings as reported.)

The Right-Leaning Take

Supporters, among them the Daily Signal’s coverage, accept the department’s premise that a same-name candidate exists to siphon votes from the incumbent and that confused voters deserve protection. Other conservatives doubt the legal route. On PBS NewsHour, National Review’s Ramesh Ponnuru said he saw no clear federal hook and that it is “hard to see this lawsuit going very far.” Murkowski’s rebuke, and the Sullivan campaign saying it did not ask for the suit, show the move is contested inside the GOP. Campaign 2026 · Georgia · Trade

At a Georgia rally for Senate challenger Mike Collins, Trump announces that Audi will build a $4 billion plant near Macon and credits his tariffs

+

Speaking Saturday in Perry, Georgia, ten days before Election Day, Trump said Volkswagen’s Audi would build its first U.S. assembly plant in Middle Georgia. Audi puts the investment at $4 billion and about 4,500 jobs. Trump and Georgia officials used $4.2 billion, and Trump also claimed a far larger jobs number. A government official told Reuters the plant is expected to build 200,000 SUVs a year. A U.S. official involved in the talks said the models would include the Q7, Q8 and Q9. Construction is set to begin in 2027, with production targeted for the early 2030s. Audi CEO Gernot Döllner appeared at the rally. “Without tariffs, they wouldn’t be coming in,” Trump said, calling it the largest investment in U.S. auto history. Reuters and Bloomberg report that Volkswagen’s plan includes talks on some form of tariff relief. In January, VW chief Oliver Blume said a U.S. Audi plant could not be funded under the existing tariff burden.

The rally backed Rep. Mike Collins, who is challenging Democratic Sen. Jon Ossoff, and gubernatorial nominee Rick Jackson. It was Trump’s second Georgia stop in about a week, and CBS Atlanta reports he repeated his claim that the 2020 election was stolen. Ossoff held a canvass launch in Atlanta the same afternoon with Pete Buttigieg, focused on health care and living costs. Public polls this month have Ossoff ahead, by more than eight points in the RealClearPolitics average.

The Left-Leaning Take

Critics see a factory announcement timed to rescue a trailing Senate candidate. The plant won’t produce a car until the 2030s, and voters are paying higher prices from tariffs now. They also note that Audi’s U.S. sales have fallen under those tariffs and that VW reportedly made its commitment alongside talks on a tariff carve-out. If so, the plant is evidence of the damage done, not proof the policy works. (No in-window Mother Jones, Vox, Slate or Guardian opinion piece found; this draws on critical context in news coverage and Georgia Democrats’ earlier tariff criticism.)

The Right-Leaning Take

The White House and its allies say this is the payoff tariffs were meant to produce. Ohio Sen. Bernie Moreno said the plant “would never have happened without tariffs, without common sense regulations and without eliminating California’s ability to regulate fuel economy standards.” Free-market conservatives, the Reason and WSJ-editorial wing, would answer that investment won through a negotiated exemption shows the tariffs work as leverage for deals, not as a general tax that pays for itself. (No in-window National Review, Dispatch, WSJ editorial or Reason piece found; the free-market point is a characterization of that wing’s long-standing position, not a quote.)

Iran · Gulf

A U.S. intelligence memo warns that Iranian plots against Americans have reached “advanced stages,” as the State Department orders non-emergency staff out of Saudi Arabia

+

Director of National Intelligence Jay Clayton warned Secretary of State Marco Rubio in an unclassified memo dated Oct. 23, copied to CIA Director John Ratcliffe, that Iran has “substantially increased” efforts to target U.S. personnel. The memo says lethal plotting against U.S. citizens has reached advanced stages in the Middle East and in some other countries. U.S. officials confirmed it is authentic after Axios first reported it. The State Department has ordered non-emergency government employees and their families to leave U.S. missions in Saudi Arabia. It later clarified that the order covers only Saudi Arabia, after earlier signals that families would also be withdrawn from other posts. On Saturday it updated travel advisories for more than a dozen countries across the Gulf and Middle East, citing the threat from Iran-aligned Houthis.

The moves come as the U.S.–Iran war nears its eighth month and Houthi attacks on Saudi Arabia escalate. Earlier in the week, Reuters reported that Aramco had told staff to evacuate its Dhahran headquarters. Brent crude ended Friday near $117, its highest close since spring, on expectations that the U.S. could resume strikes on Iran. U.S. officials have told reporters that new strikes are being considered for after the Nov. 3 midterms. The memo and the staff drawdown suggest Washington expects Tehran to act against U.S. targets before then.

Sources: Reuters via Japan Times, Oct 25, CNBC, Oct 24, Business Standard, Oct 24, Fox News, NPR Up First, Oct 23
Russia · Ukraine

Ukrainian drones kill three in Belgorod and hit an industrial plant in the Urals’ Perm region, days before a local ceasefire in occupied Oleshky is due to begin

+

Overnight into Sunday, Ukrainian drone strikes killed three people and injured 14 in Russia’s Belgorod region, according to its governor, who said dozens of apartment blocks, cars and houses were damaged. In the Perm region, deep in the Ural Mountains, Governor Dmitry Makhonin said a drone hit an industrial facility and injured five workers. He did not say what the plant makes. Russia’s Defense Ministry claimed it had downed hundreds of Ukrainian drones across more than two dozen regions, a figure Reuters could not verify. Ukraine’s air force said Russia launched 153 drones at Ukraine overnight and that 119 were shot down or suppressed.

Diplomacy is moving on a much smaller scale. Moscow and Kyiv have agreed to a limited humanitarian ceasefire in occupied Oleshky, on the Dnipro’s left bank, to let aid in and civilians out. The White House credits Melania Trump with brokering it. The two sides and Washington have given different start dates, and the Kremlin says timing is still being coordinated. Long-range strikes on both sides continue alongside it, and a broader round of talks is tentatively expected next month.

Serbia · Elections

Serbia votes in a snap parliamentary election that pits Aleksandar Vučić’s party against a list backed by the student protest movement

+

Polls opened Sunday for 250 seats, with 18 lists competing. The AP describes the vote as a referendum between President Vučić’s right-wing Serbian Progressive Party and the youth-backed Students’ List, grown from the protests that began after a railway station canopy collapsed in Novi Sad in 2024. The AP reports that the two are believed to be roughly neck and neck, making this the least predictable Serbian election in years. Seats are shared proportionally above a 3% threshold. The electoral commission has up to 96 hours to certify, and an official tally is not expected before Monday.

International and local monitors have raised concerns about the campaign, citing detentions, near-total government control of broadcast media, and reports of surveillance and cyberattacks against activists. Vučić has said his side would concede if it lost, but has also warned that protests alleging fraud would be put down. Opposition fraud protests followed the 2023 vote. NPR reports that the student movement is now trying to win at the ballot box what more than a year of street protests has not.

Sources: AP via KSAT, Oct 25, AP via WSLS, Oct 25, NPR, Oct 24

Tech · AI Infrastructure

Nvidia’s role as a backer of AI cloud companies faces its first real test, as CoreWeave, Nebius and IREN shares fall 30% or more this month

+

CNBC reports that the “neoclouds” Nvidia has helped finance are under strain as credit tightens. These are the cash-burning companies that rent out Nvidia GPUs. Shares of CoreWeave, Nebius and IREN are each down at least 30% in October. The spread on CoreWeave’s credit default swaps has risen past 11%, a level that signals real worry about repayment. Nvidia’s exposure goes beyond its equity stakes. As of July it had disclosed $36 billion in off-balance-sheet cloud agreements that act as guarantees for neocloud partners. It has also guaranteed up to $105 billion tied to OpenAI’s proposed 10-gigawatt Ohio data center.

Jensen Huang told CNBC that demand is not the problem. Interest in neocloud capacity is “off the charts,” he said, and “what is missing in the middle is the working capital to fuel that growth.” Skeptics such as New Constructs’ David Trainer warn that if compute demand falls short of “super lofty expectations,” the guarantees could come back to Nvidia’s balance sheet. The test comes during a week of broader AI-funding nerves, with OpenAI working to close a large round of its own.

Sources: CNBC, Oct 24, MarketAlleys summary, Oct 24
Energy · Nuclear

The UN nuclear agency’s chief says nuclear power could more than triple by 2060, driven by electrification and AI data centers

+

The head of the International Atomic Energy Agency said Friday that worldwide use of nuclear power could more than triple by 2060, according to the AP. The drivers are electrification of transport and industry, fast-rising demand from AI data centers, and tech companies seeking reliable, carbon-free power around the clock. It is one of the more bullish outlooks the agency has offered, and it comes as governments that had been phasing out reactors reconsider.

The U.S. is the clearest test case. Earlier this month the AP reported that the Energy Department is lending up to $4.4 billion to restart three closed plants: Palisades in Michigan, the Crane Clean Energy Center (Three Mile Island Unit 1) in Pennsylvania, and Duane Arnold in Iowa. Together they would restore about 2.25 gigawatts. Two have long-term contracts with Big Tech buyers. Palisades, the furthest along, began loading fuel in late August. No commercial reactor has yet resumed generating power after entering decommissioning, which is why the restarts are watched as a gauge of how fast the projected growth can actually be built.

Sources: AP via Business Standard, Oct 24, AP via Arab News, AP via Michigan Public, Oct 16 (background)

MLB · World Series

Brewers come back from 4–0 down to beat the Rays 5–4 and lead the World Series 2–0, and the game ends on a disputed check-swing call

+

Tampa Bay scored four times in the first inning at American Family Field on Saturday, then didn’t score again. Milwaukee chipped back, and Sal Frelick’s solo home run in the sixth, his first in three months, tied it at 4–4. Cooper Pratt’s RBI single in the seventh gave the Brewers their first lead, and their pitchers held the Rays scoreless over the final eight innings. Yahoo Sports pointed to Rays defensive miscues that opened the door to the comeback.

The ending will be debated. With two outs in the ninth, a full count and the tying run on third, Yandy Díaz was rung up on a check-swing appeal, and fans argued online about whether he went around. MLB.com’s recap calls it a game-ending check-swing strike. The series moves to St. Petersburg with Milwaukee two wins from the title.

English Football · Premier League

Nicolas Jackson scores twice as Aston Villa end Manchester City’s perfect league start, and ten-man Chelsea lose at home to Spurs for the first time since 1990

+

At Villa Park, Jackson scored in the 6th minute. Erling Haaland headed City level in the 52nd, and Jackson took advantage of mistakes by Marc Guéhi and Gianluigi Donnarumma to win it in the 60th. City had more of the ball and more shots, but Villa goalkeeper Zion Suzuki made several big saves. It is City’s first Premier League defeat of the season. They stay top on 21 points, and Villa climb to sixth on 11.

At Stamford Bridge, Cole Palmer was sent off for two bookings within about ten minutes. With 13 minutes left, goalkeeper Mike Penders, under pressure from Jan Paul van Hecke, palmed a Tottenham corner into his own net. Mathys Tel then cut in from the left and scored low into the corner. It is only Spurs’ second league win ever at Stamford Bridge and their first since 1990, and their second win in six days under Roberto De Zerbi. Arsenal

Konsa scores his first Arsenal goal and Gabriel heads in a second as a rotated side beats Everton 2–0 to draw level on points with City

+

Arteta made five changes from Wednesday’s 2–2 draw in Munich, and Arseblog News called it a rotated Arsenal that “ease[d] past” Everton. Gyökeres started up front. Ezri Konsa opened the scoring in the 7th minute, tapping in after Gabriel flicked on an Ødegaard corner and Jordan Pickford scrambled. It was Konsa’s first goal for the club. Gabriel, making his 200th Premier League appearance, headed in Ben White’s cross in the 68th. Arsenal had 20 shots and 1.77 expected goals to Everton’s eight and 0.48. With City losing at Villa, the champions are level on points at the top. Arteta called it “a big win” and complained about a “terrible” fixture schedule.

The bad news is Bukayo Saka. He hurt his quadriceps against Bayern, and BBC Sport and Arseblog report he could be out for six to eight weeks, possibly up to a dozen matches. Arteta said fluid around the injury made the first scan hard to read. A repeat scan in seven to ten days will show how long he is out.
