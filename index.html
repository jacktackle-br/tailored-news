GOP · After the Midterms

House Republicans turn on Stephen Miller and a fight opens over the RNC chair, as the AP reports Trump’s grip on his party is now in question

+

New since this morning: Republican criticism of Miller has spread from a few senators to the House rank and file. Several House Republicans told Fox News Digital on Saturday that Miller, who runs Trump’s immigration policy, should be fired rather than promoted. One, speaking anonymously, called him a “megalomaniac.” The same lawmaker added that Miller was not the reason Republicans lost, since few voters in their districts know who he is. Rep. Marlin Stutzman of Indiana called the appointment a “horrible decision” and said Miller’s immigration positions had cost the party Latino support. White House spokeswoman Lauren Bis defended Miller. Sen. Lindsey Graham and House Freedom Caucus Chairman Andy Harris also spoke up for him. House Democratic Leader Hakeem Jeffries, expected to become speaker in January, said Miller should be fired.

An AP analysis on Saturday reports that the party is divided on how to move forward. Florida Gov. Ron DeSantis called the results an “extinction-level event.” Sen. Ted Cruz compared them to a “Watergate-level repudiation,” and Tucker Carlson said the midterms marked “the end of the Trump era.” The AP reports early talk at the Republican National Committee about limiting Trump’s role in choosing its next chair. KC Crosbie plans to run at the January meeting, and Trump on Friday endorsed the current chair, Joe Gruters. CNN reports that Miller’s promotion signals Trump is doubling down rather than changing course.

White House · Press Office

Trump offers the press secretary job to UFC chief Dana White, and CNN reports White turned it down

Iran · Blockade

CENTCOM launches “Task Force Iron Gate” to choke Iran’s overland trade, a day after Trump confirmed “minor strikes” near Hormuz

+

On Saturday, U.S. Central Command announced a new unit, Task Force Iron Gate. Its job is to stop Iran from using land borders and the Caspian Sea to move weapons, dual-use technology and sanctioned goods, which extends the U.S. naval blockade of Iran’s ports to land routes. The Washington Post and Reuters report that the unit has about 20 civilian and military staff based in Tampa and will work with other federal agencies. CENTCOM did not say whether it will carry out military operations. The Pentagon says the blockade has kept Iran from exporting more than $25 billion in crude, and that Tehran has moved more trade to land routes, including Chinese rail freight across Central Asia. Late on Friday, Trump said U.S. forces had carried out “minor strikes” on an Iranian anti-aircraft site: “we knocked it out in about 15 minutes, maybe five.” Iranian media said two missiles hit Qeshm Island and claimed a U.S. drone was shot down. CNBC notes that Trump did not explicitly link his remarks to the Qeshm strikes.

Analysis: Washington is increasing pressure on Iran while saying a deal is close. Trump says “a lot of progress” has been made and spoke with Qatar’s emir. Qatar has rejected reports that the talks have failed, and Tehran says it wants more than “paper commitments.” Going after land routes puts the U.S. in more direct conflict with Iran’s overland trading partners, especially China, than the maritime blockade did. How Beijing responds is the main thing to watch. None of the Iranian damage or drone claims has been independently confirmed.

Sources: Washington Post, Nov 7, Reuters via ThePrint, CNBC, Nov 7, Al Jazeera, Nov 7, Euronews, Nov 7 (Washington Post is paywalled, so this was read through search excerpts only.)
Paraguay · Prosecution

Ousted Paraguayan president Peña is charged over the Ueno Bank collapse and ordered held at a military facility, as Alliana starts naming a cabinet

+

New since this morning: on Saturday, prosecutors charged Santiago Peña with two crimes linked to the collapse of the digital lender Ueno. Reuters names the charges as breach of trust and administration for personal gain. Former central bank chief Carlos Carvallo was charged in the same case. Judge Humberto Otazú then ordered Peña held at a military facility pending trial, replacing the house arrest imposed after Friday’s removal. The judge said detention was needed to keep him in the country. Peña’s lawyer says he will plead not guilty. The reports differ on sentencing: the AP says he faces up to 20 years if convicted of both charges, while Reuters says up to 10 years on each.

Analysis: President Pedro Alliana, sworn in Friday to serve until August 2028, began naming a new cabinet on Saturday. Prosecutors are also investigating him in connection with the bank’s collapse, so the new government starts with a cloud over it. He has promised to compensate the nearly three million people whose accounts were frozen. Peña was moved from house arrest to a military facility within a day of his removal. That suggests the ruling Colorado Party has decided to separate itself from him quickly rather than protect him.

Economy · Jobs

Employers add 165,000 jobs in October, far above forecasts, and unemployment falls to 4.1%, raising expectations of a December rate hike

+

The Labor Department reported on Friday that the economy added 165,000 jobs in October. Economists had expected about 60,000 to 65,000. The unemployment rate fell to 4.1% from 4.2%. August and September were revised up by a combined 84,000 jobs. Private employers added 159,000 and government 6,000. Labor-force participation rose to 61.9%. There were also weak spots. The broader U-6 underemployment rate rose to 7.9% from 7.6%, and the average spell of unemployment reached 27.1 weeks, the longest in a decade outside the pandemic.

NPR reports that inflation was 3.7% in September and that prices have outpaced wages for much of the year since the Iran war pushed up energy costs. Average hourly pay rose 0.4% in October. The Fed raised rates in September and held them last week. Moody’s Mark Zandi said the report gives the Fed “latitude” to raise again in December, though he said noise in the data overstates its strength. Treasury yields rose after the release. The report came three days after an election in which Democrats campaigned mainly on affordability.

Paraguay · Impeachment

Paraguay’s Senate removes President Peña over the Ueno Bank collapse, and he is placed under house arrest as Vice President Alliana is sworn in

+

On Friday, Paraguay’s Senate convicted President Santiago Peña of misconduct in office and removed him. All 44 senators present voted in favor. Vice President Pedro Alliana was sworn in to replace him. Peña is the second Paraguayan president removed by impeachment since democracy returned in 1989. He was later placed under house arrest on suspicion of breach of trust and influence peddling, among other charges. The attorney general has opened three criminal investigations. Peña denies wrongdoing. He had submitted his resignation on Thursday, but the Senate voted 24–20 against accepting it, and a joint session to process it collapsed for lack of a quorum. The removal also takes away the lifetime Senate seat Paraguay’s constitution gives elected former presidents.

Analysis: The charges come from the central bank’s closure of Ueno Bank, which froze accounts used by about three million people. They focus on Peña’s former stake in Ueno’s holding company and his choice of a former Ueno executive to run the regulator. Because his own Colorado Party, including former president Horacio Cartes, turned against him, Alliana takes over a party that controls Congress. That makes an abrupt policy change less likely. Paraguay is one of the few countries that still recognizes Taiwan, so Taipei and Beijing will watch whether the new government keeps that position.

Ukraine · War

Russian missiles hit Kyiv’s Northern Bridge and the power lines from two nuclear plants, days before new three-way talks

+

Reuters reports that Russian missiles and drones hit energy infrastructure in and around Kyiv overnight into Saturday, and damaged the Northern Bridge, one of the capital’s main Dnipro crossings. Mayor Vitali Klitschko said several ballistic missiles hit the bridge, which was closed in both directions. Ukraine’s energy ministry said the strikes targeted lines that carry electricity from two nuclear plants to Kyiv and central Ukraine. President Zelenskiy said the capital faces serious problems with power, water and heat. Officials reported one person killed and eight wounded, including a child. One account puts the attack at 172 drones and 36 ballistic missiles, with most of the drones but only four missiles intercepted. Russia’s defence ministry said it was targeting military-industrial, energy and transport sites, and that the bridge was used to move weapons.

Analysis: The attack follows a week in which both sides struck each other’s energy systems. On Friday, Russia said it intercepted 142 Ukrainian drones, and Ukrainian drones hit an industrial facility at Afipsky in Krasnodar. It also comes days before another round of U.S.-backed three-way talks among Ukrainian, Russian and American officials, reported for Abu Dhabi. With winter approaching, attacks on the lines that carry nuclear power to Kyiv put pressure on Ukraine at the negotiating table.

+

Reuters, citing Iranian media, reports that Iran’s navy said on Saturday that an oil tanker hit a naval mine east of the Strait of Hormuz and caught fire. An Iranian army spokesman said the fire was small and no one was hurt. The navy said the ship had turned off its identification systems and entered a restricted zone without permission, as it says other recently damaged tankers did. Space · Planetary Defense

ESA’s Hera probe sends its first close-up of the Didymos asteroids, showing boulders, craters and grooves likely left by NASA’s DART impact

+

The European Space Agency’s Hera spacecraft has sent its first close-up image of the Didymos binary asteroid system. It was taken with the Asteroid Framing Camera from about 60 km away. Didymos is the main subject because its small moon Dimorphos, which NASA’s DART spacecraft hit in 2022, is mostly in shadow. Didymos still has the ridge around its equator that DART saw, but the image also shows boulders, craters and patterns of grooves or ridges. Scientists think some of these may come from impact debris. ESA mission scientist Michael Küppers said a lot of material thrown off Dimorphos may have crossed the roughly 1 km gap and landed on Didymos.

This is the start of the main reason Hera was launched: to measure in detail what DART’s collision did, including the crater, the mass of Dimorphos and how much its orbit changed. Those measurements are needed to turn a one-time test into a dependable way to deflect asteroids. ESA says detailed images of both bodies will be released on 25 November, and the close investigation of the crash site will run for at least six months.

EVs · Tesla

Musk says Tesla will end Cybertruck production in Texas around mid-2027 to make room for the Cybercab

+

Elon Musk said on Friday that Tesla will wind down Cybertruck production at Gigafactory Texas around mid-2027, freeing capacity for the Cybercab robotaxi. He called the truck the “best vehicle Tesla has ever made” and the decision the “end of an era.” Around the 2023 launch, Musk predicted annual sales of 250,000 trucks. Electrek estimates actual sales at about a tenth of that. It is not clear whether production will move or end completely. The Austin plant is the only known Tesla factory set up to make the truck’s stainless-steel body parts.

Tesla is giving up a weak product while the wider EV market grows. BYD reported a record 305,743 battery-electric sales in October, more than U.S. EV sales for the whole third quarter. Germany’s battery-EV share reached a record 37% last month, and in the UK EVs nearly matched petrol cars at 32% of sales. Tesla registered just 282 cars in the UK in October. The Cybercab, which has not yet been tested commercially, will now take the Cybertruck’s place on the production line.

Energy · Oil

Brent ends the week down about 16%, near $98, as hopes of a U.S.–Iran deal outweigh new tanker attacks

+

Brent crude settled at $98.32 a barrel on Friday, up 3 cents on the day, after briefly trading above $100 on reports of new tanker attacks near Hormuz. On Saturday Iran reported another tanker hitting a mine, so Monday’s trading will show whether the market still expects a deal. The COP31 climate talks open the same day in Antalya, Turkey, without a U.S. delegation.
