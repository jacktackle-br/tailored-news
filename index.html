Pentagon · Impeachment

Jeffries says he is a “hard yes” on impeaching Hegseth, putting House Democrats behind Massie’s Republican-led resolution ahead of a vote expected this week

+

Asked on ABC’s “This Week” on Sunday whether he would vote to impeach Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth, House Democratic leader Hakeem Jeffries said “I’m a hard yes” and that “Pete Hegseth has got to go.” He pointed to Hegseth’s handling of the Iran war and its effects on prices and national security. Jeffries predicted “incredibly strong support” among Democrats and said the caucus would discuss the resolution when members return Tuesday. Two days earlier he had called for Hegseth’s removal but told NPR he did not want to “get out ahead of the caucus” on impeachment.

The resolution belongs to Rep. Thomas Massie, a Kentucky Republican. It has eight articles, including a charge that the Iran war violates the War Powers Resolution. Massie says he will force a vote this week unless Hegseth resigns. Axios reports that House Republican leaders expect enough GOP defections for it to pass. Retiring Rep. Don Bacon says he is undecided. Even if the House impeaches, removal would take a two-thirds vote in the Senate, a high bar even with Democrats in control from January. Hegseth calls the effort a “sham,” and Trump has said he intends to keep him.

No in-window piece on Jeffries’ announcement from Mother Jones, Vox, Slate or Guardian opinion could be verified. Democrats are making the case in terms of accountability and cost: Jeffries ties Hegseth to the Iran war’s effect on prices, the same affordability message that carried the midterms.

The Right-Leaning Take

No in-window piece from National Review, The Dispatch or the WSJ editorial page could be verified. On the right, the fight is among elected Republicans. Massie and a handful of colleagues (Davidson, Barrett and Fitzpatrick backed the September effort) call the war unauthorized. Most of the conference, along with the White House, still stands behind Hegseth. Some right-leaning outlets call Jeffries’ move the start of Democratic “overstepping.”

Justice Department · Oversight

Attorney General Blanche says “there could be” investigations of Democrats who plan to probe the administration, as the incoming Oversight chair lists targets including Trump’s family and Musk

+

New today: on CNN’s “State of the Union” on Sunday, a day after Trump told the Justice Department to look into what he called Democratic “extortion,” Attorney General Todd Blanche would not rule out investigating Democratic lawmakers over their planned oversight. Asked whether there might be such investigations, he said “there could be.” Asked whether Democrats should be jailed, he said “it depends on what the crime is and what they’ve done.” He said he welcomes oversight but “at some point, you have to draw the line.” He did not name a criminal statute or confirm that an investigation is open. He also said the department would represent administration officials Congress investigates, including Hegseth if he is impeached.

The Washington Post reports that Rep. Robert Garcia, in line to chair House Oversight, said committees will look at Trump’s family, the Department of Government Efficiency (DOGE) and Elon Musk. Garcia said Donald Trump Jr. and Jared Kushner could be asked to testify. He called Blanche’s remarks a threat meant to deter oversight. Rep. Jamie Raskin, expected to chair Judiciary, has said impeachment hearings for Blanche could be possible. None of the planned investigations can start before Democrats take control in January.

Ukraine · Diplomacy

Europe will have a seat at this week’s U.S.-brokered Ukraine talks in Abu Dhabi despite Kremlin objections, France’s foreign minister says

+

French Foreign Minister Jean-Noël Barrot said Sunday that Europe will be represented at the U.S.-Ukraine-Russia talks in Abu Dhabi on Tuesday and Wednesday. He did not say who would attend or in what format, but said France would make sure Europe’s concerns are heard as the party most directly affected. Days earlier, Moscow had rejected any European role. Jared Kushner and Steve Witkoff lead the U.S. side, and Kushner has said Washington will consult European partners on the sidelines, with a broader format possible if talks progress. Russia’s delegation is led by Vladimir Medinsky and Ukraine’s by Rustem Umerov.

Analysis: A European presence over Russia’s objection tests whether Moscow will keep to the format at all. Russia still demands that Ukraine give up large areas of territory and renounce NATO membership, and NPR reports the gaps remain wide. Trump’s suggestion that a deal could cover “all of Europe” has added to European worries about being left out of a settlement on their own continent. Semafor quotes experts who say a major breakthrough is unlikely.

Poland · Terrorism

Poland begins national mourning after a gunman kills six in Kraków’s Main Market Square, in what officials call an act of terror

+

A gunman opened fire on Saturday in Kraków’s Main Market Square, killing six people, four women and two men, and wounding about 15. Reports put the number of wounded at 15 or 16. Police shot and wounded the suspect, reported to be 24, in an exchange of fire. They later found his mother, father and sister dead at his home, apparently from gunshot wounds. Poland’s defense minister called the shooting an “act of terror,” and authorities say the suspect was “fascinated by far-right ideologies.” Two days of national mourning began on Sunday.

Analysis: Mass shootings are rare in Poland, which has some of the European Union’s strictest gun laws. Treating the attack as far-right terrorism will put pressure on the government to explain how the suspect got a weapon and whether security services missed warning signs. It also adds to concern across Europe about extremists who radicalize alone.

Pentagon · Impeachment

Watchdogs say Hegseth’s staff-made video attacking his critics probably broke federal law, as Massie readies a House impeachment vote for this week

+

The Washington Post reports that a roughly six-minute video of Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth, filmed outside the Pentagon, is part of what the paper calls a “highly unorthodox” Defense Department messaging campaign. In it, Hegseth defends the Iran war and his record, praises the president, calls Rep. Thomas Massie “disgruntled,” and calls his impeachment articles a “sham.” Hegseth’s office confirmed that department staff recorded and produced the video, but would not say what it cost or how it was reviewed. Watchdog groups including CREW and Common Cause say using government staff and money for it probably violated federal law: the bans on publicity and propaganda spending and on lobbying with appropriated funds, and the Hatch Act. A Hatch Act case would have to come from the Office of Special Counsel, whose head Trump fired early in his second term, so enforcement looks unlikely. A Pentagon spokesperson said Hegseth “won’t back down.”

Massie, a Kentucky Republican, says he will force a House vote on impeachment when Congress returns, possibly as soon as Monday, unless Hegseth resigns. His September attempt failed. CNN reports that several GOP sources now expect it could pass, though that has not been independently confirmed. Sen. Thom Tillis compared the video to the $200 million taxpayer-funded ad campaign that preceded Kristi Noem’s exit from Homeland Security. CBS has reported that Trump has asked advisers whether Hegseth should stay.

No in-window piece from Mother Jones, Vox, Slate or Guardian opinion on the video or the impeachment vote could be verified. Coverage from Democrats has focused on the ethics questions the watchdogs raised. House Democratic leader Hakeem Jeffries has so far stopped short of endorsing impeachment, which leaves Massie’s Republican-led effort as the vehicle.

The Pentagon and its allies call the effort a political attack.

House GOP · Leadership

Scalise and Jordan head into Tuesday’s vote for House Republican leader, a rematch of their 2023 speakership fight

+

The Washington Post reports that the contest to lead House Republicans in the minority is now a two-man race between Majority Leader Steve Scalise of Louisiana and Judiciary Chairman Jim Jordan of Ohio. Speaker Mike Johnson said last week he would not run. The conference votes on Nov. 10 by secret ballot. Unlike a speakership vote, which needs a majority of the whole House, the winner needs only a simple majority of Republican members. The two last met in October 2023, when Scalise beat Jordan in the conference vote for speaker but withdrew a day later because he lacked the votes on the floor.

Their pitches differ. Scalise points to more than a decade of recruiting and electing Republican candidates and calls the House GOP “the first line of defense” against a Democratic majority. Jordan, a co-founder of the Freedom Caucus, says he is “battle-tested on oversight,” a pointed claim as Democrats prepare their own investigations. Some members, such as Rep. Greg Steube, have urged the current leadership to step aside entirely.

No in-window piece from Mother Jones, Vox, Slate or Guardian opinion on the leadership race could be verified. MS NOW, a left-leaning outlet outside the usual list, argues that Republicans are holding a leadership fight to avoid a real reckoning with why they lost.

Saudi Arabia · Houthis

Debris from intercepted Houthi missiles damages an Aramco tower in Riyadh’s financial district, with no casualties reported

+

Saudi Arabia’s energy ministry said an administrative building owned by Saudi Aramco in Riyadh was damaged on Sunday by “falling shrapnel” from intercepted missiles, and that no one was hurt. An official called it an “attempted targeting.” Several news agencies identified the building as the Aramco tower in the King Abdullah Financial District, and a Reuters witness saw windows blown out across several floors. The coalition spokesman, Turki al-Maliki, said air defenses destroyed two ballistic missiles fired toward Riyadh and another aimed at the Eastern Province. Other reports also describe cruise missiles. Houthi military spokesman Yahya Saree claimed operations against Aramco facilities in Riyadh.

Analysis: The Houthis have stepped up attacks on Saudi Arabia since Yemen’s truce collapsed in July. A strike on Riyadh’s airport on Oct. 10 killed at least 13 people, and Aramco’s headquarters was badly damaged in October. A hit on the capital’s financial district is a new kind of target. It comes while Iran is keeping the Strait of Hormuz effectively closed, the United States has carried out limited strikes on Iranian air defenses, and Qatar says talks continue. Attacks on Saudi energy assets add to the pressure on oil supply.

Ukraine · Diplomacy

The Kremlin names a stronger team for this week’s Abu Dhabi talks, adding deputy foreign minister Ryabkov for the first time

+

Reuters reports that Kremlin spokesman Dmitry Peskov told Interfax on Saturday who will represent Russia at the U.S.-Ukraine-Russia talks in Abu Dhabi on Nov. 10–11. Presidential aide Vladimir Medinsky will again lead. He will be joined by deputy foreign ministers Sergei Ryabkov and Mikhail Galuzin, military intelligence chief Igor Kostyukov, and Yury Zhigarlovsky, a senior General Staff officer. Ryabkov, who handles relations with the United States, arms control and the Iran nuclear file, is joining these negotiations for the first time. Kostyukov led Russia’s team at the Abu Dhabi rounds in January and February. Peskov said Kremlin foreign policy aide Yuri Ushakov will oversee the talks. Ukraine’s delegation is led by Rustem Umerov.

Analysis: Bloomberg reads the larger team as a sign Moscow may be ready to discuss political and military questions in more detail. Kyiv has previously said Medinsky lacks real decision-making authority. This is the first three-way round since February, and earlier rounds broke down over a ceasefire and territory. Zelensky has named energy infrastructure and grain exports as immediate priorities and hopes the talks lead to a leaders’ meeting. The Russian strikes on Kyiv’s power links in the past few days show the pressure Ukraine is under going in.

Climate · COP31

COP31 opens Monday in Antalya with low expectations, as Turkey pushes a voluntary goal for electricity to supply 35% of energy by 2035

+

The UN climate summit opens Monday at the Antalya Expo Center in Turkey and runs through Nov. 20, with world leaders due on Nov. 11–12. The arrangement is unusual: Turkey hosts and holds the presidency, while Australia, through climate minister Chris Bowen, runs the negotiations. AFP reports that enthusiasm has slumped, and an analyst it quotes says this “won’t be the one that changes the world.” Climate Home News reports that Australia is trying to avoid an agenda fight at the opening session.

The Turkish presidency’s main proposal is a non-binding “35 by 35” pledge to raise electricity’s share of global energy use to 35% by 2035. Delegates are also expected to work on adaptation and on the first funding requests to the loss-and-damage fund. The gap between national pledges and the warming they imply is still the central problem, and critics note that neither Turkey nor Australia has a plan to phase out fossil fuels. Energy prices pushed up by the Middle East war are expected to weigh on the talks.

Tech · AI & Data Centers

Anthropic’s IPO filing names organized opposition to data centers as a risk, days after voters rejected such projects in 40 of 41 local ballot measures

+

CNBC reports that Anthropic, which is preparing a mid-November Nasdaq listing, lists about $895.4 billion in commitments in its amended prospectus, “substantially all of which is related to compute and infrastructure.” Its risk factors say opposition to data center construction has become more organized across jurisdictions, and that the company’s profile could draw particular scrutiny to projects associated with it. The filing comes after Tuesday’s elections, in which voters backed restricting or blocking data centers in 40 of 41 local ballot measures, many in Ohio. An NBC News exit poll found more than half of voters called AI data centers a “crucial” or “major” factor in their House vote. Anthropic declined to comment on valuation.

The backlash has already reached markets. The Philadelphia semiconductor index had its worst day since March 2020 on Thursday before a partial rebound on Friday. Reports say investor expectations for Anthropic’s valuation have fallen from about $2 trillion to $1–1.5 trillion. Whether data centers are actually raising household power bills is disputed. The Washington Post, reviewing recent studies, reports that grid replacement, disaster recovery and gas prices explain more of the increase, though data centers have added costs through PJM’s capacity market. Space · Launch Tech

SpaceX targets Friday, Nov. 13, for Starship Flight 15 and its first attempt to catch the upper stage, pending an FAA license change

+

SpaceX vice president of launch Kiko Dontchev said the company is aiming for Nov. 13 for Starship’s 15th test flight. The plan is to catch both stages with the “Mechazilla” arms at Starbase’s Pad 2. SpaceX has caught the Super Heavy booster several times since October 2024 but has never caught the Ship. The FAA still has to approve a license change for the Ship’s return, which would bring the vehicle back over land, including northern Mexico. As of late last week the FAA’s Starship page did not list a Flight 15 license. An FAA notice covers Nov. 13–20 for a possible Indian Ocean splashdown, which is the backup.

Gwynne Shotwell has said SpaceX expects the license to cover return-to-launch-site and tower catch for both stages. Flight controllers will allow the Ship to come home only if it meets FAA-approved conditions. Elon Musk puts the odds of a successful Ship catch at “more likely than not.” Catching and reusing the upper stage is central to Starship’s economics and to NASA’s plans. NASA has restructured Artemis III as a crewed Earth-orbit test of docking with both the SpaceX and Blue Origin landers, and Blue Origin has pushed New Glenn’s return to flight into early 2027 while it rebuilds its launch pad. Starship dates are “no earlier than” and often slip.
