White House · Health

Trump signs an order, with Melania Trump at his side, creating a women’s health division at NIH and a White House task force led by Susie Wiles and RFK Jr.; the order sets no funding

+

On Monday President Trump signed an executive order that creates a Women’s Health Division inside the National Institutes of Health and a White House Women’s Health Task Force. The task force will coordinate women’s health policy across federal agencies. Chief of staff Susie Wiles, who recently finished breast cancer treatment, and HHS Secretary Robert F. Kennedy Jr. will lead it. The NIH division is to be set up within 90 days. It will not replace the existing Office of Research on Women’s Health and will have a wider mandate than research alone. The order includes no specific funding goals.

Reports say first lady Melania Trump pushed for the initiative and organized meetings with physicians. She added her signature below the president’s, though hers has no legal force. She said, “Beginning today we will change the reality of women’s health.” Trump called it a “historic action.” The signing comes about two weeks before the Nov. 3 midterms, at a time when Trump’s approval is falling, including among women. NBC noted that the administration has already cut NIH funding and is reviewing the abortion pill mifepristone.

The Left-Leaning Take

Democrats and progressive groups say the order contradicts the administration’s own record. Rep. Rosa DeLauro, the top Democrat on the House health appropriations subcommittee, said she agrees with the first lady but that “her husband’s policies tell a different story.” Protect Our Care says women are paying more for care and have less access under HHS’s current leadership. Common Dreams called the order “blatant hypocrisy” meant to change headlines. (No in-window Mother Jones, Vox, Slate or Guardian opinion piece found.)

The Right-Leaning Take

The White House presents the order as a lasting structural commitment and a sign of the first lady’s growing policy role. (No in-window National Review, Dispatch, WSJ editorial, Reason or Bulwark commentary found as of this evening.)

Supreme Court · Education

Supreme Court agrees to hear a challenge to Montgomery County, Md.’s magnet-school lottery and will decide what evidence is needed to prove a race-neutral policy discriminates

+

In Monday’s order list the justices took up Association for Education Fairness v. Montgomery County Board of Education. The case concerns admissions to four selective magnet middle schools. The county screens applicants on grades and test scores and then uses a lottery to pick who gets seats. The challengers are mostly Asian American parents. They argue the policy is unconstitutional even though it never mentions race, because it was designed to reduce Asian American enrollment and raise Black and Latino enrollment. A federal district judge dismissed the claims, and the Fourth Circuit affirmed in a short February opinion.

The Court did not agree to rule on whether the policy itself is constitutional. It will answer a narrower question: whether a plaintiff challenging a facially neutral policy must show that the targeted racial group suffered an aggregate “disparate impact.” Arguments are expected next year, with a ruling by summer. The case is the Court’s first major test of race-conscious admissions goals since its 2023 decision ending affirmative action in college admissions. SCOTUSblog also reported that in a separate copyright case, four justices recused, which left the Court without a quorum and automatically affirmed the lower-court judgment.

The Left-Leaning Take

No in-window left-leaning commentary on the grant found. Civil-rights advocates have previously argued that lower courts are right to require proof of real disparate harm, and that a lower bar would put most diversity-minded but race-neutral policies at risk.

The Right-Leaning Take

No in-window right-leaning commentary on the grant found. Conservative legal groups backing the parents argue that schools should not be allowed to accomplish through neutral-looking proxies what SFFA forbids them to do directly.

Ukraine · Russia

Russian missiles and drones hit Ukraine’s grid again and cut power, heat and water to more than half a million people; Zelenskyy says partners gave no early warning this time

+

Overnight into Monday, Russia struck Ukrainian energy infrastructure with ballistic missiles and jet-powered drones. Zelenskyy said more than 500,000 people lost electricity, heat and running water. He said Russia fired 16 ballistic missiles at Kyiv and the surrounding region, and a 21-year-old woman was killed in the capital. The AP reports more than 10 energy facilities were damaged; other accounts put the number of substations hit at 28. Zelenskyy said Kyiv again received no advance warning from partner countries that had previously shared that intelligence. Trump says the U.S. has not stopped sharing intelligence with Ukraine. Russia’s defense ministry described a “massive strike” on a data center, drone and missile component sites, and a locomotive depot it said stores missiles.

Analysis: The strike follows Sunday’s 1,000-drone Ukrainian attack on Russian oil sites and Trump’s charge that Zelenskyy doesn’t want peace. Both sides have now hit the other’s energy systems despite Trump’s announced “energy ceasefire.” Whether the missing warnings reflect U.S. pressure or a gap in the system, the question now matters in Kyiv’s talks with Witkoff and Kushner. Ukraine is heading into winter needing air defense more than ever, at the moment its access to U.S. intelligence looks least certain.

Sources: AP via Anchorage Daily News, Oct 19, NBC News (AP), Channel 4 News
Iran · Russia · Israel

Lavrov makes his first trip to Tehran since the war began and offers Russian mediation, while Netanyahu releases a call in which he tells Trump “we have a job to finish” on Iran

+

Russian Foreign Minister Sergey Lavrov arrived in Tehran from Bahrain on Monday. It is his first visit since U.S.-Israeli strikes on Iran began the war in February. He was scheduled to meet President Masoud Pezeshkian and Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi. Iranian media said the agenda was regional tensions and “preventing the resumption of war.” Lavrov called the U.S.-Israeli attacks illegal. Iran’s top security official called for closer security cooperation with Moscow, and Pezeshkian said Tehran is willing to resolve tensions through dialogue. Putin told his Iranian counterpart earlier this month that Russia was ready to help mediate. Pakistan and Qatar have been mediating between Washington and Tehran. Iran says its conditions for fully reopening the Strait of Hormuz, including lifting the U.S. naval blockade, have not changed.

Separately, Netanyahu released video of a phone call with Trump, a day after Trump endorsed him ahead of Israel’s election next week. In it Netanyahu says “we have a job to finish” on Iran. He gave no details, and nothing in the call shows that a new joint operation has been agreed. Analysis: Russia is positioning itself as Iran’s diplomatic partner just as Trump is easing pressure on Moscow over Ukraine, and the two sides could link those issues. Netanyahu’s remark during his campaign suggests Israel may push for renewed strikes after the elections. Both developments make the April ceasefire, already badly weakened, more fragile.

Justice · Civil Liberties

Obama breaks his usual reserve to denounce the federal pursuit of Rep. Ilhan Omar, saying it should “outrage all Americans”; the White House says Trump has not weaponized the Justice Department

+

On Sunday former President Barack Obama posted a long statement on X criticizing the Trump administration’s investigation of Rep. Ilhan Omar (D-Minn.). He said the government “can’t selectively target” people for their political beliefs or speech, and that this is what it is doing to Omar. He said the administration is trying to deport her based on an “unsubstantiated theory” that she was already 18 when her father was naturalized. Obama says she arrived in the U.S. at 12 and became a citizen at 17. He described last week’s search of her Washington home as an effort to intimidate her. The Washington Post and NBC both noted that Obama rarely comments this directly on his successor’s actions. White House spokeswoman Lauren Bis replied: “Unlike Joe Biden, President Trump has not weaponized the Department of Justice against his political foes.”

Background: Homeland Security Investigations agents searched Omar’s D.C. home on Oct. 12 and seized her phone, computers, notebooks and passport. The warrant listed immigration fraud, false statements and conspiracy. Investigators are reportedly looking at whether she or her late father lied about her age on immigration papers before she became a citizen in 2000. The Wall Street Journal reported that officials want to strip her citizenship and deport her. More than a dozen grand-jury subpoenas have gone to relatives. No charges have been filed. Omar denies wrongdoing, and her lawyers have demanded the seized property back. The Justice Department called their letter “riddled with false allegations.” The Post reports that a 2019 review of the same allegations found too little evidence to open a full investigation. Legal experts note that denaturalization requires court proceedings and is rare. The midterm election is Nov. 3, and Omar is heavily favored to win reelection.

The Left-Leaning Take

Writers on the left see the raid as a political prosecution timed for the election. An MS NOW opinion column argues the search is meant to discredit Omar before the vote and is part of an administration plan to denaturalize citizens more aggressively than any before it. Left-leaning outlets welcomed Obama’s statement as a long-awaited intervention. They note that it is unusual for the Justice Department to take major action against a candidate this close to an election. (No in-window Mother Jones, Vox, Slate or Guardian opinion piece found.)

The Right-Leaning Take

National Review’s explainer “Who Is Ilhan Omar?” treats the probe as a legitimate inquiry into her immigration paperwork. Conservative commentators beyond the usual list, such as Townhall, call Obama’s statement hypocritical and point to Obama-era investigations of conservatives. Border czar Tom Homan has said that proven fraud would mean loss of citizenship. (No in-window Dispatch or WSJ editorial on Obama’s statement found; no Bulwark piece found.)

Russia · Sunday Shows

Bessent, Wright and Rubio give different defenses of the Putin diesel deal: “no sanctions were lifted,” a “carrot” for peace, and the diesel “was going to be sold one way or the other”

+

Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent appeared on NBC’s “Meet the Press” on Sunday and disputed the premise that Trump had eased pressure on Moscow. He said the only thing lifted was the embargo on Russian diesel. He pointed out that Britain waived its own ban on fuel refined from Russian crude in May. He said “no one has been tougher on Russian sanctions” than Trump and noted the earlier sanctions on Rosneft and Lukoil. He said the Graham Act gives the president 60 days to assess the situation. Host Kristen Welker said gas prices are up 44% since the Iran war began. Bessent said prices could fall by the new year, depending on “what happens with the Iran conflict.” He also accused European governments of continuing to “gorge on Russian energy.”

Axios noted that other officials explained the deal differently. Energy Secretary Chris Wright called it “a carrot to try to end the war, not an economic lifeline” on ABC. On CNN he argued that Russia can export more diesel only if it scales back the war. Secretary of State Marco Rubio told reporters at Joint Base Andrews that the diesel “was going to be sold one way or the other.” He said Ukraine’s request for access to SpaceX’s Starshield is “under discussion.” Zelenskyy was on the same “Meet the Press” broadcast and said a U.S. tilt toward Putin would be a “historic mistake.” Congress is out until after the Nov. 3 election.

The Left-Leaning Take

The left’s argument is that the officials’ differing explanations show there is no strategy behind the deal. Critics note the administration called the deal a tool for lowering fuel prices before the midterms, and in the same week Wright called it a peace incentive. Legal experts quoted in mainstream coverage argue that buying Russian diesel conflicts with the 2022 import ban. (No in-window Mother Jones, Vox, Slate or Guardian opinion piece on the Sunday interviews found.)

Pro-administration conservatives repeat Bessent’s argument that the Graham sanctions were paused, not repealed, and that Europe’s purchases of Russian energy undercut its complaints. From the anti-Trump right, The Bulwark argues that “the pro-Ukraine right is running out of excuses for Trump.” It disputes Trump’s claim that Ukraine’s refinery strikes are driving up diesel prices and says Putin, not Zelenskyy, has turned down ceasefire offers. Egypt · Saudi Arabia · Yemen

Egypt’s parliament takes up Sisi’s request to authorize military help for Saudi Arabia after Houthi strikes on Riyadh’s airport and Aramco

+

On Saturday President Abdel Fattah al-Sisi said Egypt would take “necessary measures” to defend Saudi Arabia if the kingdom asked. He requested an emergency session of parliament to authorize the use of the army, one of the largest in the Arab world. Reuters reports the presidency pledged “all necessary and possible forms of support,” including protection of Saudi resources, territory and the Islamic holy sites. The request follows Houthi attacks on Riyadh’s international airport and on Saudi Aramco, during renewed fighting between the Houthis and Saudi-backed forces in Yemen. Lawmakers convened on Sunday. Reports differ on the outcome: one says members backed the move, and a later Reuters account says further debate was postponed to Monday.

Analysis: Egypt joined the Saudi-led coalition in 2015 but played little part in the fighting. Analysts told Al Jazeera any Egyptian role would probably be defensive, such as air and naval protection, not ground troops. The step still shows how far the war that began with U.S.-Israeli strikes on Iran in February has spread into the Gulf and the Red Sea. The Houthis are now hitting Saudi infrastructure directly, and Cairo depends heavily on Gulf money and Suez Canal revenue, so it has strong reasons to help.

Chips · AI Memory

Samsung will reportedly use TSMC’s 3nm process to make base dies for Nvidia’s custom HBM, its first time relying on its foundry rival for any part of memory production

+

The Seoul Economic Daily reported Sunday, and Reuters relayed, that Samsung Electronics will have TSMC build the logic “base die” at the bottom of the high-bandwidth memory stacks for Nvidia’s next-generation custom HBM. Samsung has until now made both the base die and the DRAM core dies itself. According to the report, Nvidia asked all three of its HBM suppliers, Samsung, SK hynix and Micron, to use TSMC’s 3nm process for the base die. It also asked them to standardize the core dies on a sixth-generation DRAM process. Samsung reportedly agreed after an internal review and will keep in-house production for standard HBM and for other customers.

None of the companies has confirmed the arrangement, and volumes and timelines have not been disclosed. If accurate, the report shows Nvidia setting manufacturing terms for its memory suppliers, not only buying from them. It would also deepen TSMC’s central role in AI hardware, since TSMC’s 3nm capacity is reportedly booked through 2027. And it would be a setback for Samsung’s strategy of offering memory, logic and packaging together.

Sources: Seoul Economic Daily, Oct 18, Reuters (syndicated), Oct 18, Business Standard, Oct 18
Wildfire · California

All evacuation orders lifted as the Norte Fire in San Clemente reaches about 60% containment; at least six homes burned and the Santa Anas have eased

+

Orange County officials lifted all remaining evacuation orders for the 570-acre Norte Fire on Sunday, and residents of San Clemente and San Juan Capistrano returned home. Containment figures differed through the day. Local outlets reported 50% to 56% in the morning and about 60% by Sunday afternoon. The burn scar and part of Via La Mirada stayed closed. At least six homes burned in San Juan Capistrano. About 467 firefighters were still assigned Sunday morning, and the cause is under investigation.

The Santa Ana winds that drove the fire Friday have subsided, and light rain is forecast for Orange County later this week. Crews have moved to mopping up hotspots and repairing fire lines and erosion damage before the storms. Rain on fresh burn scars can bring debris-flow risk. This was the first serious wind-driven fire of Southern California’s fall season.

Sources: NBC Los Angeles, CBS Los Angeles, MyNewsLA, Oct 18, Fox LA, OC Sheriff
Weather · Atlantic

Hurricane center watches a low forming off the Southeast coast that could turn subtropical midweek and bring high winds, coastal flooding and dangerous surf to parts of the East Coast

+

The National Hurricane Center’s Sunday outlook shows no tropical cyclones in the Atlantic. It is tracking a non-tropical low expected to form off the southeastern U.S. coast and drift slowly through midweek. Forecasters give it near-zero odds of subtropical development in two days and 30% over seven. They warn that whether or not it develops, it could bring high winds, coastal flooding and rough surf to parts of the East Coast.

In the eastern Pacific, Hurricane Trudy had 80 mph sustained winds off southwestern Mexico as of Sunday evening. NOAA still forecasts a below-normal Atlantic season.

Diego Gómez scores from near halfway as Brighton beat Crystal Palace 5–0 and go third; Calvert-Lewin’s equalizer leaves Manchester United winless in five
