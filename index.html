House · Democrats · Leadership

Democrats nominate Jeffries for speaker 251–11, with seven incoming progressives openly voting no, and elect Clark, Aguilar and Lieu to the top posts

+

House Democrats on Wednesday nominated Hakeem Jeffries as their candidate for speaker, 251–11, in the secret ballot he had asked for. The total was announced but individual votes were not; reports also cite two abstentions. Nobody ran against him. Seven members-elect then said in a joint statement that they had voted no: Darializa Avila Chevalier and Claire Valdez of New York, Adam Hamawy of New Jersey, Frederick Haynes of Texas, Melat Kiros of Colorado, Donavan McKinney of Michigan and Chris Rabb of Pennsylvania. Five of them were backed by the Democratic Socialists of America. Don Leonard of Ohio and Angela Gonzales-Torres also said they voted no. Coverage ties much of the opposition to Jeffries’ record on Israel and his ties to AIPAC. Nancy Pelosi played down the 11 no votes.

The caucus also chose the rest of its leadership. Katherine Clark becomes majority leader, the first woman in that job. Pete Aguilar moves up to whip, the first Latino whip. Ted Lieu takes over as caucus chair and Joe Neguse as assistant speaker. In the one close race, Lauren Underwood beat Sara Jacobs 143–118 for vice chair. Outlets disagree on whether Lieu had an opponent. The caucus vote is only a nomination: Jeffries needs 218 votes on the House floor on January 3, 2027, to become the first Black speaker.

Senate · Judiciary · Nominations

Cornyn withholds his blue slip and stalls Trump’s nomination of DOJ legal counsel chief Elliot Gaiser to a Dallas judgeship

+

Sen. John Cornyn (R-Texas) has refused to return his “blue slip” for T. Elliot Gaiser, head of the Justice Department’s Office of Legal Counsel, whom Trump nominated to a federal district judgeship in Dallas. A blue slip is the Senate custom that lets home-state senators approve or block a nominee to a judgeship in their state. As a result, Gaiser did not appear at Wednesday’s Senate Judiciary Committee hearing. “I just refuse to be jammed by a non-Texan who has no trial experience, who hasn’t been vetted,” Cornyn said. He says the White House skipped the bipartisan screening committee Texas senators use to vet judicial candidates. He has proposed former federal prosecutor Steve Fahey instead and told CBS News that Gaiser’s nomination is “no longer viable.”

Sen. Ted Cruz pushed for Gaiser to advance. The Justice Department called the decision “disappointing,” and a White House official said it still expects all of Trump’s judicial nominees to be confirmed by year’s end. Cornyn lost his primary runoff after Trump endorsed Ken Paxton and leaves the Senate in January. Republicans have until then to confirm judges before Democrats take control of the Senate on January 3. Reports say two other Trump nominees have hit resistance from Republican home-state senators since the midterms.

The Left-Leaning Take

Search found no piece on the hold from Mother Jones, Vox, Slate or Guardian opinion in the window, so none is summarized here. Left-leaning coverage (Raw Story) pointed to concerns about Gaiser’s role in Trump’s efforts to challenge the 2020 election results. In that reading, the blue slip is a rare Republican check on a lame-duck rush to confirm judges.

The Right-Leaning Take

Search found no piece on the hold from National Review, The Dispatch, the WSJ editorial page or Reason in the window, so none is summarized here. Pro-Trump outlets such as RedState called the hold a parting shot from a spurned senator. They note that Gaiser clerked in Texas, has family there and has worked on Texas cases, and they take Cruz’s side that Texans will pay for a vacant seat.

Israel · Gaza · Ceasefire

Israeli tanks cross the Yellow Line into central Gaza in the first ground operation since the ceasefire, a day after Trump’s Board of Peace warned the truce could collapse

+

Israeli tanks moved west of the “Yellow Line” into Hamas-held central Gaza on Wednesday, near refugee camps, in Israel’s first ground push since the October 2025 ceasefire. The military said it was targeting “Hamas infrastructure” in response to ceasefire violations. It closed part of Salah al-Din Street and has not said how long the operation will last. Al Jazeera reported three people killed and families fleeing the area. Hamas called the advance “a flagrant violation of the ceasefire agreement.” Reuters-based reporting said it was unclear whether Washington had been told in advance, and search found no U.S. government statement on the operation.

The move came a day after the U.S.-led Board of Peace, which oversees Trump’s Gaza plan, sent a report to the UN Security Council faulting both sides. It said Israel has restricted aid and delayed the deployment of Palestinian police, and that Hamas has kept up armed activity and taxed goods entering Gaza. The board warned that a ceasefire “that delivers nothing visible” cannot hold indefinitely. Major fighting stopped under the truce, but the later stages of the plan have stalled: Israeli withdrawal, Hamas’s disarmament and reconstruction. About $18 billion pledged through the board cannot be spent until international troops or Palestinian police can enter Gaza. Israel says Hamas is rearming, and Hamas says Israel is trying to sink the deal. The incursion makes the board’s end-of-month briefing to the Security Council a test of whether Washington will press either side.

Iran · Strait of Hormuz · Shipping

An ADNOC-operated ship is hit in the Strait of Hormuz, leaving one crew member missing and 11 injured, as Iran’s army chief threatens retaliation “without geographical limitations”

+

The UAE’s ADNOC Logistics & Services confirmed that one of its vessels was attacked in the Strait of Hormuz. One crew member is missing and 11 were injured. The company said the rest of the crew were evacuated and a search and rescue operation is under way. The UK Maritime Trade Operations agency said an “unknown projectile” hit the tanker’s engine room about 13 nautical miles off Oman, near Khasab, as it was leaving the strait. Reports differ on timing: The National puts the attack on Tuesday evening, while live blogs logged it on Wednesday. Iran’s Revolutionary Guard navy claimed several tankers had caught fire or hit mines in a zone it has declared off-limits east of the strait. Reuters could not verify that claim.

Iran’s army chief, Maj. Gen. Amir Hatami, said Iran would respond “more forcefully” and “without geographical limitations” to further U.S. action, according to Iranian state media. This follows Iran’s drone fire at Kuwait and Bahrain, covered this morning. Attacks on shipping now come almost daily, and the U.S. says it is enforcing a naval blockade of Iran’s Gulf ports. Wednesday’s threat makes it more likely that the next round reaches beyond the bases in Bahrain and Kuwait. Neither side’s damage or casualty claims have been independently verified.

China · Russia · APEC

Xi hosts Trump and Putin at the Shenzhen APEC banquet after separate Xi–Putin talks, with the first three-way meeting set for Thursday

+

After Trump’s arrival, Xi Jinping and Vladimir Putin held their own talks and shared a meal. The Kremlin said they discussed the war in Ukraine. Xi then hosted the APEC leaders’ banquet, where Chinese television showed Putin walk over to Trump, shake his hand and speak briefly. Interfax said the two had no substantive conversation. Reports disagree on the seating: one says Trump and Putin flanked Xi and Peng Liyuan at the main table, while another says Putin was placed farther away. In their APEC speeches, Xi criticized trade protectionism and Putin attacked Western sanctions.

On Thursday, Shenzhen time, Trump is due to hold a bilateral meeting with Xi focused on trade and then the first three-way meeting with Xi and Putin. The Kremlin says no more than an hour has been set aside for it. A senior U.S. official told Reuters that Washington does “not expect to get to any kind of agreement” and called the meeting the start of a conversation. Officials say nuclear arms control is the main topic. New START expired in February, leaving no limits on U.S. or Russian arsenals, and Beijing has previously called three-way disarmament talks “neither reasonable nor realistic.” Analysts see a joint statement, or an agreement to keep talking about arms control, as the most likely result. Close allies worry about a different outcome, in which the three powers carve out spheres of influence.

House · Democrats · Leadership

House Democrats vote today on their leaders for the new Congress, with Jeffries unopposed for speaker nominee but facing a tallied secret ballot he called himself

+

House Democrats hold closed-door leadership elections for the 120th Congress on Wednesday and Thursday, their first since winning the majority in this month’s midterms. Hakeem Jeffries, 56, has no declared challenger for the party’s speaker nomination and needs a simple majority of the caucus. On Tuesday he confirmed he will ask for a recorded secret ballot rather than nomination by acclamation, so the overall tally will be disclosed but not how individual members voted. Reports say some incoming progressives had threatened to force a vote themselves to extract concessions; Punchbowl described Jeffries’ move as defusing that threat.

Several incoming Democrats have not committed to Jeffries. Rep.-elect Don Leonard of Ohio has said he has “red lines,” including on military aid to Israel and campaign finance. The New Democrat Coalition endorsed Jeffries on Tuesday. Katherine Clark is expected to become majority leader, current caucus chair Pete Aguilar is running for whip, Ted Lieu is in line for caucus chair and Joe Neguse is running unopposed for assistant speaker. The caucus vote is only a nomination: Jeffries needs 218 votes on the House floor when the new Congress convenes on January 3, 2027, which would make him the first Black speaker.

Courts · Immigration · Higher education

Federal judge throws out DHS’s four-year cap on foreign students’ stays, ruling the department exceeded its authority

+

U.S. District Judge F. Dennis Saylor IV in Boston on Monday vacated a Department of Homeland Security rule that would have capped the stays of international students, exchange visitors and foreign journalists at a fixed term of up to four years, replacing the long-standing “duration of status” system tied to enrollment. Saylor, a George W. Bush appointee, held that the rule was arbitrary and capricious under the Administrative Procedure Act and that the statute does not give DHS “such broad and open-ended authority” over the F-1 program. He also faulted the department for not weighing less burdensome alternatives or showing a rational connection to its stated fraud and security goals.

DHS published the rule in July; Saylor had paused it in September just before it took effect. The administration’s appeal of that earlier order to the First Circuit had been put on hold pending a final ruling, so an appeal of this decision is expected. DHS General Counsel James Percival publicly criticized the ruling. Separately, Judge Julia Kobick in Boston enjoined two August DHS directives limiting internship and training options for foreign students.

China · APEC · Diplomacy

Xi breaks precedent to greet Trump on the tarmac in Shenzhen, a day before the first three-way meeting with Putin

+

President Trump arrived in Shenzhen on Wednesday for the APEC summit and was met at the airport by Xi Jinping, with a red carpet, marching troops and dancers. Xi has not previously gone to the airport to receive a visiting U.S. president. China’s top envoy called the coming Xi–Trump meeting “a historic moment” that would help avoid missteps. Trump, Xi and Vladimir Putin are due to meet on Thursday on the summit’s sidelines; officials have said nuclear arms control will be the focus while playing down hopes of an agreement, and Trump has said Russia’s war in Ukraine is also on his agenda. Secretary of State Marco Rubio said Trump does not see a possible meeting with Kim Jong Un as a concession.

The bilateral track is mostly about trade. The truce agreed at last year’s APEC summit expires on January 10, and Washington proposed expanded semiconductor export controls days before the trip, Fortune reports. CNN and Asia Times both expect a narrow outcome (tariff management and crisis communication rather than a breakthrough), with talks likely to run up to the January deadline. It is Trump’s second visit to China this year, and comes about two weeks after Republicans lost both chambers in the midterms.

Iran · Gulf · U.S. military

Iran widens its Gulf attacks, firing drones at a U.S. air base in Kuwait and again at Bahrain after the U.S. strike on Qeshm Island

+

Early Wednesday Iran fired on Kuwait’s Ali Al Salem Air Base, which hosts U.S. forces, and launched several attacks on Bahrain, home of the U.S. Navy’s 5th Fleet, the AP reports. Iran’s army said its drones targeted Patriot air-defense systems and drone and missile launch platforms at the Kuwaiti base. Kuwait’s army and Bahrain’s military both said they intercepted the incoming weapons, and neither country reported damage. It is the first time in this round that Iran has struck beyond Bahrain.

The attacks follow Tuesday’s U.S. strike on a Revolutionary Guard missile launch site on Qeshm Island in the Strait of Hormuz, which a U.S. official described as a response to Iran’s drone attack on a U.S. radar site in Bahrain — Iran’s third attack there in about five days, after roughly two months without such strikes. Iranian state media said the Qeshm strike caused no serious damage. Iran has also continued to attack tankers trying to transit the strait. Trump has sent mixed signals, calling new strikes unlikely while dispatching a third aircraft carrier and thousands more troops to the region. Casualty and damage claims on both sides have not been independently verified.

Tech · Chips · Earnings

Nvidia reports $115.8 billion in quarterly revenue and guides to $130 billion in its first earnings since Jensen Huang’s exit

+

Nvidia on Tuesday reported fiscal third-quarter revenue of about $115.8 billion, up roughly 103% from a year earlier and well above the roughly $109 billion analysts expected. Data-center revenue roughly doubled to about $108 billion, and adjusted earnings were $2.68 a share against forecasts near $2.47. The company guided fourth-quarter revenue to $130 billion, plus or minus 3%, versus about $125 billion expected; the quarter includes an extra week, and the outlook again assumes no data-center compute sales to China. Gross margins are guided to 71–72%, down from about 75%.

It was the first report under interim CEO Colette Kress since Huang resigned on November 12. Kress said the business was continuing as usual and raised the company’s fiscal 2028 growth outlook to about 75%, from 70%. Shares, down about a quarter from their peak going in, rose roughly 2–5% after hours depending on the report, a smaller move than the ~8% options had priced in.

Climate · COP31 · Antalya

COP31’s first draft deal offers two versions of fossil-fuel language and a 2035 electrification goal as talks enter their final week

+

Australia’s Chris Bowen, who is running the negotiations at the UN climate summit in Antalya, released an 18-page draft “cover decision” with 144 numbered items. It offers two competing phrasings on fossil fuels; the stronger calls for accelerated action including an “immediate transition away from fossil fuels in energy systems.” It includes a Turkish-backed goal for electricity to supply 35% of global energy by 2035, and floats tripling adaptation finance for developing countries to at least $120 billion. COP30 in Belém ended without a fossil-fuel roadmap, and Brazil promised to produce one by this meeting.

Bowen said the text is meant to “draw out parties” and that at least one more draft will follow ministerial consultations this week before the scheduled close on Friday. He has acknowledged consensus on fossil-fuel wording could be very difficult, and because decisions require consensus, a single country can block the package. Developing countries say current finance pledges fall short. Australia has drawn criticism for not committing to phase out its own fossil-fuel exports.

Oceans · Deep-sea mining · Courts

Judge voids the wildlife approval behind the first U.S. deep-sea mining lease sale off American Samoa, days before the auction

+

U.S. District Judge Jill Otake in Honolulu ruled that the National Marine Fisheries Service violated the Endangered Species Act by reviewing only the lease sale itself and ignoring the seabed mining the leases would enable. She vacated the agency’s letter concluding the sale was unlikely to harm listed species, rejecting the government’s argument that deep-sea mining is “nascent” and not reasonably certain to occur; the law, she wrote, “demands foresight.” The plaintiffs, Faʻasao Amerika Samoa and the Conservation Council for Hawaiʻi, were represented by Earthjustice.

The ruling does not itself cancel the lease sale scheduled for Thursday, the first such U.S. offshore minerals auction. Otake wrote that if it proceeds, bidders “will be on notice” that the court found an ESA violation. The Marine Minerals Administration said it is “working to address the issues raised.” Guam’s press notes the reasoning could also cloud a planned sale near the Northern Mariana Islands.

NBA

Cunningham and Jenkins lead a second-half surge as the Pistons end the Spurs’ 12-game winning streak, 105–93

+

Detroit beat San Antonio 105–93 in San Antonio on Tuesday night, snapping the Spurs’ 12-game winning streak. Cade Cunningham scored 27 points and Daniss Jenkins had 21 of his 25 after halftime. From midway through the second quarter to 7:43 left in the fourth, the Pistons outscored the Spurs 60–35, turning a 14-point deficit into an 11-point lead after Victor Wembanyama went to the bench. Spurs guard Dylan Harper scored 20 but left with 53 seconds remaining after his left foot slid out from under him; his status is unknown. The Spurs fall to 12–3 and Detroit improves to 9–4.
