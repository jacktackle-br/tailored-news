Congress · Impeachment

Hegseth holds a news conference this morning to answer Massie’s eight impeachment articles, as Bacon says he is “torn” and the House must act by Wednesday

+

Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth said he would hold a press conference Tuesday morning to respond to the impeachment resolution Rep. Thomas Massie (R-Ky.) filed on Monday. The eight articles center on the Iran war. They accuse Hegseth of waging war in defiance of war-powers resolutions passed by Congress, of extrajudicial killings in the Caribbean boat strikes, and of suppressing free speech by targeting Sen. Mark Kelly. Because the resolution is privileged, the House must act on it within two legislative days: it can vote it up or down, table it, or refer it to committee. That puts a vote by Wednesday. Speaker Mike Johnson opposes the effort and has called it a stunt that would be tabled. Massie told CNN on Monday there is a “high probability” Hegseth “won’t make it until December 31st.”

The vote math is tight. Republicans hold a 218–214 majority, so a handful of GOP defections alongside a unified Democratic caucus could carry the resolution. Minority Leader Hakeem Jeffries says he is a “hard yes,” but he has not claimed a whip count. Rep. Don Bacon (R-Neb.) told The Hill he is “torn.” Trump called Massie “a total loser” and said Hegseth should be praised, not impeached. The Pentagon called the effort “vindictive and baseless.” Even if the House impeaches him, removal would need two-thirds of the Senate, which CNN describes as unlikely.

House GOP · Leadership

House Republicans pick their minority leader today in a secret-ballot rematch between Scalise and Jordan

+

House Republicans meet behind closed doors Tuesday to elect the leader who will take them into the minority in January. Speaker Mike Johnson is not running and is stepping out of leadership. The contest is between Majority Leader Steve Scalise and Judiciary Chairman Jim Jordan, a rematch of their 2023 fight to succeed Kevin McCarthy, which ended with neither of them winning and Johnson becoming speaker. Only members who won on November 3 can vote, which leaves a conference of fewer than 180. The winner needs a simple majority in a secret ballot.

NPR describes Scalise as the more moderate, continuity candidate, whose stated top goal is winning back the majority. Jordan, a Freedom Caucus co-founder and former wrestling coach, has pitched a more aggressive posture, arguing that offense wins elections. Axios reports that Jordan’s allies privately claim more than 100 votes, which Scalise’s camp disputes. Majority Whip Tom Emmer wants to stay on as whip and may be challenged by Republican Study Committee Chair August Pfluger. Trump has stayed out of the race. In 2023 he endorsed Jordan.

The Left-Leaning Take

No in-window Mother Jones, Vox, Slate or Guardian opinion piece on the race could be verified. MS NOW frames the choice as “continuity or confrontation.” In that reading, a Jordan win would show a conference that took the opposite lesson from voters’ rebuke.

The Right-Leaning Take

No in-window National Review, Dispatch or WSJ editorial piece on the race could be verified. Reporting captures the split on the right. Scalise’s camp argues that Jordan is too risky a choice to lead the party back to a majority. Jordan’s backers say that after “the worst defeat in modern history, you need a new playbook,” in the words of one GOP lawmaker quoted by Axios.

Ukraine · Diplomacy

U.S.-mediated Russia–Ukraine talks open in Abu Dhabi, the first since February, with security guarantees on the agenda and territory still unresolved

+

A two-day round of talks to end the war in Ukraine began Tuesday in Abu Dhabi. It is the first such trilateral meeting since February. Envoys Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner lead the U.S. side. Russia’s delegation is headed by presidential aide Vladimir Medinsky, and Ukraine’s by Rustem Umerov. The AP reports that the session will focus on security guarantees. Umerov said his team has a mandate to discuss all issues. He said the day opened with a meeting between Ukraine, the U.S. envoys, and representatives of the UK, France, Germany, Italy, NATO and the European Commission. Medinsky said Russia’s delegation had “become bigger and more professional” and hoped the round would advance “the path towards peace.”

Expectations are modest. Negotiators do not expect to resolve the territorial dispute in Abu Dhabi. Bloomberg reports that the administration wants a framework for more frequent talks, not a final deal, and that Witkoff and Kushner may meet Putin and Zelensky afterward, depending on how the round goes. The EU is sending a joint Commission–Council team, even though Moscow has made clear it does not want Europeans at the table. Analysts cited by Al Jazeera see little sign that the Kremlin will make major concessions.

Iran War · Diplomacy

Tehran denies there are any planned talks and repeats its conditions, a day after Trump called the war “effectively over”

+

Iranian officials on Tuesday rejected Trump’s account of progress. On Monday, after meeting Qatar’s prime minister, Sheikh Mohammed bin Abdulrahman Al Thani, and with Vice President JD Vance in the room, Trump said the war was militarily “effectively over” and that there was “positive momentum” toward a deal. He also said the U.S. stays “locked and loaded.” Mohsen Rezaei, a senior Iranian security official, told Washington to stop “manufacturing fake news about imaginary talks.” Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi said the conflict will not be resolved by military means or tighter sanctions. Tehran again listed its conditions for reopening the Strait of Hormuz: an end to the war on all fronts, unfrozen assets, the lifting of oil sanctions, and an end to the U.S. counter-blockade of Iranian ports.

Trump said renewed strikes look unlikely and that Gulf partners such as Qatar “would prefer the status quo,” meaning the naval blockade continues without further escalation. Secretary of State Marco Rubio has said a breakthrough is not close. Qatar and Pakistan continue to mediate, and no agreement has been announced. Markets took Trump at his word: Brent crude fell below $90 on Tuesday (see Science, Tech, Energy & Climate).

Israel · Coalition

Eisenkot’s outreach to the ultra-Orthodox parties runs into the draft, as Shas holds back and Netanyahu works on the rabbis

+

Gadi Eisenkot, recommended by 67 of the Knesset’s 120 members, now has 28 days, with a possible 14-day extension, to form a government. He and Avigdor Liberman have agreed to open talks with the Haredi parties. To reach 61 seats with them, Eisenkot would need both United Torah Judaism’s seven seats and Shas’s six. Kan reports that the ultra-Orthodox parties will seek a one-year, possibly 18-month, transitional arrangement on military service while a broader framework is written. Shas is taking a wait-and-see approach: its leader, Aryeh Deri, recommended Netanyahu, and Jerusalem Post sources in Haredi circles doubt it will even enter talks.

Haaretz argues that the talks will turn on one issue, conscription, and that the anti-Netanyahu bloc has campaigned for years on universal service and will not give the Haredim the exemptions they want. Netanyahu, now heading a caretaker government, is reportedly pressing Haredi spiritual leaders to oppose any Eisenkot-led coalition. Without the Haredi parties, Eisenkot could in theory form a minority government that relies on confidence votes from Arab parties.

Energy · Oil Markets

Brent falls below $90 for the first time since August as Trump downplays new strikes on Iran

+

Brent crude, the international benchmark, fell below $90 a barrel on Tuesday for the first time since August, after Trump said the Iran war was “effectively over” militarily and that new strikes were unlikely “at this point.” Finwire put Brent near $89.66 Tuesday morning, its lowest level since August 31. Earlier, Euronews had quoted it at about $91.36, with U.S. crude (WTI) at about $85.47. Both benchmarks have lost roughly 20% this month.

The drop does not mean supply is back to normal. The Strait of Hormuz remains largely shut, and OilPrice.com notes that LNG and refined-product supplies are still constrained, which keeps diesel prices high even though crude flows have nearly recovered. Analysts warn that the regional talks are fragile and that the risk to energy infrastructure has not gone away.

Tech · Chips

Nvidia closes down more than 4% on the first trading day of the board’s inquiry into Jensen Huang, and Trump says the FBI is investigating

+

Nvidia shares closed Monday down more than 4%, at $178.67, well below the October high of $243.37. It was the first session since the board opened an independent investigation into CEO Jensen Huang. The inquiry is led by a committee of independent directors with outside counsel. It follows reports in the New York Times and Financial Times that U.S. intelligence agencies believe Alice Huang, an executive at the cloud company Megaspeed with whom Huang has acknowledged an affair, has been passing information to Beijing. The board says it has no reason to believe national security or company secrets were compromised.

Trump said the FBI is investigating but added, “I think he’ll be in good shape.” Analyst Dan Ives estimates about a 15% chance that Huang is removed. He expects the review to take four to six weeks. Shares were up about 0.6% in Tuesday premarket trading.

Biomed · FDA

FDA approves Bayer’s Sunharbo, the first of a new class of blood thinners, to cut the risk of repeat stroke

+

The FDA on Monday approved Bayer’s asundexian, sold as Sunharbo, for adults who have recently had a mild-to-moderate non-cardioembolic ischemic stroke or a high-risk transient ischemic attack (TIA). It is taken alongside antiplatelet therapy. Sunharbo is the first factor XIa inhibitor approved in the U.S. This new class of anticoagulants targets a clotting protein that researchers hope matters more for dangerous clots than for normal clotting that stops bleeding. In the Phase 3 OCEANIC-STROKE trial, the recurrent-stroke rate was 6.2% on the drug versus 8.4% on placebo, about a 26% reduction, with no increase in major bleeding.

The approval does not cover stroke prevention in patients with atrial fibrillation. The label advises against combining the drug with strong CYP3A4 inducers such as carbamazepine, which can reduce its effect. The dose is 50 mg once daily, and Bayer lists a 30-day supply at $748.50. China approved the drug in late October, the first approval anywhere for the class.

Climate · COP31

COP31’s second day turns to electrification and transport, as the host pushes a voluntary pledge to make electricity 35% of energy use by 2035

+

On its second day, the Antalya climate summit moved from Sunday night’s fight over adding climate finance to the agenda to its themed program. About 140 of Tuesday’s roughly 370 sessions were on energy and transport, including a Global Electrification Ministerial hosted by Türkiye’s presidency and the Electrify Now initiative. The presidency’s main deliverable is the “35-by-35” pledge to raise electricity’s share of final energy use from roughly 20–23% today (estimates differ) to 35% by 2035. It is a voluntary Action Agenda initiative and not part of the negotiated text.

India has said it will not sign. Climate advocates say the pledge does not require that the extra electricity come from low-carbon sources. Climate finance is still expected to be the hardest issue of the two weeks, with leaders arriving for the high-level segment from Wednesday. The talks run through November 20, with no U.S. delegation. No outcome from Tuesday’s ministerial had been reported at press time.
