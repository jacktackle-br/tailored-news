Election 2026 · Senate Count

Alaska and Mississippi are still too close to call, with Democrats narrowly ahead in both and a fight over Alaska’s certification looming

+

On Wednesday, two days after the election, the AP had still not called the last two Senate races. Democrats already control the chamber, so these two seats decide how big the majority is, not who holds it. In Mississippi, Democrat Scott Colom led Sen. Cindy Hyde-Smith by 1,138 votes out of about 929,000 counted on Wednesday night, 49% to 48.9%, with 96% of the expected vote in (ABC News). Around midnight on election night the gap had been only a few dozen votes. Hyde-Smith declared victory on Tuesday night, and Colom says he expects to win once all ballots are counted. NPR reports that thousands of affidavit (provisional) ballots still need to be verified. Colom’s campaign puts the number at about 15,000, but the Secretary of State’s office has not given one. Absentee ballots that were postmarked in time can still arrive during a five-day grace period. A first look at how many affidavit ballots will count may not come until next week.

In Alaska, Democrat Mary Peltola led Sen. Dan Sullivan by a few hundred votes with roughly three-quarters of the vote counted. If no candidate gets a majority, the result goes to ranked-choice tabulation. The count is more contested than usual. Last week Gov. Mike Dunleavy ordered a state investigation into claims that Democrats recruited a second “Dan Sullivan” for the ballot. He has said certification “could be stopped” depending on what that investigation finds. A federal judge dismissed a Justice Department suit asking to block certification if Sullivan lost narrowly. The judge said the department lacked standing and that the Constitution gives states authority over elections. DOJ has said it will appeal. Peltola calls the investigations baseless. Sullivan says he will accept the result “once every legal vote is counted and certified.” Alaska is scheduled to certify on November 25. Reported margins in both states differ between outlets because they were taken at different points in the count.

Congress · The New Majority

Schumer promises “legislative razor wire” and Jeffries lays out an agenda, but Trump answers “I have the power of the veto”

+

On Wednesday, the day after winning both chambers, Democratic leaders described how they plan to use their new majorities from January. Chuck Schumer, expected to become Senate majority leader, said Democrats would investigate the administration, take it to court and use “legislative razor wire” to box Trump in when he defies the public or the law. He called lowering costs for housing, groceries and health care the top legislative priority, “with the president or without him.” He would not say whether he would try to end the filibuster. Hakeem Jeffries, expected to become Speaker, pledged to reverse the cuts to Medicaid and the ACA tax credits, roll back tariffs and end the Iran war. He acknowledged that Democrats have not agreed on everything on health care, housing and gas prices. Schumer said he has not spoken to Trump since the election, and as of Wednesday Trump had not called either leader.

Trump told reporters, “I have the power of the veto.” Democrats are nowhere near the two-thirds of both chambers needed to override him, and most Senate bills still need 60 votes. Reuters notes that the main powers a Democratic Congress will have are the budget and oversight: subpoenas, investigations and must-pass spending bills. Semafor reports that House Democrats are divided. Progressives do not want the veto threat to limit what they pass. Many centrists want to focus on bills that can clear a filibuster and that Trump would sign.

Iran · Strait of Hormuz

Iran says four tankers are burning after hitting mines in Hormuz and calls the U.S. reply to its plan “unconvincing,” as Qatar keeps talks going

+

Iran’s Revolutionary Guards said four oil tankers were destroyed after hitting naval mines on what they call a prohibited route near the Strait of Hormuz, Reuters reported. The Guards said the ships were still burning and that two other vessels had been seized. An earlier IRGC statement had said three tankers and a tugboat. Reuters could not verify the claims, and U.S. Central Command has repeatedly called reports of mine strikes inaccurate. It says the main transit lanes have been cleared. The UK’s maritime agency, UKMTO, reported a separate projectile strike on a tanker off Oman with no casualties, and it is not clear whether the two incidents are linked. The IRGC has made similar claims at least twice in the past week.

On diplomacy, Mohsen Rezaei, secretary of Iran’s Supreme National Security Council, called Washington’s latest reply “not convincing.” He said the strait stays shut until the U.S. fully meets Tehran’s conditions: lifting oil sanctions, ending the naval blockade of Iranian ports and releasing frozen funds. Trump has confirmed limited U.S. strikes on Iran’s Qeshm Island. Qatar’s spokesman Majed al-Ansari said both sides “remain committed to the negotiation process,” with Doha passing proposals between them in real time. Analysis: Both sides are holding out. Tehran is betting the midterm result weakens Washington’s appetite to escalate, and oil markets are betting the same way (see Energy below). But neither side has offered anything new in public, and the mine claims add to the risk of a miscalculation at sea.

China · Anti-Graft Campaign

China indicts former Politburo member Ma Xingrui on bribery charges, a week after the party expelled him

+

Chinese prosecutors have indicted Ma Xingrui, a former member of the Communist Party’s Politburo and former party chief of Xinjiang, on charges of taking bribes, the state news agency Xinhua said, as reported by Reuters. Prosecutors say he used his positions to get benefits for others and accepted bribes in “particularly large amounts.” Xinhua gave no details of the amounts, dates or what he did in return. An indictment is not a conviction, and Ma made no public response to Reuters.

Analysis: The indictment follows a communique from the party’s fifth plenum last week that confirmed Ma’s expulsion. He was one of 33 officials expelled, along with former top general Zhang Youxia, former senior diplomat Liu Jianchao and former industry minister Jin Zhuanglong. Prosecuting someone who recently sat on the Politburo, the top 24 officials of the party, shows that Xi Jinping’s anti-graft campaign now reaches the very top of the leadership.

Energy · Oil Markets

Brent crude falls almost 9% in a day as traders bet the midterm result will push Trump toward ending the Iran war

+

Oil prices dropped sharply on Wednesday. Brent crude futures, the international benchmark, fell 8.7% to settle at $94.62 a barrel. U.S. West Texas Intermediate fell 8.3% to $88.12. CNBC reports that traders were betting the Democratic sweep, and Vice President Vance’s statement that “the reason we lost is the war,” would push the White House toward a deal rather than escalation. Reuters reported Brent at about $93.82 early Thursday in Asia, a fourth straight daily decline. Outlets give slightly different settlement prices.

The risk has not gone away. Eurasia Group said in a note that renewed escalation could send Brent to $110–125. Commercial oil inventories in major consuming countries are near their lowest level in 15 years, which leaves little cushion for another supply shock. Some reports say crude shipments through Hormuz have returned to close to prewar levels, at higher cost, but Iran says the strait stays closed (see Foreign Affairs). For U.S. households, Wednesday’s fall points to cheaper gasoline. The cost of energy was one of the main issues of the campaign.

Arm beats revenue estimates on data-center demand, but its shares slip after hours

+

Qualcomm, which also designs chips on Arm’s architecture, reported after the close on the same day. Its full figures could not be verified before publication.

Space · Astrophysics

NASA’s Swift observatory falls back to Earth over Indonesia after 22 years, three months after a failed attempt to raise its orbit

+

The U.S. Space Force confirmed that NASA’s Neil Gehrels Swift Observatory re-entered the atmosphere at 7:39 a.m. EST on Wednesday over North Sumatra, Indonesia. The spacecraft weighed about 1,450–1,500 kg (3,200–3,300 lb). Most of it burned up, but NASA said some parts were expected to survive and could be scattered over a wide area. It had put the chance of anyone being harmed at about 1 in 2,100. Swift had no thrusters of its own. Higher solar activity thickened the upper atmosphere and increased the drag that pulled it down.

A private mission launched in July to boost Swift to a higher orbit failed, and NASA said in August that it had not worked. Swift launched in 2004 to catch gamma-ray bursts, the most powerful explosions in the universe. It could turn quickly to point its X-ray and ultraviolet/optical telescopes at a burst within minutes, and it became one of the main instruments for following up on fast-changing events in the sky. Sources disagree on when science operations ended, giving October 20 or October 23.

Arsenal · Champions League

Madueke and Gyökeres put Arsenal 2–0 up in Prague, and they hold on for a 2–1 win that Arteta takes “full responsibility” for

+

Arsenal beat Slavia Prague 2–1 on Wednesday night. Arseblog’s verdict: “Slight concerns but another away day win.” The first half was the best of it. Viktor Gyökeres played Noni Madueke through to score in the ninth minute, then added the second himself before half-time. Flashscore made him man of the match, and Arseblog says Arsenal were in control by the break. After the break it was a different game. Slavia had 17 shots in the second half, Arsenal needed clearances off the line, and Wiktor Nowak scored for Slavia in the 90th minute. Arseblog says Arteta looked visibly frustrated on the touchline.

Arteta called the first half “a really high level in terms of intensity, tempo, quality and clarity.” He said, “I take full responsibility for the drop in the second half,” and that the team has “a lot to learn” from it. Arsenal are still unbeaten in Europe, with 10 points from four games, and moved up to third in the league phase. Slavia have now gone 23 Champions League games without a win, equalling the competition’s longest winless run. Note: Arsenal won 3–0 in this fixture last season, so check the date on any match report you see. (The Athletic could not be reached for corroboration.)

English Football · Champions League

Liverpool lose their perfect record 3–2 at Fenerbahçe, while Haaland’s late goal gives Manchester City a 3–2 win in Leipzig

+

Andoni Iraola’s Liverpool arrived in Istanbul with three wins from three and led twice before losing 3–2 to Fenerbahçe. Trey Nyoni opened the scoring in the 25th minute. Mason Greenwood equalised. Two substitutes, Cody Gakpo and Alexander Isak, combined to put Liverpool back ahead in the 69th minute. Enzo Fernández headed City back in front in the 58th from Iliman Ndiaye’s cross, and Antonio Nusa equalised again three minutes later. Erling Haaland scored the winner in the 78th minute, his 13th goal in nine games against Leipzig, and a late Brajan Gruda goal for Leipzig was ruled out for offside. City have nine points and are seventh. English clubs finished this round with three wins (Arsenal, City, Manchester United) and two defeats (Liverpool, Aston Villa).
