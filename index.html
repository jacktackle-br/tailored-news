White House · Congress · Oversight

Trump orders federal contractors to clear any information they give Congress with the White House first, weeks before Democrats take control

+

On Friday President Trump signed an executive order requiring companies with federal contracts, and their subcontractors, to notify the White House and their contracting agency before responding to requests from Congress. They must also withhold material the administration considers confidential, Reuters reported. Material covered by executive privilege falls under separate withholding instructions, and the Office of Management and Budget is to issue guidance on how the order works in practice. One report says contractors that do not comply could lose their contracts or be suspended from future federal work. Reports do not agree on the order’s exact signing date or number, and the text itself had not been widely published.

The White House said the order protects sensitive communications, including national-security material, and argued that contractors answering Congress on their own could expose privileged information and pull the administration into costly legal fights. It follows a White House Counsel directive requiring agencies to get White House approval before employees agree to testify to Congress. Democrats take control of both chambers in January and have promised investigations that reach into private companies and law firms. Rep. Jamie Raskin, the incoming House Judiciary chair, has said his inquiries will go beyond traditional oversight of the government. Reuters described the order as setting up a potentially significant fight over presidential power and Congress’s authority to investigate.

White House · Cybersecurity · Outages

Trump fires national cyber director Sean Cairncross after a week of outages at the FAA, banks and Microsoft, saying he “wasn’t good enough”

+

Sean Cairncross, the White House national cyber director, has been removed. Reuters, citing a person close to the White House, reported he was fired, and Trump confirmed the decision at Friday’s news conference, saying he acted “because I didn’t think that person was good enough.” NBC News described it as Cairncross leaving the administration. Kirsten Davies, the Pentagon’s chief information officer, is taking over on an acting basis. In a statement, Cairncross called the job “the honor of a lifetime.”

The firing follows a run of technology failures that disrupted air traffic control and the FAA, major banks including JPMorgan Chase, and Microsoft services. Officials say they have found no evidence of a cyberattack. The explanations differ: Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy blamed a newly installed router for the FAA ground stop, Homeland Security chief Markwayne Mullin floated artificial intelligence as a cause, and Secretary of State Marco Rubio said officials had found no evidence linking the incidents. Sen. Mark Warner warned of more outages “as companies try to patch quickly.” Senate Minority Leader Chuck Schumer called Cairncross a political hack without cybersecurity experience.

Courts · Government ads · SDNY

Judge declines to extend her block on taxpayer-funded pro-administration TV ads, saying the threat of harm faded once the midterms ended

+

U.S. District Judge Jennifer Rochon in Manhattan on Friday declined to impose a longer-term ban on the administration’s taxpayer-funded television ads, Reuters reported. She found the challengers had not shown ongoing “imminent and irreparable harm” now that the November elections are over, and called their concerns “too speculative and non-imminent” to justify further emergency relief. She kept the lawsuit alive and noted the administration has not cancelled its ad contract or said it intends to stop running the spots. She said a full written opinion will follow.

In October, Rochon ordered the administration to pull three of the ads immediately. The plaintiffs had sought at least 13, and the administration ran two of them past her deadline before complying. A parallel challenge brought by the Democratic National Committee is pending in Washington, D.C., where a judge heard arguments on Thursday with no ruling yet.

Ukraine · Poland · NATO

Russia hits three Kyiv bridges in a massive barrage, and two Russian cruise missiles and a drone come down in Poland, which Tusk calls an “intentional provocation”

+

In an overnight attack into Friday, Russia struck three bridges over the Dnipro in Kyiv, which Reuters called the largest such strikes of the war. Mayor Vitali Klitschko named the Podilsko-Voskresenskyi, Darnytskyi and Pivdennyi bridges. Ukraine’s air force reported downing 380 drones, 79 cruise missiles and three ballistic missiles. Reuters counted eight killed nationwide; the AP reported three killed and nine injured in Kyiv, with power cuts. Moscow said it targeted road and rail bridges used to move weapons to the front. The attack followed Putin’s vow to retaliate for Ukrainian strikes on the Kerch Bridge to Crimea.

During the same barrage, Poland said a Kh-101 cruise missile fell near Nowy Lipowiec, about 50 km from the Ukrainian border. A second suspected missile landed at Rudnik nad Sanem and a jet-powered drone at Młodychów. No one was hurt. Prime Minister Donald Tusk called it “an intentional provocation,” noting the objects landed near where a “coalition of the willing” exercise was under way. NATO Secretary-General Mark Rutte called it a “grave violation” of Polish airspace. Analysis: whether the missiles strayed or were aimed is the open question, and Warsaw’s answer will decide how hard it presses NATO for a response. It also lands a day after Trump, at his news conference, put equal blame for the war on Zelenskyy and Putin.

Saudi Arabia · Yemen · Houthis

Saudi-led coalition says it shot down three Houthi ballistic missiles aimed at Riyadh early Saturday, the second attack on the capital in three days

+

The Saudi-led coalition said its air defenses destroyed three ballistic missiles fired at Riyadh early Saturday. An AFP journalist in the city heard explosions, and a Reuters witness saw a projectile cross the sky before a loud bang. The coalition also said it downed a Houthi drone headed for southern Saudi Arabia, and that it had struck Hodeidah port and Houthi missile and drone sites in Sanaa on Friday. The Houthis said they were hitting targets across the kingdom. No casualty figures had been reported. The attack follows Thursday’s strike that damaged the Public Investment Fund tower, Riyadh’s tallest building.

Analysis: the Houthis are now firing at the Saudi capital repeatedly, and Riyadh is answering with strikes on the group’s supply lines and launch sites. That makes the Saudi–Houthi conflict an active front alongside the U.S.–Iran war. The Houthis’ threats against Saudi Red Sea ports and airspace raise the stakes for shipping and aviation. Climate · COP31 · Antalya

COP31 goes into an extra day with no deal, as Panama demands a cap on oil production and co-chair Chris Bowen insists agreement is still possible

+

The UN climate talks in Antalya continued into Saturday after nearly 200 countries failed to agree by Friday’s deadline. The Turkish presidency was due to present a third draft of the text, called the “Antalya Accords,” on Saturday morning. Friday’s late session approved only technical items. The main dispute is still the draft’s removal of all references to moving away from oil, gas and coal after objections from Saudi Arabia and other producers. Panama has said it will formally object to any text that leaves this out, and it wants the deal to cap global oil production at current levels. Because COP decisions need consensus, one objection could block adoption. Australia’s Chris Bowen, who chairs the negotiations, said he still believes a deal is possible. No outcome had been reported at the time of writing.

Several other issues remain open: a dropped $120 billion adaptation-finance target, language promoting carbon capture, and a target of 35% of final energy from electricity by 2035 that does not require the power to be clean. COP31 President Murat Kurum has argued that leaving fossil fuels out is not backsliding, because the issue was settled at earlier summits.

+

Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent has blamed Commerce for failing to stop China from obtaining the technology. Lutnick has agreed to testify to the Senate Banking Committee.

Energy · Offshore wind · Scotland

Scotland approves the 1 GW Muir Mhòr floating wind farm off Aberdeenshire, over objections from fishermen

+

Scottish ministers have granted offshore consent for Muir Mhòr, a floating wind farm of up to 1 gigawatt and up to 67 turbines, about 63 km off the Aberdeenshire coast. It is being developed by Fred. Olsen Seawind. The turbines will sit on floating foundations moored to the seabed, rather than the fixed foundations used in shallower water, which allows them to be placed farther out in deeper water. The consent comes with conditions on fisheries mitigation and seabird protection. Aberdeenshire Council approved the onshore cabling and substation in 2025.

The Scottish Fishermen’s Federation objected, saying it is unclear whether fishing boats can work safely around the moorings and subsea cables, and compensation has not been settled. The project has not reached a final investment decision and is aiming to start generating in the early 2030s. That timeline depends on financing and grid connection, which have delayed other floating-wind projects in UK waters.

NBA Cup · Friday

James Harden scores 51 to beat the Pacers and passes Kobe Bryant for third-most 50-point games, while Gilgeous-Alexander’s 40 rallies the Thunder

+

James Harden scored 51 points on 15-of-26 shooting, including 7 of 11 from three and 14 of 14 at the line, as Cleveland beat Indiana 128–119 in NBA Cup group play. It was the 26th 50-point game of his career, moving him past Kobe Bryant into third all-time behind Wilt Chamberlain and Michael Jordan, and his best game since joining the Cavaliers in February. Elsewhere on Friday, Franz Wagner’s jumper with 2.4 seconds left gave Orlando a 113–111 win over Brooklyn, and he finished with 35. Quentin Grimes hit the go-ahead three with 57 seconds left in the Lakers’ 106–104 win over Sacramento, in which Luka Dončić scored 25. Nikola Jokić had 26 points and 11 assists as Denver beat Houston 133–124, and a short-handed Toronto beat Detroit 105–102 to end a six-game losing streak.
