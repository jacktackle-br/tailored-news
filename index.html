Courts · Election 2026

A federal judge says he will rule today on the Justice Department’s bid to set aside 20,000 California ballots, after the 9th Circuit threw out its emergency appeal

+

At a hearing on Tuesday, U.S. District Judge David Carter said he would rule by Wednesday on the administration’s emergency request. The Justice Department wants California and Los Angeles County to set aside mail ballots from about 20,000 registered voters that federal officials say are likely noncitizens. Reports from the hearing said Carter appeared unlikely to grant the request. He suggested there was no need for a last-minute restraining order, since election officials have until Dec. 3 to resolve challenges. The hearing came a day after a 2–1 panel of the 9th U.S. Circuit Court of Appeals dismissed the department’s appeal. The majority said the panel lacked jurisdiction because the government had not given the district court an adequate chance to rule first. Judge Patrick Bumatay dissented. California disputes the list. Its lawyer told the appeals court the demand has “no basis in law or in fact,” and state officials say DHS has not explained how the voters were identified.

The California case is one of several Justice Department election actions in the last week before the Nov. 3 election. The department has also sued Alaska to stop automatic certification of the Senate result if Republican Sen. Dan Sullivan loses. It argues that a second candidate on the ballot with a similar name, Dan J. Sullivan Jr., could confuse voters. Trump has said he ordered that suit. Attorney General Todd Blanche told CNN on Sunday that the administration is not laying the groundwork to overturn the result. Attorneys for Democratic candidate Mary Peltola and the DSCC have moved to dismiss the suit.

The Left-Leaning Take

Voting-rights groups and Democratic-aligned outlets treat the California and Alaska suits as one plan: create legal grounds now to hold up counting or certification if Republicans lose. Democracy Docket’s headline was “It’s happening.” Former Obama DOJ official Justin Levitt notes that no law lets the department challenge individual ballots or delay certification. (No in-window Mother Jones, Vox, Slate or Guardian opinion piece found; this draws on Democracy Docket and legal commentary as reported.)

The Right-Leaning Take

The administration’s defenders say the department is enforcing federal voter-registration law that blue states have ignored. In their view, the 9th Circuit ruled on procedure, not on the merits, and Judge Bumatay’s dissent shows the question is still open. They also cite the department’s recent charges against 28 people in California for voting crimes as evidence that the problem is real. (No in-window National Review, Dispatch, WSJ editorial or Reason piece found; this reflects the administration’s stated position and conservative coverage.)

Justice Department · Courts

“Why today, on this one case?”: Magistrate Judge Faruqui strikes DOJ’s new obstruction complaint against two ex-FBI officials from the Hunter Biden probe

+

On Tuesday, U.S. Magistrate Judge Zia Faruqui in Washington refused to approve a new complaint against former FBI officials Nikki Floris and Bradley Benavides and ordered it struck from the docket. Prosecutors had filed it on Monday night. It added obstruction and attempted-obstruction charges to the existing charges that the two lied to the House Judiciary Committee in 2023 about the bureau’s handling of the Hunter Biden investigation. Faruqui said the unsigned filing had never been reviewed by a judge for probable cause. “It is not our court’s practice … that unsigned complaints are filed in the docket,” he said, and he asked prosecutors, “Why today, on this one case, have you changed the entire history of how you charge cases?” Floris’s lawyers argued the filing was legally deficient and made in bad faith.

The original complaint, filed last week, still stands. Neither defendant has been indicted by a grand jury, and Faruqui is still holding a preliminary hearing on whether there is probable cause to go forward. During that hearing, the FBI agent on the case testified that he “had concerns that this would be political.” The defense drew out that testimony to support its claim that the prosecution is politically motivated. Outlets disagreed on whether to call the new filing a “superseding” or an “amended” complaint. The department can still try to bring the charges through the normal process.

The Left-Leaning Take

The New Republic said the judge “trashed” the department’s attempt to add charges. Critics on the left see the late-night, unsigned filing and the agent’s testimony as signs of a retaliatory prosecution of officials whose work angered the president, along the same lines as the cases against James Comey. (No in-window Mother Jones, Vox, Slate or Guardian opinion piece found; this draws on The New Republic’s news post.)

The Right-Leaning Take

Fox News led with the judge’s past, noting that Faruqui once apologized to a man accused of trying to assassinate Trump. Conservative coverage presents the ruling as a procedural ambush by a hostile bench against a case about FBI officials misleading Congress. They note that the underlying false-statement charges remain. (No in-window National Review, Dispatch, WSJ editorial or Reason piece found; this reflects conservative news framing.)

Immigration · Courts

Judge in Boston pauses the rule giving refugees only 10 days to contest the loss of their status, through Nov. 6

+

On Tuesday, U.S. District Judge Julia Kobick temporarily blocked a USCIS rule, issued Oct. 14, that cut the time refugees have to respond to a notice terminating their legal status from 30 days to 10. Her order runs through Nov. 6. It bars the government from enforcing the rule, from enforcing termination notices issued under the shorter deadline between Oct. 14 and Oct. 27, and from ending anyone’s refugee status because they did not respond to those notices. The Department of Homeland Security has estimated that as many as 186,000 refugees could be affected.

The order is short-term. It does not decide the merits of the lawsuit, and it does not block the administration’s wider review of refugees or its green-card policies. Another judge will decide whether to extend the pause. The plaintiffs are refugee advocacy groups and individual refugees, represented by Democracy Forward. The administration says the shorter process is needed for national security and to fight fraud. A USCIS spokesperson called the suit a “frivolous attempt to disrupt the Trump administration’s commitment to enforce our country’s immigration laws.”

The Left-Leaning Take

Advocates say a 10-day deadline is a due-process trap for people who were already vetted before they arrived, many of whom need translators and lawyers to respond. Democracy Forward says the rule overturned a protection that had been in place for 45 years. (No in-window Mother Jones, Vox, Slate or Guardian opinion piece found; this reflects the plaintiffs’ arguments as reported.)

The Right-Leaning Take

Supporters of the rule say refugee status is a privilege granted by the executive branch, that faster review is how fraud gets caught, and that a district judge should not freeze a national immigration process days before an election. (No in-window National Review, Dispatch, WSJ editorial or Reason piece found; this reflects the administration’s stated position.)

Israel · Election Count

With about 85% counted, Netanyahu’s bloc leads 54–49 but is still seven seats short; the shekel and Israeli bonds fall

+

Partial results from the Central Elections Committee, with about 84.5% of ballots counted, give Benjamin Netanyahu’s bloc 54 of the Knesset’s 120 seats. Gadi Eisenkot’s bloc has 49, and the Arab-led parties (the Joint List and Ra’am) have 17. Neither side is close to the 61 seats needed for a majority. Likud is still the largest single party, at about 27 seats, followed by Eisenkot’s Yashar! at about 22. The count has moved back and forth. At about two-thirds counted, Netanyahu’s bloc had 56, and at 76% the Arab parties had briefly risen to 19. Election-night exit polls had shown the two blocs roughly even, and they varied widely by channel. Official results are due by Nov. 4. After that, each party will recommend a candidate for prime minister to the president, who will choose someone to try to form a government.

Neither leader has an obvious path to 61. Eisenkot has all but ruled out a government that relies on Arab parties. Netanyahu has called for “a strong, right-wing, nationalist government” and warned against one “that relies on anti-Zionist Arab parties.” Bloomberg reported that Netanyahu had moved ahead in the count. Markets reacted to the prospect of a long deadlock, and CNBC reported that the shekel and Israeli government bonds fell on Wednesday. Expect weeks of coalition bargaining, and possibly another election.

Brazil · United States

Flávio Bolsonaro leaves Brazil despite a court order to surrender his passports, and the State Department says it will give him and his family U.S. visas

+

Three sources told Reuters that Flávio Bolsonaro, who lost Sunday’s presidential runoff to Lula, has left Brazil. The U.S. decision to admit him is likely to strain relations with Lula’s incoming government.

AI · Mathematics

Anthropic posts its own claimed refutation of the Exponential Time Hypothesis hours after OpenAI’s, and computer scientists start checking both

+

Hours after OpenAI said an internal model had disproved the Exponential Time Hypothesis (ETH), Anthropic released its own claimed refutation, with the paper crediting “Claude (Anthropic).” ETH is a long-standing conjecture that 3-SAT cannot be solved substantially faster than exponential time. Scientific American describes it as a stronger version of P ≠ NP, and OpenAI’s claimed algorithm is only slightly faster than exponential. Reports say both labs reached the theorem by different routes. Neither paper has been peer-reviewed, and no outside group has confirmed either proof. OpenAI says its result was checked in the Lean proof assistant. Anthropic’s repository warns that a machine check does not confirm that the formal file matches the written paper.

MIT’s Ryan Williams is coordinating the field’s review. He called parts of the proof routine but said there are “two or three alien-looking parts” that the community needs to work through together. He has previously criticized OpenAI’s proof releases as a “DDOS attack on mathematicians.” Russell Impagliazzo and Ramamohan Paturi, who first stated the hypothesis, are also reported to be examining it. Even if the result holds, it would not settle P vs. NP.

Sources: Scientific American, TechCrunch, Oct 27, Washington Times, Oct 27
Energy · Grids

IRENA chief says the world will add less clean-energy capacity this year than last, the first drop in five years, and blames grids rather than demand

+

In an interview with Reuters in Singapore on Tuesday, Francesco La Camera, director-general of the International Renewable Energy Agency, said clean-power additions in 2026 will fall below 2025’s level. That would be the first year-on-year decline in five years. In 2025 the world added a record 692 gigawatts, including hydropower, a 15.5% increase that brought total installed capacity to 5,149 GW. La Camera blamed “the mismatch between generation capacity and the capacity to distribute and transmit electricity,” along with trade barriers, and said appetite for building has not weakened. He did not give a figure for this year.

The main drag is China, where grids have been turning away clean power at their limits. In the first half of 2026 China curtailed enough electricity to power Mexico for a year, and La Camera said growth elsewhere will not make up the difference. Governments have pushed renewables harder since the U.S.-Israeli war with Iran disrupted fossil-fuel supplies. La Camera said that expanding and modernizing grids is now the most urgent energy-security task.

Sources: Reuters via TimesLIVE, Oct 27, IBTimes UK, Green Building Africa
Biotech · Cell Therapy

Artiva shuts down its autoimmune NK-cell program and cuts 94% of staff after two patient deaths, the third company recently to report deaths in such a trial

+

On Tuesday, Artiva Biotherapeutics said it will discontinue AlloNK, its off-the-shelf natural-killer-cell therapy for autoimmune disease, cut its workforce by about 94%, and look at strategic alternatives. One participant in the Phase 2a study died of a neurological complication. The investigator and an independent safety review judged that death likely related to fludarabine, the chemotherapy given before the cells to prepare patients. A second participant died suddenly about two months after treatment. An investigator judged that death unrelated to AlloNK, and its cause has not been determined. After Artiva reported the first death, the FDA asked it to pause the trial, and the company also stopped planned dosing in a Phase 3 study. Artiva said the responses it saw were not durable enough to justify continuing development. Its shares fell about 30%.

STAT notes that Artiva is the third company to report recent deaths in an autoimmune cell-therapy trial, after Novartis and Bristol Myers Squibb. That matters for one of biotech’s most crowded areas, which is trying to bring the “immune reset” seen in CAR-T lupus studies to wider use. The deaths in Artiva’s trial point mainly at the preparatory chemotherapy rather than the cells. This is likely to strengthen the case for approaches that skip or reduce that step.

Sources: STAT, Oct 27, Reuters via KFGO, Oct 27, BioPharma Dive

MLB · World Series Game 4

Rays avoid the sweep: Ben Williamson’s pinch-hit single wins it 4–3 in the 12th after a Joey Ortiz error, and Milwaukee now leads 3–1

+

Tampa Bay stayed alive at Tropicana Field. Chandler Simpson’s double gave the Rays the lead in the second inning, and Cooper Pratt’s RBI double tied it for Milwaukee in the fourth. Pratt then hit a solo home run in the top of the 12th to put the Brewers ahead 3–2. In the bottom half, shortstop Joey Ortiz made an error with no outs on a grounder by Junior Caminero. The Rays’ bullpen allowed one run over eight innings, including two innings of relief from Game 1 starter Nick Martinez.

The Rays are the fifth of 26 teams down 3–0 in a World Series to win Game 4. The win cost them something: Caminero aggravated a left foot injury running out the grounder that started the rally. Milwaukee still needs one win for its first championship.

English Football · Manchester United

After crisis talks at Carrington, United keep Michael Carrick in charge for Saturday’s trip to Chelsea

+

After meetings on Monday involving Sir Jim Ratcliffe, chief executive Omar Berrada and director of football Jason Wilcox, Manchester United have decided Michael Carrick will stay on, at least for now. BBC Sport’s sources, cited by AFP, say he will lead the team at Stamford Bridge on Saturday and get a chance to improve results. United are one place above the relegation zone after a 3–0 home loss to Bournemouth, which followed a 4–1 Champions League defeat at Como. The club had looked at replacements, with Eddie Howe and Julian Nagelsmann under consideration. Asked whether he expected to be in charge at Chelsea, Carrick said: “I’m not expecting anything in particular, to be honest.”

This is a reprieve, not a vote of confidence. United-focused outlets report that compensation costs weighed on the decision. One says Carrick now has three matches to save his job: Chelsea, Roma in the Champions League, and Aston Villa at home. That timeline comes from a single outlet and is unconfirmed. Carrick, whose contract runs to 2028, is due to speak to the media on Thursday.

Arsenal · Carabao Cup

“A dismal night at Highbury”: Arsenal knocked out by League Two Fleetwood, 1–1 and 5–3 on penalties, with Dowman sent off

+

At Fleetwood’s Highbury Stadium, Fulham loanee Aaron Loupalo-Bi gave the hosts the lead after 21 minutes, in a first half that included an eight-minute stoppage while a drone flew over the ground. Viktor Gyökeres equalised in the 50th minute with his first goal of the season. In stoppage time, Fleetwood goalkeeper Jay Lynch made saves from Ben White and Martin Ødegaard. Sixteen-year-old Max Dowman was sent off for violent conduct with about five minutes left. In the shootout, Lynch saved from Bruno Guimarães, and Conor Haughey scored the fifth Fleetwood penalty to win it. Arsenal played without Declan Rice, Gabriel and David Raya. Fleetwood, 18th in League Two, reach the quarter-finals for the first time in their history. Coverage said it is the first time reigning Premier League champions have gone out of the League Cup to a fourth-tier side.

Arseblog’s match verdict is in its headline: “A dismal night at Highbury.” Arseblog News also published its report, player ratings and Arteta’s reaction, though its full text could not be read for this brief. Arteta called it “a very sad day.” He said he “cannot fault” his players’ attitude, but added that “if you want the margins to be bigger than going to penalties, you have to capitalise on that. We didn’t.” His angry reaction to Dowman as the teenager walked off has been criticized, including by Paul Merson.
