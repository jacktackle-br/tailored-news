House · Democrats · White House

Jeffries names health care as the first place House Democrats could work with Trump, and won’t rule impeachment in or out

+

House Democratic leader Hakeem Jeffries, who is expected to become speaker when Democrats take the House majority in January, said on CBS’s “Face the Nation” on Sunday that Democrats “can explore coming together” with President Trump on health care. He pointed to Trump’s comment on Friday that he is “open to great healthcare.” Jeffries proposed lowering the Medicare eligibility age and adding vision, hearing and dental coverage. He said Democrats would also try to reverse the Medicaid cuts in last year’s Republican spending law, and that an early House vote would be on banning members of Congress from trading stocks. He named the cost of living and immigration as other possible areas of agreement. Asked about impeaching the president, he said: “We haven’t ruled anything in, and we haven’t ruled anything out.”

On the Senate side, Sen. Chris Van Hollen told NBC’s “Meet the Press” on Sunday that his support in the challenge to Chuck Schumer is “north of 15” and that “momentum is in our direction.” About 28 votes are needed to win the Dec 1 secret ballot, and Schumer’s allies say he will get more than 40. Van Hollen called the filibuster “an anti-democratic provision” but suggested exceptions or a talking filibuster rather than abolishing it. He said he would not run for president in 2028 whether or not he wins. No independent count confirms either side’s numbers.

Economy · Federal Reserve · Markets

Bessent warns the Fed against “overtightening” after Wall Street’s worst week in six years, as futures bounce back

+

Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent said on CBS’s “Face the Nation” on Sunday that the Federal Reserve risks a policy error if it tightens too far. He argued that inflation driven by the energy shock will fade on its own and that financial conditions have already tightened sharply over recent months. He did not name any Fed officials or say what he wants the Fed to do at its Dec 8–9 meeting. Reports read his remarks as a rebuttal to Fed Governor Christopher Waller, who said last week that a half-point increase should be on the table. Markets have largely priced in a quarter-point rise; CME FedWatch odds were reported at about 92%. The Fed raised its target range to 3.75%–4.00% in September and held it in October.

The comments came after the Nasdaq Composite and the Dow had their worst week in more than six years, leaving all three major indexes in correction territory, according to CNBC. Friday’s slide was led by investors pulling out of AI stocks. Stock futures rebounded when trading resumed Sunday evening: Nasdaq-100 futures were up about 3% early Monday. Analysts said a bounce was likely after seven straight losing sessions and were cautious about whether it would last.

Iran · Hormuz · Blockade

Trump says Iranian tankers that run the U.S. blockade will end up “at the bottom of the sea,” and Tehran vows to use “every means” to counter it

+

Asked by Fox News on Sunday whether Iran would try to break the U.S. naval blockade of its ports, President Trump said he hoped it would, and that any tankers that tried would end up “at the bottom of the sea” like Iran’s navy. Tehran’s municipal newspaper Hamshahri had reported that Iran gathered 14 oil tankers at Kharg Island, the terminal for most of its crude exports, to run the blockade. Euronews reported a tanker incident “involving military forces” near the blockade line on Sunday, but accounts conflicted on whether any tanker had actually left Kharg. On Monday, foreign ministry spokesman Esmaeil Baghaei called the blockade “military aggression” and said Iran would use “every means” to counter it. He said Iran’s conditions for reopening the strait include lifting U.S. sanctions, ending the blockade and carrying out commitments under the Islamabad memorandum of understanding. CENTCOM had not commented as of Monday.

The exchange raises the stakes for Qatar’s mediation. Qatar’s prime minister was expected to meet Secretary of State Marco Rubio, and Iran says it is still willing to talk through mediators. Some analysts doubt the convoy threat is real: a United Against Nuclear Iran analyst called the plan “farcical” and said U.S. forces could stop several ships at once. Oil markets are calm for now. Brent was around $92 a barrel early Monday after a third straight weekly loss, as fears of oversupply offset the tanker risk.

Ukraine · Russia

Dymer death toll rises to 11 as a boy and a 79-year-old woman are pulled alive from the rubble after 22 hours

+

The death toll from Sunday’s Russian drone strike on a five-story apartment building in Dymer, north of Kyiv, rose to 11 on Monday after rescuers recovered another body, Kyiv regional governor Tymur Tkachenko said. Two people are still unaccounted for. Rescuers pulled out a seven-year-old boy, Davyd, and a 79-year-old woman, Valentyna, alive after about 22 hours under the debris, Ukraine’s emergency service said. Air-raid alerts repeatedly interrupted the search. The Kyiv region declared Monday a day of mourning.

Early reports on Sunday put the toll at four, with 14 residents out of contact. The strike comes days after Vladimir Putin promised a “far more painful” response to Ukraine’s repeated attacks on the Crimean Bridge and ruled out a truce on energy and shipping targets, which makes further heavy strikes on Ukrainian towns likely while the talks Washington is pushing have no date.

AI · Safety · OpenAI

OpenAI’s chief scientist proposes industry-wide “limits” on AI development, measured by perplexity

+

Jakub Pachocki, OpenAI’s chief scientist, published an essay on Sunday, “Perplexity and Intelligence,” proposing that AI developers agree on “common limits, based on perplexity and other measurements,” that they would commit not to cross until the monitoring and understanding of AI behaviour reaches set milestones. Perplexity measures how well a model predicts held-out text. Pachocki argues that it can serve as “a shared benchmark for general intelligence” now that ordinary benchmarks quickly go stale. His example of a milestone that would let developers pass a first limit is a successor to today’s chain-of-thought monitors. He also warns that AI is beginning to contribute to its own development, writing that recent OpenAI models benefited from “meaningful design contributions coming from AI.” Sam Altman called it “the most important thing OpenAI has published in awhile.”

Early analysis points to practical problems. Perplexity reflects only gains from pretraining, not from later training or tool use. A limit would only bind other labs if they measured on the same held-out data, and OpenAI’s reported figures come from a private code snapshot. Outlets also report different numeric thresholds from the essay, so the specific numbers should be treated as unconfirmed.

Chips · Nvidia · Markets

Trump again urges Nvidia’s board to rehire Jensen Huang, and Nvidia shares jump in early trading as tech stocks rebound

+

In a Truth Social post on Sunday, President Trump wrote, “I hope the Board of Nvidia rehires Jensen Huang.” Huang resigned this month as chief executive amid reports of an affair with a woman suspected of spying for China, and the board has said it does not plan to reinstate him. Congressional Democrats are separately investigating whether the company improperly influenced a federal chip-smuggling inquiry.

Nvidia shares rose as much as 9.7% in premarket trading on Monday, and Anthropic, which listed on Nasdaq last week, rose about 5%, as tech futures recovered from last week’s sell-off. Analysts linked part of Nvidia’s gain to Trump’s post, but said the company’s leadership, the investigations and the Fed’s December decision still hang over the stock.

Climate · EU · Carbon Pricing

Eight EU governments, including Germany, tell Brussels not to delay the new carbon market for heating and road fuel

+

Ministers from Denmark, Finland, Germany, Luxembourg, the Netherlands, Portugal, Spain and Sweden wrote to EU Climate Commissioner Wopke Hoekstra asking the Commission not to change or postpone ETS2, the second emissions trading system, which will put a carbon price on fuel for buildings and road transport from 2028. Euronews, which saw the letter, reported on Monday that the signatories argue further delay would weaken energy security, prolong dependence on imported fossil fuels and put clean-technology investment at risk. They also warn the Commission not to use its current review of the carbon market to reopen and weaken the law.

The letter answers a bloc led by Italy and Poland, which has cited household costs and wants the start pushed back to 2030 or later. In October, EU leaders asked the Commission to reassess the impact of ETS2 but left the 2028 start in place. Germany’s signature matters: the fight is no longer only the Nordic and Benelux states against delay. It comes a few days after COP31 in Antalya broke down over fossil-fuel language, which leaves domestic carbon pricing as one of the EU’s main remaining climate tools.

Premier League · Sunday

Pochettino’s United debut ends 4–0 at Anfield after an eighth-minute red card, and McBurnie’s stoppage-time double sinks Brighton at Hull

+

Mauricio Pochettino’s first game as Manchester United manager went wrong almost immediately. Leny Yoro was sent off in the eighth minute for a last-man foul on Alexander Isak, which Sky Sports said was the earliest red card for a United player in a Premier League match. Dominik Szoboszlai scored from the free kick on 10 minutes and Florian Wirtz added a second on 13. Goals from Ngumoha (66) and Muñoz (82), per United’s own match report, completed a 4–0 win. Pochettino called the defeat “so painful” and disputed the red card. United stay in the relegation zone, a point off the bottom. Liverpool move back up to fourth, a point behind Manchester City.

At Hull, Brighton were kept out by goalkeeper Konstantinos Tzolakis, who made five first-half saves, a season high for any Premier League keeper according to reports. Hull’s Costinha was sent off on 89 minutes, but Oli McBurnie scored twice in stoppage time (90+2, 90+5) to win it 2–0. The result ended Hull’s long winless run and Brighton’s eight-game winning streak. Brentford host Everton on Monday night to close the round.
