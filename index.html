Election 2026 · Supreme Court

The Supreme Court refuses, within hours, the Trump administration’s bid to make California set aside 20,000 mail ballots

+

On Monday the Supreme Court denied the Justice Department’s emergency request to order California to set aside and hold mail ballots tied to about 20,000 registered voters the department says are likely noncitizens, until their citizenship could be checked. Per The Hill’s report, the order was a single unexplained sentence with no noted dissents, and the Court ruled without asking California to respond. Solicitor General John Sauer had argued that once the ballots leave their envelopes and are mixed with others, any unlawful votes can never be traced. One report referred to dissents by Justices Thomas and Alito, but it appears to describe a different stage of the case. The no-dissent account is the one confirmed.

The Court’s order ends the department’s effort, which had already lost twice in the lower courts. U.S. District Judge David Carter denied a temporary restraining order last week, and a 9th Circuit panel then ruled 2–1 against the department. In dissent, Judge Patrick Bumatay argued that California’s refusal encourages the “violation of federal law.” California disputes that the federal list is reliable. California election offices had already received hundreds of thousands of mail ballots before Tuesday, and the state accepts ballots that arrive through November 10. This was one of two federal attempts, along with the Alaska certification suit dismissed on Sunday, to set some votes apart before the count.

Election 2026 · The Closing Argument

Trump spends his last pre-election speech mostly on the Reflecting Pool and allows that a president’s party “doesn’t win the midterms”

+

President Trump gave his final speech before Tuesday’s vote on Monday afternoon at the Lincoln Memorial Reflecting Pool, where the National Park Service has extended a closure for renovation work through November 20. CNN and CNBC report that he spent more than 20 minutes on the pool’s restoration and other Washington projects, including a municipal golf course, before he mentioned the election. He repeated his claim that vandals were to blame for the repair problems. NBC News reports that he said Republicans “gave this a very strong shot” and acknowledged that the president’s party historically “doesn’t win the midterms.” JNS reports that he also warned of “jihadists” in Congress.

The Washington Post describes the speech as ending his campaign on a project he sees as a success, which Democrats call a sign of misplaced priorities. Reporting from the past two days describes a resigned mood in the White House, with officials bracing for losses. After the speech, Trump also answered a question about Iran in a way that moved oil markets (see Global Affairs). Polls open Tuesday morning. Republicans hold the Senate 53–47 and the House narrowly.

Iran · War & Diplomacy

Asked if he is weighing strikes on Iran after the midterms, Trump says “No,” and oil falls sharply

+

After his Reflecting Pool speech on Monday, a reporter asked Trump whether he was considering strikes on Iran after Tuesday’s elections. He answered “No.” Reuters describes a spoken answer, while CNN says he mouthed it. He also posted that “Iran wants a settlement, as soon as possible. It could happen!” Vice President JD Vance said no deadline has been set for the talks. Trump repeated that Iran will never be allowed a nuclear weapon. The remarks follow an Axios report that Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu, in a call Saturday that an Israeli source called “tough,” pressed for joint strikes right after the vote, while Trump wanted more time for talks. A Wall Street Journal report last week said Washington had told Gulf states to prepare for a short, intense campaign once the midterms were over.

Markets took the remarks as a signal for de-escalation. Brent fell as much as about 7% during the day, to roughly $105 a barrel, after dropping more than 3% in the morning on the Axios report. Analysts warned of “false dawns.” The underlying dispute has not moved. Tehran says “the ball is in the U.S. court” and wants the naval blockade lifted before it reopens Hormuz. Iran’s Revolutionary Guard claimed Monday, without independent confirmation, that 11 tankers had burned or sunk in the strait over nine days. Trump’s answer takes away the date many in the region had treated as a deadline. It does not settle whether he will choose a deal or resume strikes, and he has changed course on Iran before.

Ukraine · Russia

Russian bombs hit a school used as an aid point in Zaporizhzhia region, killing at least 24, as Zelenskyy warns of a bigger strike during the U.S. vote

+

On Monday Russian guided aerial bombs struck a school in the frontline village of Komyshuvakha, in Zaporizhzhia region, during its weekly humanitarian aid handout. Ukraine’s State Emergency Service says at least 24 people were killed, including two rescuers and four police officers. Some outlets put the toll at 26, and regional officials say 27 people are missing. President Volodymyr Zelenskyy called it an “exceptionally horrific strike.” He said that after the first bomb, nine more were dropped on the shelter where people had taken cover, and that Russian forces “could not have been unaware” that aid was handed out there every week. The region has declared Tuesday a day of mourning. Aid workers have stopped deliveries to the village and are focusing on evacuating residents. There was no Russian comment.

On the same day, Zelenskyy warned that Russia is preparing a new large-scale strike on energy and logistics targets. He suggested Moscow may time it for the U.S. midterms, when the world’s attention and Ukraine’s request for more Patriot interceptors are likely to get less notice. The strike came a day after the Oleshky local truce expired. The timing shows how little that small, U.S.-brokered pause changed the wider war. It also shows how Kyiv fears the next few days could go: heavy strikes while Washington is busy with the vote.

Election 2026 · Eve of the Vote

Final Times/Siena polls show Democrats ahead or tied in all seven Senate battlegrounds as Trump closes a 30-rally blitz in Kansas

+

The last New York Times/Siena polls before Tuesday’s midterms, released Sunday, show Democrats leading or tied in the seven Senate races most likely to decide control: Alaska, Iowa, Kansas, Maine, Michigan, Ohio and Texas. Democrat James Talarico leads Ken Paxton 50% to 43% in Texas. Troy Jackson leads Sen. Susan Collins 51% to 45% in Maine, and Sherrod Brown leads Sen. Jon Husted 49% to 44% in Ohio. Alaska (Mary Peltola 48%, Sen. Dan Sullivan 46%) and Iowa (48–47) are within the margin of error. The Michigan poll, which showed Abdul El-Sayed up 11 points, was taken before Sunday’s Democratic rupture there. Siena’s Don Levy cautioned that with so many races inside the margin of error, either party could still win the Senate. Republicans hold it 53–47, so Democrats need a net gain of four seats.

President Trump ended his campaign Sunday night in Salina, Kansas, after a stop in Cedar Rapids, Iowa. He called it his 30th and final rally and urged voters to re-elect Sen. Roger Marshall, who is in a close race with Democrat Adam Hamilton. He had cancelled both trips earlier in the week and then reinstated them. The Washington Post says Democrats could win both chambers, which would stall much of Trump’s agenda for his last two years, but that the Senate is especially uncertain. NPR says control of the House may be clear on election night, while the Senate count could take days. CBS News reports that more than 54 million people have already voted.

Slate argues that Democrats, who two years ago saw little hope of retaking the Senate, now have a real shot at the four seats they need, largely because Trump’s approval on the economy has fallen into the low 30s. It says the night could go either way: a historic Democratic win, or a string of impressive but narrow losses in red states. (Dated Nov 2026 by URL; full text not independently read.)

Election 2026 · Michigan Senate

Two days before the vote, Democratic Rep. Haley Stevens endorses Republican Mike Rogers over her party’s Senate nominee

+

Rep. Haley Stevens, who lost Michigan’s Democratic Senate primary to Abdul El-Sayed in August and later said she would back him, endorsed Republican Mike Rogers on Sunday and appeared with him in Detroit. The move followed a weeklong feud. Stevens had called an outside group’s ad antisemitic and demanded that El-Sayed condemn it. Her fiancé, Jason Turkish, said he could not vote for El-Sayed, pointing to his ties to the streamer Hasan Piker and other critics of Israel. Stevens says El-Sayed then told her she was no longer welcome at his events. She told CNN her decision was “rock solid” but did not explain why she had changed her mind.

Democrats reacted fiercely. Senate Minority Leader Chuck Schumer called the endorsement “a despicable betrayal,” and DNC Chair Ken Martin said it betrayed the party’s values. Michigan Democratic Party chair Curtis Hertel said Stevens is no longer a party member. Her chief of staff and many of her staff resigned. Rogers praised her courage, and Trump praised her on Truth Social. El-Sayed said, “I actually don’t think about Haley Stevens.” NPR notes that a Rogers win would raise the number of seats Democrats must flip to win the Senate from four to five.

The Left-Leaning Take

Mother Jones frames the endorsement as Stevens abandoning fellow Democrats. A companion piece lists her own past attacks on Rogers, including calling his record on LGBTQ rights “repulsive,” to argue that the switch is about her feud with El-Sayed, not about principle.

The Right-Leaning Take

Reason (libertarian) presents the endorsement as Stevens “spurning” a “leftist” nominee. It calls El-Sayed’s campaigning with Hasan Piker “a huge unforced error” and credits Rogers with spotting the opening. It also faults Rogers for suggesting that El-Sayed has links to Islamic terrorists.

Election 2026 · Alaska & the Courts

A federal judge throws out the Justice Department’s bid to hold up Alaska’s Senate result over the second “Dan Sullivan,” and the department appeals within an hour

+

On Sunday, U.S. District Judge Sharon Gleason dismissed the Justice Department’s lawsuit seeking to delay certification of Alaska’s Senate race if Republican Sen. Dan Sullivan loses to Democrat Mary Peltola. The suit had also asked her to order ballots ranking the second candidate, Daniel J. Sullivan, kept apart. Gleason ruled that the federal government lacked standing. She found that its requests were “infeasible” because many Alaskans had already voted and that the department offered “no workable plan” to separate ballots. Separating them for a law-enforcement inquiry, she said, would violate voters’ right to a secret ballot. She refused to let the department amend the suit, calling another rewrite futile.

The department filed a notice of appeal to the 9th Circuit about an hour later. It said the dismissal was procedural and that the judge had not disputed its claim that the second Sullivan entered the race to confuse voters. Its separate criminal investigation is unaffected, and no one has been charged. Sen. Sullivan told CNN he will accept the result. Peltola’s campaign called the suit “baseless” and the work of a “weaponized Department of Justice.” There is no new public filing yet on the threatened Supreme Court appeal over California’s mail ballots.

The Left-Leaning Take

No in-window piece on Sunday’s ruling from Mother Jones, Vox, Slate or Guardian opinion could be verified this run. Their Alaska coverage found is from October, before the dismissal.

The Right-Leaning Take

At Reason’s Volokh Conspiracy (libertarian), law professor Ilya Somin welcomes the ruling. He argues that it goes beyond standing: no federal law lets the executive branch block certification of a state’s results over “voter confusion,” and the Constitution leaves the time, place and manner of elections to the states.

Iran · Diplomacy

Iranian negotiators leave Doha as a draft U.S. deal circulates, and Tehran warns it will hit back at any new attack

+

Iran’s negotiators left talks with Qatari mediators in Doha on Sunday. An official briefed on the talks told Reuters that a draft proposal for a deal with the United States is now being passed around, and that another meeting is possible. The official said the Iranians did not leave because the talks ran into trouble, but gave no details of the draft. Deputy Foreign Minister Kazem Gharibabadi had been in Doha to pass messages to Washington through the Qataris. On Monday, foreign ministry spokesman Esmaeil Baghaei said the talks are built on the seven-step plan Iran drew up in September, now shortened so it could be carried out in two days. He said the Strait of Hormuz stays closed, and there will be no final deal, until the U.S. naval blockade is lifted and Iran’s demands are met.

Baghaei also said Iran would answer any renewed attack with a strong and immediate response. Asked about Tuesday’s U.S. midterms, he said Tehran did not expect the vote to produce “a new US.” Trump has said the U.S. will not launch new strikes on Iran before the election, and he has called the talks productive, but he has not ruled out more military action. The coming days are the real test. A draft on paper and Tehran’s tougher words point to a decision window that opens once the polls close.

Ukraine · Russia

Russian strikes black out hundreds of thousands of Ukrainian homes as the Oleshky truce runs out

+

Russia hit power infrastructure in four Ukrainian regions overnight into Sunday, cutting electricity to hundreds of thousands of households, President Volodymyr Zelenskiy said. “The intensity of the strikes is very high, and the Russians are seeking to maximally exploit the deficit of ballistic missile interceptors,” he said. Ukraine’s air force said it shot down 371 of 433 long-range drones Russia launched from Saturday evening. Ukraine struck back. Zelenskiy said Ukrainian drones hit the Kamensky plant in Russia’s Rostov region, which he said helps make Russian missiles. Russian officials reported “significant damage” to an industrial site in the Nizhny Novgorod region, home to the NORSI refinery.

The strikes landed on the last day of the six-day local ceasefire around the Russian-held town of Oleshky, which Melania Trump helped broker so food could get in and civilians could get out. The truce held, but fighting went on everywhere else. Ukraine says it backs a full ceasefire on energy targets and accuses Moscow of refusing one. The Oleshky pause showed that small, practical deals are possible. This weekend showed how far that is from a wider stop to the war, as winter nears and both sides keep going after each other’s energy systems.

Sources: Reuters (via U.S. News), Nov 1, CNBC (Reuters), Nov 1
Europe · Bulgaria

Bulgaria elects Iliana Iotova, a skeptic of arming Ukraine, as its first woman president

+

Acting President Iliana Iotova won Sunday’s presidential runoff in Bulgaria. With 99.95% of precincts counted, her ticket had 57.58% of the vote against 39.47% for challenger Andrey Gyurov, Reuters reported. Turnout was low, and her rival conceded. Iotova, a former vice president, took over as acting president in January when Rumen Radev resigned to run for parliament. She will be the country’s first elected female head of state.

The presidency is mostly ceremonial, but the result still matters. Iotova favors dialogue with the Kremlin and opposes military aid to Ukraine, and she and Radev have both called for a negotiated end to the war. That puts another skeptical voice at the top of an EU and NATO member on the Black Sea, at a moment when Europe’s support for Kyiv is already under strain.

Electrification · EVs

BYD sells more than 300,000 battery-electric cars in a month for the first time as China’s EV makers post October records

+

BYD reported on Sunday that it sold 490,168 vehicles in October, up about 11% from a year earlier and its best month of 2026. Sales of fully electric passenger cars reached 305,743, up 37%, the first time the company has topped 300,000 in a single month. Plug-in hybrid sales fell 17%. Several rivals also set records. Leapmotor delivered 106,127 vehicles, its fourth straight month above 100,000. Xpeng delivered 50,237, its first month above 50,000, though still short of its 60,000-a-month target for the quarter. Xiaomi also passed 50,000. Nio (34,246) and Li Auto (29,025) both fell from a year earlier.

Energy · Oil

OPEC+ holds December oil output steady as the Iran war keeps disrupting Gulf exports

+

Seven OPEC+ members (Saudi Arabia, Russia, Iraq, Kuwait, Algeria, Kazakhstan and Oman) agreed at an online meeting on Sunday to keep their production targets for December unchanged. The group said it expects no further policy changes until next year. Saudi Arabia’s target stays at 10.478 million barrels a day. The United Arab Emirates used to be part of this group but left OPEC in May.

The group finished reversing a 1.65 million barrel-a-day cut in September. About 2 million barrels a day of other cuts stay in place through the end of 2026. The harder decision comes at the full OPEC+ meeting on November 29, which is expected to set quotas for 2027 once a review of each member’s production capacity is finished, now expected by mid-November.
