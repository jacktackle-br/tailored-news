White House · Press Office

Katie Zacharia is out as White House press secretary after five days, the shortest tenure on record, after Trump publicly tells her to “reconsider”

+

On Friday, two White House officials told The Washington Post that Katie Zacharia is leaving the press secretary job five days after she started. A White House official told NPR and Fox News that she is “transitioning out” of the role. Before her departure was confirmed, Trump told reporters he had just spoken with her: “Katie, you know, you don’t have to do this if you don’t want,” adding that she “should reconsider.” Outlets disagree on how she left. The New York Times, citing three people familiar with the matter, reported that Trump fired her. CNN also used “fires” in its headline, though a Trump adviser told CNN she would leave in the next few days. Other outlets described the exit as a transition. No successor had been named as of Friday evening.

Trump announced on Oct. 9 that Zacharia would succeed Karoline Leavitt. She had been a senior communications adviser at Trump Media and, before that, briefly a DHS spokesperson and a Fox News and Newsmax commentator. Her first briefing, on Tuesday, lasted about 14 minutes, and she took questions from only three reporters. Scrutiny then turned to her credentials. A CNN analysis reported that she had presented herself as a lawyer although she does not meet California’s requirements to use the title, and that she had failed the state bar exam. Breitbart reported that she acknowledged not having passed the bar.

Midterms · Georgia

At a Dalton, Georgia rally, Trump calls Jon Ossoff “radical-left” and repeats 2020 fraud claims, while the GOP’s governor nominee skips mentioning him

+

Trump rallied Friday in Dalton, in conservative northwest Georgia, for Senate nominee Rep. Mike Collins and governor nominee Rick Jackson, 18 days before the Nov. 3 election. AP reports that he spent much of the speech attacking Sen. Jon Ossoff, whom he called a “weak, arrogant, hate-filled, radical-left senator,” and other Democrats. He also defended the Iran war, promoted his border policies and repeated his claims of election fraud in Georgia. When a protester interrupted over immigration, Trump suggested Ossoff had paid the protester. Jackson, a billionaire businessman, appeared briefly onstage before Trump arrived and did not mention the president. A Collins spokesperson called him a “bipartisan workhorse who will work with anybody.”

Ossoff said he was “surprised Georgia Republicans want the president here” and focused on rising household costs. The polls have Collins behind. CNN describes his race as an uphill battle, and The Washington Times cites a Trafalgar poll with Ossoff up 8. The governor’s race against Democrat Keisha Lance Bottoms is closer, with one poll showing Jackson ahead by 2. AP describes the visit as a test of whether Trump can energize his base in a state where his candidates also need swing voters, a day after its poll put his approval at 36%.

Black Sea · NATO

Romania resumes the search for seven sailors after drones hit two tankers off its coast, blames Russia, and asks NATO for a joint Black Sea command

+

Reuters reported that Romanian rescuers resumed the search Friday for seven of the 12 crew of the San Marino-flagged chemical tanker Wine Trader. Drones hit the ship about 13 nautical miles southeast of the Sulina canal, just outside Romanian waters. Five sailors were rescued, and high waves had halted the search. About 12 hours later a second tanker, the Comoros-flagged Yara J, was hit late Wednesday. Its crew was unharmed and the ship was towed to Constanța. Both ships were anchored near the mouth of the Danube’s Sulina channel, a main export route for Ukraine. President Nicușor Dan confirmed the drone strikes but at first would not say whose drones they were. A NATO military source told Reuters the drones came from Russia. Euromaidan Press reports that Romania’s foreign minister has since said Russia “bears full responsibility.” Bucharest is proposing a joint NATO Black Sea command, and alliance ambassadors are due to discuss the attacks next week. NATO Secretary General Mark Rutte said counter-drone capabilities on the eastern flank have been increased.

Analysis: Reuters counts at least five other cargo ships hit off Romania and Bulgaria in the past ten days, with 23 sailors dead or missing. Drone strikes this close to NATO territorial waters, on a route that matters to Ukraine’s exports, are a gray-zone campaign. The alliance now has to decide whether to respond, as the war’s energy truce collapses and Washington and Kyiv are openly at odds. A joint command would be the first structural NATO response. Turkey’s control of the straits under the Montreux Convention limits what non-littoral navies can do there.

Sources: Reuters (via WHBL), Oct 16, Reuters (via Arab News), Oct 16, gCaptain, Euromaidan Press, Oct 16

White House · Money

Leaked records name the donors behind $330 million for Trump’s ballroom: Nvidia, Apple, the Adelsons and Charles Ergen at $25 million each, and nothing from Trump

+

The Washington Post reported Thursday that it had obtained an internal fundraising spreadsheet for the White House ballroom. As of late September it listed 67 gifts above $10,000, totaling about $333 million. The money runs through the Trust for the National Mall, a D.C. nonprofit, under an agreement that keeps donors anonymous unless White House staff approve disclosure. At least $77 million came from donors who asked for anonymity. The largest gifts were $25 million each, from Nvidia, Apple, the Adelson family and Dish founder Charles Ergen. Google gave $22 million through its settlement of a Trump lawsuit. Lockheed Martin, Microsoft and Palantir gave $10 million each, and Meta, Amazon and Cisco $5 million each. Nvidia had asked to stay anonymous. Its gift came about three months after it reached a deal with the administration to sell advanced chips to China. Trump does not appear on the list, although he has said the project is paid for “a hundred percent by me and some friends of mine.”

The Post found that about half the money came from companies or executives with federal contracts or other close ties to the administration. It also says its reporting does not show that any gift led to favorable government action. Nvidia and Cisco confirmed their gifts to CNBC, and Nvidia said its support is “independent of any business interests.” White House spokesman Davis Ingle called the donors “a wide array of great American companies and generous individuals.” He vowed to “relentlessly pursue those responsible for this cowardly leak.” Sen. Elizabeth Warren called the Trust “a vehicle for politicized fundraising, influence peddling, and donor access.”

The Left-Leaning Take

The early left reaction, from Warren and from The New Republic (not a listed outlet), treats the list as a menu of donors with business before the government: chipmakers, defense contractors and companies settling with Trump. It also highlights that the president gave nothing to a project he said he was paying for. (No listed opinion-outlet pieces found.)

The Right-Leaning Take

No in-window piece from National Review, The Dispatch, the WSJ editorial page or Reason turned up. The White House line is that the donors are patriotic companies and individuals paying for a project taxpayers won’t fund. In that view, the real scandal is the leak of documents the donors were promised would stay private. Reason’s earlier ballroom coverage objected to how the project skirted Congress, not to private money as such. (No listed opinion-outlet pieces found.)

Elections · Justice Department

Justice Department sues California to hold back mail ballots sent to 20,000 people it calls “likely noncitizens,” less than three weeks before the vote

+

On Thursday the Justice Department sued California Secretary of State Shirley Weber and Los Angeles County Registrar Dean Logan in federal court in Los Angeles. The suit asks a judge to order the state to set aside, and not count, mail ballots sent to more than 20,000 people whom DHS-compiled data flag as “likely noncitizens,” until each voter’s citizenship is verified. The department says it is not seeking to purge anyone or cancel votes, and it bases its case on the Supremacy Clause. The suit follows letters Acting Deputy Attorney General Trent McCotter sent Tuesday with a password-protected thumb drive of names and a 48-hour deadline. L.A. County declined to open the drive, citing data-privacy and security protocols. Federal officials had earlier claimed that 190,000 noncitizens were registered in California.

Attorney General Todd Blanche called the issue potential “state-facilitated fraud.” U.S. Attorney Bill Essayli said the administration “will not allow California to dilute the votes of American citizens.” Weber said the department provided no evidence or method behind its list and is “demanding unprecedented measures that threaten thousands of Californians’ right to vote.” Gov. Gavin Newsom called the suit “a sad, desperate attempt.” Election-law experts described the case as a long shot. NPR notes that the complaint cites no statute that would impose an added citizenship check on voters. The Justice Department has sued 30 states and D.C. for voter-roll data and has lost 26 of those cases without a win.

The Left-Leaning Take

No in-window piece from Mother Jones, Vox, Slate or Guardian opinion turned up. The voting-rights side, voiced by Weber, Newsom and the election-law group Democracy Docket, sees a major escalation. In their account, an unverified federal list delivered on a thumb drive with a 48-hour deadline is being used to put lawfully cast ballots in limbo in the state most likely to decide the House. (No listed opinion-outlet pieces found.)

The Right-Leaning Take

No in-window piece from National Review, The Dispatch, the WSJ editorial page or Reason turned up. The administration’s case, echoed in conservative outlets such as The Daily Signal, is that federal immigration data is the best check on citizenship and that it is asking only to verify ballots, not discard them. On this view, a state that refuses even to open the list is the one putting election integrity at risk. (No listed opinion-outlet pieces found.)

Polling · Midterms

AP-NORC poll: 8 in 10 voters want major change in Washington, and Trump’s approval sits at 36%

+

An AP-NORC poll of registered voters, taken Oct. 8–12 and released Thursday, found that 80% want either “substantial change” or “complete and total upheaval” in how the country is run. That includes 97% of Democrats, 90% of independents and 58% of Republicans. About 3 in 10 voters overall chose upheaval. Trump’s job approval is 36%, with 64% disapproving. About 8 in 10 disapprove of his handling of the cost of living, and about three-quarters disapprove of his handling of the economy. The margin of error is ±3.9 points.

The results do not amount to an endorsement of Democrats. Voters prefer Democratic control of the House by 9 points and of the Senate by 8, but only about 4 in 10 view the Democratic Party favorably. The enthusiasm gap favors Democrats: about three-quarters of Democratic voters call this election extremely important, compared with about 6 in 10 Republicans. AP frames the findings as a hard picture for a Republican majority defending Congress under an unpopular Iran war and rising prices. Separately, Trump said Thursday on Truth Social that Sen. John Cornyn and Karl Rove would be to blame “if Ken Paxton loses” the Texas Senate race.

The Left-Leaning Take

No in-window piece from a listed left outlet specifically on this poll. Slate’s recent polling coverage argues that the numbers point to a strong Democratic year. The caution on the left is the one the poll itself contains: voters want change more than they want Democrats. (No poll-specific listed pieces found.)

Iran War

U.S. draws up strike plans that could use the “Mother of All Bombs” as Iran’s army chief vows Hormuz stays shut and Treasury adds new sanctions

+

CNN reported Thursday, citing sources, that the U.S. military has drafted new strike options against Iranian energy infrastructure and nuclear sites that could include the GBU-43/B, the largest non-nuclear bomb in the U.S. arsenal. The plans would apply if Trump orders combat to resume, which he has said he will not do before the Nov. 3 midterms. Officials privately questioned whether the options fit any strategy for ending the war. One source called some of them a “fireworks show” meant to offer an off-ramp. The White House said Trump “keeps all options on the table.” At a Montana rally Wednesday night he complained, “I don’t know who we’re negotiating with.” AP, citing U.S. officials, reported that Secretary of State Marco Rubio had Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi’s delegation leave New York early after indirect talks stalled. Iran denies it was expelled.

Iran’s army chief, Maj. Gen. Amir Hatami, said Thursday that the Strait of Hormuz will stay “closed” until “the rights of the Iranian people” are respected. He warned that any strike on Iranian infrastructure would bring a response “several times greater.” U.S. Central Command maintains that U.S. and partner forces control the waterway. The same day, Treasury sanctioned 16 people and 10 entities, among them Iraq’s International Development Bank, Iranian offshore banks and foreign-exchange fronts. CBS counts at least 10 vessels attacked in or near the strait since Sunday, the most since the war began. Analysis: Washington is pairing escalation threats with financial pressure while holding off on strikes until after the election. Tehran says the strait reopens only on its terms, so a deadlock through Nov. 3 is the likeliest outcome.

Russia · Ukraine

Russia launches its biggest attack of the war on Kirovohrad, nearly 360 drones over ten hours, as the energy truce collapses and Trump calls Kyiv’s refinery strikes “a disgrace”

+

Ukraine’s air force said Russia launched 580 drones and two missiles overnight into Thursday and that 536 drones were shot down, with impacts recorded at 31 locations. Gulf · Economy

IMF says Gulf economies won’t return to their pre-war growth paths soon, and forecasts a 4.8% contraction for the GCC this year

+

The IMF’s Middle East and Central Asia director, Jihad Azour, said Thursday that Gulf economies will rebound but will not regain their pre-war growth trajectories in the medium term. He cited lasting damage to several sectors, destroyed infrastructure and the cost of building resilience. The fund now expects the six GCC economies to shrink 4.8% in 2026 before rebounding as much as 9.1% in 2027. Saudi Arabia is projected to contract 3.3%, Kuwait 16.7% and Iraq 16%. “These buffers bought time, but they are finite,” Azour said of the inventories and spare capacity that have softened the oil shock. “Renewed disruption could generate much larger price adjustments.” Brent is about 47% above its pre-war level.

Analysis: The outlook arrives as Houthi strikes on Saudi Arabia escalate. An attack on Riyadh’s airport on Oct. 10 killed at least 12 people, and Aramco’s headquarters was hit this week. The IMF’s point is that the damage outlasts the shooting: the region’s fiscal cushions are running down while the strait stays contested.

Quantum Computing

Google shows “magic state cultivation” on its Willow chip, cutting errors about 40-fold on a key ingredient for fault-tolerant quantum computers

+

In a paper published in Science on Thursday, Google Quantum AI reported the first hardware demonstration of magic state cultivation on its 105-qubit Willow processor. Magic states are special quantum states needed for the non-Clifford gates that universal, error-corrected quantum computing requires, and producing them has been one of the field’s most expensive bottlenecks. Google cites estimates that they could take up to 95% of a quantum computer’s resources. The team, which includes Craig Gidney, Noah Shutty and Cody Jones, reached a fidelity of at least 0.9999, with errors about 40 times lower than for uncultivated states. It then moved the states from a distance-3 color code into a distance-5 surface code.

The cost is the discard rate: only about 8% of attempts were kept. Sundar Pichai called the result “magical progress,” which is the company’s framing. Google itself says the technique has to be shown at larger scales before it matters for practical machines.

AI · Chips

Nvidia releases Nemotron 3.5 open models, a 120B multimodal model for enterprise agents and a 24GB model that runs on a desktop RTX card, with training data included

+

Nvidia expanded its Nemotron family of open models on Thursday. The headline release, Nemotron 3.5 Super VL, is a 120-billion-parameter mixture-of-experts model that uses 12 billion parameters per token and handles text, images, documents and video for AI agents. Nvidia says it matches a much larger reasoning model on multimodal agent tasks at about a fifth of the size. It is available now on Hugging Face, OpenRouter and as an Nvidia NIM microservice. It was built with partners in a “Nemotron Coalition” that includes Mistral AI, Thinking Machines Lab, Perplexity and Black Forest Labs.

Two edge models came with it. Bolt 74B-A9B fits in 24GB of memory for RTX and DGX Spark machines and is available now. Bolt E4B, a 4GB model for voice and chat agents, ships Oct. 23. Nvidia is publishing the datasets and training recipes along with the weights, under its OpenMDW license. That goes further than most “open-weight” releases from U.S. labs. The launch landed in a rough week for chip stocks, which CNBC says shed more than $1 trillion in AI-linked market value after the hot inflation report.

Electrification

Europe sets a monthly EV sales record of nearly 600,000 as North America falls 44% a year after the U.S. tax credit expired

+

Benchmark Mineral Intelligence data reported by Electrek shows Europe sold almost 600,000 electric vehicles in September, up 39% from a year earlier and about 10% above the previous record set in June. Battery-electric sales rose about 50% and plug-in hybrids 16%. North American sales fell 44% year on year. Most of that drop is a comparison effect: September 2025 was the last month before the federal EV tax credit expired, so many buyers bought early. Benchmark expects the decline to narrow from the fourth quarter.

Global sales dipped 2% to 2.15 million, the first year-on-year decline in seven months, as China fell 18%. Global sales are still up about 3% for the year so far. With oil prices high because of the Iran war, Europe’s surge suggests the fuel-price shock is pushing buyers toward electric cars where policy support remains in place.

Sources: Electrek, Oct 15
EVs · Tesla

Nine years late, Tesla unveils the new Roadster at a SpaceX test site, claims 0–60 in under a second, gives no price, and says production starts in 2028

+

Tesla held its long-delayed Roadster event Thursday night at SpaceX’s McGregor, Texas, test site, two weeks after weather postponed it. It showed three versions: a two-seat Performance model, a “Thrust” version with rear cold-gas thrusters, and a single-seat “Rocket” that Tesla marks as not approved for public roads. Claimed specs include 0–60 mph in under 1.6 seconds for the Performance model, under a second for the top version, a top speed above 230 mph and more than 300 miles of range. A run on stage clocked about 0.81 seconds. Accounts differ on which version made that run.

The specs fall short of what Tesla promised in 2017: 620 miles of range, more than 250 mph and 2+2 seating. Tesla gave no price. Reservations require $5,000 now and $45,000 within ten days, and the only official price is still the 2017 base figure of $200,000. Executives said production begins in 2028. Coverage noted that the event was heavy on stunts and light on detail.
