House · Democrats · Leadership

Jeffries calls for a recorded, tallied vote on his speaker nomination as House Democrats prepare to pick their leaders on Wednesday

+

House Democrats will meet behind closed doors on Wednesday to choose their leaders for the new Congress, which starts January 3. Their majority comes from this month’s midterm wins. The AP reports that Hakeem Jeffries, Katherine Clark and Pete Aguilar are running unopposed for the top three posts. Most other leadership races are expected to be settled by voice vote. On Tuesday Axios and Punchbowl reported that Jeffries will ask for a recorded, counted vote on his speaker nomination instead of the usual nomination by acclamation. Al Jazeera and others read this as a way to take away the progressives’ leverage: they had threatened to demand a vote themselves, and now they would have to go on record against him even though no challenger has declared. Winning the nomination is only the first step. Jeffries still needs a majority of the whole House on the floor in January.

Some members still object. Several incoming progressives say they are uncommitted. Rep.-elect Don Leonard of Ohio has set conditions on campaign finance and Israel aid. The AP notes complaints about Jeffries’ private meeting with Jared Kushner in August and about a proposed rule that would penalize members who defy leadership on procedural votes. An online petition urging Rep. Ayanna Pressley to run has passed 110,000 signatures, but the Boston Globe reports she has no plans to run. One pre-vote count found 49 Democrats pledged to Jeffries, 23 uncommitted and none opposed. Time and TheGrio note that he would be the first Black speaker of the House.

Banking · Epstein · Trump

Leaked Deutsche Bank files show $582 million in flagged Epstein-linked payments and a 2017 slip that sent Trump’s tax documents to an outside accountant; Trump calls the disclosure “illegal”

+

A consortium of journalists that includes the Financial Times and the New York Times published findings on Tuesday from about 15 million leaked Deutsche Bank files. According to the FT, the bank filed suspicious-activity reports covering about $582 million in Epstein-linked transactions between 2013 and 2019. That is more than twice the roughly $250 million Senate investigators had identified. The flagged payments include money sent to women, transfers routed through shell companies, and possible misuse of charitable donations. A suspicious-activity report is not a finding that a crime occurred. The files also show that in March 2017 a staff member sent year-end tax statements for 1,586 private-banking accounts, including three of Trump’s, to another client’s accountant. The White House was not told. Christian Sewing, who ran private banking then and is now CEO, called it “an awful mistake.” The files also report suspicious payments to Trump companies in 2017 and 2019 that bank analysts linked to Russian and Azerbaijani laundering networks. Those reports did not identify the companies as Trump’s, and the outlet Follow the Money says the files do not show Trump took part in any laundering.

Asked about the tax disclosure in Manila, Trump said, “Well, I paid a lot of tax,” and called it illegal. Deutsche Bank says the cases go back as much as two decades and that it reviewed them at the time and “worked closely with regulators.” Its chair backed Sewing. The shares fell more than 6% on Tuesday before recovering some of the loss. Reuters said it could not immediately verify the leaked records.

Global Affairs
Iran · Gulf · U.S. Military

U.S. jets strike a Revolutionary Guards missile site on Qeshm Island after Iran’s third drone attack on Bahrain, as Trump warns Tehran over a reported dirty-bomb plot

+

On Tuesday U.S. fighter jets used precision munitions on an IRGC missile launch site on Iran’s Qeshm Island, in the Strait of Hormuz. U.S. military officials called it self-defense. The strike came hours after the Guards said they had targeted a U.S. radar at Bahrain’s Isa Air Base in response to U.S. drone flights over Iran. CENTCOM said several Iranian one-way attack drones were intercepted, with no damage to U.S. equipment and no injuries. A provincial official in Iran said the U.S. strike did not hit residential, commercial or critical sites, and Iranian media said the target was an air-defense launcher. AP reportedly described the exchange as ending a two-month lull, with Iran’s third strike on U.S. targets in Bahrain.

The strike came on top of a CNN report, sourced to seven U.S. officials, that American intelligence shows Iran developing plans to attack senior administration officials in Washington with a dirty bomb or chemical weapon. The officials described the planning as giving Tehran options, not a sign of an imminent attack. In Manila, Trump said that if Iran tried, “they will pay a price the likes of which no country has ever paid before,” and that “we take it very seriously.” The White House said it saw no credible threat to the homeland, and Iran’s foreign ministry called the reports baseless. Analysis: In one day the ceasefire was strained at sea and the rhetoric escalated, while Tehran is still offering its two-day Hormuz plan through Qatari and Pakistani mediators. This raises the stakes for Thursday’s Shenzhen meeting, where Trump, Xi and Putin are expected to discuss the Iran war.

Asia · Trade · Diplomacy

In Manila, Trump and Thailand’s Anutin sign a joint statement on a trade deal that would drop Thai tariffs on U.S. goods, as attention turns to Thursday’s Xi–Putin meeting in Shenzhen

+

After the Quad meeting on Tuesday, Trump and Thai Prime Minister Anutin Charnvirakul signed a joint statement in Manila. Trump said Thailand will eliminate its tariffs on U.S. exports. It would also remove non-tariff barriers on vehicles, medical devices, pharmaceuticals, ethanol and poultry, and buy about $2.6 billion more in U.S. farm goods. Reuters and Thai officials stress that this is not yet a finished agreement. Anutin called it a “substantial conclusion” but said “there is still a little more to do,” and the pact still needs legal drafting and approval by Thailand’s parliament. Thailand had faced a threatened 36% U.S. tariff in 2025, which was later cut to 19%. Trump meets Philippine President Ferdinand Marcos Jr. on Wednesday before flying to Shenzhen.

Analysis: Thursday’s APEC sideline meetings carry more weight. There will be a bilateral with Xi and then the first U.S.–China–Russia leaders’ meeting, with Putin. U.S. officials call the Xi session an “implementation check-in” on a trade truce that expires January 10. They say Trump will not raise the spying allegations involving Nvidia. The White House says nuclear arms reduction is on the agenda and Ukraine is not. Trump goes into these meetings after his party’s midterm losses, and Reuters notes Beijing’s long-standing view that the Quad he just convened is meant to encircle China.

Energy · Senate · Permitting

Senate votes 70–27 to cut off debate on the bipartisan permitting overhaul, setting up final passage before Thanksgiving as House Democrats balk

+

On Monday the Senate voted 70–27 to invoke cloture on Sen. Shelley Moore Capito’s substitute amendment to the Bipartisan American Affordability and Jobs Act. That clears the way for a final Senate vote before the Thanksgiving recess. The bill would speed approval of energy, mining, transmission and other infrastructure projects. It would also limit legal challenges brought under the National Environmental Policy Act, the Clean Water Act, the Endangered Species Act and the National Historic Preservation Act. According to Sen. Martin Heinrich, the substitute was changed over the weekend in response to concerns from tribal communities and labor unions. Reports say the changes add provisions on data centers and let tribal and state agencies act as “lead agency” on reviews. Adopting the substitute and passing the bill are still separate votes.

The coalition behind the bill is bipartisan, and so is the opposition. Sen. Jeff Merkley has said he will vote against final passage if the fossil-fuel provisions in Title III stay in the bill. Reports say close to half of the Senate Democratic caucus has reservations. The Congressional Progressive Caucus has formally opposed the bill as drafted, and six state attorneys general have asked Senate leaders to reject it. The House is the bigger problem: Democratic leader Hakeem Jeffries opposes the bill as written and has said he will not “rubber-stamp” whatever the Senate sends. Only a few weeks remain before Democrats take control of both chambers in January.

More than 90 legal scholars, writing through the Center for Progressive Reform, urged senators to remove 12 provisions, arguing that even under favorable modeling the bill leaves emissions about where they are now. Progressives quoted by MS NOW say Democrats negotiated “from a place of weakness.” On the other side, climate investor Tom Steyer has urged progressives to back the bill for its transmission provisions.

Before leaving for Asia, Trump presses Congress for air-traffic-control money after the ground stop, while GOP appropriators doubt a $50 billion check

Russia · Ukraine · Crimea

A section of the Crimean Bridge’s road span appears to fall into the Kerch Strait after a second straight night of Ukrainian strikes, and Russia halts traffic

+

Ukraine struck the Crimean Bridge for a second night in a row early on November 17. Footage circulating online appeared to show the main arch of the road span collapsed into the Kerch Strait. Reuters, as cited by other outlets, said the footage and the extent of the damage could not be independently verified, and Russian authorities have not confirmed a collapse. Russia’s transport ministry suspended road and rail traffic. Kyiv Post reported that the road later reopened to alternating one-way traffic. Moscow opened a terrorism investigation. Zelensky called the attack a “seven-phase operation” using Palianytsia drones on the road section and Neptune missiles plus Ruta and FP-5 Flamingo weapons on the rail section. Independent confirmation of which weapons hit which spans was not available.

Kremlin spokesman Dmitry Peskov said a restoration plan would be drawn up and that the bridge would be “even better” than before. Crimea’s Russian-installed head, Sergey Aksyonov, told people to stay away from the crossing. The strike came hours after Russian ballistic missiles hit Kyiv’s Northern and Southern bridges over the Dnipro. Ukrainian officials say Russia also hit energy targets that cut power in eight regions. Both sides are now targeting the other’s transport links as well as its energy system ahead of winter. The Crimean Bridge is Russia’s main land supply route to occupied Crimea, and wreckage in the strait could also disrupt shipping between the Black Sea and the Sea of Azov.

Iran war · Strait of Hormuz

Iran’s foreign minister says a 10-nation regional bloc backs Tehran’s plan to reopen the Strait of Hormuz within two days, on Iran’s terms

+

Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi told reporters in Islamabad on Monday that the Economic Cooperation Organization had “responded positively” to Iran’s two-day plan to reopen the Strait of Hormuz. He said the group hoped the plan would lead to “a definitive and comprehensive ceasefire.” Officials from Pakistan, Türkiye, Azerbaijan, Kazakhstan, Kyrgyzstan, Tajikistan, Turkmenistan and Uzbekistan attended; Afghanistan, also an ECO member, did not. Under the plan, worked out with Qatari mediators, Iran would reopen the strait within two days if Washington meets its conditions. Those include sanctions relief, release of frozen funds, an end to the U.S. naval blockade, and recognition of a shipping route drawn up by Iran and Oman. Iran had first proposed seven days, and Qatar had suggested five. Pakistani outlets reported that Araghchi also met army chief Field Marshal Asim Munir.

The endorsement so far rests on Araghchi’s own account. Other attendees have not publicly confirmed it, and one regional outlet said it found no independent confirmation. No U.S. response has been reported. The proposal comes as the ceasefire is fraying: Iran attacked a U.S. base in Bahrain last week, and the U.S. struck Qeshm Island earlier in the month. The ECO’s membership is mostly Central Asian and Turkic states rather than the Gulf states most exposed to the closure, so even if the endorsement is real, it adds little pressure on Washington.

Climate · Energy · Data centers

Harvard-led study estimates America’s 403 biggest data centers emitted about as much CO2 as Denmark in a year, with over half their power from fossil fuels

+

A Harvard-led study published Monday in Nature Sustainability estimates that the 403 largest U.S. hyperscale data centers used about 82 terawatt-hours of electricity between May 2024 and April 2025. That is roughly 1.8% of U.S. electricity, or about what Michigan uses. The researchers estimate the centers emitted about 25.7 million metric tons of CO2, within a range of 21 to 31 million and comparable to Denmark’s annual emissions. About 54% of their electricity came from fossil fuels. Companies do not publicly report facility-level power use, so the team modeled it. Outside experts quoted in AP-syndicated coverage called the approach reasonable.

Some caveats apply. The study does not cover data centers that generate their own power on site, which is a growing practice. Its data end in April 2025, before much of the current AI build-out came online. Its central estimate is lower than in the team’s earlier preprint, which put emissions at 37–54 million tonnes. Even so, it gives one of the first facility-level baselines for the sector. That matters for the Senate permitting bill (see Politics), which includes data-center provisions, and for state fights over who pays for new gas plants built to serve data centers.

Computing · Supercomputers

The new TOP500 list leaves the top 10 unchanged, with China’s LineShine still No. 1, and records the fewest new systems in its 33-year history as AI data centers absorb hardware

+

The 68th TOP500 ranking of the world’s supercomputers was released Monday at the SC26 conference in Chicago. China’s LineShine kept first place at 2.198 exaflops on the HPL benchmark. El Capitan, Frontier, Aurora and Europe’s JUPITER Booster follow, and the entire top 10 is the same as in June. Only 27 new machines made the list, about 5.4% of it, the lowest turnover since the ranking began in 1993. The highest new entry is South Korea’s KISTI system at No. 12, with 342.6 petaflops. Lux, an AMD-based Department of Energy machine at Oak Ridge, debuted at No. 14.

Coverage linked the low turnover to AI: the newest accelerators are going into commercial AI clusters that are never benchmarked for the list, not into public scientific machines. AMD says its chips are in 202 of the 500 systems, more than 40%, and in half of the new entries. Those are vendor figures. The list measures traditional 64-bit scientific computing, so it no longer reflects where most new computing capacity is being built.

English football · Premier League

Bottom-placed Crystal Palace sack Pierre Sage after four months and appoint former Brentford and Spurs manager Thomas Frank

+

Crystal Palace named Thomas Frank as head coach during the international break. He replaces Pierre Sage, who was sacked about four months into the job with Palace bottom of the Premier League after 10 matches. Under Sage, Palace lost seven of those 10 league games and won only once in the Europa League, though they did reach the Carabao Cup quarter-finals. Chairman Steve Parish said he was “delighted” to bring in a manager with Frank’s Premier League experience. The appointment brings Frank back to English management after an eight-month spell at Tottenham that ended badly, which followed seven successful years at Brentford. His first game is at home to newly promoted Coventry City on Saturday, as Palace look for their third win of the season in all competitions. Elsewhere in the break, Northern Ireland play Hungary in Budapest on Tuesday needing to avoid defeat to win promotion to Nations League A. Michael O’Neill’s team are unbeaten and have not conceded a goal in the group, and he will not be on the touchline.
