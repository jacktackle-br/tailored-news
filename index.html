Commerce · Nvidia · China

The Nvidia scandal reaches Howard Lutnick: aides weigh his future and Trump says he still has confidence in him, while Congress lines up December testimony from him and Huang

+

Reuters reported Thursday that senior White House officials are discussing whether Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick should be pushed out over the Jensen Huang scandal, citing two people familiar with the matter. A White House official answered on the record that Trump “maintains complete confidence in the secretary and his ability to carry out the position,” and Axios reported the same day that the president is not distancing himself from Lutnick. The case against Lutnick rests on two Axios reports. One says Commerce received a classified warning about Huang’s relationship with Alice Huang, the former Megaspeed executive U.S. officials suspect of working for Chinese intelligence. The other says Trump approved action against suspected diversion of Nvidia chips to China three times this year, in May, August and November, and Lutnick stalled each one, warning it “will start a trade war with China.” Both reports rely on anonymous sources. Commerce has not said on the record whether Lutnick saw the memo.

Congress is setting dates. The chairs of the House Foreign Affairs and Intelligence committees and the Select Committee on China have summoned Huang to a closed-door, transcribed interview on December 1 and say they will subpoena him if needed. Senate Banking Chairman Tim Scott asked Huang to appear on December 3 and also called Lutnick. A Lutnick spokesperson said he has “proactively agreed to appear voluntarily,” and CNBC reports the date as December 10. Democrats Ron Wyden and Richard Neal sent Lutnick a letter Thursday expanding their inquiry into the H200 sales to China. Republican Sen. John Kennedy also criticized Lutnick, and a GOP senator separately urged banning Chinese memory maker CXMT from the U.S. market. Reports conflict on whether Huang has formally agreed to the House date. Separately, Reuters reported that Huang had a relationship with a second Nvidia employee.

Economy · Federal Reserve

The Fed that expected rate cuts this year now looks set to hike again in December, with some officials openly discussing a half-point move

+

October’s consumer price index rose 0.4% for the month against a 0.3% forecast, and core prices rose 0.3% for a third straight month. Since then, the Fed’s debate has moved to how much to raise rates, not whether. The central bank already raised rates a quarter point in September, its first increase since 2023. Governor Christopher Waller said he would back another quarter-point hike at the December 8 meeting and might favor a half point if the data worsen. Chicago Fed President Austan Goolsbee said the Fed has “a real inflation issue on our hands.” Dallas Fed President Lorie Logan has argued for at least two more increases. According to CNBC, about 88% of traders expect a quarter-point move, and a Reuters poll found most economists expect a December hike.

There is no official 12-month inflation rate for October, because BLS did not collect October 2025 data during last year’s shutdown. Axios puts its estimate at about 4%. Reuters and Axios both point to higher oil prices from the Iran war and to tariffs as drivers. The rate outlook matters for the lame-duck Congress, which faces a December 11 government-funding deadline with no agreed plan, according to the Washington Post.

Iran · Gulf

Iran’s Revolutionary Guards claim a strike on a U.S. early-warning radar in Bahrain, the first claimed attack on U.S. assets in a Gulf state since September

+

The IRGC said Friday that its aerospace force had hit a U.S. early-warning radar in Bahrain. It called the strike retaliation for an air attack Thursday on a telecommunications tower in Sirik county, in Iran’s Hormozgan province. State outlets IRNA and Tasnim first described that attack only as an “enemy attack.” Later IRGC statements blamed the U.S. military. Neither Washington nor Bahrain had confirmed either the Sirik strike or Iran’s claim when this edition went out. Reuters-style wire copy notes that this is Iran’s first claim of an attack on American military assets in a Gulf country since early September. The Guards also said another tanker hit a mine in the Strait of Hormuz, a claim that could not be independently verified.

The exchange tests what has been a fragile lull, in a war that began with U.S. and Israeli strikes on February 28 and has since settled into a U.S. naval blockade of Iranian ports and a contest over the strait. It follows Trump’s statement Thursday that Washington no longer needs a deal with Tehran. Hegseth has declared U.S.-protected shipping lanes “free and clear.” Iran continues to claim near-daily mine and missile hits on vessels. If the U.S. confirms damage in Bahrain, pressure to resume combat operations will grow, and Rubio has said there is “no need” to restart them.

China · APEC

+

The summit also comes as the Nvidia scandal revives the question of U.S. AI-chip sales to China, an issue Beijing is likely to raise.

Climate · COP31

COP31’s leaders’ summit ends with clashing demands, leaving negotiators in Antalya to choose between an electrification target and a renewed push against fossil fuels

+

Two days of heads-of-state speeches at the UN climate talks in Antalya, Turkey, ended Thursday with the main blocs far apart, Reuters reported. Venezuela’s acting president said his country has a sovereign right to expand oil production, and other producers argued that oil, gas and coal should stay in use. Small island states called for immediate emissions cuts. Saint Lucia’s prime minister said “major emitters must move beyond the usual promises.” Developing countries pressed for more finance. UN Secretary-General António Guterres called for credible national roadmaps away from fossil fuels, which he said “must go the way of the dinosaurs.” The United States is skipping the talks for a second year.

The clearest proposal on the table is the Turkish presidency’s non-binding “35-by-35” pledge to raise electricity’s share of final energy use to 35% by 2035. European leaders and the UK prime minister back it. Critics note that it does not require the new electricity to come from clean sources, and Turkey wants it written into the final decision. Australia’s Chris Bowen, who is running the negotiations, said that is not settled. WWF called the leaders’ speeches underwhelming at the first COP since scientists said the world is set to breach 1.5°C. The talks run to November 20.

Biotech · Regulation

Reuters: the FDA has quietly shelved Makary’s fast-track “national priority voucher” program, which HHS says is ongoing

+

Citing six people familiar with the matter and internal documents, Reuters reported Thursday that FDA leadership has told companies it is not moving forward with the Commissioner’s National Priority Voucher pilot. Former commissioner Marty Makary launched the program in June 2025 to cut some drug reviews to one or two months. Applications are still being accepted but are not advancing. No new vouchers have been awarded since Makary left in May. Twenty-two drugs received vouchers and 12 have since been approved. For the nine still in the program, career reviewers have resumed control of timelines.

HHS and FDA spokespeople disputed the characterization, saying the pilot is ongoing and the agency is refining it after a June public hearing. The report comes as the FDA has been run by an acting commissioner since May. The Senate HELP Committee votes November 18 on nominee Heidi Overton, and Chairman Bill Cassidy has not said whether he will support her.

Science · Biology

A Florida swamp mushroom is the first fungus known to make both psilocybin and the deadly amatoxins of the death cap

+

Researchers at the University of Florida report in Current Biology that Galerina indica, a small brown mushroom from Florida swamps nicknamed the “dark magic mushroom,” produces both the psychedelic compound psilocybin and amatoxins, the toxins behind most fatal mushroom poisonings. No fungus was previously known to make both. The team suspects the genes arrived through horizontal gene transfer.

The authors propose that the combination may work as a defense: fungus-eating animals such as nematodes, which are affected by both classes of compound, would find it hard to evolve resistance to two at once. That idea has not been tested, and the study did not measure the mushroom’s toxicity in animals. The finding is a reminder for foragers that a mushroom that bruises blue, a common sign of psilocybin, can also be lethal. Nature’s news desk covered the study on November 12.

English Football

Kane breaks England’s caps record and scores the winner as England beat Croatia 2–1 to reach the Nations League quarter-finals, and Manchester United appoint Pochettino

+

At Wembley on Thursday, Morgan Rogers put England ahead in the 24th minute from Jude Bellingham’s pass. Petar Sučić equalised on 59 minutes after a soft Jarell Quansah header fell to him. United are in the relegation zone with one league win from ten. United must pay U.S. Soccer a release fee reported at about $10 million, plus more for his staff. Pochettino is expected to take the USMNT for its two Concacaf Nations League quarter-finals against Haiti, on November 14 in Miami and November 17 in Cincinnati, before his first United match at Anfield on November 22. U.S. Soccer has not named a successor.
