Congress · Lame Duck

Congress returns today with a House vote on impeaching Hegseth expected this week, after Trump tells “60 Minutes” he still has confidence in him

+

Lawmakers return to Washington on Monday for the first time since Democrats won control of both chambers. NPR’s preview says the House is likely to vote this week on Rep. Thomas Massie’s effort to impeach Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth over the Iran war. In theory Republicans still have enough votes to defeat it, but the vote could be hard for members in competitive districts. House Democratic leader Hakeem Jeffries said Sunday he is a “hard yes.” Hegseth has called the articles a “sham.” If the House does impeach him, removal would still require a two-thirds vote in the Senate.

In a “60 Minutes” interview that aired Sunday, President Trump said he had confidence in Hegseth and said he might make “a few changes” in his administration, without naming anyone. He also said the Iran war had hurt Republicans in the midterms. CNN describes the impeachment vote as an early test of how much control Trump still has over House Republicans. The lame-duck session also has to deal with government funding, which runs out after December 11, and with the annual defense policy bill, where disputes over funding for the Iran war could come to a head.

White House · Staff

Stephen Miller starts as chief of staff today, after an Indiana Republican says Trump needs to “fire people,” Miller included

+

Stephen Miller takes over as White House chief of staff on Monday, replacing Susie Wiles. Trump announced the move after Republicans lost Congress. On CNN’s “Inside Politics” on Sunday, Rep. Marlin Stutzman (R-Ind.) called the promotion a “bad mistake.” He said a leader whose team takes a beating like that has to “fire people,” and he criticized Miller’s tone and his record on immigration, adding that Miller is not liked in the Midwest. The Washington Post called it a rare public rebuke from a House Republican. Stutzman is backing Rep. Jim Jordan for House GOP leader.

Other Republicans defended the choice. Rep. Jim Banks, also of Indiana, called Miller an “excellent pick,” and Sen. Lindsey Graham also backed him. Democrats said on Sunday that promoting Miller was “really odd” after an election in which voters rejected the administration’s most divisive policies. NBC News reports that the move signals Trump intends to strengthen his ties to the MAGA base.

The Left-Leaning Take

No in-window Mother Jones, Vox, Slate or Guardian opinion piece on Miller’s first day could be verified. The Democratic argument, as reported by the Post, is that elevating the architect of the administration’s immigration agenda ignores what voters said last Tuesday.

The Right-Leaning Take

The right is split. A Reason column argues that Miller, with his hostility to judicial review and his position on birthright citizenship, will “reinforce Trump’s worst instincts” rather than calm them. Banks and Graham describe him as a loyal, experienced aide with strong ties to Republicans on the Hill. Intra-right (anti-Trump): The Bulwark has called the appointment a declaration of war and has asked whether Miller could become the “de facto president.” No National Review, Dispatch or WSJ editorial piece on the appointment could be verified in the window.

Iran War · Strait of Hormuz

Iran says U.S. strikes hit Qeshm Island overnight, which Trump calls “minor,” as Rubio says a breakthrough in talks is unlikely

+

Iranian state media reported four explosions on Qeshm Island, in the Strait of Hormuz, late Sunday. A provincial official told the Fars news agency on Monday that no vital infrastructure or services were hit. A Revolutionary Guards Navy official said navigation aids in the south of the strait were destroyed. Trump confirmed a “minor strike” on the island over the weekend, and Secretary of State Marco Rubio called U.S. actions “defensive.” On Saturday, both sides said ships had been hit. The U.S. military said it fired on the stern of a commercial vessel in the Gulf of Oman and disabled it as it tried to get through the U.S. blockade. The Guards said two tankers caught fire after hitting mines on an “unauthorized route,” and Fars later reported a third. Reuters could not confirm any of these strikes, and Iranian media suggested some of the blasts came from Iran’s own military activity.

The strikes were limited, and that matches Trump’s recent signals. Before the midterms he had hinted at resuming major attacks, but there is no sign of a large-scale operation, and he has said new strikes are “highly unlikely” if negotiations make progress. Rubio said Monday that the talks are not close to a breakthrough and called some of Iran’s demands “outrageous.” Qatar, a mediator, denied reports that the talks had failed. The Strait of Hormuz is still effectively closed: Iranian media said no ships crossed on Sunday.

Israel · Coalition Talks

Eisenkot formally receives the mandate to form Israel’s next government and has 28 days to build a coalition that Liberman says must have 61 seats

+

At a ceremony at the President’s Residence on Sunday, President Isaac Herzog formally gave Gadi Eisenkot, the former army chief who leads the Yashar party, the first chance to form a government. Herzog had announced the choice on Saturday. Factions with 67 of the Knesset’s 120 seats recommended Eisenkot, against 46 for Benjamin Netanyahu, whose right-wing coalition lost its majority in October’s election. Eisenkot has 28 days, and Herzog can extend that by up to 14 more. Netanyahu stays on as caretaker prime minister in the meantime.

The 67 recommendations do not add up to a coalition. Avigdor Liberman of Yisrael Beytenu has ruled out a minority government that depends on abstentions from Ra’am and the Joint List, and he insists on at least 61 lawmakers. Neither Arab-led party has committed to joining Eisenkot’s government or guaranteeing it support. Eisenkot was due to meet Naftali Bennett on Sunday and Liberman afterward. Reports differ on whether the Liberman meeting is set for Monday. If Eisenkot fails, Herzog can give another lawmaker the mandate.

Climate · COP31

COP31 opens in Antalya with an early fight over climate finance and no U.S. delegation

+

The UN climate talks opened Monday in Antalya, Turkey, and run until November 20, with 196 parties and the EU taking part. The first dispute was over money. African negotiators want adaptation finance and trade added to the formal agenda, and the EU, the U.K. and Japan oppose adding them. Disputed agenda items like these are often moved into consultations run by the presidency. UN Secretary-General António Guterres, who is leaving office, told leaders after a year of extreme weather that the time for deflecting responsibility is over. He is also calling for windfall taxes on fossil-fuel companies to help the countries hit hardest.

The State Department told NPR that the U.S. will not send a delegation. Washington is withdrawing from the UN climate convention itself, on top of leaving the Paris Agreement. Turkey’s Murat Kurum is COP31 president, and Australia’s Chris Bowen leads the negotiations. Leaders arrive for their summit on November 11–12. The AP reports that the U.S.-Iran war and the surge in electricity demand from AI are putting pressure on climate goals going into the talks.

Tech · Chips

Nvidia’s board opens an investigation after Jensen Huang admits an affair with a woman intelligence officials call a Chinese spy, and the stock falls about 5% premarket

+

In a note to staff on Sunday, Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang acknowledged an extramarital relationship with a businesswoman whom intelligence agencies identify as a Chinese spy. He apologized and said he took full responsibility. Nvidia’s board has opened an independent investigation, overseen by a committee of independent directors with outside counsel. The company says Huang will keep running Nvidia without interruption. Axios reports that Secretary of State Marco Rubio had earlier warned senior administration officials about the woman. The New York Times reported that she was recruited to seek information and to push for advanced chips to be made available in China. Trump has called Huang “an honorable guy.”

Nvidia shares fell about 5% in premarket trading Monday, according to CNBC. Futures were already fragile after last week’s selloff in AI stocks. Analyst Dan Ives told clients that Huang’s removal is a real possibility, depending on what the board finds. The case matters beyond Nvidia because the company makes the most advanced AI chips and has been at the center of the U.S. fight over chip exports to China.

Energy · Oil Markets

Brent slips toward $97 after its steepest weekly drop since the pandemic began, as Trump says new Iran attacks are “unlikely”

+

Brent crude fell about 0.85% early Monday, to roughly $97.48 a barrel, after Trump said new strikes on Iran are very unlikely while talks progress. CNBC reports that Brent fell about 17% last week. That was its steepest weekly drop since the COVID pandemic began and the largest in dollar terms in LSEG data going back to 1988. Traders had been weighing whether the war would restart now that the midterms are over. Even after the drop, prices are about 50% higher than a year ago, though well below the wartime peak near $120.

Analysts are not sure the decline will last. Shipping through Hormuz is still disrupted, and both sides reported attacks on ships over the weekend. Rubio says the talks are not close to a deal. One KCM Trade analyst said the market is waiting to see whether the election has really changed Trump’s approach. Rapidan Energy Group’s Bob McNally put the odds of fighting resuming at 80% and said prices could go back above $100.
